### How the baseline is handled

The billing guide's Example 1 says a Pro organization with one basic project inside allowances is $25/month, including basic compute after the $15 credit. We use **$25 as a conditional documented baseline**, not a reconciled all-in quote. Database capacity means allocated volume, and a 1 GB application may require more than 1 GB after indexes, auth and platform tables. We assume the project is allocated enough space while remaining inside Pro's 10 GB organization allowance. Shared compute performance and the exact billed allocation remain unverified. Sources: [Billing, Compute/Volume capacity/Example 1](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/billing), [Compute Resources, Pro allocation](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/compute-resources), [Pricing](https://nhost.io/pricing). Checked: 2026-11-04.

Our calculation of the minute rates explains why a precise compute invoice cannot be promised:

- 730 hours × 60 = 43,800 minutes.
- 1 shared vCPU × 43,800 × $0.00034 = $14.892; $25 + max($14.892 − $15 credit, 0) = **$25**.
- A literal reading of the resources page's 2 shared vCPUs gives 2 × 43,800 × $0.00034 = $29.784; $25 + max($29.784 − $15, 0) = **$39.784**.
- A dedicated allocation of 1 vCPU with its paired memory gives 43,800 × $0.0012 = $52.56; $25 + $52.56 − $15 = **$62.56**. The displayed $50/month rate would instead give $60, while billing Example 4 incorrectly prints $66.

These are alternative interpretations of published inputs, **not a supported $25–$39.784 price range or proof that these allocations are sufficient**. No rounding to cents is assumed; minute boundary and invoice rounding may change amounts. Source: [Compute Pricing](https://nhost.io/pricing), [Billing Examples 1/4](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/billing), [Compute Resources](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/compute-resources). Checked: 2026-11-04.

### 1. Small SQL app

Inputs: one database, 1 GB application data for a month, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet egress and 730 always-on hours.

Starter's 1 GB database/5 GB egress allowances superficially match the data and transfer inputs. The 1 GB is not all available to user rows, and reads/writes do not determine CPU. Therefore **$0 is an eligibility candidate, not a confirmed fit or always-on guarantee**; weekly inactivity pause also applies. The published configuration default of 100 connections is not a performance guarantee for 10 simultaneous queries. Sources: [Starter allowances](https://nhost.io/pricing), [Postgres connection configuration](https://docs.nhost.io/reference/configuration), [Allocated volume](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/billing). Checked: 2026-11-04.

Paid-path known formula, under the baseline assumptions: $25 + max(allocated GB − 10, 0) × $0.20 + max(5 − 50, 0) × $0.10 = **$25 conditional known subtotal** when allocated capacity is at most 10 GB. No read/write operation price was found; this is not an invented $0 request charge. CPU sizing and billing mapping prevent an exact full total. If organization allowances were already used elsewhere, the incremental capacity/egress component for 1 additional allocated GB and 5 GB transfer would be 1 × $0.20 + 5 × $0.10 = **$0.70**, before compute and other unknowns. Sources: [Usage rates](https://nhost.io/pricing), [Aggregate usage and compute](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/billing). Checked: 2026-11-04.

Backup variant: daily database backups with 7-day retention are included in Pro; file bytes/Run disks are excluded. PITR starts at an additional $100, so $25 + $100 = **at least $125 conditional known subtotal**, with its precise add-on meter unresolved. A high-availability database variant cannot be priced from GraphQL/Auth/Storage replicas; the replica guide says Postgres is coming next. Sources: [Backups/PITR/exclusions](https://docs.nhost.io/products/database/backups), [PITR add-on](https://nhost.io/pricing), [Supported replica services](https://docs.nhost.io/platform/cloud/service-replicas). Checked: 2026-11-04.

### 2. Full app backend

Inputs: SQL scenario plus 1,000 monthly active users, 1 GB files, 100,000 file reads, 10,000 uploads, **20 GB total** egress and 10,000 functions. Assume each function lasts 1 second and consumes **1 GB billed memory**.
