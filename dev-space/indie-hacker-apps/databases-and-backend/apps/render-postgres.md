## Cost examples — our calculation

Checked 2026-11-05. Use a PostgreSQL 18 instance in **Oregon**, with no other workspace services consuming the included bandwidth. The checked [price table](https://render.com/pricing) does not publish region-specific rates. This is a price calculation, not a query benchmark or vendor quote. Prices retain `$`; tax and any currency ambiguity remain as recorded above.

Sources for inputs: [Pricing, Postgres and workspace plans](https://render.com/pricing), [Compute Plans](https://render.com/docs/compute-plans), [Create & Connect, storage/limits](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-creating-connecting), [Outbound Bandwidth](https://render.com/docs/outbound-bandwidth), and the official [hosting-cost explanation, Postgres and estimation worksheet](https://render.com/articles/how-much-does-cloud-application-hosting-cost-for-small-businesses). Compute and allocated storage are separate; the current table does not promise that the first paid GB is free.

**Time convention:** the directory's always-on workload uses 730 hours. Render publishes monthly prices and prorates to the second, with calendar-month invoicing in [Terms](https://render.com/terms). We show the **normal full-month price**, normalized to the 730-hour comparison convention. We do not invent a vendor hourly rate or an exact invoice across calendar-month boundaries. For partial periods, the general formula is `monthly rate × billed seconds / applicable monthly denominator`; the exact denominator/rounding was not established from the inspected pages.

### Small SQL app

Inputs: one production database, 1 GB stored, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer, always available for the comparison month.

- Workspace: Hobby, **$0/month**. Paid database compute does not itself require a paid workspace.
- Smallest priced paid database candidate: **0.1c-256mb**, **$6/month**, 0.1 CPU/256 MB, **100 direct connections**.
- At a 1 GB allocation, the arithmetic price floor is `$6 + 1 × $0.30 = $6.30/month`. But the workload already contains 1 GB of data. This leaves no room for indexes, WAL, filesystem/engine overhead or growth; a full database can be suspended.
- **Named 5 GB allocation variant:** choose 5 GB, the next permitted allocation above 1 GB, assuming the complete database including overhead fits it. Storage `5 × $0.30 = $1.50/month`.
- Transfer: `max(0, 5 GB − 5 GB Hobby allowance) × $0.15 = $0`, if no other resource consumes that shared allowance.
- Conditional full-month sum: `$0 workspace + $6 compute + $1.50 storage + $0 egress = $7.50`.

Ten peak connections fit the published connection ceiling. The read/write counts do **not** show that 0.1 CPU and 256 MB meet the application's latency/performance needs. This is the smallest priced candidate, not a verified smallest adequate machine. A larger chosen instance changes the compute line. Storage autoscaling can also permanently grow the allocation beyond the chosen 5 GB.

**Free path:** Free is $0 for at most 30 days, one instance with 1 GB capacity and no managed backup/pooling. It is explicitly unsuitable for production and does not provide lasting retention. A literal 730 hours is longer than 30 days (`30 × 24 = 720 hours`), so the time-limited offer cannot cover this entire fixed always-on production workload. Do not publish a recurring free total. [Free Postgres rules](https://render.com/docs/free).

**More bandwidth:** if all other assumptions stay fixed but public transfer is 20 GB, Hobby egress is `(20 − 5) × $0.15 = $2.25`, giving **$9.75/month**. If the app and database communicate over the same-region private network, that internal traffic is exempt; only the actual qualifying external bytes count. [Traffic rules](https://render.com/docs/outbound-bandwidth).

### Backup and workspace variants

[PITR](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups) is part of paid database capabilities, with no separately listed PITR meter in the checked price table. The $7.50 Hobby example has a **3-day** recovery window. Upgrading the workspace to Pro changes the sum to `$25 + $6 + $1.50 = $32.50/month`, with **7-day** PITR and a 25 GB shared transfer allowance.

Paid on-demand logical exports are retained **7 days** on both workspace plans. Long-term external backup storage and export transfer need their own costs; this example does not provision an S3 bucket or a backup cron service. PITR excludes the latest ten minutes and restores into an additional billed database; its duration and allocation must be added if a restore is run. A database backup does not preserve application assets stored elsewhere.

### High availability and replica variants

[HA requirements](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability) need at least 1 CPU, so the $6 instance cannot enable it. The lowest listed qualifying compute plan is **1c-2g at $40/month**. Keep the named 5 GB allocation for this 1 GB data workload:

`2 × ($40 compute + 5 GB × $0.30 storage) = $83/month`

This covers the primary and matching standby under a Hobby workspace, before extra transfer or additional resources. The standby is in the same region in another zone, cannot serve read queries, and asynchronous failover can lose recent writes. This is our resource-cost sum, not an SLA or zero-data-loss claim.

[Read replicas](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-read-replicas) instead require at least 0.5 CPU and 10 GB storage. The lowest listed qualifying plan is **0.5c-1g at $19/month**. Primary plus one matching read replica:

`2 × ($19 compute + 10 GB × $0.30 storage) = $44/month`

No HA standby is included in that $44 variant. A replica's lag means it may not immediately return a newly committed primary write.

### Idle project

Keep the same **5 GB allocation**, no requests, zero job execution, and keep the paid primary **online** for the comparison month:

`$0 Hobby + $6 provisioned compute + 5 × $0.30 retained storage + $0 outbound = $7.50/month`.

The 1 GB price-floor variant would be $6.30/month if the complete retained database fits inside 1 GB. Once storage grows, it cannot be reduced in place.

Manual suspension exists in the [CLI](https://render.com/docs/cli-reference), but the reviewed pricing, database docs, [maintenance guide](https://render.com/docs/platform-maintenance) and [Terms](https://render.com/terms) did not establish the complete residual Postgres bill or retention limit while suspended. No suspended-database total is published here. The Free web-service 15-minute sleep rule and Sandbox idle suspension do not prove a Postgres scale-to-zero offer.

### Small vector search app

Fixed inputs: 100,000 vectors, 1,536 float32 values/vector, 1 KiB metadata/vector; 100,000 searches and 10,000 updates/month; 5 GB internet transfer.

Our raw-payload calculation:

`100,000 × (1,536 × 4 + 1,024) = 716,800,000 bytes = 0.7168 decimal GB`.

This excludes PostgreSQL row overhead, vector indexes, WAL, temporary files and backup/replica copies. The dimension count is within the current upstream pgvector HNSW vector limit, conditional on a compatible installed extension. [Render extension support](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-extensions); [upstream pgvector limits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pgvector/pgvector/master/README.md).

Using 5 GB allocated storage and the smallest paid compute gives a **$7.50/month price floor** as above, with the same shared transfer assumption. It is **not** a confirmed adequate vector configuration: index size, peak memory, CPU and query latency are unknown. For comparison, choosing 0.5c-1g changes the floor to `$19 + $1.50 = $20.50/month`, still without a capacity/performance guarantee. Embedding generation, reranking and any app worker are additional. **Complete appropriate-configuration total remains unknown.**
