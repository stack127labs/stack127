# RabbitMQ

**Current status and ownership notice:** RabbitMQ is active and still releasing the open-source server. Broadcom completed its VMware acquisition on 22 November 2023. Current RabbitMQ pages identify VMware Tanzu, a Broadcom business, as the owner/steward and seller of the commercial edition. No shutdown, retirement, or later acquisition notice was found in the RabbitMQ blog, GitHub releases, Broadcom support notices, or Broadcom newsroom pages checked.

**License and support notice:** The RabbitMQ server and tier 1 plugins remain MPL-2.0. Some server OCF files use Apache-2.0. A 2024 policy announcement changed who receives direct help from the core team and how older versions get patches; it did not change the open-source license. Community help is best-effort, while Tanzu RabbitMQ is a paid, per-core subscription with additional closed-source features, longer patch support, and 24/7 commercial support.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- The 4.3 community-support end date differs across official pages: Release Information says 31 January 2027; the older Commercial Features page says 30 November 2026. This file follows the current release table and flags the difference; Broadcom's portal governs commercial lifecycle dates.
- Community support pages say best-effort public support, while the 2024 policy narrows direct core-team assistance. The record distinguishes public community channels from guaranteed or direct engineer support.
- No public Tanzu per-core price or minimum subscription was found. The operator must get contract terms from Broadcom or its authorized licensing channel.
- No one-size-fits-all maximum throughput, total queue count, or production machine size is published. Queue type, message size, disk, topology, persistence, and replication change capacity.
- A future-delay maximum for RabbitMQ 4.4 quorum messages was not published in the checked release article, quorum queue guide, or limits guide.
- RabbitMQ's website Terms of Use link redirects to Broadcom's Terms of Use page, which the web reader rendered only as a single embedded iframe. The software license was verified in the repository, and Tanzu commercial rights in Broadcom's product-specific program document; website-use terms were not used as software-license evidence.
- Broadcom's privacy policy was checked. Because this record covers self-hosted software rather than a RabbitMQ-managed SaaS, that policy is described only for Broadcom website/support interactions and not applied to locally stored message bodies.
