## Cost examples — our calculations

All monetary examples use **USD**, excluding taxes, as specified by the [Cloud Terms, fees section](https://www.dragonflydb.io/cloud-terms). They are components, not account quotes. We use the [Cloud Pricing example's AWS us-east-1 Enhanced rate of USD 11.0 per provisioned GB-month](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/pricing), its supported 12.5 GB example shape and its 730-hour normalized month. Standard/Extreme, other regions, Business/BYOC, SSD-tiered configurations and negotiated commitments need their own rates. No trial credit, assumed compression saving or provider account-wide free-transfer allowance is applied.

### Small vector-search app

Inputs: 100,000 vectors, 1,536 float32 values/vector, 1 KiB metadata/vector, 100,000 searches and 10,000 updates/month, 5 GB internet transfer.

Our calculation of **raw data only**, using four bytes/float32 and 1 KiB = 1,024 bytes:

- Vector bytes = 100,000 × 1,536 × 4 = **614,400,000 bytes**.
- Metadata bytes = 100,000 × 1,024 = **102,400,000 bytes**.
- Sum = **716,800,000 bytes**, before record keys/JSON or hash representation, allocator overhead, FLAT/HNSW index structures, search working memory, replicas and headroom.

[FT.CREATE](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/command-reference/search/ft.create) supports FLOAT32 and FLAT/HNSW but does not publish a universal RAM multiplier, maximum dimensions or billable-size conversion. Do not equate that raw sum with provisioned Cloud GB. If a 12.5 GB AWS us-east-1 Enhanced primary were selected and proved suitable, its allocated-memory component would still be **12.5 × USD 11.0 = USD 137.50/month**; this is a conditional illustration, not a fit guarantee or cheapest-plan result. [Cloud Pricing](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/pricing) does not add a separate per-search or per-vector price in its standard price summary. Embedding/reranking models, exact transfer, backup charges and actual index allocation remain unknown. **Full vector-app cost: incomplete.**

### Small job workload and retry/wait variant

Inputs: 10,000 jobs/month, one second of active work/job, 0.5 vCPU and 0.5 GB worker RAM where available, five peak concurrent jobs; base case has no retries or waits.

[BullMQ](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/integrations/bullmq) and [Sidekiq](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/integrations/sidekiq) use Dragonfly as their broker and require separately hosted workers. Our base calculation is **10,000 × 1 = 10,000 active worker-seconds**, not 10,000 billable Dragonfly function invocations. No worker-host rate was chosen, so worker compute cannot be priced. Queue data size/retention, worker network placement and broker memory requirements are also unspecified. The named 12.5 GB example would contribute **USD 137.50/month** if kept active, independently of the number of jobs, subject to that allocation being adequate.

Retry variant: one extra full one-second attempt per job gives **20,000 active worker-seconds**. Waiting variant: 30 seconds per job gives **300,000 waiting seconds**, or **310,000 total job seconds** including the base work; an external host/framework decides whether those waits consume billable worker capacity. These timing calculations are not a worker bill, and the database's memory charges continue while its store remains active. Failed-job history, logs, model/API calls and transfer are additional unknown inputs. **Full job cost: incomplete.**

### Other reference scenarios

The full-backend scenario adds 1,000 monthly active users, 1 GB of independent files, 100,000 file reads, 10,000 uploads, 20 GB internet transfer and 10,000 short function calls. A bundled app-user auth/file/function platform was not established in [Data Stores](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/datastores), [Getting Started](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/getting-started) or [Cloud Terms](https://www.dragonflydb.io/cloud-terms); those external services and a function duration/memory are missing. **No full-backend total** is offered.

The 100 GB hot file-store scenario does not fit a native Dragonfly file-bucket product. Binary strings and external S3 snapshot destinations are not a substitute file-storage tariff. This scenario has **no applicable native file-store total**, rather than a fabricated zero.

MCP documents [manual stop/start](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/mcp) and permanent deletion. [Pricing](https://www.dragonflydb.io/docs/cloud/pricing) says active stores are charged and deleted stores are not. Public documentation does not settle retained data and all continuing charges during a stop, so the idle estimate above keeps the active datastore. It does not assume stop-and-resume safely retains data for free.
