### 1. Small SQL app

Inputs: one production PostgreSQL database; 1 GB stored for the whole month; 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer, and continuous availability across the approved **730 hours**. For allowance checking, assume 5 GB is the combined inbound/outbound database traffic for this one account, with no separate high-volume migration.

Solo is the smallest published always-on PostgreSQL path: $5/month, 10 GB account storage, 768 MB / 1 vCPU pool and one database pinned awake. Its documented finite client cap is 100, despite the contradictory “uncapped” pricing headline.

**Our calculation:** $5 base + $0 selected pool blocks + $0 extra seats + $0 metered query/storage/transfer invoice = **$5/month before any applicable tax**, conditional on the workload actually fitting CPU/RAM and the stated quotas. There is no 730 × hourly rate: the plan bills monthly regardless of activity. Read/write counts alone do not establish that the chosen capacity meets a performance target.

The 5 GB assumption is below Solo's 25-GB daily, 125-GB seven-day and 375-GB thirty-day transfer thresholds. Real incoming query bodies, results and other account traffic also count; if the supplied 5 GB describes only outbound traffic, total policy usage must include inbound too.

**Free alternative:** an eligible pre-launch/personal project with 1 GB and 10 connections can have **$0/month** plan cost if it meets Free's other rules. It must sleep at least 25% of each rolling week and cannot be held awake by artificial pings. It therefore **does not satisfy this continuously available 730-hour scenario**. A recurring-revenue production product also belongs on a paid plan regardless of its numerical fit. The Pro trial is a separate seven-day promotion, not a free monthly production path.

**Backup variant:** Solo's scheduled dumps with seven-day retention and three manual slots are included in the **$5** plan. Exact cadence conflicts between the daily pricing label and 12-hour detailed guide. **PITR variant:** always-on PostgreSQL on Pro with PITR explicitly enabled gives a **$15/month plan subtotal** with 30-day regular dump retention; precise PITR lookback is not publicly specified. **HA variant:** unavailable as a managed standby option at any plan, so no fabricated second-instance quote is added.

A single-tenant alternative is **$35/month** for the US 4-GB Dedicated server. ### 5. Small job workload

Inputs: 10,000 jobs/month, one second active execution each, 0.5 vCPU/0.5 GB RAM at the external receiver, peak five concurrent jobs, no initial retries or waits. For VQueue admission, assume small 1-KiB bodies and that pending plus failed local payloads stay below 1,000 per account/server. The receiver must return success within VQueue's 15-second attempt deadline.

**Our calculation:** 10,000 × 1 second = **10,000 active receiver-seconds**. VQueue coordination adds **$0 separate queue subscription** to an eligible Free Valkey database; a paid production account has at least the **$5 Solo plan**. Complete cost = chosen database plan + external worker execution/hosting + external logs/transfer.

**Result: incomplete total and unresolved recurring-workload fit.** VQueue's bounded history is **10,000 records per account**, not a resettable allowance of 10,000 jobs every month. Archives have no automatic deletion and removal remains blocked pending recovery qualification. The approved monthly workload reaches that history ceiling, and admission can stop there. No published paid-tier increase/cleanup path was established, so upgrading to Solo or Pro is not assumed to solve it. Five concurrent external workers are also not an included Layerbase resource allotment.

**Retry/wait variant, our calculation:** if 1,000 jobs retry once, 11,000 total attempts × 1 second = **11,000 active receiver-seconds**. Delaying 1,000 jobs by 60 seconds adds **60,000 queued waiting-seconds**, within the published seven-day delay feature. No separate waiting/attempt fee is published for VQueue, but quota and external worker charges still apply; receiver duplicates need durable idempotency. A delayed webhook is different from an external worker staying allocated during a wait.

PostgreSQL pg_cron is an alternative only for SQL jobs. Its default one/two concurrent jobs and skipped sleep-time schedules do not satisfy an arbitrary five-concurrent code-worker workload, and pg_net/HTTP callbacks are unsupported.

### 6. Idle project

Inputs: retain the same 1 GB SQL data as scenario 1, zero requests and zero jobs for the month.

**Our calculation:**

- Existing eligible Free project: $0 subscription + $0 usage invoice = **$0/month** while within its quota/usage rules.
- Solo retained unchanged: $5 subscription + $0 metered idle execution + $0 extra selected resources = **$5/month**, before any tax.
- Pro retained unchanged: **$15/month**; any selected $10 pool blocks and $3 seats remain billed until removed.
- US 4-GB Dedicated server retained unchanged: **$35/month**, even with zero requests.

A Free database previously used will hibernate, then archive after about 14 hibernated days; a never-connected one can archive after seven days. Archive means connection attempts fail until explicit Restore. The 90-day archived offload is a later stage, with last backup kept; do not confuse it with a 90-day expiry of the Free offer. Paid databases do not auto-archive, but unpinned Standard databases can sleep. None of that automatically refunds a monthly paid plan.

If a paid plan is cancelled, it remains paid/active through the current period and then moves to Free limits. Extra/unsupported databases can lock after warning; data is retained. This is not an immediate prorated way to turn an idle paid month's cost into zero.
