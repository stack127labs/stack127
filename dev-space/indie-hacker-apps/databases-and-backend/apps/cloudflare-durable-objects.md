## Cost examples — our calculations

All inputs and tariffs were checked on 2026-11-05. These are proposed workloads, not measurements or vendor quotes. Use new SQLite-backed objects and an EU jurisdiction constraint for persisted data. The published USD rates are global rather than a quoted Frankfurt/London price. Taxes are additional. Account allocations are otherwise unused; an existing application's usage would reduce them. Source sections: [DO pricing, compute/storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), [Workers pricing, account minimum and caller meters](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/), [location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/) and [Self-Serve terms, 4.4 Taxes](https://www.cloudflare.com/terms/).

### Small database app

Reference inputs: one production database, 1 GB stored for the month, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak clients and 5 GB internet transfer. Durable Objects do not expose ten SQL connections: callers invoke object code. For a conditional example, place the database in one object and assume **1 GB is its complete billable size including indexes and metadata**, every read/write is one separate object request, each request keeps the object active for 0.1 seconds without overlapping other requests, and there is no remaining non-hibernatable idle time. Assume the queries bill exactly 100,000 rows read and 20,000 rows written; real scans/index updates can increase those figures.

The calling Worker receives the same 120,000 requests, with an assumed 1 CPU-ms each. Logs are disabled for this example. These additional inputs are ours; read/write counts alone do not determine them. Allocations and rounding come from [DO Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05; caller rates and Paid egress treatment come from [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05.

| Component | Our formula | Our result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DO invocations | 100,000 + 20,000 | 120,000/month, within Paid 1 million |
| DO active duration | 120,000 × 0.1 seconds × 0.128 GB | 1,536 GB-s/month, within Paid 400,000 |
| SQL operations | assumed 100,000 rows read; 20,000 written | Within Paid 25 billion / 50 million monthly allocations |
| SQL retained data | 1 GB for one month | Within Paid 5 GB-month |
| Calling Worker | 120,000 requests; 120,000 × 1 CPU-ms | Within Paid 10 million requests / 30 million CPU-ms |
| Paid account minimum | 1 account × USD 5/month | USD 5 |
| Worker/DO-route internet transfer | 5 GB × no additional Paid egress charge | USD 0 additional |
| Conditional Paid total | USD 5 + USD 0 DO overages + USD 0 caller overages | **USD 5/month before tax** |

This is the smallest published paid commitment for that assumed profile. The subscription is counted once for the account. PITR is the documented SQLite recovery capability, without a separately priced backup add-on in the tariff. There is no purchasable replica variant in this example. Allocations shared with other projects and additional log usage could change the bill. Sources: [DO Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) and [Storage API, PITR](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/), all checked 2026-11-05.

**Free path:** on an evenly spread 30-day operational month, 120,000 / 30 = 4,000 DO requests/day; 1,536 / 30 = 51.2 GB-s/day; rows average 100,000 / 30 = 3,333⅓ read/day and 20,000 / 30 = 666⅔ written/day. Averages are not enforced quotas: assume no individual day exceeds its limit. The assumed 1-ms calling Worker also fits its distinct 10-ms invocation CPU limit and 100,000 daily requests. Those published compute/row/storage-account components would cost USD 0 on Free. At exactly 1 GB of complete billable size, the object is at the FAQ's stated 1 GB cap. If the reference means 1 GB of application data, metadata/indexes add storage and could exceed that cap. The table's different limit would permit more headroom. Thus the assumed compute/storage subtotal is USD 0, but a real project's Free fit and complete benchmark total remain unconfirmed. Dividing data across smaller objects is a different architecture and loses the single-object transaction boundary. The Paid egress statement above is not used as an independent Free transfer guarantee. Sources: [DO Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), [Limits, Free size table/FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/) and [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05.

### Idle and non-hibernating variants

Retain the same 1 GB and make no requests or job executions. If the object is hibernatable/inactive for the month, DO duration = 0 seconds × 0.128 GB = 0 GB-s; 1 GB remains within the 5 GB-month Paid storage allocation. **Conditional Paid idle total = USD 5 account subscription + USD 0 additional DO storage/compute = USD 5/month before tax.** Free's documented stored-data component is USD 0 while inside the relevant caps; the one-object size conflict still prevents an unconditional Free fit claim. No fixed idle-deletion deadline was found. Sources: [Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), [Lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/), [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) and [Developer terms, post-termination access](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/), checked 2026-11-05.

For contrast in billing behavior, assume a standard non-hibernating WebSocket or other qualifying activity keeps objects active for all 730 hours. The connection setup requests and any other meters must also fit their allowances.

- One object: 730 × 3,600 × 0.128 = **336,384 GB-s**. This remains within the otherwise-unused Paid 400,000 GB-s allocation; the USD 5 account minimum remains.
- Five such objects: 5 × 336,384 = **1,681,920 GB-s**. Excess = 1,681,920 − 400,000 = 1,281,920 GB-s. Applying the current page's round-up rule: ceil(1,281,920 / 1,000,000) × USD 12.50 = **USD 25 additional duration**, or **USD 30/month including the account minimum**, with all other meters assumed inside allocations.

This is our calculation, not an idle project: those objects remain non-hibernatable. Concurrent requests on the same object share duration; separate objects each contribute their own duration. Sources: [DO Pricing, duration and round-up paragraph](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/) and [Lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/), checked 2026-11-05.

A request-rounding example also matters: 1.5 million DO requests/month − 1 million included = 500,000 excess; ceil(500,000 / 1,000,000) × USD 0.15 = **USD 0.15 additional requests**, rather than a silently prorated USD 0.075. Hold all other meters inside allocations to get USD 5.15 including the account minimum. Source: [Pricing, Compute billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05.

### Native alarm jobs

Reference: 10,000 jobs/month, 1 second active time each, peak five simultaneous jobs, no retry/wait in the base case. Durable Objects offer a fixed 128 MB billing allocation, **not** a selectable 0.5-vCPU/0.5-GB worker. This is a named native-alarm variant, so it is not a machine-equivalent benchmark. Use at least five objects, each running at most one alarm handler at a time. Assume total schedule/payload storage remains below the relevant per-object cap and 5 GB account allowance. Sources: [Alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/), [Limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/) and [Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05.

- Base invocation usage: 10,000 alarm calls; base duration = 10,000 × 1 second × 0.128 GB = **1,280 GB-s**.
- Scheduling: at least 10,000 setAlarm calls = **10,000 SQLite rows written**, plus any schedule/payload/index writes. Assume those extra writes remain within allocations. Calls to the scheduling endpoint also create their own Worker/DO request usage; allow another 10,000 of each and 1 CPU-ms per scheduling Worker request.
- Under otherwise-unused Paid allowances, all these assumed meters fit. **Conditional Paid account total: USD 5/month before tax.** With no transfer or logs in this base case, the same components can cost **USD 0 on Free** if each day's aggregate requests, duration and rows remain inside all Free limits. Scheduling all 10,000 once and executing them on one day uses 1,280 GB-s and at least 10,000 row writes, below those stated daily limits; do not add unrelated account usage.
- Retry variant, one extra 1-second attempt per job: **20,000 alarm invocations; 20,000 × 1 × 0.128 = 2,560 GB-s**. This still fits the stated Paid allocations. Automatic retries are not asserted to perform another billable setAlarm write; application rescheduling would do so.
- Wait variant, one 1-second execution plus 30 seconds network waiting per job, with no overlapping active intervals on the same object: **10,000 × 31 × 0.128 = 39,680 GB-s**. The assumed Paid duration remains included, but a same-day Free run exceeds 13,000 GB-s and would hit the relevant limit. Waiting for a future scheduled alarm while hibernated is different from this in-handler I/O wait.

All job arithmetic is our calculation from [DO Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), [Alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/), [Lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/) and [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), checked 2026-11-05. External API charges, job output transfer and application-specific payload work are omitted because no workload inputs define them. Enabling logs adds the explicitly linked [Workers Logs meter](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/), checked 2026-11-05; the base example has logs disabled.

### Scenarios that do not map to this offer

The full-backend scenario has no complete total here: 1,000 monthly active users do not determine authentication code or provider costs, and Durable Objects do not bundle the reference file-bucket service. A 100 GB file-store workload is not priced as R2, and a 100,000-vector workload is not priced as Vectorize. Adding those separate products requires their own rates, limits and measured/assumed application calls. The 10 GB Paid per-object database cap also prevents treating one object as the reference 100 GB store.
