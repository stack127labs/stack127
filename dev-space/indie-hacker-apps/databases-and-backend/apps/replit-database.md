## Cost examples — our calculation

Checked 2026-11-05. Scope: current SQL production database in a Project published to **North America**. [Project geography](https://docs.replit.com/features/publishing/project-geography) publishes broad geography choices, not exact database-region IDs or regional price differences. Preserve the original `$` symbol and exclude location-dependent tax.

Inputs: [August 2026 price changes](https://docs.replit.com/billing/aug-cloud-billing-updates), [Usage-based Billing](https://docs.replit.com/billing/about-usage-based-billing), [Data Recovery](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/data-recovery), [Pricing](https://replit.com/pricing), [Core membership](https://docs.replit.com/billing/plans/replit-core) and [spend controls](https://docs.replit.com/billing/managing-spend). Current production prices are **$0.16/active compute hour**, **$0.35/GiB-month database storage**, **$0.20/GiB-month PITR history**, **$0.09/GiB-month scheduled backups**.

Let:

- `A` = billed active database hours, including the five-minute post-query activity window.
- `S` = the monthly maximum billed database storage in GiB.
- `R` = billed PITR-history quantity in GiB-month.
- `K` = billed scheduled-backup quantity in GiB-month.
- `X` = any direct database-transfer or other required charges not established by the inspected public rate pages.

Known database usage formula:

`U = $0.16 × A + $0.35 × S + $0.20 × R + $0.09 × K`

Complete usage would also need `X`. The generic billing page describes storage including history, while the detailed recovery page separates three rates. Do not count the same history bytes in both `S` and `R`. Exact monthly aggregation/rounding for each protection meter was not established, so chosen `R/K` quantities below are billing assumptions, not generated from write counts.

For a normal **monthly Core** cycle with its $20 shared credit fully available, the conditional account cash formula is:

`$20 subscription + max($0, total eligible usage − $20 credit)`.

This does not add the full usage on top of the subscription or deduct the credit twice. Agent, publishing, AI integrations and other eligible products compete for that credit. Pro's base monthly case uses $100 subscription/$100 credit instead; rollover is conflicting and is not assumed. Annual plan equivalents require upfront annual payment and are not treated as monthly cash commitments here.

### Small SQL app

Fixed workload: one production database, 1 GB stored, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer; compare a 730-hour month when always active.

A 1 GB payload is not a complete billed database size. Billing docs say an empty database itself uses **33 MB**, and indexes/history add more. For the illustration, **assume a measured peak database billable quantity of 1 GiB**, including the workload data and engine overhead, instead of silently equating 1 GB with 1 GiB. No source says that every 1 GB payload will fit this assumed billed quantity.

Read/write counts and ten peak connections do not determine active hours, engine CPU/RAM needs or PITR growth. Exact connection ceilings were not found. No performance benchmark was run.

**Conditional intermittent example:**

- `A = 10 active hours`, including all five-minute tails.
- `S = 1 GiB`.
- `R = 0.1 GiB-month`, an assumed metered history quantity.
- `K = 0`, scheduled snapshots disabled.
- Compute: `10 × $0.16 = $1.60`.
- Database: `1 × $0.35 = $0.35`.
- PITR history: `0.1 × $0.20 = $0.02`.
- **Known database usage subtotal: $1.97.**
- With no other credit consumption, `$20 + max(0, $1.97 − $20) = $20` for the **priced portion of a monthly Core account**.

This is not an all-in standalone database quote. Production is created through publishing; any necessary app deployment/runtime consumes separate credits. Direct-database egress and precise allocation/performance remain unresolved. If other work consumes the included credits first, the database subtotal becomes additional usage.

**Always-active variant:** replace `A` with `730`:

`730 × $0.16 + $0.35 + $0.02 = $117.17` known database usage.

Conditional Core cash for just this priced usage is `$20 + ($117.17 − $20) = $117.17`, before the same unresolved/excluded components. Query traffic separated by fewer than five idle minutes can keep the database active, even when query execution itself is short. This is why query counts alone cannot pick between $1.97 and $117.17.

**Transfer:** the fixed 5 GB decimal transfer is `5,000,000,000 / 1,073,741,824 = approximately 4.656613 GiB`. Core's **100 GiB publishing** allowance can cover that amount if it is qualifying publishing traffic and no other publishing has consumed the allowance. The sources do not establish an identical treatment for a database's direct external-client responses, so no universal $0 database-egress line is invented.

### Free path

Current development database compute/storage is included free up to its hard cap. It cannot be used as a general publicly accessible production database: Helium's connection is app-scoped and development is separate from the published production database. [SQL Database](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/sql-database); [Connection details](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/connection-details); [Development and Production](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/development-and-production).

Starter has monthly cloud credits for production resources, a **1 GiB production cap**, and one published link expiring after **30 days**, with republishing available. The exact recurring credit quantity and database-active-hour allowance were not published in the inspected [Starter](https://docs.replit.com/billing/plans/starter-plan), [billing](https://docs.replit.com/billing/about-usage-based-billing), [pricing](https://replit.com/pricing) or [terms](https://replit.com/terms-of-service) pages. Therefore the fixed SQL workload has **no exact verified recurring-free production total** in this research. A published-link expiry is not proof the retained SQL data is deleted at day 30.

### Backup variants

Default production PITR is seven days. Pro/Enterprise may choose 28 days, increasing billable history; the [recovery guide](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/data-recovery) gives the rates and restore restrictions.

For a chosen additional `K = 7 GiB-month` of billed scheduled-backup storage:

`7 × $0.09 = $0.63` extra usage.

This is an illustration of a billing quantity, not a claim that every seven-day retention setting always bills exactly seven GiB-month. Backups are full snapshots and skip while the database is archived; the actual retained size and time matter. A 28-day window also cannot be computed from the number of logical writes alone.

For any measured additional `R` of history, add `$0.20 × R`. Do not multiply the live database's size by 28 and call that the PITR bill. Exporting long-term external backups requires its own storage/transfer costs.

### Idle project

Keep the same assumed `S = 1 GiB`, with no queries or job execution for the month.

- After the existing five-minute activity window has elapsed, `A = 0`.
- Known live-data storage: `1 × $0.35 = $0.35`.
- Add `$0.20 × R + $0.09 × K` for any billable retained history/backups. These do not automatically become zero when traffic stops.
- With only $0.35 of known usage and no other credit use, the priced monthly Core account remains **$20**, not $0.35 or $0.
- If one five-minute tail falls inside this month, the additional compute component is `(5 / 60) × $0.16 = $0.013333…`, before any unestablished billing rounding.

The [24-hour archive rule](https://docs.replit.com/features/data-and-storage/data-recovery) skips daily backup creation while inactive; existing retention/history rules still apply. Query statistics reset on suspension. Free-account deletion after one year without login is a separate account policy, and paid subscriptions are exempt from that account-inactivity termination rule. [Account inactivity](https://docs.replit.com/legal-and-security-info/account-inactivity).
