## Lifecycle, sunset and acquisition notices

**Active product; v1/v2 must be separated.** The July 20, 2026 notice announced a move of MPGv2 to general availability and disabling new MPGv1 creation “in the upcoming days.” It explicitly said existing v1 clusters would keep running. Do not turn that prospective wording into an exact cutoff date or a retirement date for existing clusters. The September 15 API launch supports v2 and returns 404 for v1. Sources, checked 2026-11-09: [v2 GA notice](https://fly.io/changelog/managed-postgres-v2-ga/), [API announcement](https://fly.io/changelog/managed-postgres-machines-api/).

News check 2026-11-09 found no whole-product shutdown, sunset or vendor acquisition announcement. The older unmanaged Fly Postgres is a separate unsupported product; its deployment and scale-to-zero guidance must not be assigned to MPG. Sources: [MPG overview](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/index.md), [support scope](https://docs.fly.io/about/support.md), [Fly's product distinction](https://fly.io/learn/fly-vs-railway/), [status page](https://status.flyio.net/).

**Billing change:** current docs distinguish used-storage billing for v2 from provisioned-storage billing for old unbadged v1 clusters. Inter-region private MPG network traffic has been billable since February 2026. [MPG pricing](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/index.md), checked 2026-11-05.

Scope: vendor-managed PostgreSQL, with legacy v1 behavior called out. Separate Fly Machines, Sprites, Tigris and user-managed databases are not editions bundled into this price. The apps.md managed-docs URL and “hosting and deploy” overlap are reasonable; no row correction proposed.

## Cost examples — our calculations

These are **our calculations**, not vendor quotes or performance tests. All rate inputs checked **2026-11-05**. Region **iad (Ashburn, Virginia)**; new organization using granular transfer pricing; v2 unless stated otherwise. Currency **USD**, before taxes and optional general support. Sources: [regions](https://docs.fly.io/reference/regions.md), [resource pricing](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing.md), [MPG prices](https://fly.io/pricing/), [Terms taxes](https://fly.io/legal/terms-of-service/).

Define B(H) as the Basic cluster plan charge for H hours and G(H) as its billed storage GB over time. The published base is $38/month; storage is explicitly $0.28/GB per **30-day (720-hour) month**, metered hourly. The precise MPG base-plan hourly divisor/rounding was not found; Machine per-second billing does not prove it. New v2 uses measured storage rather than the initial 10GB allocation. A “1GB app database” need not equal exactly 1 billed GB once indexes, WAL, system overhead and replication-meter scope are considered. Sources: [MPG overview Pricing](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/index.md), [Plans schema](https://docs.fly.io/api/machines/postgres-clusters/list-postgres-plans.md), [creation defaults](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/create-and-connect), [metrics storage scope](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/metrics.md).

### 1. Small SQL/document app

Inputs: one production database, 1 GB retained for a month, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer; approved always-on assumption 730 hours. Basic's 200 pool-client/50 pool-backend slots accommodate 10 connections numerically; CPU/query fit is not established by counts alone. [Client limits](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/client-configuration.md).

Normal published-month database reference **assuming 1 GB is the billable storage measure**: $38 + (1 × $0.28) = **$38.28**, including built-in HA/pooling/backups. For the actual 730-hour comparison, storage = 1 × $0.28 × 730/720 = **$0.2838888889** (rounded to 10 decimal places), plus B(730). If the plan also prorates over 720 hours, the conditional database subtotal is ($38 + $0.28) × 730/720 = **$38.8116666667**, or **$38.81 rounded to cents**. That compute divisor is an assumption, not a verified invoice rule. [MPG rates](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/index.md), [plan price](https://docs.fly.io/api/machines/postgres-clusters/list-postgres-plans.md).

MPG has no direct public internet endpoint. To supply 5 GB to internet users, assume an existing app/gateway in iad: same-region DB-to-app route adds no private transfer charge; app-to-internet adds 5 × $0.02 = **$0.10**. Its runtime/gateway cost must still be added. If the recipient joins WireGuard directly instead, the exact metering of that route needs separate confirmation. Sources: [private connection requirement](https://docs.fly.io/postgres/create-and-connect), [network rates](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing.md). **Complete exact workload total unknown**: physical storage, exact base proration and app/gateway resources remain inputs.

No recurring free discount applies. An eligible, unused trial balance can reduce the first bill by **up to $1**, shared with other products and subject to its seven-day expiry; it does not remove a monthly database floor. [Trial](https://docs.fly.io/about/free-trial).

HA variant: primary and replica already included, so do not double $38 for ordinary built-in failover.
