## Notices

- DBOS is active. The product blog lists an October 29, 2026 update, and the official SDK repositories have recent releases. I checked the company blog, product updates, GitHub organization and SDK repositories for acquisition, shutdown, or retirement notices and found none.
- The official `/changelog` URL could not be opened. I used the company’s monthly “What’s New” posts, GitHub release pages, and the self-hosted Conductor release notes as update sources instead.
- In October 2026, DBOS moved the Conductor client into a separate commercial Transact Enterprise package. The open-source DBOS Transact libraries remain MIT-licensed. The commercial extension and self-hosted Conductor use DBOS’s proprietary Conductor Software License; production use requires a paid license.
- Pricing and terms conflict on compliance: the current pricing page advertises SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA for Teams and DBOS Cloud, while the October 2024 Terms of Service say the Services are not tailored for HIPAA. I leave the vendor’s current marketing claim in the facts and flag the older legal terms as a conflict, not a legal conclusion.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- The current DBOS pricing page says Cloud is contact-sales; the Cloud monitoring guide refers to usage against an “execution time limit” but gives no rate or limit. I used the current pricing page for public price availability and marked Cloud rates unpublished.
- The 2024 Terms of Service say services are not tailored for HIPAA. The newer pricing page advertises HIPAA and a BAA. The pages conflict; this research does not determine which contract terms apply.
- Cloud has a documented current app-hosting region, AWS us-east-1, but a complete selectable-region list and hosted Conductor region were not found.
- The product `/changelog` page could not be fetched. The monthly blog, GitHub releases, and Conductor self-host release notes were available and used instead.
- The user-facing fields do not define whether SQLite counts as a Postgres requirement exception. I treated Postgres as the recommended production database while recording documented Python/Go SQLite support and TypeScript/Java differences.
