Zeplo is a hosted service that holds HTTP requests and sends them to your endpoint later or on a retry schedule. It queues HTTP calls; it does not run your application's code. Its public plans are Developer Free, Company, and custom Enterprise. The pricing and legal pages disagree about what happens when a request allowance is exceeded, so the overage rates should not be treated as a confirmed bill without clarification from Zeplo.

## Cost examples

Both examples are **our calculations**, using the plan figures shown by Zeplo. The prices use the $ symbol; the page does not state an ISO currency code. Tax treatment is unpublished.

- **Free allowance:** 400 requests in one month, one user, no paid add-ons. Formula: 400 requests are within the 500-request allowance, so the displayed plan cost is $0/month.
- **Company included volume:** 1,000,000 requests in one month, up to three members, no add-ons. Formula: the Company subscription is $39/month and includes 1,000,000 requests, so the displayed plan cost is $39/month.
- No above-quota total is stated as an invoice estimate. Pricing publishes overage rates, but Terms say excess requests are rejected with HTTP 429 and do not incur overage fees.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Overage behavior:** Pricing and Limits publish $20/100,000 requests for Developer Free and $10/1,000,000 for Company. Terms, updated February 21, 2026, say excess requests receive HTTP 429 and Zeplo will not charge overages. No page explains which rule controls. The rates are reported as published, not as guaranteed fees.
- **Plan names and retention:** Pricing uses Developer Free, Company, and Enterprise and says 30-day log retention for all. Terms use Free, Starter, and Pro; Privacy says Free 7 days, Starter 30 days, and Pro 90 days. The pages do not map the differently named tiers.
- **Payload size:** The limits table gives plan payload maximums of 64 KB Free and 1 MB Company, while the general request limit says 256 KB inline and unlimited via S3 offload. The FAQ says bodies above 256 KB are offloaded. These statements do not agree on the effective plan maximum.
- **Retry syntax:** Requests says retries: 3 means three retries plus the first attempt. Retry says max_attempts: 3 means three total attempts. It also names idempotency_key in prose while the schema exposes requestId; aliasing is not explained.
- **Maintenance evidence:** The website, v2 docs, registration, Terms/Privacy, and status page were live; status showed the service operational. The latest blog date visible is March 10, 2026. Public GitHub core/site/CLI repositories have no recent changes after 2022, and no standalone changelog could be fetched. It appears reachable and operating, but recent core-code maintenance is not confirmed.
- **Changelog check:** Tried https://zeplo.io/changelog and https://zeplo.io/changelog/; both failed to load in the public-page tool. The official blog and GitHub organization were also checked.
- **Usage beyond published limits:** Pricing FAQ says a monthly request limit can be set, but exact behavior and charges beyond that limit are inconsistent across Pricing, Limits, and Terms.
- **Retry billing, region, and export:** Pricing/FAQ/Retry do not say whether retries count as separate billable requests. Pricing, docs, Terms, and Privacy do not identify Cloud regions or an egress rate. Privacy permits a full export request by email, but API docs do not describe a bulk workspace archive format.

## Field problems

- Pricing and Privacy use different plan names and retention values. Their rows stay conflicting rather than assigning one plan mapping by guess.
- The pricing page was checked for billing-period switches and sliders. It showed monthly prices and a plain plan comparison; no annual switch or slider was present.
- The API docs mix v2 request-field names with legacy v1 headers and retry terminology. The field names and attempt-count convention are kept separate.
- API request rate is not the same as maximum job delivery throughput. No concurrency number was published in the pages checked.
- The status page is a point-in-time signal, not a contractual SLA.
- The operator's legal entity and country were not published on the official About, Terms, or Privacy pages. No private individual is named.
