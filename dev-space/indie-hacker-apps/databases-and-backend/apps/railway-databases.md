## Cost examples — our calculation

Checked 2026-11-05. These are arithmetic examples, not Railway quotes or measured benchmarks. Use one PostgreSQL service in Virginia (`us-east4-eqdc4a`) for the SQL examples. Published resource prices have no region split on the checked table; the region choice does not prove legal residency. Prices retain the published `$` symbol; taxes are extra where applicable. No new-customer free deployment is assumed while the [November 2 reopening rule](https://status.railway.com/incident/O1VGXWU5) applies.

Inputs come from [Pricing, resource rates](https://railway.com/pricing), [Pricing Plans, subscription/usage](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/plans), [Understanding Your Bill](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/understanding-your-bill), [volume billing](https://docs.railway.com/volumes/reference), and [Storage Buckets Billing](https://docs.railway.com/storage-buckets/billing). Use `t = 730 × 3,600 = 2,628,000 seconds`. Let `M` be average measured GB RAM, `C` average measured vCPU, `B` average billable volume GB including engine overhead and filesystem metadata, and `E` billable service-egress GB.

Using the pricing page's **per-second** inputs:

`service usage = t × (0.00000386M + 0.00000772C + 0.00000006B) + 0.05E`

For a normal billing cycle, Hobby total is `max($5, workspace usage)`; Pro is `max($20, workspace usage)`. The plan credit offsets usage once; it is not an extra charge on top of all consumption. Add other services, files, backups and agents to workspace usage before applying the minimum. Subscription and overage invoice timing differs from this steady-cycle comparison.

### Small SQL app

Required workload: one production database, 1 GB stored, 100,000 reads, 20,000 writes, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer and 730 hours. Query counts do not determine CPU/RAM; 1 GB of database data does not determine indexes, WAL, temporary files or filesystem metadata. Free/Trial's 0.5 GB volume is too small, even before current access restrictions. Hobby is the least priced candidate; this research does not benchmark its capacity or verify a deployed connection limit.

**Conditional resource example:** choose average `M = 0.5 GB`, `C = 0.05 vCPU`, `B = 1.2 GB` (assumed to include the workload's 1 GB plus overhead), and `E = 5 GB`. These are additional assumptions, not conversions from the query count.

- RAM: `0.5 × 2,628,000 × $0.00000386 = $5.07204`.
- CPU: `0.05 × 2,628,000 × $0.00000772 = $1.014408`.
- Volume: `1.2 × 2,628,000 × $0.00000006 = $0.189216`.
- Service egress: `5 × $0.05 = $0.25`.
- Usage subtotal: `$6.525664`; conditional Hobby total `max($5, $6.525664) = $6.525664` (about **$6.53**, explicitly rounded). Pro minimum would be **$20** under these same assumptions.

The plan docs' per-minute rates instead give `43,800 × (0.5 × 0.000231 + 0.05 × 0.000463 + 1.2 × 0.000003472222222) + 0.25 = approximately $6.50537`. This difference is preserved, not silently reconciled. Neither subtotal is an exact invoice promise.

**Backup variant:** [volume backups](https://docs.railway.com/volumes/backups) add exclusive incremental GB-time at volume rates. [PITR](https://docs.railway.com/volumes/point-in-time-recovery) adds rounded bucket GB-month archive storage and `$0.05 × compressed archive-upload GB`, plus restored-service resources when used. Archive growth is unknown, so the protected total is incomplete.

**HA variant:** [PostgreSQL HA](https://docs.railway.com/databases/postgresql-ha) adds two or more data replicas, consensus nodes, proxies and their volumes; [PgBouncer](https://docs.railway.com/databases/postgresql-pgbouncer) is another running service if enabled. Sum each component's measured resource usage. Multiplying the single-node price by two or three would omit components. HA total is incomplete.

### Idle project

Keep the same assumed `B = 1.2 GB`; zero requests and job execution.

- Always-on, retaining the illustrative `M = 0.5`, `C = 0.05`: usage `$5.07204 + $1.014408 + $0.189216 = $6.275664`; conditional Hobby **about $6.28**.
- If the service is genuinely stopped/asleep for the whole billing period: volume usage `$0.189216`, no CPU/RAM in that idealized interval, Hobby **$5 minimum**. Wake/sleep-transition resource time and any backups remain additional.
- Eligible existing Free accounts have $1/month credit but cannot retain this 1 GB database on their 0.5 GB volume. Do not publish a free result for this scenario.

[Serverless](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/serverless) samples outbound inactivity and may never sleep a database with pooler, replication, telemetry or other traffic. No automatic HA sleep savings are assumed. Ordinary idle memory is billable according to [billing docs](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/understanding-your-bill).

### Full app backend

Required additions: 1,000 monthly active users, 1 GB files, 100,000 file reads, 10,000 uploads, 20 GB **total** internet transfer, and 10,000 short function calls. Assume each call actively uses 0.5 vCPU and 0.5 GB RAM for 1 second. Railway [Functions](https://docs.railway.com/functions) run as Services; these calls do not establish the services' average RAM/CPU between requests. The app must supply user authentication/authorization; the Enterprise domain gate is a separate feature.

General formula, using combined database/app/Function measured averages:

`t × (0.00000386 × combined M + 0.00000772 × combined C + 0.00000006 × B) + $0.05 × E + $0.015 × ceil(bucket GB-month)`

**Conditional example:** suppose all compute, including those calls and idle intervals, averages `M = 0.75 GB`, `C = 0.06 vCPU`; volume `B = 1.2 GB`; all 20 GB leaves services. Retaining exactly 1 GB of files throughout 730 hours is `730 / 720 = 1.013888… GB-month`, rounded to 2 GB-month under bucket billing.

`$7.60806 RAM + $1.2172896 CPU + $0.189216 volume + $1 service egress + $0.03 bucket = $10.0445656`; conditional Hobby **about $10.04**. Do not add the one-second Function compute again: it is already part of these assumed combined averages.

If 15 GB of the 20 GB instead downloads directly from the bucket via signed links and only 5 GB is service egress, subtract `15 × $0.05 = $0.75`: **about $9.29**, same additional assumptions. These route choices follow [Uploading & Serving Files](https://docs.railway.com/storage-buckets/uploading-serving). No email/SMS, identity provider, backups, HA, paid model APIs, or extra logs are included. The fixed workload's actual total remains incomplete because combined compute and engine overhead are not supplied by user counts.

### Small file store

100 GB average Standard storage for exactly 30 days, 100,000 writes, 1 million reads, 100 GB internet transfer. Assume ordinary whole-object writes, direct S3 uploads and signed-link downloads, without app proxying or transformations.

- Storage: `ceil(100 × 30 / 30) × $0.015 = $1.50`.
- S3 operations: `(100,000 + 1,000,000) × $0 = $0`.
- Direct bucket delivery: `100 GB × $0 = $0`.
- Usage **$1.50**, conditional standalone Hobby workspace total **$5**, Pro **$20**.

This fits Hobby's aggregate 1 TB bucket cap, but exceeds Free's 10 GB-month and Trial's 50 GB-month limits. There is no applicable free total. [Bucket billing](https://docs.railway.com/storage-buckets/billing) defines the rounding and no minimum retention. If a Railway service uploads the 100 GB, add `100 × $0.05 = $5`; total usage **$6.50**, Hobby **$6.50**, plus that service's compute. Proxying the 100 GB download through a service adds another **$5** plus compute. These alternatives are not part of the direct-bucket baseline.

### Small vector search app

100,000 vectors × 1,536 float32 components, 1 KiB metadata/vector; 100,000 searches, 10,000 updates and 5 GB internet transfer.

Our raw payload calculation: `100,000 × (1,536 × 4 + 1,024) = 716,800,000 bytes = 0.7168 decimal GB`. This excludes SQL rows, indexes, WAL, filesystem metadata, replicas and backups. The 1,536 dimensions fit upstream pgvector's ordinary vector HNSW limit; Railway's [PostgreSQL guide](https://docs.railway.com/databases/postgresql) directs pgvector users to a custom template, and [upstream pgvector](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pgvector/pgvector/master/README.md) documents the limit. Installed version and image features were not inspected.

Compute/query costs cannot be derived from those counts. Minimum candidate is Hobby **$5**, plus overages; transfer alone is `5 × $0.05 = $0.25` if service-public egress. Add actual volume and measured CPU/RAM using the formula above. Embedding/reranking, model hosting and index-build resources remain additional. **Complete total unknown.** Stock HA conversion cannot be assumed for a custom vector image.

### Small job workload

10,000 jobs; each actively runs 1 second at 0.5 vCPU and 0.5 GB RAM; peak five concurrent jobs; no retries/waiting. Use ordinary service/container workers, **not** the distinct Sandbox/VM meter. [Functions are Services](https://docs.railway.com/functions); [Cron Jobs](https://docs.railway.com/cron-jobs) explain the scheduling/exit model.

Active resource component:

`10,000 × 1 × (0.5 × $0.00000772 + 0.5 × $0.00000386) = $0.0579`.

If that were all workspace usage and workers stopped between jobs, Hobby would still be **$5**. A five-concurrent-job burst requires 2.5 measured vCPU/2.5 GB RAM at those assumptions, with service execution capacity and worker logic configured accordingly. One cron service skips overlapping runs; it is not five-way queue concurrency. The five-minute minimum also means 10,000 separate cron triggers cannot all fit one 730-hour service schedule: `730 × 60 / 5 = 8,760` triggers. Dispatch multiple jobs per run or use a worker/queue design.

**Retry variant:** 10% of jobs make one identical extra attempt: `11,000 × (0.5 × 0.00000772 + 0.5 × 0.00000386) = $0.06369` active usage.

**Wait variant:** each of 10,000 jobs retains 0.5 GB RAM for 10 extra seconds with no additional CPU assumed: `10,000 × 10 × 0.5 × $0.00000386 = $0.193` extra memory; combined active-plus-wait **$0.2509**. This is a chosen waiting-memory assumption, not a special discounted waiting tariff.

Startup/shutdown, baseline worker memory, queue/database storage, logs, payload transfer and retry coordination are not specified. Full job total is therefore incomplete; no exactly-once delivery or unlimited runtime is priced into these subtotals.
