# Google Memorystore

**Shutdown notice: Memorystore for Memcached.** Google marks it deprecated from 2026-01-20, restricts creation in new projects after 2027-02-01 unless Memcached instances already exist there, and ends the service and Cloud Customer Care on **2029-01-31**. Google recommends migration to Valkey. Source: [official deprecation notice](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/memcached/deprecation/memcached) and [migration guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/memcached/deprecation/migrate-to-valkey); checked 2026-11-05.

**Company-news check:** searched Google's Cloud blog and press/newsroom for acquisition, shutdown, sunset and deprecation news. No acquisition or shutdown of the whole Memorystore family was found. Valkey remains actively developed; [Google's September 25, 2026 blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/memorystore-for-valkey-9-1-3x-qps-caching) announces Valkey 9.1 GA. [Google Cloud press/newsroom](https://cloud.google.com/press); checked 2026-11-05. The positive retirement finding above takes priority over an unqualified active-service label.

A 730-hour month is a comparison assumption, not an hourly-rounding rule. Rates come from the separate [Valkey](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/valkey/pricing), [Redis Basic/Standard](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/redis/pricing), [Redis Cluster](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/pricing) and [Memcached](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/memcached/pricing) price tables. Taxes follow [Cloud Terms §2.2](https://cloud.google.com/terms). No Google Cloud trial credit or other product's free allowance is subtracted from the normal paid path.

### Small cache/database and idle month

Reference inputs: one production datastore, 1 GB application data, 100,000 reads and 20,000 writes/month, 10 peak connections, 5 GB internet transfer, 730 hours. A key-value cache can be priced for this workload's capacity, but it does not replace an authoritative persistent database without a deliberate persistence/HA design.

**Unit and fit limits:** standalone Redis and Memcached tariffs use **GiB**; Valkey/Redis Cluster node labels and persistence charges use **GB**. They are preserved as published. A nominal 1 GiB allocation is not treated as exactly 1 GB of writable application data. Engine bookkeeping, keys, reserved RAM and fragmentation also consume memory. Operation counts do not establish a suitable CPU profile. Consequently the figures are allocated-capacity components; they do not prove the least safe production allocation or a complete monthly bill.

The tables show the published least allocations and named variants, not a universal cheapest safe server. No separate per-GET/SET request tariff appears in these engine prices. Four material additions prevent an exact full reference total:

- **Networking:** the services have private endpoints. The 5 GB internet input requires an application host/public delivery path and its own egress tariff. It cannot be entered as 5 GiB of direct public Memorystore traffic. For illustration only, **5 GiB** of eligible different-zone PSC client traffic would cost 5 × USD 0.01 = **USD 0.05** on Valkey/Redis Cluster, excluding other traffic; it is a different input/route, not a silent conversion of the reference's 5 GB. Redis within-region Memorystore-side transfer and Memcached same-region service transfer have their own scoped exemptions. See [Valkey network pricing](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/valkey/pricing), [Cluster network pricing](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/pricing), [Redis network pricing](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/redis/pricing) and [Memcached network pricing](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/memcached/pricing).
- **Valkey/Redis Cluster backups:** at Iowa's USD 0.00011111 per backup GB-hour, one backup of **1 actual billed GB** retained for 24 hours costs **1 × 24 × 0.00011111 = USD 0.00266664**. Deleting it after one hour still incurs the 24-hour minimum. Keeping that one GB snapshot for 730 hours costs **USD 0.0811103**. Actual backup sizes, number of snapshots and retention must be supplied; an automatic 35-day schedule can retain many copies. Instance deletion leaves collections billable. This is not the price for all daily backups combined.
- **Valkey/Redis Cluster AOF:** optional persistence adds USD 0.00054795 per **provisioned instance GB-hour**, not per observed log GB. For **one billable provisioned GB** retained for 730 hours, our calculation is **1 × 730 × 0.00054795 = USD 0.4000035/month**. Multiply by the applicable total billable provisioned capacity for the selected persistence-enabled configuration; do not substitute observed AOF-file bytes. The node examples above do not silently include an AOF configuration. Do not add AOF to Redis Basic/Standard, which does not support it.
- **External resources:** client compute, VPC connectors/network services where needed, exported Cloud Storage objects, optional paid support and taxes remain outside the cache capacity amounts. Their location and usage are unspecified.

Sources for extras: [Valkey Pricing > AOF/Backups](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/valkey/pricing), [Redis Cluster Pricing > AOF/Backups](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/pricing), [Valkey backup retention](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/manage-backups) and [Cluster backup retention](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/manage-backups). The [CUD guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/cuds) excludes backup/persistence/network usage even though the price tables display discount columns; these examples therefore use on-demand rates.

### Eligible trial path and closure

[Google Cloud's Free Trial](https://cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features) provides USD 300 shared credit for up to 90 days to eligible first-time accounts. Our credit formula is:

`eligible cash usage component = max(eligible accrued Google Cloud charges - remaining unexpired shared credit, 0)`

It cannot establish a fixed free database size/month, because the same credit pays for other cloud resources and depends on previous use. If remaining credit covers the capacity-only examples, their covered cash component can be zero temporarily; normal usage still accrues against credit. Expiry/exhaustion without upgrading stops resources and can lose cache data. There is no recurring Memorystore allowance to apply after the trial. CUD financial obligations continue when resources are unused and cannot be canceled just by deleting a store.

### Small vector-search app

Inputs: 100,000 vectors, 1,536 FLOAT32 values/vector, 1 KiB metadata/vector, 100,000 searches and 10,000 updates/month, 5 GB internet transfer.

Our raw-data calculation, with FLOAT32 = four bytes and 1 KiB = 1,024 bytes:

- Vectors: 100,000 × 1,536 × 4 = **614,400,000 bytes**.
- Metadata: 100,000 × 1,024 = **102,400,000 bytes**.
- Raw sum: **716,800,000 bytes**.

All three applicable vector-engine limitations pages say vectors are duplicated in keyspace and index. Merely adding a second vector copy gives **716,800,000 + 614,400,000 = 1,331,200,000 bytes**, before the graph/FLAT index structures, hash keys/records, allocator overhead, replication and headroom. This is a planning lower bound, not a guaranteed billed-RAM multiplier. See [Valkey](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/vector-search-limitations), [Redis](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/redis/vector-search-limitations) and [Redis Cluster](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/vector-search-limitations) vector limitations.

The vector tariff follows the selected engine's allocated capacity, without a separately published per-query vector fee. As a conditional illustration, one Valkey standard-small costs **USD 0.1425 × 730 = USD 104.025** (USD 104.03 rounded), or **USD 208.05** with one replica. Actual fit, search CPU and index overhead require verification. The smaller raw-data amount must not be used to claim a custom-pico/nano instance can fit this index.

Embedding/reranking inference, public internet delivery, backups/AOF and client compute remain additional inputs. Native full-text score fusion and JSON vector-index support are not assumed; the latter has conflicting command documentation. **Full vector-app cost is incomplete.** Memcached has no applicable native vector-index scenario in the inspected service documentation.

### Small job workload, retries and waits

Inputs: 10,000 jobs/month, one active second/job, 0.5 vCPU and 0.5 GB external worker RAM where that shape exists, peak five concurrent jobs, no retries/waits in the base case.

Our base timing calculation: **10,000 × 1 = 10,000 active worker-seconds**. Redis/Valkey Streams/lists can coordinate work, but Memorystore does not supply those external worker execution instances. Broker cost follows its allocated cache capacity, queue data retention and networking; worker-host rates, framework rules, logs and transfer remain unspecified.

Retry variant: one extra full attempt per job yields **20,000 active worker-seconds**. Waiting variant: 30 seconds per job yields **300,000 waiting seconds**, or **310,000 total job seconds** including base work. Whether waiting consumes billed worker resources depends on the external host/framework. Neither variant becomes a Memorystore per-invocation fee. Keeping the broker allocated during zero jobs still incurs its full idle capacity charge. **Full job-service total is incomplete**; separate Google Cloud Tasks/Pub/Sub prices or semantics were not imported.

### Full backend and file-storage scenarios

The full-backend workload adds 1,000 app users/month, one GB of files, 100,000 file reads, 10,000 uploads, 20 GB internet traffic and 10,000 short function calls. Those auth, object-storage and application-function resources are outside the documented Memorystore capacity tariff. Function duration/memory and external providers are also unspecified. **No full-backend total** is offered.

The 100 GB hot file-store workload does not fit a native Memorystore upload-bucket product. Key/value bytes and external backup/export objects do not create a native file-storage price. **No applicable file-store total** is offered.

The inventory's broad **active** family status remains reasonable for Valkey/Redis, but it omits this engine-specific retirement and needs that warning before publishing an unqualified listing. No inventory file was edited.
- **Company blog/newsroom:** opened the [Google Cloud press/newsroom](https://cloud.google.com/press) and [September 25 Valkey 9.1 announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/memorystore-for-valkey-9-1-3x-qps-caching); searched official blog and press content for Memorystore acquisitions, shutdown, sunset and deprecation. The positive lifecycle finding is Memcached retirement, while Valkey release notes show active development. No acquisition or family-wide shutdown announcement was found in this bounded search.
- **SLA inconsistency:** [Memorystore SLA](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/sla) still lists 99.9% for Memcached but excludes deprecated services; [Memcached's own notice](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/memcached/deprecation/memcached) says it is deprecated. This remains conflicting rather than a guaranteed current entitlement. Other engine coverage depends on HA configuration, node type and geography, including Stockholm's lower multi-zone objective.
- **CUD extras inconsistency:** [Valkey](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/valkey/pricing) and [Redis Cluster](https://cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/cluster/pricing) price tables show discounted AOF/backup columns, whereas [the eligible-resources guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/cuds) excludes backup storage, persistence and transfer. Custom Valkey nodes and standalone Redis M1 are also excluded in their applicable documentation. Examples avoid the conflict by using on-demand rates and no purchased commitment.
- **Version/data-model discrepancies:** [Valkey FT.CREATE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/ftcreate) says HASH-only; [FT.SEARCH](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/ftsearch) describes JSON indexing, with [release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/release-notes) separately confirming the JSON module. ## Field problems

- **One directory row covers four managed offers.** Redis Cluster needs its own pricing/limits, even though 'Redis' appears in both names. The retiring Memcached engine cannot inherit the current Valkey lifecycle.
- **C03/H01:** this is a hosted service entry. Upstream open-source engines are separate products; their licenses and self-host hardware budgets should not be assigned to a downloadable Memorystore edition that does not exist in the inspected service docs.
- **C12/C13/C21:** provisioned GB/GiB, node capacity, writable keyspace and backup size are different quantities. Inclusive Standard Redis HA pricing also differs from per-node read-replica pricing. Allocation components are useful; claiming the cheapest allocation safely fits the whole workload would not be supported.
- **C16:** Google's budget system now has both alert-only and spend-cap features. The latter needs an eligible-service check and does not make every Google Cloud resource automatically capped.
- **C17/C20:** location pickers, supported-region matrices, retirement notices and SLA tables need reconciliation; global marketing claims do not settle deployment availability or contractual coverage.
- **F fields:** five native file-service fields are not applicable. Conditional binary-value limits and external export storage are kept under F04/F05 to avoid confusing a cache with file buckets.
- **V/J fields:** native vector features and messaging/broker building blocks make these groups applicable for Redis/Valkey. Memcached does not inherit them. 'Hybrid' filtering is not automatically full-text score fusion; broker hosting is not external worker execution.
- **D05/C19:** included internal recovery snapshots, independently retained paid backups, exported customer objects and contractual deletion windows have different scopes. The 100-year omitted on-demand backup TTL is separate from the 35-day automated default.
