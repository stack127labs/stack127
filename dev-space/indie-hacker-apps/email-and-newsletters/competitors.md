# Competitor pages: email and newsletters

Checked 2026-11-09. I selected pages that match newsletter platform, Substack alternative, email marketing pricing, and transactional email searches. This is a review of page structure and public information, not a claim about a fixed search-result position. I did not create accounts or submit forms.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. EmailToolTester: newsletter software comparison

[Best Newsletter Software: Top 5 Compared](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/best-newsletter-software/)

- **Who runs it:** EmailToolTester, a specialist email marketing review and comparison publisher. Its [methodology page](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/our-methodology/) describes its review process.
- **Layout:** A wide, interactive side-by-side comparison table followed by short editorial sections about common needs. The table lets readers add or remove products and select up to five email tools.
- **Data for each app:** Product rating; ease of use; editor and personalization; message types; template choice; automation; list management and opt-in/opt-out; AI; monetization; deliverability and authentication; analytics; languages; spam/design tests; custom tracking domains; user access; integrations; landing pages; migration help; support; and price points.
- **Filters and sorting:** Industry and priority selectors, plus a show-all-features control and product selector. I did not see a sort control in the page text.
- **Price display:** A monthly price row with multiple list-size and send-volume points. It says prices are based on subscriber count, but some entries use email volume instead. It includes more than one currency.
- **Sources and dates:** Product columns link to full reviews and plan pages. The comparison view did not show a clear page update date or a source beside every cell. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** This is the closest model for scanning feature differences and list-size prices together. Industry and priority selectors make a large matrix easier to approach.
- **What is weak or missing:** The table is very wide and dense. Price points do not all use the same unit, currency, or plan basis, so readers still have to normalize them. The visible page text also mixes newsletter tools with a broader product set; its selection message mentions live chat tools, which is out of place here.

### 2. EmailToolTester: transactional email providers

[15 Best Transactional Email Services: We Tested Their Deliverability](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/blog/best-transactional-email-service/)

- **Who runs it:** EmailToolTester, the specialist publisher above.
- **Layout:** Editorial guide with a ranked provider list, a comparison table near the top, and longer provider write-ups below.
- **Data for each app:** Monthly price at selected email volumes; deliverability for four test rounds and an average when tested; comments such as free tier, speed, support, or integrations. The article says the services offer SMTP relay and transactional API connections.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sort controls; this is a ranked article.
- **Price display:** Monthly costs at provider-specific volume points, including examples such as 10k, 50k, and 100k emails. Not all providers have the same breakpoints, and the table does not normalize for regions or every plan condition.
- **Sources and dates:** Dated Nov 4, 2026, with an update log. Its [testing note](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/blog/best-transactional-email-service/) says it sent four rounds using GlockApps and identifies some providers it could not test. Prices and reviews link to vendor pages. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** It pairs cost with real-world delivery results and makes untested providers visible. This answers a useful question that marketing-only directories usually skip.
- **What is weak or missing:** The test sample is limited and not every provider has a result. Volume breakpoints differ, so price comparison needs care. It does not cover newsletter policies, self-hosting, deployment, or company lifecycle in a consistent provider record.

### 3. EmailToolTester: Substack alternatives

[Best Substack Alternatives in 2026: 11 Options to Take Your Newsletters Further](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/blog/substack-alternatives/)

- **Who runs it:** EmailToolTester. The page is an editorial guide based on the writers' experience and the team's product knowledge.
- **Layout:** Long article with a table of contents and 11 numbered alternative profiles.
- **Data for each app:** Who it suits; newsletter, publishing, audience-growth, and monetization features; free-plan limits; selected paid prices; fees where relevant; and prose lists of strengths, limitations, and verdicts. The sections are not a fixed comparison schema.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting. Readers navigate with the article contents and numbered order.
- **Price display:** Plan examples in prose, often with a named subscriber/member count and monthly price. Transaction fees can also appear, but the example and level of detail vary by product.
- **Sources and dates:** Byline and update date are visible (Aug 26, 2026); product links, screenshots, and some linked comparisons support the profiles. It says recommendations draw on individual experience and team knowledge. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** It understands that “Substack alternative” can mean more than an email sender. It explains tradeoffs around paid newsletters, websites, audience discovery, and creator businesses, including when a membership product is only a partial substitute.
- **What is weak or missing:** Price and feature detail varies by product, which makes direct comparison hard. Readers cannot filter to self-hosted, free, API-first, or specific email workloads, and exact supporting sources are not attached to each claim.

### 4. G2: Email Marketing category

[Best Email Marketing Software](https://www.g2.com/categories/email-marketing)

- **Who runs it:** G2, a software review and discovery platform built around business software listings and user reviews.
- **Layout:** Category overview, satisfaction/market-presence Grid, then extensive product profiles with review summaries and category stats. The page says it tracks 811 products and dates its category stats to Nov 2026.
- **Data for each app:** Product description; average rating and review count; category measures such as performance, sending, and email building; seller and company details; industries and company sizes; review excerpts; and AI-generated pros, cons, and review summaries. A product description may mention a free plan, trial, or starting price.
- **Filters and sorting:** The page text exposes a category Grid and ranked product content, but no clear reader-controlled filter or sort control was exposed in the public text view I checked. The Grid plots satisfaction against market presence.
- **Price display:** No consistent price-by-subscriber or price-by-send-volume table on the category page. Pricing references vary by vendor description.
- **Sources and dates:** Category stats show a last-updated date of Nov 1, 2026. G2 describes its input as moderated verified reviews and publishes a ranking-method explanation on the page. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** It gives buyers a large review pool, user-reported satisfaction, company context, and a visual way to compare market presence with satisfaction.
- **What is weak or missing:** Review volume and satisfaction are not the same as verified product limits or current plan terms. The category is focused on business marketing software, so creator publishing, transactional-only sending, self-hosting, policy restrictions, and normalized cost are hard to compare here.

### 5. AlternativeTo: Substack alternatives

[Substack Alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/substack/)

- **Who runs it:** AlternativeTo, operated by 27 Kilobyte AB. The [about page](https://alternativeto.net/about/) describes it as a community-sourced software alternative service.
- **Layout:** A ranked list of alternatives, with an app card for each rather than a comparison table.
- **Data for each app:** Likes, short description, cost/license labels such as freemium or open source, application types, country of origin, supported platforms (including web and self-hosted where applicable), and screenshots.
- **Filters and sorting:** No reader-controlled filters or sort controls were exposed in the page text view. The list is numbered and shows likes; AlternativeTo says its alternative lists are crowd-sourced.
- **Price display:** Broad cost/license labels only. It does not show numeric monthly prices, audience limits, or sending quotas.
- **Sources and dates:** The page says it was last updated May 5, 2026. Product cards link to app records, and the [AlternativeTo FAQ](https://alternativeto.net/faq/) explains its community listing model. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** It quickly surfaces alternatives outside the usual email-marketing set and makes license and platform choices visible, including open-source and self-hosted options.
- **What is weak or missing:** Likes and broad app categories do not establish that a product can send newsletters or is a close substitute. There is no comparable price, audience or send limit, newsletter policy, or operational status.

### 6. Zapier: newsletter software guide

[The Best Email Newsletter Platforms and Software in 2026](https://zapier.com/blog/best-email-newsletter-software/)

- **Who runs it:** Zapier's editorial team.
- **Layout:** Long roundup with article navigation, a compact “at a glance” comparison, and individual product reviews.
- **Data for each app:** The summary table has three columns: best for, free plan, and pricing. The longer sections discuss features, usability, segmentation, analytics, monetization, strengths, and weaknesses.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting; the seven products are editorially selected and listed in the article.
- **Price display:** Monthly starting prices tied to an audience count and sometimes an email-send limit or key plan features. For example, the summary pairs a starting monthly price with contacts and plan allowance.
- **Sources and dates:** Dated Feb 2, 2026. The writer says the list considered 80 apps, tested nearly 30 for about a week, and describes the same campaign, template, scheduling, segmentation, and reporting workflow across tools. The page includes an affiliate disclosure. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** The short table answers the first questions quickly, while the reviews explain who each app fits and how it was assessed.
- **What is weak or missing:** The list is small and static. Prices are starting examples, not costs recalculated at shared list sizes or sending workloads. It does not record source URLs and check dates for each plan fact.

### 7. TechRadar: email marketing platforms

[Best Email Marketing Platform in 2026](https://www.techradar.com/best/best-email-marketing-software)

- **Who runs it:** TechRadar Pro, published by Future US, Inc. The article itself identifies the Future group and discloses affiliate links.
- **Layout:** Long editorial ranking with jump links for “best for” categories, individual product reviews, a comparison-table section, and separate pricing tables within product profiles.
- **Data for each app:** Award or use-case label; reviewer summary; reasons to buy and avoid; feature overview; and plan details. Pricing tables vary, but may show plan, monthly price, contacts, monthly sends, or seats.
- **Filters and sorting:** Jump links move readers to an editorial category. They are not product filters, and no sort control was visible.
- **Price display:** Monthly plans with the audience or sending allowance where available. Coverage is uneven: for example, some tables compare contacts and email sends, while others show contacts or seats only. Some product profiles mention more than one regional currency.
- **Sources and dates:** Last updated Nov 2, 2026. The article says the editor tested services and links to a “How we test” section; vendor/deal links and an affiliate disclosure are visible. Checked 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** “Best for” labels make a long list easier to scan. Per-product price tables can show the limits behind an advertised monthly cost.
- **What is weak or missing:** There is no common set of fields across all price tables, so a reader has to piece together the comparison. It is marketing-focused and does not systematically cover transactional-only providers, consent rules, self-hosted editions, or lifecycle events.

## Patterns across these pages

- Most give a short description of what a product is for, a feature summary, and either an editorial “best for” label, user rating, or both.
- Free availability and some form of starting price are common. A few pages show audience or sending limits beside a price, but few use the same limits for every product.
- Product lists are usually static editorial rankings. EmailToolTester’s side-by-side table is the strongest filterable comparison; AlternativeTo adds community likes and platform/license labels; G2 adds user-review and market-presence scores.
- Sources and dates are uneven. Some show an explicit publication or update date and explain testing. Few attach a source URL and checked date to each individual price, feature, or policy fact.
- Newsletter, marketing, transactional, and publishing products are often mixed into one list or split across separate articles. Readers must work out whether each service supports their actual sending use.

## What these pages do not show from our dataset

None of the seven pages combines the following information in a sourced, consistent record for each app:

- Prices calculated at our fixed newsletter scenarios: 1k, 5k, 10k, and 50k subscribers with four campaigns per month; and transactional scenarios at 3k, 10k, 50k, and 100k emails per month. Some competitors show similar isolated tier points, but they do not apply one shared workload across the category.
- A clear product-scope distinction between newsletters, marketing automation, transactional email, and multi-channel notification tools, with separate prices and limits when the vendor sells distinct products.
- Whether the terms allow newsletters or marketing campaigns, along with consent, list import, unsubscribe, or prohibited-content rules.
- Hosted versus self-hosted editions, license, required sending service, and the costs or dependencies needed to operate a self-hosted installation.
- Exact source pages and checked dates for each fact, plus ownership changes, rebrands, shutdowns, retired plans, breaches, or other material lifecycle news.

## Five ideas for our directory page

1. **Start with the sending job.** Let readers choose newsletter, marketing automation, transactional, or multi-channel email before comparing products. Keep separate products from the same company separate when their pricing or limits differ.
2. **Make costs comparable.** Show the fixed subscriber and email-volume scenarios used in our research. Put the assumptions, monthly total, currency, plan, send allowance, and whether the value is a vendor price or our calculation beside each result.
3. **Use filters that answer indie-builder questions.** Add filters for audience size, send volume, free tier, self-hosting, API, newsletter permission, required store/platform, and billing basis. Use “best for” paths as starting points, not unsupported winners.
4. **Put evidence and freshness beside the fact.** Link each price, limit, policy, and ownership claim to its exact source and checked date. Flag a sale, rebrand, shutdown, breach, or retired plan where it changes whether someone should shortlist the product.
5. **Keep the scan fast, with details one click away.** Use a compact comparison table for scope, price at the reader’s workload, limits, and deployment. Expand each app for costs, source notes, policy details, self-host dependencies, and lifecycle history.
