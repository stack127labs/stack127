## Cost examples — our calculations

Baseline inputs: one publication, one user, 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 / 50,000 unique subscribed email contacts, four newsletters to each per month, no paid-reader revenue, no optional custom domain, no discount or attachment workload. Amounts are before tax. The [official cost page](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost), checked 2026-11-04, says free publishing does not depend on subscriber count. It does not publish an unlimited transport-speed guarantee.

| Subscribed contacts | Recipient-email formula | Ordinary monthly platform cost | Separate annual software commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 1,000 × 4 = 4,000/month | $0 | None; ongoing free publishing. |
| 5,000 | 5,000 × 4 = 20,000/month | $0 | None; ongoing free publishing. |
| 10,000 | 10,000 × 4 = 40,000/month | $0 | None; ongoing free publishing. |
| 50,000 | 50,000 × 4 = 200,000/month | $0 | None; ongoing free publishing. |

These are price-model calculations, not tested delivery capacity. Consent review, content restrictions, reader notification choices and possible platform enforcement still apply. A list import is not guaranteed immediate sending access.

Paid-newsletter variant: none of these audience counts requires a fixed paid software tier. Costs instead depend on successfully charged readers. [Paid setup](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037459952-How-do-I-set-up-a-paid-publication-on-Substack), checked 2026-11-04, requires connecting Stripe/enabling payments. Its USD 5/month and USD 30/year minimums are reader prices for US-dollar Stripe accounts.

**Monthly US web-card example, our calculation:**

- Inputs: 1,000 contacts in total; 100 paying readers; USD 10 per reader each month; a US Stripe account; one successful US domestic card payment in USD per reader; no tax, international card, currency conversion, refund, dispute, discount, domain or IAP. Four monthly newsletters remain 4,000 recipient emails; fee calculation depends on 100 payments, not on email count.
- Gross subscription revenue = 100 × USD 10 = **USD 1,000/month**.
- Substack share = USD 1,000 × 10% = **USD 100**.
- Card processing = USD 1,000 × 2.9% + 100 × USD 0.30 = **USD 59**.
- Recurring Billing at the current cost page's 0.7% = USD 1,000 × 0.7% = **USD 7**.
- Listed fee subtotal = USD 100 + USD 59 + USD 7 = **USD 166/month**; remainder = **USD 834/month before other costs**.
- If the older payment-method guide's 0.5% Billing rate applied instead, Billing would be USD 5, fees USD 164, and remainder USD 836. This USD 2 difference is shown because official help conflicts; it is not silently rounded away.

Sources checked 2026-11-04: [current Substack costs](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost), [reader payment methods](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/18687769631252-How-can-readers-pay-for-a-subscription-on-my-Substack-publication), [linked Stripe card pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) and [Stripe Billing](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing). The calculation uses the published US example rather than applying US rates worldwide. It is not a guaranteed take-home quote for every connected account.

**Annual-reader variant, our calculation:** Same 100 paying readers, each charged USD 100 once per year, gross USD 10,000/year. This is a chosen reader offer, not an annual Substack software plan or a promotion.

- Platform = USD 10,000 × 10% = USD 1,000/year.
- Card = USD 10,000 × 2.9% + 100 × USD 0.30 = USD 320/year.
- Billing at 0.7% = USD 70/year.
- Listed fees = **USD 1,390/year**, leaving **USD 8,610/year before other costs**. At the conflicting 0.5% rate: fees USD 1,370, remainder USD 8,630.

The annual fee basis and assumptions use the same sources above. No exchange-rate conversion is performed. Actual taxes, refunds, disputes, international cards, local methods, settlement, special agreements and fee-waiver countries can change the result.

**Optional custom-domain variant, our calculation:** Free publication + **USD 50 one-time connection** + the chosen registrar's registration/renewal charge. The [domain guide](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051222571-How-do-I-set-up-my-custom-domain-on-Substack), checked 2026-11-04, says no VAT/sales tax on Substack's connection fee and registration is separate. The registrar/domain is unspecified, so the full first-year or renewal total cannot be exact. This fee buys a website-domain connection, not custom-domain email sending. The product page's conflicting domain-purchase claim needs recheck.

**Apple IAP:** No web-card fee total is reused. The [current iOS guide](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19718129670420-Can-my-subscribers-pay-on-the-Substack-iOS-app), checked 2026-11-04, lists Apple fees of 15%–30%, price-point adjustments and different payout routes. Actual IAP price, fee phase, seller country and any direct-bank early-release fee are unspecified. The official [IAP FAQ](https://on.substack.com/p/in-app-payments-iap-faq), checked 2026-11-04, bases Substack's share on original web price. A single exact worldwide IAP take-home figure cannot be calculated from these inputs.

**App-email scenarios:** 3,000 / 10,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 arbitrary application emails are not priced. General sending-interface access, required audience/workflow assumptions and transport quotas are not established. Read-only MCP is not a sending relay.

## Field problems

- F06/F08/F09 need to separate software fees, paid-reader transactions and displayed paid-audience counters. Gifts/trials/comps can appear in paid counts without current subscription revenue. Subscriber-count tiers are the wrong model here.
- F04/F14 need creator country, connected account type, reader location and web/App Store payment route. An ordinary Stripe supported-country list cannot settle Substack's exceptions or exact IAP payout access.
- F14 needs an effective date. The announced tax service starts after this check and has explicit exceptions. Reader tax added at checkout is different from platform cost or creator income tax.
- F15/F16 need item-level portability. Names are explicitly not exportable; address export and subscription ownership do not transfer Apple billing. General incoming comps and provider-specific Stripe transfers should not be flattened into one yes/no.
- F19/F21 need separate evidence for a marketing claim, account eligibility and actual setup controls. Advertised drip sequences/email CSS do not justify either an unrestricted confirmed workflow or an unsupported claim of no feature.
- F20 must distinguish notification targeting from content access. A post sent only to one segment can still be visible to other web/app readers.
- F24/F26/F27 should not force a newsletter publisher into a relay comparison. Website CNAMEs, PDF upload limits, email throughput, API requests and independent IP streams are different capabilities.
- F28/F30 need processing role and data type. Empty appendix extraction is a check failure. No fixed purge deadline can be invented from as soon as possible, and report lookback is not backend retention.
- F29 needs delivery channel and geography. Current open/delivery metrics combine app and email; France/Italy email-open exclusions can affect engagement-based segmentation. Comparing these figures directly with SMTP-only reports would mislead.
- F32 should preserve the distinction between an expected response target and a contractual uptime/resolution SLA.
- F37 cannot use promotional claims as independent tests under the official-source-only rule. No qualifying original independent study was established.
- No `apps.md` row error found. The row remains a reasonable newsletter classification; this research also identifies creator-hosting, community and media overlaps.
