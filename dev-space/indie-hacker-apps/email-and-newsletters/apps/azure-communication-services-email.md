- `apps.md` row status: `active` (row checked 2026-11-04). **This status is stale:** Microsoft now lists ACS Email as retiring on 2028-09-30. The row should be updated by the directory owner; I did not edit `apps.md`.

- Research finished: 2026-11-06 12:15:15 UTC (`date -u`).

- Retirement/news check: Microsoft says ACS Email is available until 2028-09-30, in maintenance mode for security and critical fixes. It recommends moving workloads before that date. Microsoft’s Azure retirement guide is the product-specific notice. I also searched Azure Blog, Azure Updates, and Microsoft Newsroom for a separate shutdown or acquisition notice; I found no separate acquisition notice or additional product announcement.
- Newsletter policy: opted-in marketing and bulk email are supported. Promotional email requires prior written consent, and every email needs a working unsubscribe link. The sender must remove opt-outs from its own list; spam is prohibited.
- Price scope: this file covers only ACS Email send and data-transfer charges. It excludes Azure subscription, Monitor, Event Grid, Blob Storage, and other Azure products. The Azure pricing table shows `$-` for email rates. Microsoft Learn has sample rates but warns they may not be current. Cost examples below are our calculations from those illustrative rates, not current quotes. The Azure pricing page labels examples USD and assumes East US 2; it does not publish a regional email rate table.
- Research scope: official public pages only; no account, signup, forms, or sending tests. Prices and technical behavior are vendor documentation, not measured inbox performance.

## Cost examples

All totals below are **our calculations** from Microsoft Learn’s illustrative rates, not current quotes. Microsoft’s current Azure pricing table returned `$-` for both Email sent and MB transferred. Learn says its examples may not reflect latest Azure pricing. The rate page’s example context is USD and East US 2; no region-by-region email rate schedule was available. Assumption: 0.2 MB per recipient, one recipient per message, no attachments, four newsletter sends per month, no taxes, one user and one verified sender domain.

- Formula: `recipient emails × $0.00025 + transferred MB × $0.00012`.
- Newsletter baseline: unique subscribed contacts × 4 sends monthly. At 50,000 subscribers, default 100/hour throttling is too low for the monthly volume; a higher custom-domain quota needs approval. Smaller weekly campaign bursts can also need a higher quota.
- App-email baseline: one recipient per email, spread evenly over 30 days. At 100,000/month, the average is about 139/hour, above the initial custom-domain rate of 100/hour. The price estimate does not mean the default account can deliver that burst pattern.
- Totals: 1,000 newsletter subscribers: `$1.096`; 5,000: `$5.48`; 10,000: `$10.96`; 50,000: `$54.80`. App-email volumes: 3,000: `$0.822`; 10,000: `$2.740`; 50,000: `$13.700`; 100,000: `$27.400`. Currency: USD in the East US 2 example context. Exact current totals are unknown.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Retirement and signup:** the discovery row says `active`, but Microsoft’s September 2026 guide calls ACS Email retiring with an end date of 2028-09-30. The guide says customers can currently onboard workloads to new ACS Email resources, with availability subject to change. Treat it as usable during transition, not a continuing product.
- **Price:** Azure pricing table `https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/communication-services/` displays `$-` for Email sent and MB transferred. I tried the official Learn pricing guide `https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/communication-services/concepts/email-pricing`; it gives sample rates but warns they may not be current. I did not turn these into a current price.
- **Quota failure rate:** Service Limits says below 1% (last updated 2025-08-08), while Quota Increase says below 2% (last updated 2026-09-23). These official pages conflict. The newer Quota Increase page is used for planning at below 2%; the older Service Limits page remains inconsistent.
- **Region/currency:** East US 2 and USD are the Azure pricing page’s example context, not a regional ACS Email price table. Current local-currency rates and quote-specific price were not established.
- **Privacy:** email body is processed in the chosen Data Location, but Azure Monitor workspace and global Event Grid events have separate locations/retention. “Global” resource location is not a statement that message content is stored globally.
- **Newsletter mechanics:** product docs support bulk/marketing delivery but do not provide a newsletter editor, contact database, native signup forms, or native list export. The sender app must implement consent, unsubscribe, list suppression, and campaign scheduling.
- **Source search for shutdown/acquisition:** checked the ACS retirement guide, Azure Blog (`https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/`), Azure Updates (`https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/updates/`), and Microsoft Newsroom (`https://news.microsoft.com/`). The retirement guide is the product-specific notice; no separate acquisition notice was found. The 2022 preview announcement is history, not current status.
- **Other unpublished values:** checked the product overview, email pricing, service limits, quota increase, support, privacy, logs/Insights, SDK, and terms pages for language and customer eligibility, annual commitment, contact storage, idempotency, complaint thresholds, IP assignment, and log retention. For the SLA, the current index showed a November 2026 edition, but its DOCX download failed with an unsupported-content-type error. ## Field problems

- F06’s usual subscriber-billing questions do not fit a delivery API. ACS Email bills recipients and data, while the customer’s own application owns list counts and deduplication.
- F09 and F10 can be calculated only as illustrative estimates. The official pricing table’s `$-` placeholders prevent an exact current cost; even the Learn rates carry an explicit warning.
- F17 consent and unsubscribe work is a customer responsibility. The suppression-list management API is in preview without a production SLA.
- F27 combines message-size and recipient-batch limits with account-level sending quotas. Custom and Azure-managed domains have different limits; quota upgrades are manually reviewed and may change during retirement.
- F28 needs separate reporting of email processing geography, Azure Monitor location, and global Event Grid data. These are not interchangeable.
- F32 links to an ACS SLA, but the exact email-specific availability commitment was not established from the current SLA material checked. No percentage is guessed.
- F33 and F34 are not applicable because ACS Email is hosted. F13, F14, F15, F16, F20, and F35 are also not applicable to its delivery-only scope.
- F40 is the evidence-recording method in the approved schema, not a product capability.
