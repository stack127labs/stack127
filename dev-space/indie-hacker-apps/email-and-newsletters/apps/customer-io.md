## Cost examples

All figures below are our calculations using Customer.io’s displayed dollar prices. The page does not show an ISO currency code. Monthly prices are before tax. Newsletter assumptions: one workspace, one profile per subscriber, no custom objects or other profiles, four full-list campaigns, and no other email sends. App-email assumptions: one recipient per message, no other email sends, 5,000 total profiles or fewer, and monthly volume spread across 30 days.

### Marketing newsletters

| Subscribers | Inputs | Formula | Essentials monthly total |
| ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1,000 | 4 full-list campaigns = 4,000 emails | $100 base; profiles and email volume within included limits | $100 |
| 5,000 | 4 full-list campaigns = 20,000 emails | $100 base; 5,000 profiles and email volume within included limits | $100 |
| 10,000 | 4 full-list campaigns = 40,000 emails | $100 + (10,000 − 5,000) × $0.009 profile overage | $145 |
| 50,000 | 4 full-list campaigns = 200,000 emails | $100 + (50,000 − 5,000) × $0.009 profile overage | $505 |

All four workloads are under the included 1,000,000 monthly email sends. These are standard Essentials estimates using built-in delivery. Any other email sends, including transactional messages, would use the same assumed monthly allowance. Eligible Startup Program accounts may instead receive up to 30,000 profiles at no charge for up to 12 months; the program requires application, and its detailed email/overage treatment above the included profile count is not shown on its public page. If combined sends exceed one million, the published email overage is $0.12 per additional 1,000 emails.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Newsletter versus one-time-send names:** The July 2026 UI calls the feature “One-time sends,” while API endpoints and older docs still call it “Newsletters.” The release notes say API names and endpoints remain unchanged. This is a naming transition, not a pricing conflict. Source: https://docs.customer.io/release-notes/ (checked 2026-11-06).
- **Marketing and transactional email allowance:** Pricing says Essentials includes 1M monthly email sends and separately marks transactional email as included. Billing counts emails sent from the account, including bounced and suppressed sends, but does not spell out separate marketing and transactional buckets. Cost examples use one shared allowance and flag that assumption. Sources: https://customer.io/pricing ; https://docs.customer.io/accounts/billing/how-we-bill/ (checked 2026-11-06).
- **Newsletter policy:** Customer.io allows permission-based newsletters, but the AUP requires express brand-specific consent and prohibits purchased, scraped, partner-sourced, and two-year inactive/unengaged addresses. Affiliate marketing requires a customer-owned SMTP provider. Source: https://customer.io/legal/acceptable-use-policy (checked 2026-11-06; revised November 2024).
- **Trial and sending ramp:** Trial messages have a 5,000 cross-channel cap; new Essentials accounts also begin with a lower rolling daily email limit. These are separate limits. Sources: https://docs.customer.io/accounts/billing/plan-features/ (checked 2026-11-06; updated October 19, 2026).
- **Blog/newsroom:** `https://customer.io/blog` redirects to the official Learn page. The attempted `/newsroom` page was inaccessible. Learn, official release notes, Terms, About, and status were checked for company and product changes. A Gist acquisition notice appeared; no Customer.io shutdown or acquisition notice was found.
- **Country and language availability:** Terms and region docs were checked, but no full account eligibility or dashboard/support-language list was published on those pages.
- **Transactional-only pricing:** No separate transactional product price is listed. Customer.io pricing is based on the platform plan, profiles, and email volume. A custom plan or custom SMTP may add costs outside the Essentials examples.
- **Delivery limits:** The new-account daily ramp is published, but the final standard daily limit after warming is described as the plan’s standard limit without a number. Maximum message/attachment size was not found in the API and sending pages checked.
- **Data export and deletion:** People, events, deliveries, devices, relationships, consent history, and suppressed identifiers have documented exports. A complete workspace export for all templates, workflows, and reports was not established. DPA allows deletion after data download, but does not give one fixed retention duration for every data type.
- **No independent delivery test:** The pages checked include Customer.io’s own performance and deliverability claims, but no reproducible independent inbox-placement result.

## Field problems

- F04 combines account eligibility, recipient geography, and interface/support languages. Customer.io publishes US/EU data regions but not a complete supported-country or language list.
- F06 profiles and email sends are both billable units. The same record in multiple workspaces can count multiple times, and deleted/re-added profiles may count twice during a billing period.
- F09 is profile-priced as well as email-priced. Scenario totals assume each subscriber is one profile and ignore other people or custom objects in the account.
- F10 has no separate transactional price. The examples use the lowest Essentials subscription and assume the included email allowance is shared, because the billing page counts account emails but does not name separate marketing and transactional quotas.
- F17 includes a strict permission policy. Affiliate marketing must go through the customer's own SMTP, even though other marketing email can use Customer.io's built-in sender.
- F26 separates transactional IP routing from marketing domains, but moving to the transactional pool requires approval and is unavailable during trial.
- F27 API request limits, monthly email allowance, profile caps, and daily warm-up limits are different measures. Keep each in its own row.
- F37 asks for independent deliverability evidence; no third-party test was used as proof in this official-source research.
- F40 records the approved local field schema rather than a comparable product feature.
