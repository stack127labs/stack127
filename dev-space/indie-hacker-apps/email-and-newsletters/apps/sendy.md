## Cost examples — our calculation

Inputs: one publication, one user, one verified sender domain, one recipient per message, shared sending IP and no attachments. ### Lifecycle-only scenarios and the app-email limitation

Sendy documents list-based autoresponders and campaign APIs. It does not document a general direct-recipient transaction-send endpoint in the current API. These are therefore **conditional lifecycle component estimates**, not prices for arbitrary receipts, password resets or a drop-in SMTP relay.

To make both contacts and sequence entries explicit, our model starts with zero contacts, adds **1,000 new contacts in a 30-day month**, and starts **1,000 sequence entries**, one per new subscriber. Each contact receives 3, 10, 50 or 100 sequence emails within that month, producing the required volumes below. The model ends with 1,000 contacts; keeping later cohorts increases stored records. No other sequences, confirmations or campaign emails are included. Sendy does not publish a workflow-run billing unit; “sequence entry” here is our modelling unit.

Spread new entries over the month, at most 34 on a day. A conservative model that permits all steps for that day's entrants gives planned daily peaks of **102 / 340 / 1,700 / 3,400 emails**, respectively. These are our workload assumptions, not published limits. Pace sending at no more than one email/second and obtain the actual required SES production quota. The 200/day sandbox is not the selected configuration.

**The maximum supported sequence length, run count and required processing capacity were not established.** In particular, a 100-email sequence is an assumption used to expose the cost arithmetic, not a verified Sendy capability guarantee. Before using any lifecycle total operationally, check that the intended delays, steps, events and server capacity fit. External application work for arbitrary transaction events remains unpriced.
