## Status and scope

CoinPayments is an active custodial crypto payment gateway for merchants. New accounts use its separate New platform; the Classic platform is for accounts opened before 2025. Both remain accessible during the transition, but account data, funds and verification do not transfer automatically. The public pricing page has a Legacy/New switch for network-fee tables; it has no visible seller-country, billing-period or fiat-currency switch. Standard processing is 0.5% for coins and 1% for tokens on New, versus 3% for both on Legacy. High-risk business pricing may be adjusted.

CoinPayments says it stopped serving EU/EEA clients on 1 July 2026 because of MiCA requirements. Affected legacy balances remain held separately until the owner contacts support and passes identity checks. The listing is active for supported markets, but this is a material regional service pause.

An official CoinPayments post says a withdrawal-control error on 5 June 2017 let some long-term users withdraw excess XRP from hot wallets. The company says it was not caused by external hackers and did not call it a hack; all affected users were reimbursed in full by December 2017. It says it added safeguards. The current official News & Insights archive and the older official blog were checked for acquisition, licence, regulatory, security and shutdown notices. I found no CoinPayments acquisition or company shutdown notice. A March 2026 OSL partnership post mentions OSL’s January 2026 acquisition of Banxa; this is a partner acquisition, not a CoinPayments acquisition. The EU/EEA suspension above is the current material region change.

## Cost examples

- **Our calculation, New platform coin payment:** USD 100 × 0.5% = USD 0.50 CoinPayments processing fee, before any applicable blockchain/network cost or conversion-partner charge.
- **Our calculation, New platform token payment:** USD 100 × 1% = USD 1.00 CoinPayments processing fee, before any applicable blockchain/network cost or conversion-partner charge.
- **Our calculation, Legacy payment:** USD 100 × 3% = USD 3.00 processing fee, plus a network fee for ASAP/Nightly settlement where applicable.

The standard fee page does not define a separate universal fee base for every industry or contract. High-risk clients may receive adjusted pricing; use the quote shown for the account.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- The current FAQ says service to EU/EEA clients ended on 1 July 2026 under MiCA. The New platform and general global marketing pages remain live; I use the FAQ’s dated restriction for EU/EEA eligibility and status.
- New and Legacy pricing differ materially: 0.5% coins/1% tokens on New, versus 3% on Legacy. The fees page includes a New/Legacy network-fee switch; the Classic page is not the New rate card.
- The company’s 2018 statement describes a June 2017 XRP theft caused by a withdrawal error. It explicitly says external hackers were not involved and reports full reimbursement by December 2017. I preserve that account and do not label it an external hack.
- Seller signup is country-routed through regional platform instances, but no exhaustive seller allow-list, sole-trader matrix, fiat payout country list, or standard bank payout fee is public. The “180 countries” statement is not evidence that businesses in all 180 can sign up.
- Current FAQ describes fiat bank settlement, while the integration guide labels its older Fiat Settlement tool “TBD.” The FAQ describes the broader account-managed off-ramp, and the guide’s older dashboard feature is not treated as proof of universal availability.
- Public docs do not give universal payout minimums, fiat conversion spreads, merchant reserve percentage/duration, or individual account caps. Live asset-specific limits and compliance decisions can vary.
