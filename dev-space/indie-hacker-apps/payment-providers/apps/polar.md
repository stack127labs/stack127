## Cost examples: our calculation

Calculated on 2026-11-04. All amounts below are USD. These are comparison examples, not a Polar quote. They exclude refunds, disputes, fee taxes, receiving-bank charges and unpriced conversion spreads. Calculations keep the published percentage plus fixed-fee structure; they do not add a second ordinary Stripe processing fee.

### One USD 100 sale

Inputs: one successful USD 100 charge, zero buyer sales tax, US-issued card, no conversion. Platform inputs: [home](https://polar.sh/); legacy and subscription inputs: [fees](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees); included processor cost: [why](https://polar.sh/resources/why). Checked 2026-11-04.

Our calculation: payment fee = charge amount × plan percentage + fixed transaction fee.

| Plan and transaction | Inputs and formula | Our calculated payment fee | Separate monthly plan cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | 100 × 0.05 + 0.50 | USD 5.50 | USD 0 |
| Pro | 100 × 0.038 + 0.40 | USD 4.20 | USD 20 |
| Growth | 100 × 0.036 + 0.35 | USD 3.95 | USD 100 |
| Scale | 100 × 0.034 + 0.30 | USD 3.70 | USD 400 |
| Early Member one-time | 100 × 0.04 + 0.40 | USD 4.40 | USD 0 |
| Early Member subscription | 100 × (0.04 + 0.005) + 0.40 | USD 4.90 | USD 0 |

For the same charge on a non-US card, our calculated extra is 100 × 0.015 = USD 1.50. Add that amount to each row. The resulting Starter fee is USD 7.00. The geographic input is card issuance, not seller residence. Source: [finance](https://polar.sh/features/finance), checked 2026-11-04.

### One hundred USD 10 transactions in one month

Inputs: 100 successful USD 10 transactions, USD 1,000 total charged, US cards, zero tax, no conversion, no payout. Same plan sources and checked date as above.

Our calculation: monthly total = monthly plan charge + 100 × (10 × rate + fixed transaction charge).

| Plan | Formula | Our calculated total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | 0 + 100 × (10 × 0.05 + 0.50) | USD 100.00 |
| Pro | 20 + 100 × (10 × 0.038 + 0.40) | USD 98.00 |
| Growth | 100 + 100 × (10 × 0.036 + 0.35) | USD 171.00 |
| Scale | 400 + 100 × (10 × 0.034 + 0.30) | USD 464.00 |
| Early Member, one-time | 0 + 100 × (10 × 0.04 + 0.40) | USD 80.00 |
| Early Member, recurring | 0 + 100 × (10 × 0.045 + 0.40) | USD 85.00 |

The public breakeven comparison does not state its assumed average transaction size. Our example fixes transaction count and ticket size so its result is reproducible. This example does not claim that the advertised sales-only thresholds apply to every seller.

### Tax-inclusive fee base

Inputs: Starter; USD 100 before tax; illustrative 20% buyer tax; non-US card; no conversion. The tax rate is an example input, not a claim about a specific jurisdiction. The pricing page's example establishes the tax-inclusive fee base: [pricing](https://polar.sh/resources/pricing), checked 2026-11-04.

Our calculation:

- Gross charge = 100 × 1.20 = USD 120.00.
- Base transaction fee = 120 × 0.05 + 0.50 = USD 6.50.
- Non-US card extra = 120 × 0.015 = USD 1.80.
- Total payment fees = 6.50 + 1.80 = USD 8.30.
- Supplier balance contribution before payout = 120 − 20 buyer tax − 8.30 = USD 91.70.

### One bank withdrawal

Inputs: eligible USD 1,000 balance; one USD payout in the month; no cross-border conversion; the USD 2 active-month charge has not already been applied. Source: [payouts](https://polar.sh/docs/features/finance/payouts), checked 2026-11-04.

Our calculation: payout costs = 2 + (1,000 × 0.0025) + 0.25 = USD 4.75. Remaining amount = 1,000 − 4.75 = USD 995.25, before any unpriced receiving-bank charge. This is a nominal payout-fee calculation. The active-month component is counted once, not once per withdrawal.

## Field problems

- F20–F21 use “domestic” and “international,” but Polar's published boundary is US versus non-US cards. Supplier country does not reset that boundary.
- F19 and F24 need organization creation date and plan history. A single present-day headline rate would erase the legacy exception.
- F15 needs interface and release-version scope. Dashboard availability, a preview feature and a Next API operation are different claims.
- F22 needs two conversion stages: customer charge to USD ledger, then USD ledger to bank currency. A single FX percentage cannot represent both.
- F29 needs a registration/responsibility matrix. “Global” coverage and named registrations cannot fill every country cell.
- F33, F36, F40 and F44 describe different cash delays. Settlement, review, risk, migration and terminated-contract rules cannot become one universal payout delay or reserve percentage.
- F34's table names payout currencies but explicitly measures every minimum in USD. Changing those amounts into the named payout currency would change the fact.
- F35 mixes API input bounds with real collection limits. Both were attempted, with the distinction recorded.
- F37 needs a distinction between ledger APIs, an unverified CSV route, copying saved methods and recreating a working subscription. Secure credential transfer alone does not recreate the catalog.
- F38 needs separate seller support and the support the seller owes its own buyers. Checkout translations are also separate from human support languages.
- C01–C05 are conditional crypto fields. Their not_applicable state is scoped to this documented fiat offer, not a claim that crypto or self-hosting can never exist elsewhere.
