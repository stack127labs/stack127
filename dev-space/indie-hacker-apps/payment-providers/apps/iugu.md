# iugu

- **Official URL:** https://www.iugu.com/
- **apps.md status:** active
- **Product scope:** Brazil-focused payment infrastructure and recurring billing. Covers payment processing, payment links, hosted and embedded checkout, Pix, boleto, credit cards, API billing and marketplace split. iugu is the payment intermediary; the seller remains responsible for the products and customers.
- **Current status and company news:** Active. iugu's investor site lists Goldman Sachs among its investors and says iugu acquired the Juno brand and Brazilian customer portfolio in 2022. The newsroom lists 2026 business and regulatory updates. I checked the company blog and investor newsroom and found no iugu sale, shutdown or sunset notice.

## Cost examples

These are our calculations using the public Essencial rates and the current contract's default non-settled boleto fee. Percentage examples assume the percentage applies to the R$100.00 transaction amount; they exclude tax and account-specific adjustments:

- **Example:** 100 settled boletos allow up to 300 non-settled/cancelled boletos for the monthly ratio. If the account has 350 non-settled/cancelled boletos, the excess is 50.
- **Our calculation:** 50 excess boletos × R$0.20 = **R$10.00**.
- The R$0.20 amount is the contract default when no negotiated price exists. An older help article shows R$0.12; the current contract amount is used above.
- **Card, one-time, Essencial:** R$100.00 × 3.34% + R$0.40 processing fee = **R$3.74** calculated fee.
- **Card, six installments, Essencial:** R$100.00 × 4.28% + R$0.40 processing fee = **R$4.68** calculated fee.
- **Pix, Essencial:** R$100.00 × 0.99% = **R$0.99** calculated fee.
- A paid boleto is listed at R$2.19. The price table separately lists R$0.40 per boleto/card processing but does not say whether it is additive to that boleto price, so I do not calculate a combined amount.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- iugu's current contract sets card proceeds at up to 32 days per installment. A payment-method help article says 30 calendar days. I used the current contract terms and retained the older help value in the F33 note.
- The current contract's default excess non-settled boleto fee is R$0.20; a 2024 help article says R$0.12. I used the current contract amount. Essencial settled boletos are publicly priced at R$2.19; Motor and other negotiated account rates are custom.
- The current onboarding help page says new account registration is only for legal entities, while the contract also describes CPF/PF documents for partners and some account structures. I treated the onboarding article as the current main-seller route and did not infer a CPF-only seller product.
- The prohibited-products page lists infoproducts and dropshipping as prohibited, while the contract allows some listed categories to be accepted exceptionally with evidence and iugu's approval. The default is prohibited; exceptions are conditional.
- Current official profile names Goldman Sachs as an investor but does not identify a controlling owner. The Juno transaction was an acquisition by iugu in 2022, not an acquisition of iugu.
- iugu does not publish a full list of supported foreign cardholder countries, seller account countries beyond its Brazilian route, foreign exchange rates or custom Motor plan rates. The pages checked are listed in the corresponding rows.
- The public Essencial price table shows base method rates and a separate boleto/card processing charge. It does not state the rate calculation base, tax treatment or whether that R$0.40 processing line adds to the R$2.19 settled-boleto price; I have not inferred those terms.

## Field problems

- `apps.md` describes the seller market as “Latin America; Brazil.” Official onboarding and contract materials support Brazilian PJ/CNPJ accounts and domestic service use; they do not support a list of Latin American seller countries.
- The iugu contract allows negotiated fees in the account panel/proposal, while some public support pages expose specific rules such as excess unpaid-boleto charges. I kept those narrow charges separate from the unpublished standard transaction tariff.
- iugu uses boleto/Pix subscription billing to create recurring invoices; automatic card debit and Pix Automático are separate collection flows. I recorded that distinction in F14.
