## Cost examples — our calculations

These examples use the **new Philippine Xendit route**, not an old Dragonpay quote. Inputs are current standard method fees plus the processing fee, with 12% Philippine VAT on provider fees. They assume PHP settlement, successful payments, no FX, no dispute, no separate subscription/add-on fee, and no legacy-API maintenance charge. The figures are fee components, not a promised bank deposit. Sources: <https://www.xendit.co/en-ph/pricing/>; <https://docs.xendit.co/docs/value-added-tax-vat.md>; <https://help.xendit.co/hc/en-us/articles/59516240127129-Xendit-Pricing-Policy> (checked 2026-11-05).

| Scenario / inputs | Formula — our calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PHP 100 domestic-card sale | Method fee = 100 × 3.50% = PHP 3.50; add PHP 11 processing = PHP 14.50; VAT = 14.50 × 12% = PHP 1.74. | PHP 16.24 fee including VAT; PHP 83.76 after these components, before monthly minimum/other deductions. |
| PHP 1,000 domestic-card sale | (1,000 × 3.50% + 11) × 1.12 | PHP 51.52 fee including VAT; PHP 948.48 after these components. |
| PHP 1,000 international-card sale, charged/settled in PHP | (1,000 × 4.50% + 10 + 11) × 1.12 | PHP 73.92 fee including VAT; PHP 926.08 after these components. No FX fee is assumed merely because the card is foreign. |
| PHP 100 QRPH sale | [max(100 × 1.50%, 15) + 11] × 1.12 | PHP 29.12 fee including VAT; PHP 70.88 after these components. The PHP 15 method minimum controls. |
| 100 domestic-card payments of PHP 1,000 in one month | Sales = 100 × 1,000 = PHP 100,000; fees before VAT = 100 × 46 = PHP 4,600; VAT = 4,600 × 12% = PHP 552. | PHP 5,152 total payment fees with VAT; PHP 94,848 before reserves, other services and own-balance withdrawal costs. The published PHP 3,000 monthly minimum is exceeded in this example. |

For low-volume months, the published monthly floor matters more than the per-sale illustration. The agreement describes a shortfall charge against a USD 50 monthly minimum; the policy publishes PHP 3,000 as the local equivalent and excludes chargeback fees from qualifying monthly invoices. Do not add a second full minimum after qualifying fees already reach it. The exact VAT treatment of a minimum-fee top-up was not separately established, so no all-in low-volume monthly amount is invented. Sources: <https://www.xendit.co/en/xendit-service-agreement/>; <https://help.xendit.co/hc/en-us/articles/59516240127129-Xendit-Pricing-Policy>; <https://docs.xendit.co/docs/value-added-tax-vat.md> (checked 2026-11-05).

A covered non-card remittance may also have Philippine withholding. **Our calculation:** if the qualifying fee-adjusted remittance base were PHP 10,000, withholding = 10,000 × 50% × 1% = PHP 50; PHP 9,950 remains after that withholding. This assumes the merchant is covered and has no applicable exemption. It is a tax withholding, separate from payment processing expense. Source: <https://docs.xendit.co/docs/withholding-tax-on-gross-remittances-ph.md> (checked 2026-11-05).

The legacy recurring product's PHP 20 headline is not used as a new-applicant cost example because its current contract applicability and tax treatment are unresolved. Source: <https://www.dragonpay.ph/services/recurring-payments/>; <https://www.dragonpay.ph/pricing/> (checked 2026-11-05).
