## Cost examples

All examples are our calculations using the current published AZUL tariff. Currency is Dominican pesos (DOP/RD$). The tariff does not say whether these fees include ITBIS, so the calculations leave ITBIS treatment open. AZUL sets each merchant's MDR by agreement and says it can be up to 6%; 6% examples are the public maximum, not a universal merchant quote.

| Example | Inputs and formula | Our calculation |
|---|---|---|
| One DOP 10,000 e-commerce sale in a month at the published maximum MDR | DOP 2,750 monthly e-commerce fee + (DOP 10,000 × 6% = DOP 600 MDR). | DOP 3,350 before any applicable ITBIS or contract-specific charges. The merchant's actual MDR may be lower and is reviewed monthly. |
| Ten successful and two declined recurring charges | (10 × DOP 5 per processed charge) + (2 × DOP 10 per declined charge). | DOP 70 in recurring transaction charges. This excludes any complementary tariff, MDR, monthly product fee and ITBIS. |
| 1,000 transactions with cardholder authentication | 1,000 × DOP 8 per transaction for the 1,000–9,999 monthly tier. | DOP 8,000 for the authentication add-on, before any ITBIS. |

## Conflicts and unknowns

- AZUL's tariff page was checked on 6 November 2026 and states it was last updated on 30 October 2026. It lists DOP 2,750 monthly for e-commerce and payment links, DOP 5 per processed recurring payment, DOP 10 per declined recurring payment, DOP 1,800 monthly for Data Vault, and card authentication by transaction-volume tier. The page does not say if these fees include ITBIS.
- The tariff does not disclose merchant-specific card MDR beyond a maximum of 6%, merchant activity/volume analysis and monthly review. It does not split domestic and international card rates.
- The current price table labels a DOP 649 recurring “complementary” tariff and says the footnoted fee applies to localities billing below DOP 350,000; it does not state the time period or precise stacking rule. This row stays unclear.
- AZUL's public site links an affiliation contract but does not publish the full merchant contract. Consequently, closure details, chargeback fee amounts, risk reserves and the expected-sales penalty are not publicly settled.
- The app mentions an expected-sales shortfall charge; the public FAQ does not state the calculation. Do not confuse it with the 6% maximum MDR.
- The card currency configured for a merchant ID is singular; DCC lets eligible cardholders choose their home currency. This does not establish USD settlement for the merchant.
- Pages checked for products/restrictions: AZUL e-commerce, payment page, recurring payments, payment links, FAQ, affiliation requirements, developer portal and integration guide, tariff, public app terms and privacy notice. No public forbidden-business list or general merchant terms page was found.
- Pages checked for status/ownership: AZUL Conócenos and newsroom, Grupo Popular subsidiary and newsroom pages, and current product pages. No acquisition, shutdown or AZUL-to-other-brand notice was found.

## Field problems

- F03 cannot be reduced to a global seller-country list: foreign applicants without a Dominican identity card must have an RNC and passport, while the public pages do not define whether a foreign-incorporated company can qualify.
- AZUL's tariff does not publish whether stated service fees include ITBIS. The separate 2% ITBIS withholding is calculated on ITBIS shown in the card transaction; it is not the provider's MDR.
- The general expected-sales penalty and the DOP 649 recurring complementary tariff refer to merchant-specific or incomplete conditions. Their values/stacking are not fully published.
- E-commerce settlement pages give next-day/24-hour language but do not give a separate weekend or bank-holiday guarantee for all online sellers.
- The current public terms are app-use terms; the merchant affiliation agreement itself is private/account-specific.
