## Shutdown, sunset, acquisition and migration notices

All status/notice checks below are dated **2026-11-05**.

- **App-registration migration:** the current business homepage says new users can no longer register in **Maya Negosyo from 4 September 2026**. It directs new and existing customers to the replacement Maya Business app on Android/iOS/web; existing users can log in with their existing details. This is not a statement that Maya Checkout or the whole acquiring service has closed. <https://www.maya.ph/business>
- **Company/product rebrand:** PayMaya Enterprise became Maya Business, and PayMaya Philippines, Inc. became Maya Philippines, Inc. The company's name-change FAQ says its corporate structure/SEC number did not change and existing contracts remain valid. No whole-company acquisition, whole-service shutdown or new whole-gateway sunset notice was found in the reviewed official product/company/legal material and targeted official searches. <https://www.maya.ph/business-updates>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/company-name-updates>; <https://www.maya.ph/business>
- **Bank-site move:** Maya Bank's Business Deposit page says its bank website/content will move to maya.ph without service impact; no migration date is supplied. Bank services remain separate from acquiring. <https://www.mayabank.ph/business-deposit/>
- **Legacy public pages require care:** the Enterprise page contains expired **28 February 2021** promotional rates and old PayMaya branding, and `checkout-old` has a **2015** footer. Current online pricing is taken from the current Maya Business pricing/Checkout pages. Old currency/settlement claims are retained only as unresolved legacy evidence. <https://enterprise.maya.ph/payment-gateway>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/checkout-old>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/pricing>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/checkout>
- **Integration limits:** modern Maya Business Manager and Maya Manager 1.0 have different production/sandbox roles. Technical docs distinguish hosted Checkout, saved-card Vault, linked Maya Wallet and the Payments Processing Platform; recurring capability is not a fully managed subscription engine. <https://developers.maya.ph/docs/online-payments-quick-start-guide-maya-business-manager>; <https://developers.maya.ph/docs/maya-vault>; <https://developers.maya.ph/docs/maya-account-linking>; <https://developers.maya.ph/docs/payments-processing-platform-recurring-transactions>

Scope: **Maya Business Philippine merchant acquiring**, principally Checkout, Payment Links, Digital/Email Invoice, Vault and related recurring/API products. Terminal differences are shown where a real tariff/feature changes. Optional Maya Bank Business Deposit, app transfers and Juan Tax-powered Quick Tax have separate scope and costs. Consumer Maya wallet/card/crypto investment terms are not used to invent merchant eligibility or crypto checkout.

PHP/₱ means Philippine peso. MDR is the merchant processing percentage; a fixed per-transaction charge can stack on it. MID means merchant ID; KYC checks the merchant's identity/business; DTI/SEC are local registration bodies, BIR the tax authority, BSP the central bank, and ADB a bank account's average daily balance.

## Cost examples — our calculations

Inputs checked **2026-11-05**: current public **starting/rack** online prices for Checkout/Digital Invoice/Payment Links, PHP payment amount and merchant absorbs processing cost. The acquiring contract's section 36 says fees are **inclusive of applicable Philippine taxes**; the public pricing table does not instruct adding 12% VAT. These calculations therefore do **not** add an invented VAT surcharge. Account proposals can set the actual rates.

Formula: `processing charge = payment × MDR + published fixed per-transaction charge`.

The amount after processing below subtracts only the named processing charge. It excludes any quote-specific fixed fee, fraud add-on, security deposit, tax withholding, refund/dispute, conversion or bank-transfer cost. All displayed results are exact to two decimals for these inputs; there is no currency conversion or silent rounding. Rate sources: <https://www.maya.ph/business/pricing>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/checkout>. Contract tax/fee scope: <https://www.maya.ph/business/terms-conditions>.

**Security amount — our calculation:** if the merchant's projected monthly throughput is **PHP100,000** and Maya applies the agreement's **10%** baseline, required security is PHP100,000 × 10% = **PHP10,000**. It may be a deposit, bond or bank guarantee and can be increased on review. It is **not** a 10% fee on every sale. Cash deposits do not earn interest and the contract describes return **after 30 days from termination**, less obligations; this is not guaranteed immediate access to all held proceeds. <https://www.maya.ph/business/terms-conditions>

**Withholding — our calculation:** if the provider-defined qualified gross-remittance base is PHP1,000 and no validated exemption applies, its published 1% on one-half formula gives PHP1,000 × 0.5 × 1% = **PHP5** tax withholding. This is a tax deduction, not a second MDR. The provider defines the base and describes threshold/declaration conditions; it is not automatically applied to all examples above. <https://www.maya.ph/business/withholdingtaxupdates>

**Refund example — our calculation limit:** the PHP1,000 card example originally incurs PHP45 at the starting tariff, but the reviewed agreement merely says refunds are subject to Maya-stated fees and does not establish whether the original MDR/fixed fee is returned. We cannot calculate a definitive total cost for returning that PHP1,000 principal. The principal is the buyer's refund, not itself a provider fee. <https://www.maya.ph/business/pricing>; <https://www.maya.ph/business/terms-conditions>; <https://developers.maya.ph/reference/managing-voids-and-refunds-in-maya-checkout.md>
