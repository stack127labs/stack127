# Competitor pages: payment providers

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-09  
**Audience:** indie hackers, solo developers, and digital creators  
**Scope:** Seven public pages found through searches around payment-provider comparisons, Stripe alternatives, SaaS merchant-of-record providers, and payment fees. I reviewed public pages only; I did not sign up or submit forms. This is a comparison of page design and information, not a verification of each competitor's product claims.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. G2 — Payment Processing Software

- **URL:** [g2.com/categories/payment-processing](https://www.g2.com/categories/payment-processing)
- **Who runs it:** G2, a B2B software review marketplace. G2's [company page](https://company.g2.com/about) describes its review marketplace; its [terms](https://legal.g2.com/terms-of-use) name G2.com, Inc. as the operator.
- **Layout:** Large category directory with ranked product entries and a visual G2 Grid plotting satisfaction against market presence. Each product entry expands into a profile-like block.
- **Data shown per app:** Description and feature summary; average rating and review count; answers to review questions such as support, integrations, and self-service; company name, headquarters, founding year, company size and ownership ticker; user types, company sizes and industries; selected review excerpts.
- **Filters and sorting:** The page explains its score and Grid ranking. Filter and sort controls were not visible in the readable page extract, so I could not confirm which controls buyers can use.
- **Price:** No consistent transaction-fee column. Any pricing facts are embedded in descriptions or the linked product profile, so fee structures are hard to compare here.
- **Sources and dates:** Category statistics show a last-updated date (November 1, 2026 in this review). Reviews are attributed to users and dated. G2 explains its verified-review and score method in [About G2's Reviews, Data, and Rankings](https://www.g2.com/ai-instructions).
- **Works well:** Strong review depth, user and company context, and a quick visual way to compare perceived satisfaction and market presence.
- **Weak or missing:** It is built around software reviews, not payment economics. Prices, seller eligibility, payout terms, and the legal role of a provider are not presented as a consistent set of fields. The category also includes products with different payment roles.

### 2. Capterra — Best Payment Processing Software

- **URL:** [capterra.com/payment-processing-software](https://www.capterra.com/payment-processing-software/)
- **Who runs it:** Capterra is now operated within G2. G2's [terms](https://legal.g2.com/terms-of-use) include Capterra among G2.com, Inc.'s sites; G2's [acquisition announcement](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-acquires-capterra-software-advice-getapp) records the purchase from Gartner in 2026.
- **Layout:** Searchable directory of product cards plus a Capterra Shortlist table and individual review snippets. Cards include an “Add to compare” action.
- **Data shown per app:** Overall rating and review count; ease of use, customer service, features, and value scores; popularity and rating scores; category features; short description; free trial/free version; paid plan starting price where supplied; pros and cons from dated reviews.
- **Filters and sorting:** The Shortlist explains that ranking combines verified-user ratings and public search popularity. The page extract showed add-to-compare controls but did not expose the available filters or sort menu.
- **Price:** Starting software-plan price (sometimes “not provided by vendor”) and a general category pricing guide. It does not consistently show the separate percentage-plus-fixed payment-processing fee.
- **Sources and dates:** The page was indexed as updated November 8, 2026. Review excerpts carry dates. The Shortlist describes its scoring inputs, but individual fee facts are not consistently linked to a specific vendor pricing page.
- **Works well:** Familiar rating dimensions, review detail, comparison selection, and clear visibility when a vendor has not provided a starting price.
- **Weak or missing:** Subscription price can be confused with processing cost. Broad entries can include billing software, wallets, accounting suites, or specialist payment tools. There is no consistent seller-country, settlement, or MoR/gateway field.

### 3. TechnologyAdvice — Best Stripe Alternatives and Competitors in 2026

- **URL:** [technologyadvice.com/blog/sales/stripe-alternatives](https://technologyadvice.com/blog/sales/stripe-alternatives/)
- **Who runs it:** TechnologyAdvice, a B2B technology publisher and software-buying adviser. Its [about page](https://technologyadvice.com/about-us/) says it publishes advice and connects buyers with vendors.
- **Layout:** Long article with a quick comparison table, a “best for” and starting-price table, then detailed vendor sections and a methodology/FAQ.
- **Data shown per app:** Payment software score; online rate; in-person rate; merchant account type; standout limitation; best use; starting monthly price; and, in the profiles, features, integrations, hardware, pros and cons.
- **Filters and sorting:** Static editorial shortlist in a set order. No provider filters or sorting controls were shown.
- **Price:** Starting monthly price, plus separate online and in-person transaction rates. Quote-only and revenue-share cases are labeled. This makes basic comparisons quick, but prices are presented mainly as US examples.
- **Sources and dates:** Published June 11, 2026 and updated June 26, 2026. The article describes 27 evaluation data points and discloses that vendors may pay for traffic or sales opportunities. Vendor links appear, but not every table value has its own direct source link.
- **Works well:** Puts online and in-person rates next to each other, names a limitation for each option, and explicitly distinguishes PSP, merchant account, and MoR cases.
- **Weak or missing:** A short editorial list cannot cover the market. Country-specific pricing, seller eligibility, reserves, and detailed payout terms are thin. A featured partner block appears within the article, so the commercial context matters.

### 4. Zapier — The 8 Best Stripe Alternatives in 2026

- **URL:** [zapier.com/blog/stripe-alternatives](https://zapier.com/blog/stripe-alternatives/)
- **Who runs it:** Zapier, the workflow-automation company and publisher of the Zapier blog.
- **Layout:** Long editorial guide with a quick “best for / standout feature / pricing” table, followed by provider sections with pros, cons, pricing, and use-case recommendations.
- **Data shown per app:** Best use; standout feature; starting plan or “free plus processing fees”; main features and integrations; tradeoffs; recommendation. It calls out Paddle as an MoR and describes the tax-compliance difference from direct processing.
- **Filters and sorting:** Static list of eight editorial picks; no filters or sorting.
- **Price:** Headline plan price and a short transaction-fee description. It often uses “free plus processing fees,” which is concise but does not expose all fee layers.
- **Sources and dates:** Dated May 28, 2026. The page describes testing and discloses possible affiliate commissions. Vendor links are present, but the comparison table does not give a source URL and checked date for each fee.
- **Works well:** Clear use-case labels, plain-language tradeoffs, and useful integration context for small businesses and SaaS sellers.
- **Weak or missing:** The article is not a full directory and blends in-person, ecommerce, SaaS, and enterprise providers. It does not consistently compare seller-country availability, payment-method fees, payout timing, or hold terms.

### 5. Toolradar — Merchant of Record Fees Compared (2026)

- **URL:** [toolradar.com/guides/merchant-of-record-fees-compared](https://toolradar.com/guides/merchant-of-record-fees-compared)
- **Who runs it:** Toolradar is a software directory and editorial guide publisher from Dupple, as stated on its [about page](https://toolradar.com/about).
- **Layout:** Long specialist comparison with a short list of top picks, feature/fee explanations, a provider table, scenario totals at different revenue levels, and a checklist.
- **Data shown per app:** Best for; base published rate; monthly plan fee where relevant; surcharges; tax coverage; payout and dispute costs; and modeled cost at different sales levels. Its main table compares provider, published rate, extra fees in vendor documentation, and stated tax coverage.
- **Filters and sorting:** The guide has in-page links to sections, but it is an editorial comparison rather than a filterable product table. No sorting control was shown.
- **Price:** Transaction fees and extra percentage/fixed fees, with some monthly tiers and total-cost scenarios. The article states that scenario totals are its calculations and that the provider prices were checked on October 28, 2026.
- **Sources and dates:** Rates are linked to vendor pricing and documentation pages, with an explicit checked date. The [rating and revenue disclosure](https://toolradar.com/about) says its guides are editor-reviewed, some placements or affiliate links are paid, and vendor money does not change scores.
- **Works well:** Best example in this set of showing fee stacking, fixed monthly tiers, payout/dispute costs, tax coverage, and an apples-to-apples cost model. It tells readers to compare at their real transaction mix.
- **Weak or missing:** It focuses on MoR products, not the full payment-provider market. Its comparison still does not give a full seller-country eligibility matrix or region-specific contract terms. Scenario assumptions and headline claims need checking against current vendor terms.

### 6. HustleNumbers — Payment Processor Comparison

- **URL:** [hustlenumbers.com/tools/processor-comparison](https://www.hustlenumbers.com/tools/processor-comparison/)
- **Who runs it:** The site publishes the tool under the HustleNumbers name. Its [terms](https://www.hustlenumbers.com/terms.html) and [privacy policy](https://www.hustlenumbers.com/privacy.html) do not name a legal company or individual operator.
- **Layout:** Interactive fee calculator with a result table. The public page shows inputs for transaction amount and monthly volume, and an online/in-person channel toggle.
- **Data shown per app:** Stripe, PayPal, and Square; fee dollars; net amount kept; effective percentage; monthly and annual projected fee. Results are ranked from cheapest to most expensive for the selected scenario.
- **Filters and sorting:** Users set ticket size, optional monthly volume, and payment channel. The result rows sort by cheapest for that scenario. It does not filter by country, seller type, or payment method beyond online vs in-person.
- **Price:** Applies published percentage-plus-fixed rates to the entered transaction amount and calculates net proceeds and monthly/annual costs.
- **Sources and dates:** The tool says its rates come from a verified table sourced from official pricing pages and that a verification date appears in the results panel. The date/value was not present in the text extract, so it could not be checked here. It also clearly lists excluded costs.
- **Works well:** Turns a rate into dollars kept and demonstrates why a fixed fee matters more on low-priced transactions. It is quick, local to the browser, and transparent about major exclusions.
- **Weak or missing:** Only three US-facing providers and two channels. It excludes international cards, FX, chargebacks, negotiated rates, payout timing, and product fit. A cheapest badge can overstate certainty if a user's actual fee mix differs.

### 7. ChurnWard — Merchant of Record Providers for SaaS

- **URL:** [churnward.com/learn/merchant-of-record-providers](https://churnward.com/learn/merchant-of-record-providers/)
- **Who runs it:** ChurnWard is a SaaS dunning and revenue-recovery product. Its [imprint](https://churnward.com/imprint/) names Monty Digital Limited, registered in England and Wales, as the operator.

- **Data shown per app:** Base fee, stackable surcharges, dispute fee, payout cycle, payout fees, minimums, tax handling, and commentary on dunning and product fit. It includes Paddle, Dodo Payments, Polar, Stripe Managed Payments, FastSpring, Gumroad, and Creem.
- **Filters and sorting:** Static comparison; no filters or sorting.
- **Price:** Headline transaction fee plus add-on percentages/fixed fees, payout costs, and modeled cost for one subscription price.
- **Sources and dates:** The page text links some provider pages, but did not show a clear checked date or consistent row-level citations in the search extract. Direct fetch returned a 502 error during this review, so I assessed its indexed page text and the site's [operator imprint](https://churnward.com/imprint/); interactive or current page behavior was not verified.
- **Works well:** Most payment-specific schema in this sample: it makes fee stacking, disputes, payout timing, and payout cost visible together.

It does not provide a seller-country eligibility matrix, and its claims need direct checking. The failed page fetch and lack of a visible checked date reduce confidence in freshness.

## Patterns across the pages

### What almost every page shows

- Provider name and a short description or “best for” label.
- A headline price: a plan starting price, a transaction rate, or “free plus processing fees.”
- A short feature or product-fit summary; editorial pages usually add pros, cons, or a limitation.
- A ranking, recommendation, or way to compare products. Review directories emphasize ratings; editorial guides emphasize use cases; calculators compare a modeled fee.

The details vary, so a reader can easily mistake a monthly platform plan for the actual cost of accepting a payment. “Free” commonly means no platform subscription, not no processing charge.

### What these pages do not bring together that our research has

Among the pages reviewed, none presents a complete, consistently sourced provider record that combines all of the following:

- **Fees by seller country and region**, with the original currency, plan, payment method, and tax treatment kept distinct. A few pages show headline rates or fee stacking, but not a full regional matrix.
- **Seller eligibility**, including supported business countries and legal-entity limits. Buyer-market acceptance is not the same as the seller being allowed to open an account.
- **Payment role on every listing.** Some pages distinguish MoR, PSP, merchant account, and billing software, but none applies that distinction consistently across the whole comparison.
- **Payout terms alongside eligibility and price**, including timing, minimums, payout charges, reserves, and settlement options. Toolradar and ChurnWard show parts of this, but not a country-by-country, source-linked coverage set.
- **Dated lifecycle and trust events**, such as acquisitions, rebrands, paused signups, shutdowns, major product retirements, security incidents, or regulatory action.
- **A primary-source trail for each claim.** Toolradar dates and links vendor pricing sources; other pages may link vendors or describe their method, but the evidence is not attached to every fee and eligibility value.

## Top five ideas for the stack127 page

1. **Let people filter by what the provider actually is.** Separate merchant of record, direct processor/acquirer, gateway, billing platform, and crypto or bank-payment products. Also label whether another gateway or acquirer account is needed.
2. **Make price a comparable, sourced record.** Show seller region, plan, payment method, percentage and fixed fee, monthly/setup cost, surcharge, original currency, tax inclusion, source URL, and checked date. Let readers model a typical order size and monthly volume, and show the assumptions.
3. **Put seller eligibility near the top.** Allow filtering by the seller's country and business type; show supported seller countries separately from buyer/payment-method reach. State when approval, a local entity, or a bank account is required.
4. **Show cash-flow and loss terms beside the fee.** Include payout currency and method, cadence, first-payout delay, minimum balance, reserve/hold terms, FX and payout charges, refunds, and dispute/chargeback costs.
5. **Keep a dated trust and lifecycle panel.** Show current owner and rebrand history, signup status, supported markets, and major sales, closures, product sunsets, breaches, or regulator actions with dates and direct sources. This helps people avoid choosing a product that is changing or no longer available to them.
