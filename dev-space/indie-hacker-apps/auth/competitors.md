# Auth directory competitor pages

Checked 2026-11-09. I selected seven live pages that match the search intents in the brief: auth-provider rankings and pricing comparisons, Auth0 alternatives, and open-source authentication. They cover general software discovery as well as specialist advice. This is a content and layout review, not a claim that these are the exact top seven search results in every country or on every search engine.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. G2 — Customer Identity and Access Management (CIAM)

- **URL:** [G2 CIAM category](https://www.g2.com/categories/customer-identity-and-access-management-ciam)
- **Who runs it:** G2, a B2B software review marketplace; its company page identifies the operator as G2. ([About G2](https://company.g2.com/about))
- **Layout:** A long category page: a comparison-style “Top Tools at a Glance” table followed by product sections, a G2 Grid visual, category explanation, and review content.
- **Data per product:** The short table has product, best for, and a user review excerpt. Longer sections add average rating, review count, feature ratings against the category average, a product description, seller/company information, user/company profiles, review summaries, recent reviews, and discussion prompts.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filter or sort controls appeared in the fetched category-page text. The Grid plots satisfaction against market presence; related categories link to narrower topics. I did not treat that chart as a product filter.
- **Price display:** No normalized list price or cost by MAU. Pricing appears in review-derived comments and questions, so it cannot be used as a like-for-like quote.
- **Sources and dates:** G2 says the page was researched and written by a G2 researcher and updated April 10, 2026. Product evidence is mainly G2 reviews and seller-submitted/profile content; there are no official pricing citations alongside each product’s price because this page does not provide a price table.
- **What works / what is weak:** Strong for seeing user sentiment, category fit, and review volume at a glance. The category includes large enterprise and adjacent CIAM products, so a solo builder may have to sift through irrelevant options. Ratings and review summaries do not answer what a small app will pay or which features are gated.

### 2. AlternativeTo — Auth0 alternatives

- **URL:** [AlternativeTo Auth0 alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/auth0/)
- **Who runs it:** AlternativeTo is operated by 27 Kilobyte AB in Sweden. Its terms identify the company; its About page says recommendations are community-sourced. ([Terms](https://alternativeto.net/about/terms/), [About](https://alternativeto.net/about/))
- **Layout:** Paginated alternative list. The reviewed page says 12 of 92 alternatives are on the current page.
- **Data per app:** Position, likes, short description, cost/license labels, application type, supported platforms or deployment options, country tag, screenshots, and a “Good alternative?” prompt. Values are not present for every app.
- **Filters and sorting:** No sort/filter controls appeared in the fetched alternatives-page text. The list is numbered, paginated, and includes community likes; the page does not explain a ranked scoring formula.
- **Price display:** Broad labels such as Free, Freemium, and Open Source, plus license. No plan rates, MAU levels, or cost scenarios.
- **Sources and dates:** The page says it was last updated February 18, 2026. Listings are community-submitted; AlternativeTo’s terms say it does not test or certify listed software. App pages link to more information, but the alternatives list does not show a source and checked date for each fact.
- **What works / what is weak:** Good breadth and quick discovery across hosted, self-hosted, and open-source tools; platform and license tags help narrow the field. “Free” does not show hosting or operator costs, and community metadata can be incomplete or stale.

### 3. Devshot — Best authentication providers in 2026

- **URL:** [Devshot authentication providers comparison](https://devshot.co/reviews/best-authentication-providers)
- **Who runs it:** Devshot is published by Dupple, an independent media company. ([About Devshot](https://devshot.co/about), [How Devshot rates products](https://devshot.co/how-we-rate))
- **Layout:** Ranked editorial guide with top-pick cards, a 10-tool overview table, feature/price sections, detailed product cards with pros and cons, and a FAQ.
- **Data per product:** The overview table has tool, pricing, and best for. The guide also gives product type and features, plan/price unit, included allowances, SSO and other add-on charges, strengths and weaknesses, and selection advice. It compares hosted products with software a team operates itself.
- **Filters and sorting:** No interactive filters or sorting. The editorial ranking and top picks are the navigation.
- **Price display:** Explains different billing units (for example, retained users, MAU, SSO connections, and self-hosting). The guide includes plan prices and some fixed user-count examples, plus related fee schedules. It labels arithmetic totals and says when figures are vendor-published or estimates.
- **Sources and dates:** The guide says it was updated October 20, 2026; its methodology says prices and feature rows were checked against vendor pricing pages on October 17, 2026, and that totals multiplying rates by user counts are the publisher’s arithmetic. Vendor links are provided. Product links may be affiliate links, and the publisher’s rating policy discloses that paid placements can affect ranking.
- **What works / what is weak:** Strongest specialist example for distinguishing billing units and showing the costs that can change a B2B bill. It dates research and explains its method. It is a 10-product editorial shortlist rather than a full directory, and it warns that its wider identity directory also includes workforce suites and password managers.

### 4. Clerk — Clerk Pricing Explained

- **URL:** [Clerk pricing comparison article](https://clerk.com/articles/clerk-pricing-explained)
- **Who runs it:** Clerk, an authentication vendor. This is a vendor-published comparison article.
- **Layout:** Long pricing explainer with plan sections, tables, cost examples at four user counts, and a comparison with Auth0, WorkOS, and Supabase.
- **Data per product:** The cross-product comparison explains each billing unit, plan prices at 1,000, 10,000, and 50,000 users, and plan/add-on differences. Clerk’s own section adds feature bundles, organization allowances, SSO/B2B charges, export, and cost formulas.
- **Filters and sorting:** None; it is a static article.
- **Price display:** User-count scenarios for 1,000, 10,000, 50,000, and 100,000. It distinguishes Clerk’s monthly retained users (MRU) from competitors’ MAU, states assumptions, gives add-on charges, and shows the arithmetic for its 100,000-user example.
- **Sources and dates:** Published June 25, 2026 and last updated October 8, 2026. The article says its prices were verified in June 2026 and links inline to primary pricing pages and changelogs. That distinction matters: the page update date is later than its stated price-verification date.
- **What works / what is weak:** Excellent at explaining why MAU and MRU totals differ, what features a plan includes, and which data can be exported. It is unusually useful for SSO/organization costs and lock-in questions. It covers only four vendors and is written by one of them, so its framing and claims need to be read as vendor material.

### 5. MojoAuth — 13 Best Auth0 Alternatives for Passwordless Authentication

- **URL:** [MojoAuth Auth0 alternatives article](https://mojoauth.com/blog/12-best-auth0-alternatives-passwordless)
- **Who runs it:** MojoAuth; the page is published on the vendor’s blog and explicitly discloses that MojoAuth is one of the products listed. ([MojoAuth company page](https://mojoauth.com/company/))
- **Layout:** Long listicle with an “At a Glance” comparison table followed by ranked product sections, use-case guidance, pricing discussion, and FAQs.
- **Data per product:** The overview table has provider, best for, starting price, and free-tier MAU. Individual entries add differentiator, features, scenarios, limitations, and prose recommendations.
- **Filters and sorting:** No interactive filters or sorting; it is a numbered editorial list.
- **Price display:** Starting prices, free MAU caps, and some per-connection or usage pricing. It discusses add-on costs and gives a rough scenario for a 50,000-MAU app with enterprise connections.
- **Sources and dates:** The page shows an author byline, a July 2026 last-updated date, and says rates for several vendors were rechecked in October 2026. It links to vendors in places, but it does not provide a per-row source ledger or checked date for each price.
- **What works / what is weak:** Useful quick scan of passwordless-focused options and good prompts about SSO, MFA, and migration. The disclosure makes the commercial perspective visible. Its own sections conflict: the title and at-a-glance table say 13 providers, while a later heading says 12; MojoAuth’s overview price/free allowance also differs from the detailed MojoAuth entry. The July update date and October price-check note are not aligned. Verify every figure against the provider before using it.

### 6. OpenSourceSwap — Open-source and self-hosted Auth0 alternatives

- **URL:** [OpenSourceSwap Auth0 alternatives](https://opensourceswap.com/alternatives/auth0/)
- **Who runs it:** The page and methodology are published under the OpenSourceSwap name. The About/method page explains its process but does not identify a legal operator. ([How listings are chosen and refreshed](https://opensourceswap.com/about/))
- **Layout:** Compact comparison table followed by maintenance status, a self-hosting cost table, and a long-form explanation/FAQ.
- **Data per project:** The main table has project, summary, license, GitHub stars, latest release, deployment type, and whether an official hosted plan is confirmed. The cost table adds how it runs, software cost, and what the user must pay for.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting controls appeared on the alternatives page. It is a fixed four-project list, with links to broader category, maintenance, and price-tracking pages.
- **Price display:** Software license cost is separated from server and operator time. The page gives example server rates and states the date checked; it does not price by MAU.
- **Sources and dates:** GitHub data is marked as of November 2, 2026. The page links each project’s GitHub repository and official site. Its method page says repository metadata comes from GitHub, hosted plans are checked by hand, and activity is refreshed automatically.
- **What works / what is weak:** Clear separation between free software and the cost of running it, plus visible license and maintenance facts. It is limited to four open-source/self-hosted options and does not compare hosted auth services or explain app-specific feature/MAU pricing.

### 7. FindOpenSource.dev — Open-source Auth0 alternatives

- **URL:** [FindOpenSource.dev Auth0 alternatives](https://findopensource.dev/alternatives/auth0)
- **Who runs it:** FindOpenSource.dev describes itself as a curated open-source directory using public GitHub data. The pages reviewed do not identify a company or legal operator.
- **Layout:** Short article with a comparison table and compact project cards.
- **Data per project:** Project, GitHub stars, license, programming language, last commit, short description, tags, and forks.
- **Filters and sorting:** The Auth0 page is ranked by GitHub stars and has no per-page filters or sorting controls in the fetched text. The main directory has category navigation, but the comparison itself is a static seven-project list.
- **Price display:** Says the code is free to use under its license; no hosted plan, infrastructure, or MAU price.
- **Sources and dates:** The page is marked “Updated October 2026,” says GitHub data is updated daily, and includes last-commit dates and links to project records/repositories. It does not show a separate source URL or checked timestamp beside every table cell.
- **What works / what is weak:** Fast scan of license, implementation language, and recent activity. Star count is the ranking signal, not fit or reliability. It only covers open-source projects and omits hosted pricing, feature gates, operator costs, and migration details.

## Patterns across the pages

Nearly every page gives a product name, a short description, and some idea of intended fit. Comparison pages often add an at-a-glance table; review directories add popularity or sentiment; open-source lists add license and project activity. Pricing is uneven: some pages show only “free/freemium,” while the stronger specialist pages explain billing units and extras.

The clearest repeated lesson is to show the *meter* beside the price. “MAU,” “monthly retained user,” “SSO connection,” and “server you operate” are not comparable units. A single headline “starting at” price hides feature gates and add-ons.

## What this competitor set does not bring together

No reviewed page combines the full set of facts in one consistent, directory-wide record. There is partial overlap: Clerk’s article compares fixed user-count costs for four vendors and explains SSO, organizations, exports, and lock-in; Devshot explains several billing units and related fees; OpenSourceSwap separates software cost from server cost and shows license/activity.

Across the set, the missing combination is:

- The same sourced cost scenarios at fixed user counts across hosted providers, with each service’s billing unit and included features made explicit.
- A consistent hosted service / library / self-hosted classification, including a clear separation when one project offers more than one mode.
- SSO and organization allowances and add-on prices beside the base user price across the full directory.
- Framework and language support in a format that can be filtered.
- Data export, password-hash portability, migration friction, and other lock-in facts across hosted services and libraries.
- Dated official notices for acquisitions, product shutdowns, license changes, and breaches as part of the product record.

## Five ideas for the Stack127 page

1. **Let people choose the product type first.** Offer clear filters for hosted auth, libraries, and self-hosted software; show separate rows when a product has multiple deployment options.
2. **Make price comparable at a glance.** Show fixed user-count scenarios with the exact meter (MAU, MRU, connection, SMS, or infrastructure), billing interval, included allowance, and required plan. Put organization, SSO, MFA, and other add-ons next to the base price.
3. **Add filters that match builder decisions.** Include framework/language, B2C or B2B, self-hosting, license, passkeys/MFA, social login, organizations, SAML/OIDC, and SCIM.
4. **Attach evidence and freshness to each fact.** Link directly to official pricing/docs, show the checked date, state assumptions for calculations, and flag interactive prices or conflicting pages instead of making them look certain.
5. **Give each product an exit and lifecycle panel.** Summarize export formats, password-hash portability, migration notes, and self-host options; surface dated official acquisition, shutdown, license-change, and breach notices without mixing a product’s news with its parent company’s.
