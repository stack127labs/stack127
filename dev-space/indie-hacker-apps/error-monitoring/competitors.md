# Error and uptime monitoring directory: competitor pages

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-09  
**Scope:** Public pages found through searches for “best uptime monitoring tools,” “Sentry alternatives,” “free uptime monitor,” “status page software,” and “open source Sentry error tracking.” I chose seven pages that cover different visitor needs and page patterns. This is a relevance and page-design review, not a measured search-rank or traffic study. I read public pages only; I did not sign up or submit forms.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. [G2: Top 10 Sentry Alternatives & Competitors](https://www.g2.com/products/sentry/competitors/alternatives)

- **Who runs it:** G2 (G2.com, Inc.), a software review and comparison site. Its [review and ranking guide](https://www.g2.com/ai-instructions) says ratings and rankings use verified review and market data, and update daily.
- **Layout:** A short recommendation summary followed by ranked product cards. Each card gives the product name, vendor, star rating and review count, product description, reviewer-reported differences from Sentry, and overlapping categories.
- **Filters and sorting:** No visible filter or sort control on this Sentry alternatives page. G2’s broader monitoring category lets buyers browse subcategories and compare products; G2 says its comparison flow supports up to four products and company-size filters.
- **Price:** No prices or free-plan limits on this page. Visitors need to open product profiles or another pricing page.
- **Sources and dates:** Ratings and review counts are attributed to G2, but this page has no “checked on” date. G2 says live rankings change daily and recommends dating citations; this snapshot was checked on 2026-11-09.
- **What works:** Review scores, review volume, and user-reported tradeoffs give a buyer some social proof. The comparison connects Sentry to broader observability tools.
- **What is weak or missing:** It ranks by G2 review data rather than by a buyer’s concrete needs. It has no side-by-side free limits, monitoring intervals, self-hosting terms, or visible evidence for product-specific claims. Broad enterprise platforms can appear alongside focused error trackers without a clear explanation of the operational difference.

### 2. [AlternativeTo: Sentry alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/sentry/)

- **Who runs it:** 27 Kilobyte AB in Sweden, according to [AlternativeTo’s About page](https://alternativeto.net/about/). Alternative recommendations are crowdsourced.
- **Layout:** A ranked stream of app cards, not a comparison table. The page labels a sponsored result and shows the app name, likes, short description, cost/license tags, app type, supported platforms, country, screenshots, and sometimes user comments or “good alternative?” feedback.
- **Filters and sorting:** Cards expose clickable app-type, cost/license, and platform tags, but I found no sort control on this page. The alternatives are numbered and show likes; the page says it was last updated 2026-04-10.
- **Price:** Broad labels such as Free, Freemium, Paid, or Free Personal, plus Open Source or Proprietary. No plan prices, billing periods, or usage limits.
- **Sources and dates:** The page gives an overall last-updated date and crowdsourced recommendations, but no per-fact source links or per-app check dates.
- **What works:** The simple tags help visitors discover self-hosted, open-source, and cross-platform choices. Community votes and comments add context beyond vendor copy, and the list is broad.
- **What is weak or missing:** Likes are a rough signal, not a product-fit measure. It mixes direct error trackers with general observability products and only gives broad pricing labels. The sponsored card is marked, but appears at the top.

### 3. [Uptime Compare: 26 uptime monitoring tools](https://uptimecompare.com/)

- **Who runs it:** The Uptime Compare team. The site says it is not affiliated with a vendor and also sells advertising/featured placement.
- **Layout:** A live comparison page with a sortable table for hosted products, a separate free-plan table, and a separate self-hosted section.
- **Fields:** Hosted table: tool, entry price per month, included monitors, calculated price per monitor, fastest check, free-plan monitor count, and pricing model. The self-hosted table: tool, licence, minimum check interval, alert channels, and checked date.
- **Filters and sorting:** Search by tool name; filter by check interval, maximum monthly price, free plan, and status page. Table headers sort the rows.
- **Price:** Shows entry-plan price and billing notes, converts non-USD rates to USD using a stated European Central Bank reference date, and calculates entry price divided by included monitors. Per-seat or usage-priced tools do not get a misleading per-monitor figure. Self-hosting is kept separate because hosting cost depends on the reader’s setup.
- **Sources and dates:** Strongest source treatment in this set: it says figures come from each vendor’s pricing page, and a tool detail opens the quote and date checked. The page shows a last-verified date (2026-11-09) and explains its formula and missing-value marks.
- **What works:** It turns plan data into a useful cost comparison, lets visitors filter quickly, separates self-hosted from SaaS, and explains what unknown figures mean.
- **What is weak or missing:** Its comparison is focused on uptime and status-page tools. It does not compare error-event quotas, error tracking workflows, or release health. A single “fastest check” can also hide the plan and conditions that unlock that interval.

### 4. [UptimeRobot: 11 Best Uptime Monitoring Tools in 2026](https://uptimerobot.com/knowledge-hub/comparisons-and-alternatives/11-best-uptime-monitoring-tools-compared/)

- **Who runs it:** The article is in UptimeRobot’s vendor-owned Knowledge Hub, and UptimeRobot is one of the products in its own comparison. Its [terms](https://uptimerobot.com/terms/) say the product is owned and operated by UptimeRobot s. r. o. in Slovakia.
- **Layout:** A long article with a comparison table followed by individual product sections and recommendations.
- **Table fields:** Tool, best for, free option, uptime check interval, status pages, and uptime monitoring price.
- **Filters and sorting:** No visible filters or table sorting; readers scan the article and follow product links.
- **Price:** A “from” monthly price and free-plan summary. The table gives useful entry-level context but does not normalize the cost for a shared number of monitors or people.
- **Sources and dates:** The article says it was updated 2026-10-27. It links to product/vendor pages, but the comparison rows do not show a checked date or source citation beside each figure.
- **What works:** The table answers common first questions quickly: whether there is a free option, how often a check runs, and whether a status page is included. Longer sections give each pick a use case.
- **What is weak or missing:** It is vendor-authored, so the ranking and product framing need that context. The compact table omits plan-level monitor limits, billing units, and evidence dates; its “from” price is hard to compare across different pricing models.

### 5. [iTechGuides: Best Status Page Software in 2026](https://www.itechguides.com/best/status-page-software/)

- **Who runs it:** Yorker Media, a sole proprietorship in India, per its [terms](https://www.itechguides.com/terms-conditions/). The site’s About page says it researches and scores software. Its [disclosure](https://www.itechguides.com/about-us/) lists paid listings and sponsored alternatives; the page also says it may earn commissions.
- **Layout:** A ranked list of 31 tools, with cards, verdict links, and a comparison table.
- **Table fields:** Rank, tool, free plan, starting paid price, custom domain, private status page, incident updates, maintenance scheduling, subscriber channels, and score. Cards add a best-for line, summary, feature badges, trial details, and a “compare” action.
- **Filters and sorting:** Filters for pricing type (free, freemium, paid), free plan, custom domain, private page, incident updates, maintenance scheduling, free trial, self-hosting, and mobile app; a subscriber-channel selector; a sort selector; and per-card compare buttons.
- **Price:** Starting monthly price, free-plan presence, and sometimes trial duration. It does not estimate what a specific page/subscriber/team setup would cost.
- **Sources and dates:** The page says it was updated September 2026 and researched from official sources. Its method says editorial scores are not for sale, while the About page says paid vendors may receive fuller reviews that can change a score. The directory view does not attach a source URL or check date to each table cell. Paid placements and affiliate links are disclosed.
- **What works:** The filters match status-page buying decisions, and the feature columns make options easy to scan. A visible score and verdict give a quick path to more detail.
- **What is weak or missing:** A top-level score can hide different product types, and a starting price does not reveal plan caps. The vendor placement and affiliate disclosures mean the editorial method and the visible commercial placements should be read together.

### 6. [InventiveHQ: Best Error Tracking Tools and Sentry Alternatives](https://inventivehq.com/blog/best-error-tracking-tools-sentry-alternatives)

- **Who runs it:** InventiveHQ, which describes itself as a cybersecurity and IT services company on its [About page](https://inventivehq.com/about); the comparison credits the InventiveHQ Team.
- **Layout:** Long editorial guide with an “at a glance” comparison table, detailed tool sections, a cost-at-volume comparison, and FAQs.
- **Table fields:** Tool, open-source/self-host option, entry price, free tier, retention, session replay, and best for. Detailed sections cover price models, free-tier quotas, retention, and tradeoffs.
- **Filters and sorting:** None; it is a static article.
- **Price:** Monthly entry prices plus free-tier amounts and a cost illustration at 500,000 errors per month. The page says prices were rechecked against vendors’ own pricing pages on 2026-08-13 and distinguishes self-hosting from SaaS.
- **Sources and dates:** It gives an explicit price-check date and says it uses vendor pricing pages, but the at-a-glance table does not put a separate source link beside each price or limit. The page is credited to the publisher’s team.
- **What works:** It goes beyond “starts at” pricing by showing cost behavior at a stated event volume, retention, and session replay. It calls out self-hosting and licence distinctions.
- **What is weak or missing:** A static guide is harder to filter and goes stale as plans change. The table compresses substantial differences into a few columns, and it does not show per-app release activity or ownership/lifecycle changes.

### 7. [OSSAlt: Open-source alternatives to Sentry](https://ossalt.com/guides/open-source-alternatives-sentry-error-tracking-2026)

- **Who runs it:** The guide is published under OSSAlt Guides and credited to the OSSAlt Team; the guide does not clearly identify a legal operator.
- **Layout:** Long implementation guide with a four-way feature table, followed by separate product sections and setup instructions.
- **Table fields:** Licence, error tracking, session replay, performance/APM, log ingestion, Sentry SDK compatibility, RAM estimate, minimum hosting cost, and GitHub stars.
- **Filters and sorting:** None. It compares three approaches (GlitchTip, Highlight.io, and self-hosted Sentry) rather than offering a directory.
- **Price:** Estimated monthly VPS cost for self-hosted options and a hosted Sentry plan price. These are estimates and snapshots, not a common hosted-plan cost model.
- **Sources and dates:** The page is dated 2026-03-09. The table does not show a per-cell “checked on” date or evidence link, though the guide links out to project/setup resources.
- **What works:** It is unusually helpful for open-source buyers: licence, compatibility, operating footprint, and deployment steps sit together. It explains the cost and maintenance tradeoffs of running the software yourself.
- **What is weak or missing:** Only a few options are covered, and it does not support filtering or broad discovery. Hardware and hosting figures are estimates without a visible calculation or value-level source trail; the page also lacks maintenance and current lifecycle status for each project.

## Patterns across the pages

- Nearly every page gives a product name, a short fit statement, and some signal about price or whether a free option exists.
- Most pages organize products by rank or “best for.” Only the more structured directories expose filters and side-by-side fields.
- Free status is often a yes/no label. The pages that show a useful free limit or interval are more actionable than those that say only “free plan.”
- Editorial guides can explain tradeoffs well, while directories are quicker to scan. The strongest page models pair a scannable comparison with a deeper explanation.
- Evidence varies widely. Uptime Compare is the clearest about source quotes, dates, and formulas. Other pages often show a general update date or editorial method but not a source and checked date for each product fact.

## What these pages do not combine that our data can

There is no single page in this set that consistently puts all of these in one comparable directory: sourced free-tier amounts, check intervals, monitor/event limits, self-hosted options and licences, maintenance signals such as latest release, and dated news about acquisitions, rebrands, pivots, sign-up closures, or shutdowns. Some competitors cover parts of that list—Uptime Compare has dated pricing evidence and a small self-hosted section; iTechGuides filters for self-hosting; OSSAlt compares licences and self-hosting costs—but none makes ongoing maintenance and product lifecycle a routine field across the catalogue.

That lifecycle gap matters for solo builders: a low price or generous free tier is not useful if the project is dormant, the product has been folded into another service, or new signups have stopped.

## Five ideas for our page

1. **Lead with a useful comparison table.** Show product, job (error tracking, uptime, status page, cron, or broader observability), hosted/self-hosted model, free tier, key limit, and entry price. Keep status-page tools and error-event tools distinct where their units do not compare.
2. **Make price limits legible.** Show what the free plan actually includes, the unit each paid plan charges for, and the check interval or monitor/event ceiling. Let visitors filter and sort by free allowance, fastest interval, monitor cap, self-hosting, and licence.
3. **Separate hosted and self-hosted offers.** Label them clearly, show the self-hosted licence, and keep vendor hosting prices separate from any cost estimate for running it yourself.
4. **Add a dated maintenance and lifecycle signal.** Show latest release/commit where applicable, plus sourced acquisition, rebrand, pivot, signup-closure, sunset, or shutdown notes. Use a calm “last checked” date so readers can judge freshness.
5. **Put evidence beside the fact.** Give each price, limit, licence, and lifecycle claim its own official source link and checked date. Mark unknown information as unknown instead of implying that an unstated feature or limit is absent.
