Bugfender focuses on mobile and browser logs, crash diagnosis and user feedback. Its meter counts records, including one line for each crash, issue or feedback submission. The current Free card advertises 100K daily logs with 24-hour retention and 2000 devices. Team adds crash reporting with 7-day logs. Pro starts at EUR 127/USD 141 month-to-month, with 10 M shared monthly lines and 30-day logs; annual prices are lower but require a yearly purchase.

The headline EUR 39/USD 49 Team and EUR 89/USD 99 Pro prices are **monthly equivalents of annual totals**. The exact annual totals, month-to-month prices and plan-specific PAYG rates were recovered from the public data file used by Bugfender's pricing calculator. No exchange rate or rounded display was used to invent a price.

## Scope and evidence

Attempted **40 distinct fields**: C01–C27, E01–E04, L01–L03, A01–A03 and H01–H03. Error/log fields cover documented mobile/browser collection. Performance fields record opt-in Beta client HTTP capture and the limits of treating it as APM. Bugfender's remote-logging FAQ explicitly says backend logs are unsupported. No external uptime probes, synthetic browser flows, customer status-page product or missed-job monitor was established. The vendor's status site and Amazon SNS for two-factor authentication do not establish those product families or SMS outage paging.

Standard SaaS, vendor-managed Private Cloud, licensed On-Premises and client SDK/wrapper facts remain separate. The presence of Apache/BSD wrapper code does not make the backend or native iOS SDK open source. The complete On-Premises backend is a commercial license.

Every fact row has a field ID, value, unit, plan, edition, deployment, signal, exact official URL(s), UTC checked date, source update date(s) and a note. Dates follow the URL order. “not shown” means no explicit revision date was collected, rather than substituting a copyright/crawl date. Help article dates come from their displayed dateTime/dateModified values. The terms say 2024-07-02; privacy says 2026-03-13. The linked DPA body says 2020-02-14, while its subprocessor Exhibit C says 2026-02-05.

Only `confirmed`, `not_published`, `unclear`, `conflicting`, `not_applicable` and `check_failed` are used. A missing feature in prose is not treated as proof of absence. The explicit plan comparison, supported-platform restrictions and backend-log exclusion are identified where they establish limits. A narrower plan-specific/current source is preferred over dated generic copy, while material disagreements stay visible.

Sources were opened without signup, login, form submission or SDK installation. Public API documentation at /api/ was read as documentation only; no customer-data API route was called. The official linked DPA was read through the Drive connector. The two linked On-Premises manuals failed through both Drive and the web reader and remain a failed check. Ingestion, exports, deletion, alert delivery, support performance and production capacity were not tested.

## Cost examples: our calculation, estimated

All examples are **our calculations, estimated**, not vendor quotes. EUR and USD prices are separately published rates; no currency conversion is made. Amounts exclude tax, optional support upgrades, external storage/delivery services and negotiated terms. No annual monthly-equivalent is substituted for a month-to-month invoice.

The pricing calculator's actual public rate data is:
https://bugfender.com/wp-content/themes/bugfender2024/plans.json

The page loads it through:
https://bugfender.com/wp-content/themes/bugfender2024/js/pricing.min.js?ver=tnbc1m

The billing guide defines the shared meter, resets and rounding:
https://support.bugfender.com/en/articles/11621321-understanding-bugfender-s-pricing-model

All were checked 2026-11-04. The guide was updated 2026-01-22. Product codes identify the exact tier in that public data file.

For a chosen plan/currency:

`accepted-volume bill = base subscription + ceil(max(0, total counted lines − reserved lines) ÷ 1,000,000) × PAYG rate per million`

This formula applies only if the PAYG budget permits the excess. After reserved volume **and** allowed PAYG are spent, further capture is discarded, not billed. Annual subscriptions keep monthly quotas and monthly PAYG invoices. A count quota cannot guarantee that a noisy bug's entire context is retained.

### Error tracking: 5,000 / 50,000 / 500,000 reports per month

Held inputs: one builder, three apps, 30-day detailed-context target, no paid replay or AI extras. The current pricing advertises unlimited cloud seats/apps. Team's 7-day log history is insufficient for the 30-day context target, so use **Pro 10M** as the lowest standard comparable tier.

The billing guide counts each crash, issue or feedback submission as **one log line**. Ordinary context logs count separately. Under the explicit condition that the **entire team's total** remains at or below 10 M lines, these scenarios fit the same base:

| Error reports/month | Report-line formula | Reserved lines left for other records | Month-to-month base | Annual base and exact monthly equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|5,000|5,000 × 1 = 5,000|10,000,000 −5,000 = 9,995,000|EUR 127 or USD 141|EUR 1068/year ÷ 12 = EUR 89/month; USD 1188/year ÷ 12 = USD 99/month|
|50,000|50,000 × 1 = 50,000|10,000,000 −50,000 = 9,950,000|EUR 127 or USD 141|EUR 1068/year; USD 1188/year, with the same equivalents|
|500,000|500,000 × 1 = 500,000|10,000,000 −500,000 = 9,500,000|EUR 127 or USD 141|EUR 1068/year; USD 1188/year, with the same equivalents|

These are conditional base costs, **not guaranteed complete totals**. The requested report counts do not specify the number of associated log lines, issues, feedback or other apps' data. If that shared volume exceeds 10M, add the rounded excess and set a sufficient budget or reserve more.

For Pro 10M, the published excess rate is EUR 3.25 or USD 3.75 per million. For example, 12.5 M total accepted lines means `ceil((12.5 M −10M)/1M) = 3` charged batches:

- EUR 127 +3 × EUR 3.25 = **EUR 136.75/month**.
- USD 141 +3 × USD 3.75 = **USD 152.25/month**.

Those additional figures are **our calculations, estimated**, assuming the budget allows all 12.5M. They illustrate rounding rather than a fourth directory workload.

For an inspectable illustration, assume:

- A decimal GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes of raw message text.
- Every counted log record averages exactly **1000 bytes** of that text.
- All records are within the 32,000-byte ordinary text limit.
- The stated volume includes every counted record; no additional crashes/context/feedback are added.
- No compression ratio is assumed. Count billing does not charge separately for each byte of record metadata.
