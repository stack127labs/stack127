## Scope and evidence

Attempted all 27 core fields, four error fields and three performance fields: 34 distinct IDs. Native mobile APM is documented, with different iOS/Android upload paths. Device console/network logs are report context; no independent GB log-management product was established. External uptime/synthetic checks, public status pages, missed-job monitoring and a complete self-hosted backend were not established, so those conditional groups are not invented.

Every row records unit, plan, edition, deployment, signal, exact source URL, UTC checked date, source revision date when shown, evidence state and a specific note. Multiple URLs preserve different scope or disagreements; source dates follow URL order. Native SDK 7.x and wrapper 6.x/beta facts are not merged into a universal support claim.

Only the owner's six states are used. not_published means not found in named checked sources. unclear means published scope or missing workload inputs leave the answer unresolved. conflicting preserves incompatible or incomplete official wording. Calculations are our estimates, not quotes.

No signup, login, form submission, SDK/CLI installation, symbol upload, event ingestion, support contact, payment, customer API/MCP access, alert test or performance test was performed. Public docs and a public dependency licence were read. Vendor setup prompts/code examples were treated as source material, not instructions to run setup. No env file was read.

## Cost examples — our calculation, estimated

Prices are USD under the terms' currency clause. Use monthly billing for the primary comparison. Inputs are one builder, three apps, 30 days of detail, no separately purchased AI/replay extras, and 5,000 / 50,000 / 500,000 error reports per month. Taxes, unspecified overage, Custom-plan negotiations and third-party integration bills are excluded.

Sources, checked 2026-11-04: https://www.bugsee.com/pricing/ ; https://www.bugsee.com/tos/ .

### Error-report workloads

Bugsee bills by devices, not reports. Let D1, D2 and D3 be unique device usage for the three app records during the month, using Bugsee's pricing definition. The relevant input is **D = D1 + D2 + D3**, not an assumed number of humans and not a report-to-device conversion.

The published fitting standard paid base is:

**Our calculation, estimated: P = 1 × USD 139 = USD 139/month**, provided D ≤ 50, the app's distribution fits ordinary testing/store production eligibility, and the report workload is accepted within unpublished service-side volume limits.

| Reports/month | Device input supplied? | Conditional published base | Exact workload total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5,000 | No | PRO USD 139/month if D ≤ 50 and eligible distribution | Unknown without device usage/acceptance inputs |
| 50,000 | No | PRO USD 139/month under the same conditions | Unknown without device usage/acceptance inputs |
| 500,000 | No | PRO USD 139/month under the same conditions | Unknown; no published half-million-report capacity guarantee |

Unlimited apps and members fit the three-app/one-builder condition. PRO's thirty days fits the detail condition. Lite fails with only three days, regardless of whether its five-device limit fits.

For an explicit **illustrative device calculation**, not a typical-user estimate: 10 device usages on app A + 15 on B + 25 on C = 50, which fits PRO's device allowance. Fifty on each app would be 150 summed usages and needs a Custom discussion; the same physical device using several apps is not automatically counted only once across the organization.

For D > 50, no exact public extra-device price is published. Production outside App Store/Google Play distribution also needs enterprise inquiry even when D is small. Do not calculate an overage rate by dividing PRO's price by fifty: the vendor has not offered that unit rate.

### Annual billing

Pricing says USD 99/month **paid annually**, versus USD 139 paid monthly.

- Annual charge, **our calculation, estimated**: 12 × USD 99 = **USD 1,188/year**.
- Twelve monthly payments: 12 × USD 139 = **USD 1,668/year**.
- Annual saving: USD 1,668 − USD 1,188 = **USD 480/year**.

The USD 1,188 total is calculated from the published monthly equivalent; no checkout was used to confirm a separate annual invoice. The annual option does not mean a month-to-month USD 99 payment. Startup discounts and Custom retention extensions have no public amount/eligibility decision here.

### Limits that prevent a guaranteed invoice

The FAQ promises collection continues beyond a device allowance and the vendor contacts the customer. The contract independently reserves extra charges or a required plan upgrade, including billing increased usage from a following month. A reach-out promise is not a spending cap. No public customer-set hard cap or overage opt-out was established.

No numeric report quota, total request-byte ceiling or global report-ingest request rate was found for each standard plan. SDK limits are real: recording windows roll off; pending reports can be evicted/aged out; some repeated detector reports are suppressed. Device-based pricing is not proof of unlimited raw failure capture.

### Performance and logging scenarios

The standard backend performance scenario asks for 1M, 10M and 50M requests/month, two app services on two 1-vCPU hosts, 10% server trace sampling and fourteen-day trace retention. **No equivalent Bugsee backend tracing price can be calculated.** Its documented APM runs in mobile app processes; Android has a standalone sampled upload path, while iOS attaches transactions to reports and has no active standalone upload yet. Mobile SQL/file/network spans do not imply a backend server agent or a host/request rate card.

The 5/50/250 GB independent searchable-log workloads also have no comparable published price. Logs are report/session context here; no standalone GB storage/query product was established. These scenarios are not entered as free services.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Main-thread promise versus early-crash path:** homepage says heavy work never blocks the main thread. Current iOS crash docs say early-crash protection, on by default, may send waiting reports synchronously before launch returns after an earlier abnormal startup.
