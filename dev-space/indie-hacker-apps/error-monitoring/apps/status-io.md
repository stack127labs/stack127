## Cost examples

These are **our calculations** using the current published monthly prices and the advertised 5% annual discount. The page shows `$` without an ISO currency code. Terms say taxes are extra unless the invoice says otherwise. SMS and phone-number costs charged by Twilio/Vonage are excluded.

| Workload input | Plan and formula | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small: 1 public page, 100 subscribers | Basic: `1 × $79/month` | `$79/month`; annual: `(1 × $79 × 12) × 0.95 = $900.60/year` |
| Growing: 1 public page, 1,000 subscribers | Standard: `1 × $149/month` | `$149/month`; annual: `(1 × $149 × 12) × 0.95 = $1,698.60/year` |
| Busy: 3 pages, 10,000 total subscribers split below 5,000 per page | Plus: `3 × $349/month` | `$1,047/month`; annual: `(3 × $349 × 12) × 0.95 = $11,935.80/year` |

Each page needs its own subscription. The busy example assumes the stated subscriber distribution and no custom Enterprise requirements or High Capacity add-ons.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- The plan table’s feature checkmarks do not survive text extraction. The table provides usable plan prices and subscriber/team/metric quotas, but the exact tier gate for several features remains unclear. I used help pages for explicit gates: subscriber compliance is Plus, audit trail is Plus or Enterprise, and custom response headers are Plus.
- The Terms are dated 2016-03-10 and say online information may not be complete or current. The current pricing page is the source for present plan prices; I found no different official price in the pages checked.
- Status.io’s historical chart window (90 days), notification-log export window (30 days, longer for Enterprise), public display cap (1,000 incidents/maintenances), and account retention are different things. The vendor does not publish one general retention duration for all in-account data.
- Status.io does not publish what happens when subscriber caps are reached, a customer-selectable data region, a current public DPA, or a complete per-tier feature matrix in accessible text. The pages checked for these values are cited in their rows.
- I searched Status.io’s About, pricing, Terms, blog/release feed, support knowledge base and service-status page for ownership changes, acquisition, shutdown, sunset and plan retirement. No public notice was found. A separate newsroom page was not listed from About; the product blog is its public release feed.

## Field problems

- C06 and C16 do not fit a vendor-hosted status communication product in the same way they fit an error tracker. The Status.io API libraries manage its service; they are not application instrumentation agents. Those fields are marked not_applicable with that boundary stated.
- C11 cost examples use status-page counts and subscriber loads because Status.io sells per-page status communication. They are not comparable error-event or uptime-probe bills.
- C15’s per-tier component/container quantities and some plan feature gates are not recoverable from the published pricing table’s text extraction. The explicit unknowns are marked unclear; no checkmarks were guessed.
