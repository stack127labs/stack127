Nightwatch is Laravel's managed monitoring service. It captures exceptions, requests, database queries, logs, queue work and scheduler execution. Free includes 300000 monthly events with 14-day lookback. Pro starts at USD 20/month with 7.5 M events and 30-day lookback. Events are shared across signals and across the organization's apps, so one request or exception can consume several events.

Overages are an explicit opt-in with an additional-event cap. Without that authorization, ingestion pauses after the included allowance. Default exception sampling can preserve a full execution trace even when ordinary request sampling would omit it. Exact scenario prices therefore require the other retained events, not only an error-report/request count or a byte estimate.

## Cost examples: our calculation, estimated

These are separate scenarios, not one combined invoice. Keep **USD**, as established by the service terms. Taxes and customer agent/egress costs are excluded. No currency conversion or annual-billing discount is assumed. All calculations use one organization so included events are shared across its apps, not multiplied per app.

### Public rate formula

Let **E** be the total captured monthly events across all monitored signals and environments. For a plan with allowance **A**, monthly subscription **P** and additional-event price **r**, the known rule is:

**Estimated cost = P + r × billed 100000-event blocks above A.**

An additional-event limit must authorize enough excess volume. Otherwise data collection pauses and a low invoice cannot be treated as the price of retaining the full workload.

| Plan | P, USD/month | A, events/month | r, USD/100000 extra events | Lookback |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Free | 0 | 300000 | 0.50 | 14 days |
| Pro | 20 | 7500000 | 0.35 | 30 days |
| Team | 60 | 30000000 | 0.35 | 60 days |
| Business | 300 | 180000000 | 0.20 | 90 days |

For excess volumes that are exact multiples of 100000, block arithmetic is direct. The docs do not explicitly say how a final partial block is prorated or rounded, so no unlabelled ceiling function is used to manufacture a precise invoice. When E is known, compare all eligible tiers and their overage charges, not just the cheapest subscription fee. Enterprise is quoted, not zero.

Source rates: https://laravel.com/nightwatch/pricing. Meter/authorization/invoicing: https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/additional-events.md. Currency/taxes/agent cost conditions: https://laravel.com/legal/nightwatch/terms. Billing cycle: https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/subscriptions.md.

### Error tracking

Inputs: one builder, three applications, 30-day error detail, 5000 / 50000 / 500000 accepted exception reports/month, no paid replay or AI extras. Reports are occurrences, not unique grouped issues. Free's 14-day history fails the 30-day condition even if enough events are purchased.

Let **R** be accepted exception reports and **O** all other captured events, including their request/job/command context. Then **E = R + O**. O is not published as a fixed multiplier because it depends on application activity, sampling, filtering and repeated exceptions within the same execution.

| Workload | Known conditional arithmetic | Estimated recurring cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small: R = 5000 | Pro's remaining allowance for O = 7500000 − 5000 = 7495000 | USD 20/month if O ≤ 7495000; otherwise compare eligible tiers/extras. Exact total unknown. |
| Growing: R = 50000 | Remaining allowance = 7500000 − 50000 = 7450000 | USD 20/month if O ≤ 7450000; otherwise compare eligible tiers/extras. Exact total unknown. |
| Busy: R = 500000 | Remaining allowance = 7500000 − 500000 = 7000000 | USD 20/month if O ≤ 7000000; otherwise compare eligible tiers/extras. Exact total unknown. |

These are conditional base-plan results, not three quoted full-data invoices. Default exception sampling retains the full context for executions with exceptions even when ordinary requests are sampled out. Counting each supplied report as the whole bill would omit billed query/log/request events. The three apps and one seat fit all public plans; retention forces at least Pro.

### Logs

Inputs: 5 / 50 / 250 **decimal raw GB/month**, one builder, 30-day searchable history, no archive. One decimal GB is explicitly 1000000000 bytes. Query volume is unspecified. This byte convention is our calculation input, not Nightwatch's meter.

Let **B** be actual average raw bytes per retained log record, **N = raw bytes ÷ B** be the corresponding log count, and **O** be all other captured events. Then **E = N + O**. No compression ratio or “typical” line size is invented.

| Workload | Known formula | Estimated cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small: 5 GB | E = 5000000000 ÷ B + O | Unknown. Pro USD 20 fits if E ≤ 7500000; otherwise tier/overage formula applies. |
| Growing: 50 GB | E = 50000000000 ÷ B + O | Unknown. Same plan formula; byte volume alone cannot choose a tier. |
| Busy: 250 GB | E = 250000000000 ÷ B + O | Unknown. Larger event-count tiers or a custom quote may be needed, depending on B and O. |

Eligible retention plans are Pro, Team and Business, or a negotiated Enterprise plan. Free is excluded for 30-day history. Captured logs follow parent sampling; the above assumes the supplied workload is retained. If the GB figures describe produced logs before sampling, the retained byte count must also be measured or explicitly modeled. A cheap bill with discarded logs does not fulfill the full workload.

No separate public scan/index/export price was found, so the exact cost of any unlisted operation remains unknown rather than automatically zero. The subscription is an event meter; compressed GB or total scanned GB cannot be substituted silently.

Sources: https://laravel.com/nightwatch/pricing; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/logs.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/events.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/additional-events.md.

### Performance

Inputs: two Laravel app services on two 1-vCPU hosts, one builder, 1 M / 10 M / 50 M combined requests/month, 10% request sampling and at least 14-day detail. Free's 14 days meets this retention condition, subject to its event allowance or approved paid extras. Public plans allow two applications.

Let **q** be the average captured events per ordinarily sampled request, including the request itself. Let **U** be extra events from exceptions that override sampling, independently sampled jobs, commands/tasks and other activity not counted in those ordinary requests. The workload formula is:

**E = requests × 0.10 × q + U.**

The field list's ten-span/1 KB placeholder for byte-metered estimation is not used for this event-count service. There is no known fixed mapping from a request, a generic span or a 1-vCPU host to Nightwatch's captured-event total.

| Workload | Ordinary sampled requests | Full event formula | Exact recurring cost |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Small: 1 M requests | 100000 | E = 100000 × q + U | Unknown until q and U are supplied. |
| Growing: 10 M requests | 1000000 | E = 1000000 × q + U | Unknown until q and U are supplied. |
| Busy: 50 M requests | 5000000 | E = 5000000 × q + U | Unknown until q and U are supplied. |

For a **count-only lower-bound illustration**, set q = 1 and U = 0, retaining only request events. This is an explicit minimum-event illustration, not an assumption about a useful full diagnostic trace:

- Small: E = 100000 ≤ 300000, so Free = USD 0/month.
- Growing: E = 1000000; Free approved extras = (1000000 − 300000) ÷ 100000 × USD 0.50 = **USD 3.50/month**. This is lower than Pro's USD 20 and keeps only 14-day history.
- Busy: E = 5000000; Free = (5000000 − 300000) ÷ 100000 × USD 0.50 = USD 23.50; Pro includes 5 M within 7.5 M at **USD 20/month**. The cheaper public eligible choice at this controlled count is Pro.

All three illustration excess counts use whole blocks, avoiding the unresolved partial-block rule. Real query/cache/log/job events raise q/U and can change the cheapest plan. Exception overrides can make actual captured volume exceed the ordinary 10% calculation. Sampling also means observed counts fluctuate; these are expected-count examples.

The two monitored application hosts do not create a Nightwatch host-license line item in this rate card. Existing app hosting is outside the monitoring bill. A Vapor deployment adds a separate persistent agent host and associated network expense; a comparable all-in infrastructure total is unknown without its actual region/host-hours/transfer and a vendor rate source. No self-hosted-backend cost is estimated.

Sources: https://laravel.com/nightwatch/pricing; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/requests.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/exceptions.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/jobs.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/events.md; https://nightwatch.laravel.com/docs/guides/vapor.md.

### Scheduled jobs

The field list assumes 5 / 25 / 100 hourly jobs, each sending start and success signals. That is **jobs × 1440 pings/month** under a 30-day month: 7200 / 36000 / 144000 pings. Nightwatch does not document that two-ping interface in the checked guides.

What can be calculated is actual execution count: **jobs × 24 × 30 = 3600 / 18000 / 72000 task executions/month**. Each may generate task/command/query/log/job/exception events. Their total is not determined by job count, and independent missed-run/grace alerts were not established. ## Field problems

- C11 needs total retained events by signal. Error reports, requests and raw GB cannot be priced as though each were the whole event stream. Conditional base prices and controlled minimum-event illustrations must remain distinct from exact full-workload totals.
- C07 needs explicit partial-block rounding. A price per 100000 plus “blocks” does not establish how the final fraction is billed.
- C14 combines visible lookback, post-cancellation access, ordinary personal-data retention and physical backup erasure. The public plan duration only answers the first clearly.
- C06 must keep a repository's MIT file separate from restrictive service-contract wording. It must not label the complete backend open source.
- C15's performance-monitor count needs scope. Paid cards say per application; the Free scope is less explicit. Unlimited apps alone does not answer the threshold allowance.
- C17 must separate Nightwatch's alert channels from the Laravel Mail/Notifications event types it observes.
- C20/C21 need separate collection, MCP issue access and bulk export fields. A local agent ingest socket or an OAuth AI connection does not establish OTLP or general REST raw-data export.
- J01/J02 need a clear distinction between recording Laravel scheduler runs and checking for a run that never starts. The two-ping scenario cannot be applied until that detection path is established.
- C23/C27 need product-specific audit scope and body dates. Shared trust-center badges and an inconsistent legal-index label are insufficient substitutes.
