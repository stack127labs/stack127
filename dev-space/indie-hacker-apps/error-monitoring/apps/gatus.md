## Cost examples

All examples are **our calculation, estimated**, not quotes. Hosted amounts keep the original **$** symbol. The page's trial structured data labels currency USD, but paid pricing/terms do not separately identify the subscription currency code or tax treatment. No conversion or assumed tax percentage is added. The software's no-charge license is separate from compute, storage, domains, backups and alert-provider bills.

### Hosted uptime: one-minute checks, one page

Inputs: **5, 25 or 100 HTTP endpoints**, configured 60-second interval, one location, 30-day month, email alerts and **one own-domain status page showing all these endpoints**. One builder is the owner, so no extra team-seat capacity is required.

Formula:

`nominal scheduled runs per endpoint = 30 × 24 × 60 = 43,200`

`nominal scheduled runs = endpoint count × 43,200`

| Endpoints | Nominal runs/month | Lowest plan fitting one page and a one-minute interval | Estimated subscription subtotal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 5 × 43,200 = **216,000** | Premium, 2 pages / 50 endpoints per page | **$20/month** |
| 25 | 25 × 43,200 = **1,080,000** | Premium, 2 pages / 50 endpoints per page | **$20/month** |
| 100 | 100 × 43,200 = **4,320,000** | Business, 3 pages / 100 endpoints per page | **$50/month** |

Standard's **$10/month** has enough endpoint capacity for five or 25, but its **two-minute minimum** fails this frequency. Premium can hold 100 endpoints across **two separate 50-endpoint pages** for $20/month, but does not put all 100 on the single page held constant here. The table therefore uses Business for the busy workload.

These are subscription subtotals, **not exact full recurring totals**:

- Email alerting requires an SMTP host, sender/account credentials and recipient list. No included sending credits, chosen SMTP provider or delivery price is supplied, so email-provider costs are unknown rather than zero.
- One static monitoring egress address is documented, with no geographic selection or fixed-region guarantee. “One egress IP” is not an officially named probe city.
- Tax inclusion and a separately stated paid currency code are unresolved.
- Quota enforcement/automatic upgrades and a numeric management-API request cap are not published.

For a **three-location** workload, `endpoints × 43,200 × 3` gives **648,000**, **3,240,000** and **12,960,000** nominal location runs. These are arithmetic workload counts only. No official three-independent-region managed plan, selectable-origin setting or location multiplier was found. **Exact native three-location cost is unknown**; the bill is not multiplied by three without a supported configuration.

Consecutive failure thresholds are checks of health over time, not a published extra immediate retry tariff. No unlisted per-run charge is added. The configured interval also must not be mistaken for a measured wall-clock start guarantee: the standalone engine waits the interval after request completion and can be delayed by concurrency. Managed request-timeout/concurrency defaults are not published, so no physical network-request count or detection guarantee is invented.

The 30-day month is a calculation input. It is not a promise of 30 days of raw detail. Premium retains **100 results/50 events per endpoint**; Business **500/50**. Their audit-log durations refer to configuration/user actions, not these check results.

### Annual subscription amounts and the discount conflict

The annual totals below are values actually used by the public annual pricing view, not calculations from its “20% OFF” label.

| Plan | Monthly price | Published annual total | Our equivalent per month | Our saving against 12 monthly payments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | $10 | **$108/year** | 108 ÷ 12 = **$9** | 10 × 12 − 108 = **$12/year** |
| Premium | $20 | **$200/year** | **$200 ÷ 12** exactly; approximately **$16.67**, rounded to two decimals | 20 × 12 − 200 = **$40/year** |
| Business | $50 | **$500/year** | **$500 ÷ 12** exactly; approximately **$41.67**, rounded to two decimals | 50 × 12 − 500 = **$100/year** |
| Enterprise | $500 | **$5,000/year** | **$5,000 ÷ 12** exactly; approximately **$416.67**, rounded to two decimals | 500 × 12 − 5,000 = **$1,000/year** |

Our discount calculation is `(12 × monthly − annual) ÷ (12 × monthly)`. Standard is **10%**; Premium, Business and Enterprise are exactly **1/6**, or **16 2/3%**. These disagree with **20% OFF**. No annual amount is reconstructed by multiplying a rounded equivalent, and no missing tax/currency treatment is invented.

For the uptime scenarios, the known annual subscription component is **$200/year** for five or 25 endpoints on Premium and **$500/year** for 100 on one Business page. SMTP and region-comparability limits remain. Monthly and annual plans are separate payment choices, not an automatic extra discount layered together.

### Self-hosted uptime and running costs

The same nominal one-minute workloads give **216,000**, **1,080,000** and **4,320,000** scheduled runs for 5/25/100 endpoints. The license-fee component is **0** under Apache-2.0. This is not a zero infrastructure bill.

`estimated operating total = compute + persistent storage + backup + network/domain + alert-provider charges + 0 software-license fee`

No exact total is published here. Official docs do not size CPU/RAM/disk for these workloads; no host was selected, benchmarked or silently assumed to be free. Helm's **resources: {}** and commented **16Mi request/32Mi limit** examples are not proven minima. Its default persistence is disabled; the configurable **200Mi** size is not a capacity guarantee for 100 endpoints.

A one-minute interval is a wait between evaluations, excluding request time. Three evaluations run concurrently by default. Slow/time-out checks can delay other checks. Actual counts and necessary host size therefore depend on target behavior and concurrency, not endpoint count alone. Increasing concurrency can affect response-time accuracy.

Three independent monitoring locations require operator-provided deployment/network/storage in those locations. Experimental remote-instance aggregation can display combined statuses, but it has known limitations. Software license charges remain zero; three-region infrastructure cost and availability are unknown.

The default in-memory store loses history on restart. A persistent SQLite volume or Postgres instance, configuration, backups and alert credentials must be handled by the operator. Exact backup storage/restore cost and labor are not priced; labor is excluded from the formula.
