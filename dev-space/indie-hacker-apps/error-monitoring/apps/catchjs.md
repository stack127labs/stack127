## Scope and evidence

Attempted all 27 core fields, four error fields, three generic-log fields and three performance fields: 37 distinct field IDs. The log fields apply because catchjs.log stores independent inspectable records with its own quota. Performance fields cover browser real-user monitoring (RUM). Complete self-hosting, external uptime/synthetic checks, customer status pages and missed-job monitoring were not established as offered product families; their conditional field groups are not added.

Every fact row records unit, plan, edition, deployment, signal, evidence state, exact source URL, UTC checked date, source revision date when shown and a specific note. SaaS means the vendor-operated hosted service, with browser-side client code. Multiple URLs support different parts of a statement or preserve a conflict. Source dates are not inferred from search crawl dates or copyright footers.

Only the owner's six states are used. not_published means no value found in the named checked sources, not proof that a feature never exists. unclear means published wording or missing workload inputs leave applicability unresolved. not_applicable is scoped to the documented edition or comparison family. Calculations are our estimates, not vendor quotes.

No signup, login, form submission, installation, ingestion, support contact, alert-delivery test or performance test was performed. Public registration pages were read without entering data. The CDN client was fetched as text and was never executed. No customer-data endpoints were read.

The supplied scenario leaves domain layout unspecified. CatchJS bills account plans with associated-domain limits rather than a published app/project count. Two explicit layouts therefore produce different answers.

For **three apps on subdomains of one associated parent domain**, Production fits 5,000 and 50,000 reports: 1 × USD 89 = **USD 89/month** for each case. At 500,000 reports, Enterprise is required: 1 × USD 499 = **USD 499/month**. Development's 1,000-error allowance fails all three volumes.

### Logs: 5, 50 and 250 GB/month

Inputs are one builder, 30-day searchable retention and no archive. Exact comparable totals remain **unknown** for all three volumes. CatchJS meters generic log calls, does not publish a GB allowance, adaptively truncates large objects and does not establish the required searchable-log capability.

For illustration of what is missing, define B as the workload's mean serialized application bytes per log call before vendor truncation. Assuming decimal GB solely for this formula, required calls would be:

- 5 GB: N = ceiling(5,000,000,000 / B).
- 50 GB: N = ceiling(50,000,000,000 / B).
- 250 GB: N = ceiling(250,000,000,000 / B).

B is not supplied, and neither serialization expansion nor retained content after truncation is known. No average entry size is invented. These equations are **our calculation**, not a supported GB-to-plan conversion.

Known monthly call-count options for one associated domain are Development at USD 49 for N ≤ 1,000, Production at USD 89 for N ≤ 100,000, and Enterprise at USD 499 for N ≤ 1,000,000. With multiple quotas satisfied, select the lowest fitting option; domain count can force Enterprise. Beyond a quota the additional records are ignored rather than purchased as overage. These conditional prices do not establish that any of the three GB workloads can be stored intact or searched as required.
