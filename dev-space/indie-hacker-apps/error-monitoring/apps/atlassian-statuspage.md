## Status, ownership, and plan changes

Statuspage is an active Atlassian-hosted status communication product. Atlassian announced its acquisition of Statuspage on July 14, 2016. Current Statuspage terms list Dogwood Labs, Inc., doing business as StatusPage.io, as the contracting entity for this product. The current pricing page still lists separate public, private, and audience-specific offers. I found no Statuspage plan-retirement, sunset, or shutdown notice in the current pricing and billing pages, Statuspage article board, Statuspage blog archive, Atlassian company-news archive, or product terms checked. That is limited to these pages; it does not prove no notice exists elsewhere.

There is a past API-key migration notice, not a plan retirement: a current support article says old API keys and query-string `api_key` use end June 30, 2026; an April 2026 Atlassian announcement says query-string keys end May 15, 2026. Both dates have passed as of this check, and neither page gives a later correction. This conflict is recorded below.

The Atlassian product update guide says Statuspage release notes are not included in the general Atlassian app-update feed. I did not find a separate public Statuspage changelog. The Statuspage blog archive and Statuspage community articles board were checked for current changes and retirement notices. Atlassian's Jira Service Management product added its own status-page capability in 2026; this is a separate product feature and is not a Statuspage shutdown notice.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- API migration dates conflict: the current help page says June 30, 2026; the April announcement says May 15, 2026 for query-string removal. Both dates are past, and the support page has no visible update date.
- The current pricing table lists Slack and Microsoft Teams on Free, while the Free-plan help article lists email only. The current pricing table is the more complete current offer, but the doc discrepancy remains visible.
- Pricing uses `$` but does not identify the ISO currency or clarify tax inclusion.
- The audience-specific pricing page gives later tier price increments but not the exact capacity allocated to each tier. Costs above its 500-user base cannot be computed from the posted data.
- The checked docs do not publish behavior when active subscriber limits are exceeded, public paid support response targets, a Statuspage storage-region choice, or maximum incident-history retention. Notes name the pages checked for these unknowns.

## Field problems

- Statuspage is a status communication product, not a direct error or uptime monitor. The uptime-check and error-capture field groups do not apply to the product itself; upstream monitoring providers supply the condition that may be communicated.
- “Subscribers,” private-page “employees,” audience-specific “users,” team “members,” and audience “groups” are different populations. Their plan counts are not interchangeable.
- Audience-specific tier pricing publishes increments without complete allocation counts, so a larger-user cost example is not calculable.
- The official pricing page displays a dollar sign but no ISO currency code. The record preserves the displayed unit.
