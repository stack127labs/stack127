### How model choices change usage

Scenario: Hobby, one calendar-month allowance of 700 message credits, no previous use or non-expiring credit balance; a fixed model throughout. Each counted unit is **one text-only AI model request**, with no Action calls, attachments, voice, drafts or test-credit spillover. The request count is not a count of customer conversations, successful outcomes or visible replies.

Formula: required credits = requests × model multiplier. Shortfall = max(0, required credits − 700). Enough additional 1,000-credit blocks = ceil(shortfall / 1,000). Provisioning cost = US$40 Hobby monthly base + blocks × US$40.

These are the cost of provisioning enough published credit blocks, **not an exact forecast of automatic charges**. Auto-recharge purchases when a configured threshold is crossed and may prebuy before exhaustion. Threshold, existing non-expiring credits and their spending order affect the actual invoice. Remaining balances in the table combine both credit types before monthly expiry; the exact spending order and therefore the type of remaining credits were not established. Unused subscription credits expire monthly; purchased recharge credits do not. Therefore these monthly examples must not be multiplied by 12 to claim an exact annual usage bill.

At the published recharge rate, our arithmetic is US$40 / 1,000 = US$0.04 per purchased credit. That is US$0.04 / US$0.08 / US$0.16 / US$0.20 of top-up-credit consumption for a single 1/2/4/5-credit request. It is neither a separately purchasable single-request price nor an all-in price per customer resolution.

Basic's 4,000 included credits cover all eight restricted 100/500-request cases above, including 500 × 5 = 2,500, so their Basic subscription subtotal stays US$150 monthly or US$1,440 yearly when there is no other usage. This does not make Basic universally cheaper for a solo AI-only task; Hobby plus top-ups can cost less at these volumes.

### Why visible replies can cost more

- Pricing FAQ: one user request that triggers a calendar Action can require two model requests. Our calculation at 2 credits/request: 100 such replies × 2 requests/reply × 2 credits/request = 400 credits; 500 = 2,000 credits. Actions are not guaranteed to use exactly two requests in all workflows. Source: https://www.chatbase.co/pricing .
- Attachment example at the documented 1-credit rate: text + one image = 1 + 1 = 2 credits. Processing that attachment for an Action and again for a final response = 2 + 2 = 4 credits. Rates depend on model and content. Source: https://www.chatbase.co/docs/user-guides/chatbot/channels.md .
- Voice example: 10 minutes × 6 credits/minute + 15 one-credit requests = 75 credits, before Twilio/SIP/phone-number fees. This is a credit calculation, not an all-in call price. Source: https://www.chatbase.co/docs/user-guides/chatbot/voice.md .
- AI drafts consume message credits each time generated. Tests use their separate allowance first and then consume production message credits. Sources: https://www.chatbase.co/docs/user-guides/chatbot/help-desk/ai-draft-replies.md ; https://www.chatbase.co/docs/user-guides/chatbot/tests.md .
