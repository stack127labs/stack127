# Analytics directory competitor pages

Reviewed 9 November 2026. I searched for privacy-friendly analytics, Google Analytics alternatives, product analytics comparisons, open-source web analytics, and Plausible/Fathom/Umami comparisons. Search results change by location and over time, so this is a relevance review, not a fixed ranking audit. I picked pages with useful comparison patterns, visible audience signals, or strong coverage of one part of the category.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. [AlternativeTo: Google Analytics alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/google-analytics/)

- **Who runs it:** 27 Kilobyte AB, a company in Stockholm, Sweden. AlternativeTo says its app listings and recommendations are community contributed. ([About](https://alternativeto.net/about/); [Terms](https://alternativeto.net/about/terms/))
- **Layout and app data:** A ranked, stacked list of alternatives. Each listing shows the app name, likes, description, cost/license labels, app type, platforms, screenshots, and community comments. Some pages also show a “good alternative?” comparison prompt.
- **Filters and sorting:** Filters include app type, cost/license (free, freemium, paid, open-source, proprietary), platform/deployment (including online, self-hosted, Docker), and region. The visible list is ranked by likes; I did not find a separate sort control in the page extract. ([Directory page](https://alternativeto.net/software/google-analytics/))
- **Price:** A broad label such as free, freemium, or paid; some license labels are shown too. It does not compare plan prices at a common traffic or event volume.
- **Sources and dates:** The page shows a last-updated date (25 August 2026 in the version reviewed). I did not see a source link or check date for each app fact. Descriptions and recommendations come from users and contributors, according to AlternativeTo.
- **What works:** A familiar “find a replacement for this app” starting point, broad filters, and user feedback. It covers web, mobile, and self-hosted software.
- **What is weak or missing:** The page has a large, mixed result set. Likes show popularity, not fit or evidence quality. A last-updated date does not tell readers whether price, privacy, or license facts were checked recently.

### 2. [G2: Product Analytics Software](https://www.g2.com/categories/product-analytics)

- **Who runs it:** G2.com, Inc., a software review marketplace. ([G2 Terms](https://legal.g2.com/terms-of-use))
- **Layout and app data:** A long category page with a G2 Grid, ranked product sections, and review content. Product entries show rating and review count, category sub-scores (such as integrations and multi-product analytics), company/seller details, typical users, industries, company size, review summaries, and recent review titles. ([Category page](https://www.g2.com/categories/product-analytics))
- **Filters and sorting:** The page explains G2 Score and the Grid, which plots satisfaction against market presence. The text view did not expose a specific filter or sort panel. Products are presented in G2’s category ranking; I would not assume the ranking is a user-controlled sort.
- **Price:** This category list does not give comparable plan prices or a common event-volume cost. Price questions appear in reviews and product discussions, with details available on individual vendor profiles.
- **Sources and dates:** The page says it uses moderated reviews and an analyst methodology. It is dated “Last updated: November 1, 2026” and gives November category stats; individual reviews also show their dates. It does not attach a vendor source and check date to each price or feature fact.
- **What works:** Strong for real user feedback, product category context, and how different kinds of buyers rate products. This page covers product analytics directly, including tools that also offer replay or experimentation.
- **What is weak or missing:** Reviews and scores do not settle privacy, data residency, license, or total cost. The category can mix products with different jobs, and a high score is not a substitute for matching a solo creator’s needs.

### 3. [Elsop Insights: privacy-friendly web analytics](https://www.elsop.com/analytics-tools/privacy-friendly/)

- **Who runs it:** Published under the Elsop Insights brand. The current legal operator was not clear from the directory and methodology pages I checked.
- **Layout and app data:** Cards and a detailed table. The table covers tool, product status and check date, cookieless behavior, open-source status, EU data, free hosted plan, entry price, price at 100,000 monthly units, and tracking-script gzip size with its measurement date. ([Directory](https://www.elsop.com/analytics-tools/privacy-friendly/))
- **Filters and sorting:** Filter tags include cookieless, open source, self-hosted, EU data, and free plan. Readers can switch between cards and table and sort by script size, price, or check date.
- **Price:** Shows entry price and a monthly price for 100,000 units, with the vendor’s unit named (pageviews or events). This is the closest full-catalog match to a standard traffic benchmark, although one benchmark does not show a vendor’s full price curve.
- **Sources and dates:** Every value is said to come from vendor pages and to carry a check date; the page reviewed showed checks on 17 September 2026. Its methodology separates its own script test from vendor statements about personal data and consent. ([Methodology](https://www.elsop.com/analytics-tools/methodology/))
- **What works:** Excellent comparison density, clear units, per-value dates, a useful table, and an honest distinction between a tested script behavior and vendor claims.
- **What is weak or missing:** It is aimed at privacy-friendly web analytics, with only a small app-analytics subset. One 100,000-unit point cannot answer what happens at higher traffic or with different site counts, and privacy badges can still hide plan or setup limits.

### 4. [Opensaurus: open-source web analytics](https://opensaurus.org/category/web-analytics/)

- **Who runs it:** Opensaurus, Poland, according to its About page. ([About and funding](https://opensaurus.org/about/))
- **Layout and app data:** A project list with cards showing project name and description, repository health score/status, license and warnings, setup options/effort, GitHub stars, and latest release date. The page also summarizes license and maintenance counts.
- **Filters and sorting:** Filters include Docker, ready-made builds, Kubernetes, fully open-source only, quick setup, and interface language. Sort options are health, stars, and latest release. ([Category page](https://opensaurus.org/category/web-analytics/))
- **Price:** No hosted plan prices. It is an open-source project directory and emphasizes license and self-hosting work instead.
- **Sources and dates:** Project data is refreshed daily from public GitHub repositories; the category page says it was checked against GitHub on 6 November 2026. Its method explains how it reads licenses, tracks changes, and calculates repository health. ([How it checks projects](https://opensaurus.org/how-we-check/))
- **What works:** Strong license and maintenance detail, including paid code in repositories and historical license changes. Setup effort helps solo builders estimate the work of self-hosting.
- **What is weak or missing:** It excludes closed hosted analytics and does not compare cloud pricing, consent behavior, or data regions. Health is a code-activity signal, not a product-quality score. The site also discloses sponsored placements and partner links, while saying these do not change facts or order.

### 5. [analytics·alternatives: web analytics tools](https://analytics-alternatives.com/tools/)

- **Who runs it:** A single practitioner/editor runs the publication; its About page says there are no affiliate links or sponsored placements. The page does not name a separate operating company. ([About](https://analytics-alternatives.com/about/))
- **Layout and app data:** Cards/table toggle plus a comparison matrix. The matrix shows tool, hosting model, entry price, license, cookieless status, EU-only hosting, and open-source status. Cards add country, short fit description, feature badges, starting price, and a link to a longer review. There is also a two-tool side-by-side comparison. ([Tools page](https://analytics-alternatives.com/tools/))
- **Filters and sorting:** The page advertises filters for hosting, jurisdiction, license, and price. Visible quick filters include cookieless, self-hosting, free tier, GA4 import, EU hosting, open source, funnels, AI/MCP, revenue, A/B testing, and session replay. It offers cards/table views and a compare-any-two feature. The matrix invites readers to sort by what matters, though the text view does not expose a clear sort control.
- **Price:** The list shows a starting monthly price or “Free”; it does not normalize prices by traffic. Linked reviews go deeper: the [Plausible review](https://analytics-alternatives.com/tools/plausible/) has a feature matrix and plan tiers, including a 100,000-pageview price.
- **Sources and dates:** The list says it was updated in November 2026 and that each tool was tested on a real site for at least a week. It links to a testing-method page, but the comparison rows do not visibly link each fact to its vendor source. The Plausible review says its price tiers were verified in May 2026, older than the list’s November update. There is also a visible mismatch: the list says 22 tools tested but shows zero verified tools/features/editor checks, while the About page says 21 tools reviewed. ([Tools page](https://analytics-alternatives.com/tools/); [Plausible review](https://analytics-alternatives.com/tools/plausible/); [About](https://analytics-alternatives.com/about/))
- **What works:** Strong filter ideas, a direct compare flow, clear hosting/license labels, and editorial descriptions that explain fit.
- **What is weak or missing:** The top-line evidence counters and tool counts conflict, which makes the testing claims harder to judge. Prices are starting prices only, and a reader cannot easily trace a badge to the exact official page behind it.

### 6. [Analytics Alternative: Google Analytics alternatives](https://analytics-alternative.com/)

- **Who runs it:** SwissOps Sàrl, Renens, Switzerland. It also makes WireBoard, which appears in the comparison. ([Directory and disclosure](https://analytics-alternative.com/))
- **Layout and app data:** Ranked cards with a seven-part score ring and a short “strongest/weakest” summary. Cards show description, country, starting price, and badges such as free plan, no cookies, EU hosting, and real-time. The page also links to focused shortlists, including product analytics. ([Directory](https://analytics-alternative.com/))
- **Filters and sorting:** Quick filters cover free plan, no cookies, open source, self-hosting, EU hosting, and real-time; type filters include heatmaps/UX, product analytics, and enterprise. Results are ranked by the site’s average of seven scores; no separate sorting control was visible.
- **Price:** Shows a starting price, including “Free” or “On request.” It links to a separate price-comparison page, but the main list does not show fixed-volume costs.
- **Sources and dates:** It says prices and privacy facts were checked on vendors’ own sites on 20 October 2026. A review-method link is present, but the main cards do not show the exact vendor source beside each fact. ([Directory](https://analytics-alternative.com/); [Review method](https://analytics-alternative.com/how-we-review))
- **What works:** The filters make distinct jobs easier to find, and the ring gives readers a quick view of trade-offs. The product analytics shortcut addresses a different need from a simple website counter.
- **What is weak or missing:** The site says it makes WireBoard, then ranks WireBoard first (tied) and recommends it in its own answers. That disclosure is useful, but the conflict means its score should not be treated as independent. A single average score also hides how much each reader may value price, privacy, or features.

### 7. [Analytics Alternative: compare web analytics pricing](https://analytics-alternative.com/compare-web-analytics-pricing)

- **Who runs it:** SwissOps Sàrl, Renens, Switzerland. It also makes WireBoard, which appears in the comparison. ([Pricing page and disclosure](https://analytics-alternative.com/compare-web-analytics-pricing))
- **Layout and app data:** A long comparison page with an “at a glance” section, app cards, two static tables, and a pageview selector. The starting-price table shows product, starting cost, free-plan limit, billing unit, and a link to plan details or a calculator. A later table shows price at 10,000, 100,000, and 1 million pageviews plus included sites. Other sections compare free tiers, overages, annual billing, data retention, seats, and add-ons. ([Pricing comparison](https://analytics-alternative.com/compare-web-analytics-pricing))
- **Filters and sorting:** No product filters or sort controls. Readers can choose 10k, 50k, 100k, 500k, 1M, 5M, or 10M pageviews in a price selector. The selector prices only tools billed by pageviews; the page separately lists event, session, hit, per-plan, and quote-based models.
- **Price:** Monthly in each vendor’s own currency, without currency conversion. It distinguishes free hosted plans from self-hosted editions, explains billing units, and states where traffic-only examples leave out events or other costs.
- **Sources and dates:** The page says the prices were checked on vendor pricing pages by 20 October 2026, and points to per-tool reviews and pricing calculators. It says it uses the cheapest plan covering the selected volume. Source links are not displayed beside every number in the comparison table.
- **What works:** This is the strongest pricing example in the set. It shows a useful traffic curve, free-plan caps, site counts, overage rules, and why pageviews alone can understate a bill. It gives readers a volume picker without converting currencies or pretending different billing units are the same.
- **What is weak or missing:** It compares pageview prices at several levels, but cannot produce equivalent costs for tools charged by events, sessions, hits, or a quote. The page discloses that WireBoard is made by its owner, but puts it first in the table; this is a conflict to keep in view. ([Disclosure](https://analytics-alternative.com/compare-web-analytics-pricing))

## What nearly every page shows

Most pages show an app name, a short description, and some kind of price cue. They use ratings, score/rank, tags, or a “best for” label to help readers scan. The privacy-focused pages add badges for cookies, hosting, or open source; review directories add ratings and user comments. Search and quick filters are common, but only a few pages let readers build a direct side-by-side comparison.

Price is usually an entry price or a broad label such as free, freemium, or paid. Elsop shows one 100,000-unit point for every listed app. Analytics Alternative goes further for pageview-priced tools, with several fixed-volume points, but places other billing models in separate notes rather than giving every app an equivalent price at each volume.

## What no page combines across its full list

Other pages already cover parts of our data. Elsop has a dated benchmark price and explains its cookie checks. Opensaurus checks licenses and project activity. Analytics Alternative shows fixed pageview costs, free limits, data retention, plan caps, and some lifecycle changes. Privacy lists use cookie, self-hosting, and EU-hosting badges. The gap is the complete, consistently sourced record across web, product, mobile, and other analytics apps. None of these seven combines all of the following for every app:

- Comparable prices at several fixed levels across different billing units, with the edition, included sites, overage rules, and calculation assumptions kept together.
- Cookie and consent details that consistently separate tested behavior from vendor or legal claims, and show plan or setup limits.
- Data location at the region and processor level, rather than only a country or EU badge.
- Hosted and self-hosted editions side by side, with the license and the features each edition includes.
- A dated history of sales, shutdowns, rebrands, closed signups, major plan changes, breaches, regulatory action, and license changes across the full list. Some competitors mention a few events, such as Hotjar becoming part of Contentsquare or Smartlook’s retirement, but do not keep a broad event record for every product.
- Direct official-source links and a check date for every fact, plus explicit “not published” or “unclear” states where evidence is missing.

## Five ideas for our page

1. **Start with the job and setup.** Let people filter web, product, mobile, ecommerce, and session-replay analytics, then narrow by hosted/self-hosted, free plan, and setup effort.
2. **Make price comparable.** Show several standard traffic or event volumes, the vendor’s billing unit, plan name, site count, and overage rule. Offer a pageview selector only where a pageview is the billing unit; show event or session limits separately. Keep self-hosting server and upkeep estimates clearly marked as our calculations.
3. **Put evidence beside each fact.** Link the official pricing, docs, privacy, terms, or status page at the point of use. Show the check date and whether the value is confirmed, not published, or unclear.
4. **Explain privacy and data control in plain terms.** Separate default cookie behavior from consent claims, identify the plan/edition, show the actual data region, and label the license and self-host requirements.
5. **Keep lifecycle and trust visible.** Add dated owner, acquisition, shutdown, signup, major-plan, incident, and license-change notes. Let readers compare tools side by side without collapsing these differences into one winner score.
