## Cost examples: our calculation

Sources checked 2026-11-05: https://pagelens.io/#pricing; https://pagelens.io/terms. All figures are USD before any applicable tax. One site and one user. Website scenarios contain only pageviews; product scenarios specify the total event count directly. These are separate workloads, not a conversion between users, pageviews and events.

The pricing page's inline code publishes Pro monthly prices of $12/$24/$39/$59/$99 and Business $29/$49/$69/$99/$149 at 150k/500k/1M/2M/5M events. Its annual-charge formula is monthly price × 10. The code was read, not executed. Starter's $5/month and $50/year are also written in the FAQ. Rounded monthly-equivalent annual displays are not used as exact annual totals.

| Workload per month | Inputs and tier | Our monthly calculation | Our annual calculation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 pageviews, no other events | Starter: 20,000 events; three sites | 1 subscription × $5 = $5/month; 12 × $5 = $60 for twelve monthly payments | $5 × 10 = $50/year, paid upfront |
| 100,000 pageviews, no other events | Pro 150k: 150,000 events; ten sites | 1 × $12 = $12/month; 12 × $12 = $144 for twelve monthly payments | $12 × 10 = $120/year, paid upfront |
| 1,000,000 pageviews, no other events | Pro 1M: 1,000,000 events; ten sites | 1 × $39 = $39/month; 12 × $39 = $468 for twelve monthly payments | $39 × 10 = $390/year, paid upfront |
| 100,000 total events | Pro 150k: 150,000 events; ten sites | 1 × $12 = $12/month | $12 × 10 = $120/year, paid upfront |
| 1,000,000 total events | Pro 1M: 1,000,000 events; ten sites | 1 × $39 = $39/month | $39 × 10 = $390/year, paid upfront |
| 10,000,000 total events | Above the published 5M ladder | quote_required; no exact formula/rate published | quote_required; no annual amount can be derived |

These are our calculations, not checkout quotes. Existing plan features/gates still apply. Pro does not include Business replay. Optional outbound/custom/autocaptured interactions can increase event usage; the docs do not resolve the accounting for every additional data type. No tax-inclusive or converted-currency total can be exact without checkout information.

Other supported jobs cannot be priced from their headline workload alone:

- **1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 monthly recorded sessions:** no exact total. Business starts at $29/month or $290/year at 150k events and supplies replay, but capture quotas, rate, event usage and events per session are not fully documented. No session-to-event ratio was invented. Sources: https://pagelens.io/#pricing; https://pagelens.io/privacy.
- **1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 monthly active mobile users:** not_comparable to event prices without a specified event workload. The mobile SDKs send screen views/events; there is no published direct per-active-user tariff. Sources: https://pagelens.io/docs/install/ios; https://pagelens.io/docs/install/react-native; https://pagelens.io/#pricing.
- **$1,000 / $10,000 / $50,000 MRR:** not_comparable. The subscription tariff is metered by analytics events, not MRR. No payment-count or traffic ratio is assumed, and a complete MRR definition is not established. Sources: https://pagelens.io/#pricing; https://pagelens.io/features/revenue-attribution.

## Conflicts and unknowns

**Permitted identity use is internally inconsistent.** Terms section 3 prohibits identifying, profiling or following individual visitors, while section 4 acknowledges optional user identification with a lawful basis. Privacy and implementation docs allow user IDs and optional name/email traits. The likely intended boundary may be lawful first-party signed-in use, but the pages do not explicitly reconcile that with the broad ban. Both assertions remain recorded rather than choosing an interpretation. Sources: https://pagelens.io/terms; https://pagelens.io/privacy; https://pagelens.io/docs/install.

**Default privacy claims need mode-specific scope.** Daily anonymous hashes and no stored visitor IP do not mean that optional identified-user traits are anonymous. Customer security sessions also store IP/user agent. Replay form contents are always masked, but all-text masking is optional. The privacy policy provides these distinctions; the general cookieless feature copy is broader. Sources: https://pagelens.io/privacy; https://pagelens.io/features/cookieless-analytics; https://pagelens.io/terms.

**Usage accounting has unresolved parts.** Pricing names pageviews, custom events, autocaptured clicks and outbound clicks. The reference also documents errors, vitals, identity, replay and bot capture without a complete billable-type matrix. Multiple websites are included, but the twice-limit pause is worded per website and no explicit pooling formula was found. This affects multi-site or interaction-heavy estimates; it does not prevent the stated one-site, pageview-only calculation. Sources: https://pagelens.io/#pricing; https://pagelens.io/terms; https://pagelens.io/docs/api; https://pagelens.io/features/ai-traffic-analytics.

**Shopify has two described routes.** The install guide covers pasting the regular tracker into a theme. The privacy policy separately describes an app/embed/web-pixel workflow, including analytics-consent gating for checkout. The manual script instructions do not establish that app's checkout behavior or a public app-listing install route. Sources: https://pagelens.io/docs/install/shopify; https://pagelens.io/privacy.

Before assigning an unknown state, the following pages were tried:

- Operator company/country and market limits: terms, privacy, pricing/account setup. Only the PageLens project name and infrastructure region are established; a full country/sanctions list is not.
- Free plan, custom prices and site/seat quotas: homepage pricing/FAQ, terms and API permission/limit docs. The no-card trial is explicit, but a lasting free tier, custom-volume quote and numeric seat cap are not.
- Dashboard languages, supported editions and backend license: install index, feature index, proxy guide, terms and API. Mobile MIT licenses were opened separately and are not treated as a backend license.
- Replay quota, heatmaps and mobile parity: pricing, privacy, API and both mobile READMEs. Browser recording is documented; no native recording/capture matrix was found.
- DPA, backup location, transfer safeguards and operational retention: privacy, terms and install docs, followed by official-domain searches for data-processing/security material. Those searches did not return an additional public agreement. Primary EU storage does not prove every outside processor or backup stays there.
- DNT/GPC/consent controls: install options, proxy, privacy and terms. Developer-controlled exclusions and mobile disable methods are documented; automatic DNT/GPC defaults are not.
- Historical migration: homepage's Plausible/CSV claim, install/feature index and API reference. Exact formats, limits, supported GA history, overwrite behavior and feature gates were not established.
- Revenue accounting: revenue feature page, privacy and API. Webhooks, matching and currencies are documented; refund/chargeback netting, historical lookback, tax/fee basis and full MRR/churn definitions are not.
- Sampling/session rules and SDK delivery: API, cookieless/privacy pages and the linked mobile sources. Daily counting caveats and current network implementations are documented; no general no-sampling or durable offline guarantee is supplied.
- Anonymous record removal: privacy and terms. Site deletion and email rights requests are documented; backup purge timing and a universal per-visitor deletion endpoint are not.

No source was treated as failed merely because it omitted a fact. No gated page was accessed to resolve the remaining questions.

## Field problems

- F08/F12/F14: event pricing, incomplete type accounting and uncertain multi-site pooling need separate facts. A pageview-only scenario is calculable; a generic real-site or active-user total is not.
- F16/F21/F22: anonymous hashing, optional signed-in identity and replay have different privacy conditions. A single cookieless/no-banner flag would hide them.
- F17/F18: mobile guides contain shared web boilerplate about scripts and replay. Native support was taken from their actual SDK setup, README and source; that boilerplate was not used to assert mobile replay.
- F19: matching recurring payments to visits is revenue attribution, not proof of a complete subscription accounting product.
- F23/F25: Amsterdam primary storage, analytics history, recordings, account sessions and backups need separate scopes. No universal EU-only processing or one-number retention promise is assigned.
- F26: API permissions can include writes. MCP should not be described as read-only unless the selected key is read-only.
- F29: the under-4-KB browser claim does not establish total replay transfer size or native binary overhead. No benchmark was performed.
- F32: E applies to the default browser snippet. First-party proxies and native/server integrations need separate detection limits; no crawl or detector validation was run.
- All 33 field groups were attempted. No field-list edit is proposed.
