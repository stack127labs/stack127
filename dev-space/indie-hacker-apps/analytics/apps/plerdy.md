## Cost examples — our calculations

All inputs were checked on 2026-11-05 at https://www.plerdy.com/pricing/ and https://www.plerdy.com/faq/. Prices below preserve the public `$` symbol; the exact currency code and country-specific tax treatment were not established. These are **illustrative published-bundle costs**, not a vendor quote or a guarantee of the cheapest combination of tools and add-ons. Monthly billing is used. No currency conversion is made.

### Website pageviews

Inputs: one site/package, one account user, no custom-event workload, a **30-day month** and traffic distributed as evenly as possible. Maximum daily pageviews = `ceiling(monthly pageviews / 30)`. The chosen bundle must cover that daily maximum. Heatmaps are included; ecommerce/conversion limits on these bundles are the same daily number, but purchase capture requires its own setup.

| Monthly pageviews | Our daily-limit calculation | Example bundle and relevant cap | Our monthly base-cost calculation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | ceiling(10,000 / 30) = 334/day | Startup: 500/day | 1 × $32 = $32 |
| 100,000 | ceiling(100,000 / 30) = 3,334/day | Enterprise 1: 7,000/day | 1 × $149 = $149 |
| 1,000,000 | ceiling(1,000,000 / 30) = 33,334/day | Enterprise 3: 50,000/day | 1 × $390 = $390 |

These totals are exact base tariff arithmetic for the stated bundles, before any applicable tax or optional purchases. A different daily distribution can require another allowance despite the same monthly total. Tool add-ons can produce lower prices: the public page offers an extra 1,000/3,000/5,000/10,000 heatmap pageviews **per day** for $9/$19/$29/$49 per month. We have not assumed unlimited repeat purchases, removal of Free's three-page analysis restriction, or that a heatmap add-on raises ecommerce/conversion limits.

### Session replay

Inputs: web replay, one site/user, **100% capture of the selected eligible visits**, **three-minute recordings as an explicit example workload**, and **30-day rolling retention** with older records deleted before replacements fill the store. Heatmaps are already included in the bundle. This is a storage-capacity illustration: Plerdy publishes total stored-record limits, not a fresh monthly replay allowance. We assume other collection conditions and the chosen bundle's separate daily pageview limits are met; pageviews are not inferred from session counts.

For a steady 30-day workload, stored recordings needed = `monthly captured recordings × (30 retained days / 30 workload days)`. The store must therefore hold the monthly quantity at once. This deliberately uses 30-day retention even where the plan advertises a longer general history.

| Recorded sessions in 30 days | Our stored-record calculation | Example bundle and stored cap | Our monthly base-cost calculation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 1,000 × 30 / 30 = 1,000 records | Startup: 5,000 | 1 × $32 = $32 |
| 10,000 | 10,000 × 30 / 30 = 10,000 records | Scale: 10,000 | 1 × $64 = $64 |
| 100,000 | 100,000 × 30 / 30 = 100,000 records | Enterprise 2: 120,000 | 1 × $248 = $248 |

The base prices above are known. A guaranteed all-in quote for these recording workloads cannot be exact from the public tariff alone: a maximum recording duration, detailed capture eligibility and full-store rollover behavior are not specified. At exactly 10,000 stored records, Scale has no spare inventory buffer. Retaining more months increases required inventory; accumulating the same number of new recordings every month without deletion is a different workload. Replay add-ons may be cheaper than these bundles but are also stored-record capacity, not an independently confirmed monthly capture quota.

### Other category workloads

The proposed 100,000/1,000,000/10,000,000 monthly event comparisons are **not directly comparable**: Plerdy's conversions meter is daily pageviews rather than a published per-event tariff. No event-to-pageview conversion is invented. Native monthly-active-user and $1,000/$10,000/$50,000 MRR workloads cannot be priced using published native-user or MRR meters because those offers were not established. Ecommerce revenue features do not make Plerdy a subscription-accounting product.
