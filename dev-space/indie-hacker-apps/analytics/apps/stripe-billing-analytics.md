## Cost examples

These are our calculations using Stripe’s public United States (English) pay-as-you-go rate. They estimate the Stripe Billing fee, not an analytics-only charge. Billing analytics and Revenue Recovery analytics are listed as included, so Stripe publishes no separate fee for those reports.

| $10,000 MRR | If fee-bearing monthly Billing volume is exactly $10,000: $10,000 × 0.7% | $70/month Billing fee; no separate core analytics fee listed |
| $50,000 MRR | If fee-bearing monthly Billing volume is exactly $50,000: $50,000 × 0.7% | $350/month Billing fee; no separate core analytics fee listed |
| Annual monthly-payment plan at any of those assumed volumes | Each assumed monthly volume is below the $100,000 tier: $620/month under a 1-year contract | $620/month plus any applicable tax and payment-processing fees; included analytics |

Stripe’s fee meter is monthly Billing volume, not MRR. The three examples assume Billing volume equals the stated MRR solely to show the formula; actual monthly charges can differ. For a US Stripe Payments account, the Billing page separately displays 2.9% + $0.30 per successful card charge and 0.8% per ACH Direct Debit, capped at $5. These payment-processing charges are separate from the Billing subscription/volume fee and depend on payment method and country.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- Pricing is country-specific. The figures above are from the United States (English) page. Stripe’s public availability page says product availability varies by market, and Billing pricing may differ by account location or negotiated contract.
- The price basis is Billing volume, while the report’s recurring-revenue measure is MRR. Public pages do not provide a formula that converts MRR to billable Billing volume.
- Stripe Support says Billing Analytics data is available within one hour in most cases. A 2025 engineering post gives a best-case latency as low as 15 minutes. These describe typical versus best-case freshness, not contradictory service guarantees.
- Stripe’s terms identify the contracting Stripe entity by account country and service. The US contracting entity changed to Stripe, LLC on 2026-01-05; the product brand and owner remain Stripe.
- Core MRR and recovery reports are included. Custom reporting, warehouse sync, Revenue Recognition, and Metronome are separate or additional-cost products. Exact total cost for those options depends on region, usage, and destination.
- The exact supported processor list and parity of analytics/recovery features for third-party payment processors were not established from the current feature pages.
- The public pages do not specify an Analytics history window, API for every dashboard metric, a report-download format/quota, a fixed data purge schedule, or a public demo dashboard.

## Field problems

- F04: Stripe’s country list covers the platform generally, and Stripe warns that product availability varies. It does not provide a complete Billing-specific eligibility matrix.
- F08/F12: Billing volume and MRR are different measures. A price estimate at an MRR workload needs an explicit Billing-volume assumption.
- F11: Product marketing and pricing combine built-in Billing reports with separately paid Sigma, Data Pipeline, Revenue Recognition, and Metronome. These are separated above because the prices do not cover the same thing.
- F19/F28: Billing metrics are not visitor analytics. MRR excludes or treats trials, taxes, application fees, metered usage, coupons, and delinquency differently; the report should not be read as cash revenue.
- F23/F25: Stripe documents global processing and general retention rules, but not a Billing Analytics-specific storage region, report-history window, or backup purge schedule.
- F26: Downloadable roll-forward reports are documented, but the public product page does not state their file format or whether every built-in metric has a public API endpoint.
