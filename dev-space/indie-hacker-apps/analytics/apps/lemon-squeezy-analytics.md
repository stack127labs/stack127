# Lemon Squeezy analytics

- **Official URL:** https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/
- **Row status in `apps.md`:** active (Seed; row checked 2026-11-04)
- **Lifecycle:** Stripe acquired Lemon Squeezy on July 26, 2024. Lemon Squeezy's current terms name **Sold through Link, LLC, formerly Lemon Squeezy LLC**, as the service provider; Stripe's September 2026 affiliate list also names Sold Through Link as its merchant-of-record affiliate. Lemon Squeezy's January 2026 update says the same team is building Stripe Managed Payments and wants an easy migration route for Lemon Squeezy users. The Lemon Squeezy pricing, docs, app, API and status pages remain live. I found no Lemon Squeezy shutdown or sunset date.
- **Product changes since acquisition:** Lemon Squeezy refreshed subscription controls in December 2024, added new daily-snapshot MRR/ARR charts in May 2025, and localized checkout to 34 languages in August 2025. Stripe Managed Payments, built by the acquired Lemon Squeezy team, is now generally available to businesses in Germany for selling into 195 markets. It is a separate Stripe product; the earlier migration post stated that an easy path for Lemon Squeezy users was a goal, but I found no published Lemon Squeezy migration deadline or closure notice.

## Cost examples

**Our calculation, checked 2026-11-06.** Lemon Squeezy does not bill the built-in analytics dashboard by MRR. The published ecommerce tariff lists no separate analytics fee, so the added analytics line item is **USD $0** at each workload below. Transaction fees still apply.

Illustrative transaction-fee scenario: assume monthly subscription payments equal the MRR workload, each payment is USD $100, all payments are domestic US card payments, no tax is added, and no payout, refund, marketing or custom-volume fees apply. The fee guide says the standard fee is 5% + USD $0.50 per order, plus 0.5% on subscription payments.

Formula: `(5% + 0.5%) × monthly order total + USD $0.50 × payment count`.

| MRR workload | Assumed monthly payments | Our calculated platform transaction fees | Separate analytics fee shown in public tariff |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| USD $1,000 | 10 × USD $100 | USD $60 | USD $0 |
| USD $10,000 | 100 × USD $100 | USD $600 | USD $0 |
| USD $50,000 | 500 × USD $100 | USD $3,000 | USD $0 |

The transaction-fee estimates are illustrative calculations, not quotes. Actual fees depend on order totals, payment method, buyer location, subscription status, payout method, taxes and any custom commercial terms. MRR itself is not the pricing meter.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Trial wording:** Current ecommerce pricing says “no trial” and advertises free signup/test mode. The Terms page includes a generic 14-day free-trial clause with automatic conversion. The terms do not clearly scope this language to the current public merchant fee model, so both claims are recorded.
- **Lemon Squeezy vs Stripe Managed Payments:** Lemon Squeezy's 2026 post says Stripe Managed Payments is the team's new merchant-of-record product and that an easy migration path is a goal. The current Lemon Squeezy service, docs and status page remain live; no closure date or mandatory migration notice was found. Managed Payments is a separate Stripe product and has a separate fee schedule.
- **Analytics-only price:** No analytics add-on or analytics volume meter appears in the current public ecommerce pricing. Lemon Squeezy's Terms allow new optional features to carry a fee if one is specified; no extra current dashboard fee was located.
- **Store-only revenue scope:** The native charts are presented inside the Lemon Squeezy account and the data model is organized by Lemon Squeezy store. Stripe and Paddle migrations can import records, but the docs do not say whether that old history backfills MRR/ARR or other dashboard charts. No ongoing external processor connector was found.
- **Revenue calculations:** The vendor documents the revised and legacy MRR/ARR chart methods, but the public pages do not settle how all dashboard revenue charts treat tax, refunds, fees, currency conversion or migrated orders.
- **Privacy and retention:** Terms, DPA and privacy pages cover different data categories and give different deletion triggers. Privacy policy last updated June 1, 2021; it says U.S. or partner locations, while the DPA requires prior written consent for transfers outside the EEA. Neither identifies a single analytics storage region or an active-history retention period.
- **Newsroom check:** Lemon Squeezy has a company blog and changelog rather than a separate newsroom page that I could identify. I checked the blog archive, acquisition post, 2026 update and changelog. I also checked Stripe's newsroom and Managed Payments product page. The blog/archive contains the 2024 acquisition and later product changes, but no later Lemon Squeezy shutdown notice.
- **Pages checked for missing details:** Current pricing and fees for analytics charges, plan gates, trial and overages; dashboard and MRR/ARR docs for revenue formulas; GA/Meta integration page for storefront reports and consent settings; customer/order/subscription APIs for fields, currencies and refunds; export/API docs for data access; Stripe and Paddle migration docs for historical imports; supported-country and activation docs for seller eligibility; Terms, privacy and DPA for ownership, region and deletion; blog, changelog and status pages for lifecycle changes.

## Field problems

- F10's generic Terms trial clause does not match the current public ecommerce pricing page's “no trial” wording. The current self-serve merchant route is described as a free account with test mode; whether the legal clause applies to another account/service configuration is not stated.
- F15 calls for traffic reports, but Lemon Squeezy's main dashboard is revenue analytics. Its Google Analytics and Meta Pixel features send events to third-party reporting dashboards; treating those as native visitor reports would misclassify the product.
- F19 groups subscription metrics, sales amounts and currency/refund rules. Lemon Squeezy documents the MRR/ARR methodology change and API fields, but not one common rule for every dashboard total.
- F23/F25 combine different data categories, documents and deletion triggers. The public pages do not give one universal hosting region or one retention period for dashboard history.
- F32's class N means account-side analytics cannot be identified from a public homepage. A visible checkout may identify Lemon Squeezy commerce usage only.
