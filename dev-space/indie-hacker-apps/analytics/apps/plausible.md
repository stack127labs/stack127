## Cost examples

**Our calculation**, checked 2026-11-04. Every monetary input comes from the [official pricing selector](https://plausible.io/#pricing). The counting input comes from [subscription-plan usage rules](https://plausible.io/docs/subscription-plans): pageviews and custom events share the allowance across a team.

Assume one site, one account user, no discount, no Enterprise features and taxes excluded. Additional billable automatic events are disabled for the pageviews-only examples. Annual amounts are prepaid annual cash charges with a monthly traffic allowance; they are not monthly invoices.

Formula:

- Billable workload = pageviews + counted custom-event occurrences.
- Select the smallest published volume tier that covers the workload.
- Monthly service price = that plan's rate at that tier.
- Annual service price = 10 × that monthly rate, as the public selector specifies. No rounded monthly equivalent is used.

| Website workload/month | Selected tier/month | Starter: monthly / annual | Growth: monthly / annual | Business: monthly / annual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 pageviews; no custom events | 10,000 | $9 / $90 | $14 / $140 | $19 / $190 |
| 100,000 pageviews; no custom events | 100,000 | $19 / $190 | $29 / $290 | $39 / $390 |
| 1,000,000 pageviews; no custom events | 1,000,000 | $69 / $690 | $104 / $1,040 | $139 / $1,390 |
| 100,000 pageviews + 20,000 custom events = 120,000 billable events | 200,000 | $29 / $290 | $44 / $440 | $59 / $590 |

Examples of the formula: at 100,000 pageviews, one Starter subscription × $19/month = $19/month; annual prepayment = 10 × $19 = $190/year. The mixed workload must use the 200,000 tier; the 100,000 tier cannot cover it.

These are different feature packages. Starter counts traffic/goals; Growth adds collaboration/sharing; Business adds funnels, journeys, custom properties, revenue and Stats API. See [plan scope](https://plausible.io/docs/subscription-plans), checked 2026-11-04. Paying annually does not multiply the monthly event allowance by twelve for a single traffic spike.

## Conflicts and important qualifications

All pages below were checked 2026-11-04.

- **Form-tracking default:** the [dedicated form guide](https://plausible.io/docs/form-submissions-tracking), updated 31 October 2026, says new hosted sites enable it by default. The [metrics reference](https://plausible.io/docs/metrics-definitions), updated 28 October 2026, describes automated forms/downloads/outbound features as not enabled by default. Both remain recorded as conflicting. The [initialization options](https://plausible.io/docs/script-extensions) and [npm guide](https://plausible.io/docs/npm-package) separately explain code defaults and the package not reading site settings; that resolves the package/hosted distinction but not the broad wording in the metrics reference.
- **Bot IP ranges:** the [bot-filtering guide](https://plausible.io/docs/bot-traffic-filtering) says approximately 40,800 ranges, while the [Cloud/CE comparison](https://plausible.io/self-hosted-web-analytics) says about 32K. Different snapshots are plausible, but no shared date establishes one current figure. Neither is promoted as a measured count.
- **Lapsed subscriptions:** [terms](https://plausible.io/terms) give a general stopping/deletion policy; the [cancellation guide](https://plausible.io/docs/cancel-subscription) describes a month after dashboard lock before collection stops. The contract gives no fixed data-retention grace period. Trial expiry, paid cancellation and an overage lock are different conditions.
- **Cloud versus CE:** the [edition comparison](https://plausible.io/self-hosted-web-analytics) explicitly excludes paid analysis and advanced filtering from CE. Broad open-source branding does not establish that every Cloud feature is shipped in CE. Its [Compose wrapper MIT license](https://github.com/plausible/community-edition/blob/v3.2.1/LICENSE) also does not replace the [application AGPL license](https://github.com/plausible/analytics/blob/master/LICENSE.md).

- **EU visitor hosting versus other copies:** the [privacy policy](https://plausible.io/privacy) separates visitor processing from customer billing/support processing. [Raw exports](https://plausible.io/docs/raw-data-export) go to customer-selected storage. No universal geography promise for every customer account provider or exported copy is inferred.
- **No-sampling marketing:** the [dashboard FAQ](https://plausible.io/docs/dashboard-faq) qualifies its default with sampling on large views. The collection claim does not prove that every real visit survives blockers, exclusions or bot filtering.
- **Revenue events versus billing history:** the [revenue guide](https://plausible.io/docs/ecommerce-revenue-tracking) explicitly excludes persistent renewal/refund attribution. Provider callbacks are instrumentation, not a full Stripe/Paddle account sync.

## Field problems

- **F06/F07/F30:** one brand contains two deployment editions with different features, licenses in surrounding repositories, support and operating costs.
- **F08/F12/F13:** pageview-labelled allowances include custom events. Automatic goals can change the billable workload; annual prepayment does not create an annual pooled traffic limit.
- **F14:** sites, teams, unique login emails, guests, pending invitations and shared-link viewers need separate counting rules.
- **F16/F18:** aggregate journeys and an HTTP mobile route do not establish persistent identity, official native SDK parity or offline guarantees.
- **F17:** scroll depth and paths must not be classified as heatmaps or session replay.
- **F19:** event-based purchase attribution is not a subscription ledger. An MRR workload cannot determine an event-priced bill.
- **F21–F24:** cookieless defaults, input hashing, customer login cookies, optional opt-out storage and custom-data restrictions need separate scopes.
- **F25/F26:** active retention, locked dashboards, continuing capture, deletion, recovery backups, aggregate exports and raw backfills are separate facts.
- **F27/F28:** imported aggregates support fewer combined filters and different unique counts. Sampling, blocking and bot exclusion must not be summarized as perfect accuracy.
- **F29/F32:** a vendor size claim needs measurement context; arbitrary proxy paths and CE hosts prevent a hostname-only detection rule.
