## Cost examples — our calculation

Assume one account, one project, one app, one dashboard user, standard Pro pricing and no negotiated discount. The fee meter is **Monthly Tracked Revenue (MTR)**, not Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR). MTR counts the tracked amount charged for paid subscriptions, renewals and one-time purchases during the account's billing month, **before store commission and taxes**, in USD. The billing month can differ from a calendar month. Ad revenue is currently excluded while the documented beta policy remains in place.

Current standard Pro formula:

- Below $2,500 MTR: fee = $0.
- At $2,500 MTR and above: fee = 1% × **all** MTR.
- There is no subtraction of the first $2,500 once the fee applies. The pricing FAQ explicitly gives $2,500 → $25, and billing docs give $2,600 → $26.
- Prices below exclude any applicable tax on the RevenueCat service and separate store/payment processing costs.

Inputs and rules checked 2026-11-05: https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing ; https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/welcome/set-up-revenuecat/account-management ; https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/ad-monetization. Pricing-page FAQ answers were read from its publicly embedded structured FAQ data; no calculator purchase or checkout was performed.

| Inputs | Formula | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $1,000 USD MTR; standard Pro | Below $2,500 threshold | Our calculation: $0 per billing month |
| $2,500 USD MTR; standard Pro | 0.01 × $2,500 | Our calculation: $25 per billing month |
| $10,000 USD MTR; standard Pro | 0.01 × $10,000 | Our calculation: $100 per billing month |
| $50,000 USD MTR; standard Pro | 0.01 × $50,000 | Our calculation: $500 per billing month |

**Required MRR comparisons:** a business at $1,000, $10,000 or $50,000 MRR does not have one exact RevenueCat bill without its payment schedule, other tracked purchases, and billing-period alignment. There is no universal conversion ratio from MRR to MTR. If all subscriptions bill monthly within the measured billing period, there are no other purchases/adjustments, and the measured USD MTR equals those MRR amounts, the conditional results are $0, $100 and $500 respectively.

A concrete example shows the difference. Assume 100 active customers, no refunds, no purchase taxes, no one-time purchases, all payments completed and tracked in one RevenueCat billing period:

| Subscription inputs | Normalized MRR formula | That period's MTR formula | RevenueCat Pro fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 customers paying $10 monthly in the period | 100 × $10 = $1,000 MRR | 100 × $10 = $1,000 MTR | Our calculation: $0 |
| 100 customers paying $120 annually, all charged in this period | 100 × ($120 ÷ 12) = $1,000 MRR | 100 × $120 = $12,000 MTR | Our calculation: 1% × $12,000 = $120 |

MRR normalization input: https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/dashboard-and-metrics/charts/monthly-recurring-revenue-mrr-chart; MTR/fee inputs: https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/welcome/set-up-revenuecat/account-management and https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing, checked 2026-11-05. These are our simplified scenarios, not forecasts of a real app's future renewal months. Store commissions reduce developer proceeds but do not reduce the documented MTR base.

Enterprise, Growth Tools tracking configurations, contract discounts and any optional services require their own scope/quote. No exact annual total is computed: revenue is measured by billing period and annual prepayments can concentrate charges. No exchange-rate conversion was performed.

## Conflicts and unknowns
