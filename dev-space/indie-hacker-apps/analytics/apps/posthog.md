## Cost examples — our calculation

All amounts below are **USD per month, excluding tax**, unless stated otherwise. These are our calculations from the public incremental rate tables, checked 2026-11-05: [Analytics calculator](https://posthog.com/product-analytics/pricing), [web/mobile replay calculator](https://posthog.com/session-replay/pricing), [general billing terms](https://posthog.com/pricing) and [identity-billing qualification](https://posthog.com/docs/data/anonymous-vs-identified-events).

Assume one organization, one project, one website/app and one user of the dashboard. The allowances are pooled across the organization's projects, not multiplied by each project or each SDK. No platform package, group analytics, AI/Replay Vision, source sync, export, experiments or other usage is included unless expressly stated.

The current calculator exposes an **Analytics** schedule; the identity docs still say anonymous events may be up to 4× cheaper than identified events and describe separate invoice lines. Therefore the amounts below reproduce the **displayed schedule** for the explicitly anonymous/no-person-profile event scenario. They are not a guaranteed identified-event or mixed-event quote. We do not multiply the rates by four or silently reuse an older identified-event tariff.

### Website traffic

Inputs: only the stated pageview events are captured; no clicks, pageleaves, custom, identity, replay or other billable events. A normal installation captures additional events by default. A pageview-only configuration can also lose features that depend on pageleave/autocapture events.

Formula for up to 1 million total monthly analytics events:
`max(0, events − 1,000,000) × applicable marginal rate = $0`.

| Monthly pageviews | Counted analytics events | Our calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 10,000 | Within 1,000,000 free events → **$0/month**. |
| 100,000 | 100,000 | Within free events → **$0/month**. |
| 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 | Exactly the free allowance → **$0/month**. |

A million real pageviews with default pageleave/click capture can exceed the allowance; this table does not claim otherwise. Free-plan one-year event history differs from pay-as-you-go's longer history/features even when a paid account's usage invoice is $0.

Events, profiles, warehouse rows, recordings and temporary file exports have different lifetimes. The event guide allows delayed deletion after expiration; the general storage page mentions monthly cleanup for custom windows. Neither provides an exact per-event erasure deadline. Limiting usage, deleting data/account and legally terminating with notice are separate actions.
- **Data/model use:** the full terms' section 5 permits de-identified/aggregated customer-content use for PostHog product/internal-model development unless agreed otherwise or prospectively opted out; prior derived models generally are not undone. This was not reduced to a blanket “no training” claim.
- **Account/platform gaps:** checked terms, policy, pricing and SDK guides. A complete age/country eligibility matrix, dashboard-language list and numeric domains-per-project/extra-project tariff were not established. The export/sanctions prohibition is explicit; US incorporation is not worldwide account eligibility.
- **API/export limits:** [API overview](https://posthog.com/docs/api), [file export](https://posthog.com/docs/data-pipelines/file-download-exports) and [batch export](https://posthog.com/docs/data-pipelines/batch-exports) provide endpoint/key rate limits, temporary files, export intervals and concurrency. They do not establish a universal report CSV cap or all-in egress total. A successful capture HTTP response alone is not proof of final ingestion; quota/validation rules still apply.
- **Public demo and weight:** [demo](https://posthog.com/demo) returned media controls rather than enough content to verify an interactive sample account. Browser SDK docs describe core/slim/full/lazy-loaded variants, without one established size covering the selected deployment. No media playback, browser session, package install, tracker execution, script measurement, SDK-size test or crawl was run.

## Field problems

- F01 must distinguish a product shutdown from a removed analytics surface, ended deployment support or dependency-distribution change. The iOS SDK docs' CocoaPods notice affects updates, not the existence of native analytics.
- F07 needs license scope: MIT core and Enterprise-restricted code share one repository. A general “open source” badge would hide that distinction.
- F08–F14 need organization pooling, separate product meters and explicit identified-event uncertainty. A seat count or MRR amount cannot price event or row volume.
- F17/F18 require separate web/native replay rates, eligibility rules, masking defaults and installation needs. Heatmap collection is not replay.
- F21/F22 need explicit persistence and consent mode: cookieless tracking is not the default, and its visitor/session counts are not directly equivalent to persistent identified users.
- F23–F25 require storage vs transit/processing, retention vs deletion and account vs captured-user-data scope. A single “EU” or “7 years” value would be misleading.
- F26/F27 must distinguish raw event migration/export from GA4 daily-report syncing, with source-row costs and retention constraints.
- F32 can identify a PostHog SDK installation conditionally; it cannot infer which analytics/replay/flag products are enabled, global adoption or measured detector accuracy.
- No clear `apps.md` row error found. Its `active` status, US/EU cloud note, officially unsupported self-host qualification and class `C` match the researched scope. The removed dedicated revenue surface is recorded here for later editorial use.
