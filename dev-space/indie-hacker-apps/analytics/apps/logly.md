## Cost examples: our calculation

- Business is USD 299 early access or USD 399 after early access for 10,000,000 pageviews/month. Enterprise is quote-based, not an interpolated traffic price. Source: https://logly.uk/.
- Pageviews pool across sites; no marginal overage tariff or tax-inclusive rule is established. Early-access rate depends on invitation/activation eligibility and can change by annual HICP adjustment. Sources: https://logly.uk/docs/ ; https://logly.uk/terms/ ; https://logly.uk/main.js.

Assumptions: one site, one account operator, tracked pageviews only, no additional custom-event workload, current published monthly rate, included allowance not exceeded. No event-to-pageview ratio, currency conversion, tax percentage, Enterprise price or future inflation factor is invented. Exactly-at-allowance examples use the published allowance; the homepage explicitly says Free up to 10,000 and terms describe exceeding limits, without strict “below” wording.

Formula: **monthly base price of the lowest published plan covering the workload and site count + any applicable tax/other charges not established publicly**. These are **our calculations**, not a vendor quote or a completed checkout.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Identity and jurisdiction:** current live privacy says Logly operates from the European Union and stores analytics there. Terms name the EU member state from which it operates without naming that state. They do not identify an incorporated legal operator. Older search-index wording mentioning the UK was not copied into the record as current fact; a .uk domain does not settle incorporation, governing country or customer eligibility. Sources: https://logly.uk/privacy/ ; https://logly.uk/terms/.
- **Pricing phase and activation:** homepage offers paid invitations; UI source hides review for Starter but says Pro needs Starter usage review and Business needs Pro usage review, with estimated 24–48 hours. Docs also refer to EARLY-/STARTER- invitation families while the current homepage form uses INV-style codes. No code was obtained/redeemed, so the exact migration between code families and actual activation outcome remain unverified. Sources: https://logly.uk/ ; https://logly.uk/main.js ; https://logly.uk/docs/.
- **Lifetime lock and Free:** terms explicitly allow annual HICP CPI adjustments to early-access rates. Free’s “Forever” marketing is qualified by terms that can modify Free and do not guarantee it indefinitely. These qualifications are recorded rather than treated as promises of an unchanged bill forever. Sources: https://logly.uk/ ; https://logly.uk/terms/.
- **Trial/cancellation gaps:** invitation modal uses the word trial, but home/docs/terms/UI do not establish duration, payment-card requirement, automatic billing at expiry, cancellation cutoff, refund/proration, export grace or full account/backup purge timeline. Support deletion is not a complete published subscription-cancellation procedure. Sources: https://logly.uk/ ; https://logly.uk/docs/ ; https://logly.uk/terms/ ; https://logly.uk/main.js ; https://logly.uk/privacy/.
- **Funnel allowance:** marketing says every plan has every feature. The detailed funnel article says the first Funnel is free and Funnels are available on paid plans. Having one Free Funnel still gives access to the feature; it does not establish equal or unlimited quotas. The numeric paid count, step limit and conversion timing window are not established by examples of 3–5 steps. The article supports exact path/event steps but also contains a wildcard-like example; no wildcard matching guarantee is assigned. Sources: https://logly.uk/ ; https://logly.uk/blog/funnels-conversion-drop-off-examples/ ; https://logly.uk/docs/.
- **Legal-basis conflict:** privacy says no legal basis is required for visitor analytics because it processes no personal data. The official no-banner blog describes legitimate interests as the basis for anonymized analytics. Both positions are preserved as vendor wording; this research does not select a legal basis or endorse a universal exemption. Sources: https://logly.uk/privacy/ ; https://logly.uk/blog/analytics-without-cookie-banner/.

Public tracker text sends pathname plus query string and qualifying referrer; docs separately normalize Pages display, preserving WordPress p/page_id. Report normalization is not proof private query values never leave the browser. Server-side sanitization, IP-prefix length, security logging and every browser-storage case cannot be independently established from public client code/policy. Only the fetched default source route is confirmed; no collector was probed. “95%+” coverage, zero page-speed impact and no-blocklist claims remain vendor claims, not test results. ## Field problems

- F01/F04: Logly brand, .uk domain and EU region do not identify an incorporated operator or specific governing member state.
- F08/F10/F11/F12/F13: monthly list prices, early-access invitations, reviews, vague trial wording and CPI adjustments must be kept beside cost examples. No future fixed bill or universal immediate paid signup is promised.
- F16: “every feature” does not establish identical/unlimited Funnel quotas. Blog examples do not establish maximum steps, timing windows or authenticated identity analytics.
- F21/F22/F24: a daily identifier and query-bearing request still require a precise collected/stored distinction. Visitor cookie-free claims differ from account cookies, and vendor legal-basis explanations disagree.
- F23/F25: EU analytics storage, external assistant processing, aggregate history and raw session/event lifetime are different scopes. The public policy does not answer all of them.
- F26/F27: a 100,000-row export cap and failed API reference limit portability certainty. Examples joining exported traffic to Stripe/CRM data are not a built-in revenue product or historical importer.

- F32: match actual installed product-specific code. A generic p.js/data-site marker or WordPress plugin directory string is insufficient; other proxy/subdomain variants need validation.
