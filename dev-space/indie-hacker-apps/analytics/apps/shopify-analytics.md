## Cost examples — our calculation

Inputs: One US Shopify Basic store, one owner, pageviews only, no paid apps, no POS Pro, no paid theme, and no payment/transaction activity. Standard pricing after any promotion. USD is retained; there is no currency conversion. Sources: https://www.shopify.com/pricing; https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports; https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/free-trial (checked 2026-11-05).

Shopify lists reporting as included in the store subscription. It does not publish a per-pageview analytics tariff. These examples calculate the **known subscription subtotal before tax**, not a guarantee of an unlimited analytics allowance or the total cost of operating a selling business.

| Monthly pageviews | Inputs and formula — our calculation | Known result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 1 Basic store × $39 USD/month; no separately listed analytics fee | $39 USD/month before tax |
| 100,000 | 1 Basic store × $39 USD/month; no separately listed analytics fee | $39 USD/month before tax |
| 1,000,000 | 1 Basic store × $39 USD/month; no separately listed analytics fee | $39 USD/month before tax |

Annual alternative, at each stated workload: $29 USD/month equivalent × 12 months = **$348 USD/year paid upfront**, before tax. This is our calculation. The advertised eligible promotion, $1 USD/month for 3 months after a 3-day free trial, is not the ongoing rate.

For a store already paying for Basic, the public tariff lists analytics as included. An exact all-in bill cannot be calculated from pageviews alone: applicable tax, transactions, apps, retail extras and any other usage must be known. Plus can also have variable platform fees.

Generic product-event, session-replay, native-active-user and SaaS-MRR benchmarks are not comparable to this store subscription. No conversion between pageviews, sessions, customers, orders and revenue was assumed.

## Field problems

- F08–F14 compare standalone analytics tariffs with a bundled commerce platform. A store subscription and an analytics-only price must stay separate. The base cost examples therefore name the store plan and exclude unpriced commerce activity.
- F16 and F19 need a commerce scope: first-order cohorts, subscription counts and sales reversals are useful, but do not establish arbitrary product-event identity or normalized SaaS revenue metrics.
- F17's word “heatmap” is ambiguous. A cohort-grid visualization does not establish browser click/scroll recording.
- F18 must separate the merchant's mobile dashboard from a native collection SDK. No SDK parity or offline guarantee was inferred from mobile viewing.
- F21–F22 vary by region, configuration and migration state. Current policy, legacy docs and troubleshooting cannot safely be flattened into one cookie/no-consent badge.
- F25's earliest history dates, recovery guarantee and backup exceptions describe different things. None is a universal event-retention duration.
- F26 access depends on API version, app type, protected-data access and plan. A public report feature does not prove that every Basic-store custom API integration can read the same data.
- F32 measures public evidence, not dashboard use. Shopify hosting, a generic runtime bus, a CDN asset or third-party pixel alone cannot establish that Shopify Analytics is receiving events. No detection rule was validated.
