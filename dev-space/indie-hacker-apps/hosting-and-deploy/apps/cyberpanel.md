## Conflicts and unknowns

- **Release metadata:** CyberPanel’s official knowledge-base changelog lists v3.0.8 on October 18, 2026. The GitHub Releases page marks v1.9.1 “Latest,” and the repository page shows v3.0.5-dev. The current dated changelog is the newest official release notice found; GitHub’s release label is stale or inconsistent.
- **Security scope and fix status:** The dated vendor and maintainer notices are recorded once in the Field 30 rows. They do not publish an aggregate ransomware impact or recovery count for the 2024 event.
- **OS support:** The install guide’s June 2024 list omits newer releases covered in the 2026 changelog and support page. The guide’s 1 GB RAM and 10 GB disk are retained as the stated minimum, not represented as a recommendation for a production workload.
- **Monthly add-on price:** The add-ons page shows the annual and lifetime figures, a billing-cycle choice, and a “from $7.99/month” headline. The page text does not expose all monthly prices; no account or checkout was used.
- **Terms and privacy:** The core software license is published as GNU GPL v3. The homepage footer links privacy/contact but no general terms document. The privacy policy concerns the website and services, not a clear data-processing or residency contract for local panel installs. The pricing FAQ gives limited add-on refund details.
- **Status/SLA:** No status page or customer-server uptime SLA was found for the self-hosted panel. Its 24/7 support claim is not an uptime promise.

- Fields for metered compute, regions, provider network costs, hosted spend controls, and managed runtime limits do not map directly to self-hosted panel software. - Framework support is mainly documented as WordPress, a Laravel guide, and Docker deployments. Custom Docker images do not prove framework/version compatibility.
