One documented way to meet the daily portable-copy requirement is **external SimpleBackups**, linked by Sliplane. Configure a PostgreSQL backup daily with seven-day retention, using SimpleStorage or a chosen S3 destination. Its standard **Plus plan is $99/month**, with 200 GB included storage, 1 TB serverless bandwidth, a 250 GB maximum database size and scheduling/retention controls.     retained backup copies = 7 × 10 GB = 70 GB, within 200 GB
    monthly backup payload = 30 × 10 GB = 300 GB, within 1 TB
    configured daily-copy design:
    Sliplane €42.50/month + SimpleBackups $99/month
    plus applicable tax and any usage outside the stated inclusions

Currencies are kept separate; no exchange rate is applied. Sliplane VAT treatment is known. SimpleBackups' displayed dollar price does not establish local checkout tax or an ISO currency code here, so this is **not an exact combined final invoice**. Backup format, compression, temporary storage and actual dump/transfer size must be checked; 10 GB live storage does not prove every backup is exactly 10 GB. A restore test is still needed. No extra Sliplane bucket is charged in this design because the selected backup provider includes storage.

An own-container scheduled dump could be another design, but it would need explicit resource sizing, storage/retention setup and restore testing; no unsupported $0 complete backup claim is substituted. Sliplane's hosted container service and its separate managed database remain distinct management models. [Self-configured job containers](https://sliplane.io/background-jobs-hosting-europe); [Scheduled dump integration](https://docs.sliplane.io/databases/simplebackups.md), checked 2026-11-05.


## Conflicts and unknowns

- **US West location:** DPA v2.4 names DataPacket in Seattle for customer servers. Reverse-proxy documentation labels its US West gateway Hillsboro. These may describe different layers, but neither source explains the difference. Country/region is confirmed; exact server/gateway city should not be merged. [DPA subprocessors](https://sliplane.io/legal/dpa); [Gateway locations](https://docs.sliplane.io/services/reverse-proxy.md).

## Field problems

- **GB versus GiB:** B/C require at least 1 GiB, while public plans say 1 GB and the catalog names memory in MB without a unit definition. We choose 2 GB plans and do not call the smaller plan technically incapable.
- **Monthly versus hourly:** the discounted fixed monthly price is not the hourly rate multiplied by 730. Both are shown separately; monthly commitment/refund rules need checking before a short-lived deployment.
- **PITR and daily backups:** a seven-day restore window does not state the cadence/format of independent portable daily copies. C needs either a stated recovery-policy interpretation or a separately configured backup workflow.
- **Backup size:** 10 GB live database storage does not determine exact dump size, retained copies, temporary working space or transfer. C's capacity illustration labels those assumptions.
- **Hosted versus managed app:** Sliplane manages container hosts and offers a separate managed database. A database preset on an app server leaves application/database-specific work to the customer; those modes must not be treated as identical.
- **Flat-price safety:** included bandwidth/builds are subject to fair use. A negotiated metered overage, more servers, expanded disks or storage blocks still matter; a fixed app price is not an account-wide cap.
- **Confidential is narrower than general hosting:** data-channel exclusions, dedicated CPU requirement and additional agreement need separate fields. Country and a GDPR claim alone cannot express them.
- **Region taxonomy:** apps.md explicitly describes a discovery market, so its Europe entry is not a row error merely because runtime regions also exist elsewhere.
- No field was skipped. Deep app/database limits that could not be found were recorded with the bounded search, rather than filled from the underlying infrastructure providers.
