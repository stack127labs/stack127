## Lifecycle and current status

Amazon Lightsail is offered to new users through AWS account creation and the public Lightsail console. I found no Lightsail service shutdown, acquisition, or sunset notice. AWS’s What’s New feed instead shows Lightsail region expansion, Marketplace AMI support, and blueprint additions in 2026.

Several **images and blueprint features are retiring**:

- Starting May 19, 2026, Lightsail stopped providing newer Bitnami blueprint versions. Bitnami WordPress, WordPress Multisite, LAMP, Nginx, and Node.js blueprints are scheduled for deprecation on November 19, 2026. Joomla, Magento, MEAN, Drupal, GitLab, Redmine, Ghost, Django, and PrestaShop Bitnami blueprints are scheduled for May 19, 2027. Existing instances keep running; new instances from existing snapshots remain possible after the dates. Direct selection of deprecated images in the console/API ends on the date. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lightsail/latest/userguide/amazon-lightsail-faq-bitnami-blueprints.html
- Amazon Linux 2 reached end of support June 30, 2026; Debian 11 reached end of long-term support August 31, 2026; openSUSE Leap 15 reached end of life July 30, 2026. Lightsail no longer lets users launch new instances from those images, but existing instances and snapshot-based launches are not affected. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lightsail/latest/userguide/compare-options-choose-lightsail-instance-image.html
- Windows Server 2016 is scheduled to stop accepting new Lightsail instance launches on January 12, 2027, when Microsoft extended support ends. SQL Server 2016 Express reached end of extended support July 14, 2026; Lightsail no longer permits new launches from that image. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lightsail/latest/userguide/compare-options-choose-lightsail-instance-image.html

## Cost examples

All amounts are **our calculations**, not AWS quotes. They use Lightsail only, USD, US East (Ohio), 730 hours for a full month, no domain purchase, no tax, no outside CI/build costs, and no optional support. AWS’s pricing page says Region selection can change prices; the readable rate card does not expose the selected Region for every tier. The $5 bundle is specifically shown in an Ohio example; the $7 and $15 prices below use the displayed rate card with Ohio as the comparison Region, not a verified Ohio-specific quote. These totals do not include EC2, EBS, RDS, CloudFront, or other AWS product charges.

- **A — commercial static site:** One $5/month general-purpose Linux/Unix dual-stack VM in Ohio, with 100 MB deployed files and 100 GB outbound under its 1 TB allowance. Formula: 1 × $5 monthly plan = **$5/month known Lightsail subtotal**. The 20 × 5-minute builds need a separate local or external build workflow; current domain registration price and any external builder cost were not verified, so this is not an exact all-in workload total.
- **B — stateless API:** Smallest listed VM with at least 1 vCPU and 1 GiB RAM is the $7/month bundle (2 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, 40 GB SSD, 2 TB transfer). Formula: 730 hours reaches the **$7/month maximum bundle charge**. 100 GB outbound fits the allowance. One million requests do not create a VM request-meter fee, but this does not prove adequate throughput; builds/domain remain outside this subtotal.
- **C — API plus PostgreSQL:** One $7 VM plus one $15 Standard managed PostgreSQL database (1 GB RAM, 40 GB SSD, 100 GB allowance). Our calculation from the public USD rate card: $7 + $15 = **$22/month estimated Lightsail subtotal**; exact Ohio tier rates are not exposed in the text view. Same-Region private app-to-database traffic is free. The managed database keeps seven days of point-in-time backups without an added backup fee; 100 GB VM egress fits its bundle. Tax, external builds, and domain registration are not included.
