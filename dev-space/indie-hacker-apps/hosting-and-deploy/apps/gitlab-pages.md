## Cost examples — our calculations

Calculated on 2026-11-04. These are our calculations, not vendor quotes. All inputs use the facts and exact URLs above, checked on that date. One builder; one private-source repository; one public ordinary business website; one registered custom domain; no trial/startup/open-source-program credits; no optional support; no annual-discount substitution. Registration and buyer-specific taxes are excluded. No real deployment, performance test, or checkout was performed.

### A. Small commercial static site

Inputs: 100 MB published output; 1,000,000 HTTP requests and 100 GB outbound/month; 20 builds × five minutes; one private repository; one custom domain. Choose GitLab.com Free and the small Linux x86-64 hosted builder, factor 1. Assume the entire billed job time for each build is five minutes, with no additional test/preview jobs; source history and LFS stay within 10 GiB; output contains at most 200,000 entries; configure public Pages visibility for the private-source project and enable automatic certificates. No extra backend is added.

Source inputs:

- Free base and repeating build quota: https://about.gitlab.com/pricing/ .
- No additional infrastructure hosting fee and static-only model: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/pages/ .
- Duration and factor accounting: https://docs.gitlab.com/ci/pipelines/compute_minutes/ .
- File and site ceilings: https://docs.gitlab.com/administration/instance_limits/ and https://docs.gitlab.com/user/gitlab_com/ .
- Source storage scope: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/storage_usage_quotas/ .
- Public visibility with private source: https://docs.gitlab.com/tutorials/hugo/ .
- Certificate management: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/pages/custom_domains_ssl_tls_certification/lets_encrypt_integration/ .
- Burst limits and qualitative use restrictions: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/gitlab_com/rate_limits/ and https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/legal/acceptable-use-policy/ .

Our calculation:

`build usage = 20 × 5 minutes × factor 1 = 100 compute minutes/month`

`additional compute needed = max(0, 100 − 400) = 0 minutes`

`known monthly subtotal = Free base $0 + additional build capacity $0 + Pages hosting $0 + automatic certificate $0 = $0`

100 MB is below the published 1 GB site ceiling without needing to decide the site's exact GB byte basis. Repository history is a separate assumption, since a 100 MB deployed bundle does not determine repository size. There is no separately required delivery product in the documented Pages workflow.

**Result: known subtotal $0; a precise unconditional total/fit for the full traffic specification is not established.** No numeric monthly 100 GB allowance or Pages per-GB/request overage price was found. Monthly request totals also do not fix IP/domain bursts or TLS connection churn. The AUP can constrain excessive usage and primary content distribution. Therefore do not turn this subtotal into an unlimited-bandwidth claim or a guarantee that every advertising/e-commerce business is eligible. There is no justified paid-overage formula to add for the remaining uncertainty.

For comparison only, our arithmetic on the advertised annual Premium rate would be `1 seat × $29 × 12 = $348/year before tax`, conditional on a one-seat purchase being offered. This is not the requested ordinary-monthly scenario total. It is not required to make a private-source Pages site public.

### B. Small commercial app API

Inputs retained: 1,000,000 requests/month; 100 ms active CPU and 250 ms elapsed/request; 256 MiB working memory; 100 GB outbound; 20 five-minute builds; one private repository and domain.

**Result: unsupported by GitLab Pages; no comparable total.** The product serves static files and explicitly does not support dynamic server-side processing: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/pages/ .

Our input arithmetic, not a price: `1,000,000 × 0.100 seconds = 100,000 CPU-work seconds` and `1,000,000 × 0.250 seconds = 250,000 request elapsed seconds`. Pages has no customer application runtime to which those amounts can be priced. The temporary builder's vCPU/RAM does not meet the requirement for a running API. An outside backend would be a different combined architecture requiring its own provider research and total.

### C. App plus persistent data

Inputs retained: all B inputs plus Postgres with at least 1 GiB RAM, 10 GB storage, daily backups kept seven days, and 10 GB/month app-to-database traffic.

**Result: unsupported by GitLab Pages; no comparable total.** It inherits B's missing runtime, and the Pages product has no included customer Postgres. Source: https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/pages/ . GitLab's internal database backups are service operations, not a customer database entitlement: https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/gitlab-com/policies/backup/ .

Our formula: `C = compatible API host + customer Postgres plan + seven-day daily backup cost + app/database transfer + any required extras`. Those components cannot be fulfilled or priced as Pages resources. The known A static-hosting subtotal cannot replace B's API cost. An external API/database provider would need to be named and researched before giving a combined figure; none was selected without evidence. Rebuilding or restoring a static deployment does not restore application database data.
