## Cost examples: our calculation

Calculated on 2026-11-04. One builder, commercial public product, one private source repository and one custom domain. Prices use USD as an explicit currency assumption; no conversion, annual discount, startup/migration credits, optional support, paid previews, extra replicas, dedicated IPs, PrivateLink or HIPAA premium. Domain registration is excluded. Assume the named 100 GB is total billable public outbound usage and there is no unrelated workspace usage.

**Commercial-plan reading:** the checked general terms expressly contemplate commercial users and do not state that Hobby is noncommercial-only. Hobby's personal/prototype marketing and the recommendation against production Free compute are preserved in fields 08/10. A/B do not assume a free runtime for production API hosting. Sources: https://render.com/terms , https://render.com/acceptable-use and https://render.com/pricing (all checked 2026-11-04).

**Common build/domain inputs:** `20 builds × 5 minutes = 100 Starter pipeline minutes`; Hobby includes 500 and Pro includes 1,000, so `max(0, 100 − included minutes) = 0` purchased minutes. Starter build resources are 2 CPU/8 GB, distinct from runtime. One custom domain fits Hobby's two-domain pool and Pro's 15-domain pool; managed TLS is free. Render can build the private repository directly, so no separate GitHub Actions charge is selected. Sources: https://render.com/docs/build-pipeline.md , https://render.com/docs/custom-domains.md , https://render.com/docs/tls.md and https://render.com/docs/web-services.md (checked 2026-11-04). Extra build/pre-deploy/retry work would have to remain within the unused pool for the $0 build line to hold.

**Time and capacity:** ordinary app/database prices are published per month, with per-second proration but no explicit 730-hour divisor located. B/C treat one full billed month as the requested 730-hour comparison month. Their totals are **normalized monthly estimates**, not exact invoices for an arbitrary 730-hour interval across calendar boundaries. If the provider's proration basis is `H` hours, replace each full-month compute amount `P` with `P × 730/H`; `H` was not established. This limitation does not affect A's transfer-only hosting total. No claim of measured requests/second is made.

### C. App plus persistent data

Inputs: B plus Postgres with at least 1 GiB RAM, 10 GB allocated storage, daily backup retained seven days, and 10 GB/month app-to-database traffic. Select a same-region managed Postgres `1c-2g` database at $40/month. Its published 2 GB RAM exceeds 1 GiB regardless of decimal/binary interpretation. The cheaper advertised 1 GB/$19 plan is recorded in the facts, but its unit is not silently relabeled GiB. Database creation allows 10 GB (a multiple of 5). Same-region internal app/database traffic adds $0 Render outbound charge. Sources: https://render.com/docs/postgresql-creating-connecting.md and https://render.com/docs/outbound-bandwidth.md (checked 2026-11-04). Optional storage autoscaling is off for this fixed-capacity estimate.

Let `S` be the monthly charge for the 10 GB allocation. At the published $0.30/GB rate, the two unresolved first-GB interpretations are:

- If the first 1 GB is included on the paid database: `S = (10 − 1) × $0.30 = $2.70`.
- If that inclusion only describes Free Postgres: `S = 10 × $0.30 = $3.00`.

These are alternative interpretations of a published statement, **not a verified invoice range**. Source: https://render.com/pricing (checked 2026-11-04).

**Native seven-day recovery variant:** Pro workspace gives seven-day continuous PITR for paid Postgres. Our normalized calculation is `Pro $25 + web $25 + DB compute $40 + S + outbound (100 − 25) × $0.15 = $101.25 + S`. That is **$103.95 or $104.25** under the two storage interpretations. Pro includes the one domain, 100 build minutes and seven-day PITR. Source: https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups.md (checked 2026-11-04).

Continuous PITR can recover a state from each retained day, but the current docs do not promise automatic daily downloadable logical exports. Therefore that variant is labelled a recovery alternative, not an unqualified match to a literal daily-export requirement. PITR restore also creates an additional billable database during recovery.

**Literal daily-export architecture:** remain on Hobby and configure the official daily cron-to-Amazon-S3 backup approach, with a seven-day retention policy in the backup destination. Render documents a daily 03:00 UTC example; it does not configure that destination's retention automatically: https://render.com/docs/backup-postgresql-to-s3.md (checked 2026-11-04). S3 lifecycle rules can apply time-based expiration to the backup objects: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/intro-lifecycle-rules.html (checked 2026-11-04). This is a proposed architecture, not a claim that the Render sample already implements the required retention.

Our formula is `web $25 + DB compute $40 + Hobby outbound $14.25 + S + cron charge + S3 charges = $79.25 + S + cron charge + S3 charges`. For the entry cron compute, `cron charge = max($1/month, runtime minutes × $0.00016/minute)`; it is prorated by the second in actual use. **No exact total for strict C.** Allocated database capacity does not tell us actual dump bytes, compression, backup runtime, operation counts, destination charges or restoration/download traffic. Amazon S3 is the explicit external storage dependency; the Render hosting subtotal is `$79.25 + S`, before cron/S3/tax and subject to the stated full-month normalization.
