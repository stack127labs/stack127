# Hosting and deploy directory: competitor page review

**Checked:** 2026-11-09  
**Scope:** Six public pages found through searches for PaaS comparisons, Heroku alternatives, self-hosted PaaS and VPS comparisons. This is a review of what the pages show, not an endorsement or a claim about exact search rank. I did not sign up or submit forms. When a fetched text view did not expose a control, I say so rather than assume the live interface has none.

## Pages reviewed

### 1. G2 — Cloud Platform as a Service (PaaS) category

- **URL:** [g2.com/categories/cloud-platform-as-a-service-paas](https://www.g2.com/categories/cloud-platform-as-a-service-paas)
- **Who runs it:** G2.com, Inc. ([G2 terms](https://legal.g2.com/terms-of-use) identify the operator).
- **Layout:** A large category page with an overview, G2 Grid graphic, product listings and expanded review-oriented product sections; it also has a compact “Top Tools at a Glance” table.
- **Data shown for products:** Product name, short positioning, star rating, review count, category-specific scores (such as deployment, database management and networking), seller, company details, likely users and industries, company sizes, reviewer pros and cons, recent review titles, and discussion links. The category overview reports a product count and average category rating.
- **Filters and sorting:** G2 has related theme and free-only category pages. The fetched category text did not expose a complete filter panel or a sort control, so their availability in the rendered interface is **not confirmed here**.
- **Price:** No comparable numeric price per product on this category page. Rating and review signals are its main comparison data.
- **Sources and dates:** Shows review counts, category statistics dated November 2026, and an editorial update date of April 9, 2026. G2 describes a verified-review and analyst ranking method, but individual technical claims and prices do not have per-field source links on this page.
- **What works:** Strong buyer trust cues, review depth, product fit and company context. The category definition and review dimensions help shoppers compare user experience.
- **What is weak or missing:** The category is broad and includes serverless and general cloud products alongside app platforms. It is review- and business-buyer-led, with no exact price, free-tier conditions, deployment regions or small-workload estimate in the comparison view. The fetched page did not show usable filters or sorting.

### 2. AlternativeTo — Heroku alternatives

- **URL:** [alternativeto.net/software/heroku](https://alternativeto.net/software/heroku/)
- **Who runs it:** AlternativeTo; the site identifies 27 Kilobyte AB in its footer and says the lists are crowd-sourced.
- **Layout:** Paginated list of alternative-product cards. Each card includes a short description, likes, metadata and sometimes screenshots and comments.
- **Data shown for products:** Product name and description, “cost/license” labels (for example Free, Paid, Freemium, Open Source or Proprietary), application type, supported platforms, country tag, user likes, screenshots, comments and an alternative count.
- **Filters and sorting:** Application-type, cost/license and platform tags provide useful taxonomy. The fetched text showed pagination and “show more,” but no explicit sort control or full interactive filter panel; those controls are **not confirmed here**.
- **Price:** Usually a broad free/paid label rather than a numeric plan price or a defined billing period.
- **Sources and dates:** The Heroku alternatives page says it was last updated June 21, 2023. It links to product profiles and community comments, but does not show systematic source citations or checked dates for each fact.
- **What works:** Quick discovery, open-source/self-hosted tags and community votes make it easy to find adjacent tools. The platform and licence labels help distinguish a service from software you run yourself.
- **What is weak or missing:** The page is stale for a fast-changing market; its visible update date is 2023 and some comments are older. Votes and descriptions are crowd-sourced, and broad licensing labels do not explain exact licences, current plan terms, hosting costs or operational requirements.

### 3. DigitalOcean — Top Heroku alternatives

- **URL:** [digitalocean.com/resources/articles/heroku-alternatives](https://www.digitalocean.com/resources/articles/heroku-alternatives)
- **Who runs it:** DigitalOcean, a hosting provider whose App Platform is one of the listed options.
- **Layout:** Long guide with a side-by-side table, followed by sections describing the listed providers and migration advice.
- **Data shown for products:** The comparison table uses **solution, best for, key features and pricing**. The article then adds language/framework support, deploy methods, databases, scaling, SLA, migration notes and product-specific feature details for selected entries.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting; it is an editorial article.
- **Price:** Short entry points or simplified billing signals, such as a starting monthly price, hourly rate or “pay for AWS resources.” The article says pricing and features are based on public documentation as of February 2026 and can vary by region and workload.
- **Sources and dates:** Links to provider product pages and docs in the body and advises readers to verify current prices. It gives a February 2026 reference date, not a separate checked date for every table cell.
- **What works:** Broad coverage across managed PaaS, serverless and self-hosted options. “Best for” and key features give a newcomer a quick first pass, while the longer sections add migration context.
- **What is weak or missing:** DigitalOcean is both publisher and a listed competitor, so the page has a commercial interest. A single “pricing” cell collapses different units, plan fees, free-tier conditions and infrastructure charges; it does not make the listed prices comparable as a real workload total. A snapshot date helps, but prices can age quickly.

### 4. Fly.io Learn — Top Heroku alternatives

- **URL:** [fly.io/learn/heroku-alternatives](https://fly.io/learn/heroku-alternatives/)
- **Who runs it:** Fly.io, a provider listed in its own comparison.
- **Layout:** Long, dated guide with a comparison table, then per-provider sections describing fit, pricing and differences from Heroku.
- **Data shown for products:** The table uses **platform, best for, smallest always-on service, managed Postgres from, and $0 tier**. The provider sections add deployment model, worker support, database details, example resource sizes, costs and migration fit.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting; this is a fixed editorial comparison.
- **Price:** More useful than a single starting-price cell: it gives minimum always-on compute and managed database entry points, and qualifies some free tiers. Prices are snapshots rather than a matched workload comparison. The article says prices came from the vendors’ own pricing pages as of October 2026 and identifies October 22 as its source-check date.
- **Sources and dates:** Provider pricing pages are linked from the article, with a dated source list. The article itself is dated September 7, 2026. It also links the Heroku lifecycle announcement and its own billing explainer.
- **What works:** The “always-on service + database + free tier” columns fit common indie app decisions better than generic feature checklists. The explanatory sections surface billing units and hidden base fees that a flat starting price misses.
- **What is weak or missing:** Fly.io is included and gets its own positioning, so treat the article as vendor-authored guidance. Its selected platform list is editorial, not a directory, and figures for unlike billing models still do not equal one standard app’s monthly total. It does not compare data locations, licences or lifecycle events for every listing.

### 5. VPSBenchmarks — VPS Screener and VPS Plans

- **URLs:** [Screener](https://www.vpsbenchmarks.com/screener) · [Plans](https://www.vpsbenchmarks.com/plans) · [About](https://www.vpsbenchmarks.com/about)
- **Who runs it:** Camino Cielo Cloud Services. Its About page and terms name the operator. The site says provider subscriptions can place featured plans first; it also uses affiliate links.
- **Layout:** An interactive screener backed by a large plan table and standardized performance grades. The plans page has a searchable/filterable listing; the Screener offers side-by-side narrowing and ranking.
- **Data shown per VPS plan:** Provider and plan, monthly price, vCPU count, memory, disk, transfer allowance and performance scores for web, CPU, stability, disk I/O and network I/O. The screener shows trial-data dates and lets readers open the underlying test metrics. The plans page states its price basis: one month without commitment, before promotions, Linux, before VAT, with IPv4 added where not included.
- **Filters and sorting:** Price, vCPU, memory, disk, provider, data-center country, burstable/dedicated CPU and price-weighted grades. Users can sort by overall grade, web performance, raw CPU, stability, disk I/O or network I/O. The plans page also filters by provider, featured status, managed VPS and hyperscaler.
- **Price:** Standardized monthly Linux VPS price, excluding VAT and promotions; the site flags IPv4 charges separately when needed. A price-weighted mode changes grades to emphasize value.
- **Sources and dates:** Shows when tests were run; the screener includes plans tested in the last 18 months. The plans list was updated November 9, 2026. It documents test types and grade calculations. It openly discloses featured-provider ordering and provider support.
- **What works:** This is the strongest specialist comparison tool in the sample. It combines real measured performance, plan specifications, location and cost filters, multiple sort orders, dates and a clear price basis.
- **What is weak or missing:** Performance tests do not describe app deployment workflows, language support, managed databases, free-tier restrictions or platform limits. Sponsored plans appear first, even though the site says tests use the same method. It is a VPS tool, not a comparison of hosted PaaS or self-hosted control panels.

### 6. ngpaas.eu — Self-hosted PaaS: Coolify vs. Dokku vs. CapRover

- **URL:** [ngpaas.eu/paas/self-hosted-paas](https://www.ngpaas.eu/paas/self-hosted-paas/)
- **Who runs it:** The article describes ngpaas.eu as an independent guide and discloses that some provider links may be affiliate links. It does not identify the legal operating entity on the article page.
- **Layout:** Short explanatory article with a three-product comparison table, then one section per project and a practical checklist of operator responsibilities.
- **Data shown per project:** Interface, build methods, underlying technology, multiple-server support and one-click services; the sections explain deploy flow, databases/plugins, server requirements, TLS and who owns maintenance. It distinguishes software cost from rented-server cost and data location.
- **Filters and sorting:** No filters or sorting; it is a fixed comparison article.
- **Price:** No normalized product-plan prices. It explains that server rental is the main cost and links to a separate VPS comparison and PaaS cost calculator.
- **Sources and dates:** Published October 7, 2026. It says the comparison is based on project documentation and repositories as of October 2026 and that project docs are authoritative. Provider links and its affiliate disclosure are visible.
- **What works:** It explains the real trade-off of self-hosting: platform fees go away, but updates, backups, monitoring and security move to the owner. The columns compare the features that separate the three tools, not just their marketing claims.
- **What is weak or missing:** It covers only three projects, gives no standard workload cost or full per-project licence/release table, and is not a full hosting directory. Some important cost detail is deferred to separate pages.

## What these pages tend to show

Across this sample, a product name, short description and some clue about its use or category are the most consistent elements. Comparison articles commonly add a “best for” label, a small feature summary and a price signal. General software directories emphasize ratings, votes, licence/platform tags and user feedback. The specialist VPS tool is strongest on technical plan specifications, measured performance, filters and sorting. No single layout fits hosted platforms, cloud functions, VPS providers and self-hosted software equally well.

## What our sourced data adds

No page in this sample consistently presents the full set of facts we have for each app:

- **Free tier details:** Some competitors mark a product free or list a `$0` option, but they rarely show the precise quota, expiry, eligible use, sleep/reclaim rule and current conditions together.
- **Small-workload cost examples:** Some guides quote starting prices or minimum server sizes. They do not consistently show the inputs, formula and monthly total for the same small app workload across providers. ngpaas.eu links a separate calculator, but its article does not give each project a matching worked total.
- **Regions and currencies:** VPSBenchmarks has data-center-country filtering, but the software directories and PaaS comparison articles do not give a consistent per-product location and currency comparison. Regions can affect availability and price.
- **Self-hosting and licences:** A few pages label software as self-hosted or open source. They do not consistently distinguish a hosted service from a self-hosted edition, state its exact licence, and explain that server costs are separate.
- **Lifecycle and trust changes:** Fly.io’s article includes a Heroku product-status update, but none of these pages works as a dated per-app record for acquisitions, rebrands, shutdowns, retired free tiers, breaches or regulatory actions.
- **Evidence at the fact level:** Several pages have general source notes or review signals. None of the comparison formats routinely attaches an official source and checked date to each price, limit and lifecycle fact.

## Top five ideas for our page

1. **Let readers choose the deployment model first.** Separate managed app platforms, serverless/edge, VPS/cloud servers, server-management panels and self-hosted PaaS. Keep the full directory searchable across groups, but give people a quick starting path.
2. **Build filters around real decisions.** Offer free-tier availability and limits, lowest comparable cost, region, hosted versus self-hosted, licence, deployment type and current lifecycle status. Add useful sorts such as monthly cost for a named workload, not just a generic “best” score.
3. **Show one comparable small-workload total.** Display the inputs, formula, billing period, region/currency and what is excluded. Keep platform fees separate from rented cloud resources, databases, bandwidth and other add-ons.
4. **Make evidence and freshness visible beside each fact.** Link each price, limit, licence, region and status claim to the relevant official page and show its checked date. Label estimates and dated incidents clearly.
5. **Use a compact compare view with important conditions expanded.** Let readers select a few apps and compare free-tier limits, workload costs, regions, self-host requirements and lifecycle notes side by side. Keep the first card scan short; expand the details when the reader needs them.
