## What to enter

| Box | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Net royalty per sale | What KDP pays you for one copy, before ads. Get it from the [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator). To cover other per-copy costs too, subtract them first. |
| Desired amount left per sale | What you want to keep from each ad sale after paying for clicks. `$0.00` means break even. |
| Assumed purchase rate | Purchases ÷ clicks, as a percent. Type `10` for 10%, without the % sign. |
| CPC to check | Your average cost per click: ad spend ÷ clicks. Enter average CPC, not your base bid. Amazon can raise the base bid through dynamic bidding and bid adjustments. |
| Ad-attributed sales value per purchase | Optional. It turns on ACoS. Enter ad-attributed sales divided by ad-attributed purchases for the same campaign and date range. Use sales value, not royalty. |

Use your own ads report for a campaign that's already running. For a new campaign, the purchase rate is a guess, so treat the result as one too.

## How to read the result

- Maximum CPC. The highest average CPC that leaves your target, rounded down to the cent.
- Meets target or Misses target. The verdict for the CPC you entered.
- Modeled amount left per attributed sale. What you keep after click costs.
- Minimum purchase rate at entered CPC. How often clicks must turn into sales for that CPC to work.
- Modeled click cost per attributed sale. CPC ÷ purchase rate. On average, that's what you pay in clicks to make one sale.

<Example title="A $4.39 royalty and a $1.00 target">

At a 10% purchase rate, you can spend $3.39 on clicks per sale. That's a maximum CPC of $0.33. At a $0.30 CPC, ten clicks cost $3.00, leaving $1.39, so it meets the target. That CPC needs at least an 8.85% purchase rate. With a $14.99 sales value, your ACoS is 20.0%, under the 22.6% ceiling for your target.

</Example>
