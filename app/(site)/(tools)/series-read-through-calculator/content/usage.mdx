## What to enter

Add one row for each book in the series, in reading order.

- **Price:** the Kindle price on Amazon.com. Enter 0 for a free book. If a price is below the lowest KDP allows for your file size, the calculator uses that lowest price instead.
- **Copies sold:** use the same dates for every book. KDP Reports lists orders for each title. Readers take a few weeks to finish one book and buy the next, so a longer window, such as the last 90 days, gives a steadier number than a launch week.

Planning a book that isn't out yet? Enter the copies you expect. Book 1 at 100 and book 2 at 55 models 55% of readers moving on.

Everything under **File size, KU reads, ads, and deals** is optional. Leave it closed and you get the answer from sales alone.

- **Converted file size:** KDP charges a delivery fee on 70% sales, based on the size of your converted file. Your book's pricing page in KDP shows it. Empty uses 2 MB.
- **Kindle Unlimited (KU):** for books in KDP Select, the program that puts your ebook in KU. Enter each book's KU pages read over the same dates as its sales. Then add its KENPC, which is KDP's page count for KU, shown on the book's Promote and Advertise page in your Bookshelf. Last, add the KENP rate, which is what KDP paid per page that month, and the month it's from. The calculator never guesses a rate, so once you enter pages, the rate and month are required.
- **Ads:** what your ads cost now, the share of ad clicks that turn into book 1 orders, and a profit you want to keep from each reader. Enter the cost as ACoS, which is Amazon's ad spend ÷ ad sales, or as a dollar cost per new reader. Use cost per reader for Facebook or BookBub ads, since only Amazon reports ACoS.
- **Book 1 deal:** a sale price for book 1, or 0 for free, and whether it's a Countdown Deal or an ordinary price change. The last field is your guess at the share of deal readers who go on to buy or borrow book 2.

The deal read-through doesn't show up again in the result. It changes two numbers: what each deal reader is worth, and how many deal copies match one full-price reader. Leave it empty and the calculator uses your own book 1 to book 2 rate. Try a few values, since people who got book 1 free may be less likely to carry on.

## How to read the result

| Result | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| A new book 1 reader is worth | Everything the series earned over your dates, divided by the number of book 1 readers. It's what an average new reader brings in across all the books. |
| Break-even ACoS on book 1 | The ACoS where ads on book 1 cost exactly what the series earns back. Below it, the series makes money on those readers. |
| 10 more points to book 2 adds | How much more each new reader would be worth if 10 more out of every 100 went on to book 2. |
| Readers and earnings by book | For every 100 people who read book 1, how many reach each book, the share who carried on from the book before, and what each book adds to the reader's value. A step above 100% means a book had more readers than the one before it. |
| Ads | What a reader costs you now and what's left after it, the most to pay per reader if you set a profit, and the highest cost per click (CPC) you can bid at your conversion rate. |
| Book 1 deal | What one copy earns at the deal price, what a deal reader is worth across the series, and how many deal copies match one full-price reader. |
| Per book table | Each book's price, royalty per copy, and readers. Worth from this book on is what a reader who starts at that book brings in, which helps if you advertise book 2 or 3. |

<Example title="A three-book series at $4.99">

Book 1 sold 100 copies, book 2 sold 55, and book 3 sold 44. At $4.99 with a 2 MB file, each sale pays $3.28.

The series earned 199 × $3.28 = $652.72. Split over 100 book 1 readers, each one is worth $6.53. That's $3.28 from book 1, $1.81 from book 2, and $1.44 from book 3.

Book 1 sells for $4.99, so ads break even at about 130.8% ACoS ($6.53 ÷ $4.99). On book 1 alone, they'd break even at 65.7%.

</Example>
