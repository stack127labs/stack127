## How do you calculate ARPU and ARPA?

Both divide revenue by a count. They differ in which revenue and which count.

<Formula title="ARPU and ARPA">

ARPU = net revenue in the period ÷ active users in the period

ARPA = MRR at month end ÷ paying accounts at month end

</Formula>

[Stripe's ARPU guide](https://stripe.com/resources/more/what-is-average-revenue-per-user-why-it-matters-and-how-to-calculate-it) leaves the period, the revenue, and the meaning of "active" up to you. [ChartMogul's ARPA report](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/209-chart-average-revenue-per-account) divides MRR by paying subscribers and counts a customer with several subscriptions once.

## What's the difference between ARPU and ARPA?

ARPU counts people. ARPA counts paying accounts. One team account with 10 users paying $500 a month is one account at $500 of ARPA. Counted as users, that's $50 each.

ARPU also counts people who don't pay, which pulls it down for apps with a free tier. ARPA counts only accounts that pay.

## Can you divide quarterly ARPU by three to get monthly ARPU?

Not exactly. Dividing by three still uses everyone active across the three months, which is at least as many as in any one month. So it usually comes out lower than a true monthly ARPU. Count active users for one month instead.

## What does ARPA tell you next?

ARPA is the starting point for most per-customer SaaS math.

- The [customer lifetime value calculator](/customer-lifetime-value-calculator) turns ARPA, margin, and churn into gross profit per customer.
