## How is net revenue retention calculated?

Take the opening group's MRR, add what they gained, subtract what they lost, and divide by where they started.

<Formula title="Net revenue retention">

NRR = (opening MRR + expansion + reactivation - contraction - churned) ÷ opening MRR × 100

</Formula>

[ChartMogul's NRR report](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/143-chart-net-mrr-retention) uses this formula and leaves new customers out. Not every tool counts reactivation: [Baremetrics' formula](https://baremetrics.com/blog/what-is-net-revenue-retention) leaves it out, and ChartMogul has a setting to drop it. Pick one rule and keep it from month to month.

## What does NRR above or below 100% mean?

At 100%, your existing customers pay exactly what they paid at the start of the month. Above 100%, their upgrades outweigh their downgrades and cancellations, so revenue grows even with no new sales. Below 100%, they pay less than before.

NRR measures revenue, not people. You can lose customers and still be above 100%, as the example shows. To count the customers who left, use the [customer churn calculator](/customer-churn-calculator).

## How is NRR different from gross revenue retention?

Gross revenue retention (GRR) counts only the losses. It leaves out expansion and reactivation, so it can't go above 100%. NRR adds the gains back.

This calculator doesn't show GRR. The [revenue churn calculator](/revenue-churn-calculator) shows the share of starting MRR lost, and compares churn and retention measures side by side.

## Why doesn't my NRR match my billing tool?

Usually the starting group or a rule differs. Check these:

- Who was in the opening group. ChartMogul counts past-due subscriptions as well as active ones.
- Whether reactivation is counted.
- Whether every figure covers the same month and currency.

For the whole company's MRR, new customers included, use the [MRR waterfall calculator](/mrr-waterfall-calculator).
