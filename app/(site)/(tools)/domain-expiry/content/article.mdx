## My domain expires soon. What should I check?

Check your registrar account, not just this page. A public record can't see your payment or settings.

1. Confirm the renewal date and that auto-renew is on.
2. Check that the saved card hasn't expired.
3. Make sure renewal emails reach an address you read. Registrars must send reminders for generic endings, according to [ICANN's renewal FAQ](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/domain-name-renewal-expiration-faqs-2018-12-07-en).
4. If you renewed and the date here hasn't moved, ask the registrar to confirm.

A site that still loads isn't proof the renewal went through.

## Why do the registry and registrar dates differ?

The registry runs the ending and the registrar manages your account, and each can publish its own date. They drift apart when the registry renews the name automatically at the end of a term, before the registrar has decided to renew or delete it. The registry date is the one that decides when the domain is deleted.

## What happens after a domain expires?

The domain moves through stages, shown under the result with their usual length. What you can do changes at each one.

| Stage | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Grace period | Renew with your registrar. Its terms set the price. The site and email may stop working. |
| Redemption | Ask your registrar to restore it. Expect a higher fee. |
| Pending delete | Nothing. It can't be restored and can't be registered yet. |
| Released | Anyone can register it, first come, first served. |

These stages are for generic endings such as `.com`. Country endings set their own rules, so check the registry for that ending in the [TLD directory](/tld-directory). The [EPP status codes](/epp-status-codes) page explains each status.

## When can I register someone else's expired domain?

No public record gives a guaranteed date. The owner can renew or restore the name until pending delete, and once it's released, someone else may register it seconds before you. Use the [domain availability checker](/domain-availability) to see whether a registration still exists.

The lookup goes from stack127 to the registry and the registrar, not to the website. A registered result can be a saved answer up to 24 hours old, so a renewal you just paid may not show yet. This is a one-time check, not monitoring. For the rest of the record, use the [domain registration lookup](/domain-lookup).
