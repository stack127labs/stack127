## What is RPM?

RPM means revenue per mille: what you earn for every 1,000 pageviews (page RPM) or sessions (session RPM). It adds up every ad on the page. [Google defines it](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/190515) as estimated earnings divided by pageviews, times 1,000.

<Formula title="Page RPM">

RPM = ad revenue ÷ pageviews × 1,000

</Formula>

## What's the difference between CPM and RPM?

CPM is what an advertiser pays for 1,000 views of one ad. RPM is what you earn for 1,000 pageviews across every ad. When a page loads more than one ad, your RPM is higher than your CPM.

| | CPM | RPM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counted per | 1,000 ad impressions | 1,000 pageviews or sessions |
| Tells you | The price of one ad slot | What your traffic earns |

## Page RPM or session RPM?

Use whichever your network reports. A session usually includes more than one pageview, so session RPM runs higher than page RPM for the same site. [Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/why-does-my-rpm-change/) points out the two can't be compared directly, so compare page RPM with page RPM.

## Is my dashboard RPM before or after the network's cut?

After, for most networks. [AdSense reports your share](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195) once Google takes its part, so its RPM is your share, not the advertiser's price.

## What's a good RPM?

It depends on your niche, where your readers live, and your ad network. We don't show a typical range, because published figures mix page and session RPM and measure different sites. Your own RPM over a few months is the best benchmark.

## How is the answer worked out?

Revenue is traffic × RPM ÷ 1,000. RPM from CPM is CPM × ad impressions per pageview or session. RPM from revenue is revenue ÷ traffic × 1,000. Yearly figures use 12 months at the same traffic and RPM, and every result is rounded to the cent only at the end. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
