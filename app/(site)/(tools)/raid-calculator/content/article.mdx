## How much usable space does each RAID level give?

With equal drives, usable space is a whole number of drives. N is the number of drives, S is the size of the smallest one, and G is the number of groups.

| Level | Min. drives | Usable space | Drives that can fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RAID 0 | 2 | N × S | None |
| RAID 1 | 2 | S | N - 1 |
| RAID 5 | 3 | (N - 1) × S | 1 |
| RAID 6 | 4 | (N - 2) × S | 2 |
| RAID 10 | 4, even | N ÷ 2 × S | 1, or one per pair |
| RAID 50 | 6 | (N - G) × S | 1, or one per group |
| RAID 60 | 8 | (N - 2G) × S | 2, or two per group |
| Linear or JBOD | 1 | All drives added up | None |

## What is the RAID write penalty?

It's the number of disk operations one small write causes. To change one small block in RAID 5, the array reads the old data and parity, then writes both.

| Level | Disk operations per small write |
| --- | --- |
| RAID 0, Linear, JBOD | 1 |
| RAID 1 | 1 per drive |
| RAID 10 | 2 |
| RAID 5 and RAID 50 | 4 |
| RAID 6 and RAID 60 | 6 |

It explains why parity layouts are slower at small random writes. It isn't a speed measurement, because caches and full-stripe writes change real performance. [Dell's RAID reference](https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-us/unity-880f/unity_p_pool_config/drive-iops-by-raid-type?guid=guid-b32204b2-a11b-4f25-b086-e8c135df04f1&lang=en-us) lists the same counts.

## Can you mix drive sizes in RAID?

Yes, but every drive in a group gives only as much space as the smallest drive in that group. A mirror of a 4 TB and an 8 TB drive holds 4 TB. Linear and JBOD are the exception: they join drives end to end, so every drive counts in full, with no redundancy.

If you want mixed drives with redundancy and little waste, look at Synology SHR in the [SHR calculator](/shr-calculator) or Unraid in the [Unraid calculator](/unraid-calculator). Linux md RAID 0 can also use part of the extra space on larger drives. This calculator doesn't model that.

## Does drive order matter in RAID 10?

Yes. Each mirror pair is limited by its smaller drive, so pairing drives of the same size keeps the most space. The same four drives give different results:

| Order entered | Mirror pairs | Usable | Size mismatch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4, 4, 8, 8 TB | 4 + 4 and 8 + 8 | 10.91 TiB (12 TB) | 0 |
| 4, 8, 4, 8 TB | 4 + 8 and 4 + 8 | 7.28 TiB (8 TB) | 7.28 TiB |

RAID 50 and RAID 60 work the same way inside each group.

## How many drives can fail in RAID 10, 50, or 60?

Any one drive can fail in RAID 10 and RAID 50, and any two in RAID 60. More can fail only if they land in different groups.

An eight-drive RAID 10 survives four failed drives if each comes from a different mirror pair. It doesn't survive losing both drives of one pair. In RAID 50 and 60, losing one whole group loses the array.

## Why does my array show less space than the calculator?

Drives are sold in TB, but most computers count in TiB. A TB is 1,000,000,000,000 bytes and a TiB is 1,099,511,627,776 bytes, so a 4 TB drive is 3.64 TiB. Windows shows TiB values but labels them TB, as [Seagate explains](https://www.seagate.com/support/kb/why-does-my-hard-drive-report-less-capacity-than-indicated-on-the-drives-label-172191en/). macOS counts in TB, like the label, according to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102119).

The result is also the size of the array before anything is on it. Partitions, the filesystem, reserved space, and snapshots take more. A NAS also keeps a system partition on every drive.

## Should you use RAID 5 or RAID 6?

The difference shows when a drive fails. RAID 5 has no protection left while it rebuilds, so a second failure loses the array. RAID 6 can still lose one more drive.

A rebuild reads every remaining drive, so larger drives take longer to rebuild. The [rebuild calculator](/rebuild-calculator) estimates how long your array would stay exposed.

## Is RAID a backup?

No. RAID keeps the array running when a drive fails. It doesn't protect you from deleted files, ransomware, theft, fire, or a fault that corrupts the whole array. Keep a separate copy and test that it restores. The [3-2-1 backup calculator](/backup-321-calculator) helps you size one.

## What should you check before building the array?

Check that your controller or operating system supports the layout. Some vendors use JBOD to mean separate disks, not one combined volume. ZFS RAIDZ uses different math, so use the [ZFS capacity calculator](/zfs-capacity-calculator) for a ZFS pool, or the [storage layout advisor](/storage-layout-advisor) to compare layouts for your drives and the failures you need to survive.
