## Which transfer locks can you remove?

Only the one your registrar sets. The others need the registrar's help or time. The [EPP status codes](/epp-status-codes) page explains each code.

| Status | Who can clear it |
| --- | --- |
| `clientTransferProhibited` | You, usually with a "transfer lock" switch in your registrar account. |
| `serverTransferProhibited` | Only the registry. Ask your registrar why it's there. |
| `pendingTransfer` | Nobody. Another transfer is already running, so wait for it to finish or cancel it with your registrar. |
| `redemptionPeriod` or `pendingDelete` | The domain must be restored first, if restoring is still possible. |

## Why can't I transfer a domain I registered or moved recently?

Under ICANN's [Transfer Policy](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/accredited-registrars/transfer-policy-01-06-2016-en), the current registrar may refuse a transfer within 60 days of the registration or of the last transfer. The checker treats those 60 days as a block, so ask your registrar if you need an exception.

Changing the registrant's name, organization, or email can also start a 60-day lock on generic endings. Registrars may let you opt out, but only before you make the change. Ask before editing contact details to prepare a transfer.

The [ICANN Board adopted](https://www.icann.org/en/board-activities-and-meetings/materials/approved-resolutions-regular-meeting-of-the-icann-board-07-06-2026-en) shorter 30-day locks on 7 June 2026, and removed the registrant change lock. They don't apply until ICANN publishes a date, so the 60-day rules still hold.

## How do I move a domain without the site or email going down?

Sort out DNS before you start, because a transfer moves the registration, not your DNS, website, or email.

1. Find out where your DNS is hosted. If it's at the old registrar, copy every record to the new DNS host and switch the nameservers before the transfer.
2. If DNSSEC is on, set up signing at the new DNS host and update the DS record, or switch DNSSEC off first. A broken chain stops the domain from resolving for many visitors. The [DNSSEC checker](/dnssec-checker) tests the chain.
3. Remove the registrar transfer lock and get the authorization code from the old registrar.
4. Start the transfer at the new registrar and approve any confirmation email.

Never paste the authorization code into a lookup tool or a public message. It's the key to the transfer.

## Do country-code domains follow the same rules?

No. ICANN's Transfer Policy only covers generic endings such as `.com`. Country endings such as `.uk` or `.de` have their own steps, codes, and waiting periods, and the result says when the 60-day checks don't apply. Find the registry's rules through the [TLD directory](/tld-directory).

The lookup goes from stack127 to the registry and the registrar. A registered result can be a saved answer up to 24 hours old, so a lock you just removed may still show. To find your registrar's details, use the [registrar lookup](/domain-registrar).
