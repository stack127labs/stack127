## How is free trial conversion rate calculated?

Divide the accounts that paid by the accounts that started a trial in the same cohort, then multiply by 100.

<Formula title="Trial to paid conversion rate">

Conversion rate = paid accounts from the cohort ÷ trial starts in the cohort × 100

</Formula>

40 paid out of 500 trial starts is 8%. [ChartMogul's trial-to-paid metric](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/219-chart-trial-to-paid-conversion-rate) works the same way: it follows people who started a trial in one period and counts the ones who later paid.

The common mistake is dividing one month's new customers by the same month's trial starts. Those are different people.

<Example title="100 trial starts in August">

20 of the 100 paid within 30 days, some of them in September. That cohort's rate is 20%.

August's billing report shows only 6 of those first payments. Dividing 6 by 100 gives 6%, which mixes up the month someone paid with the month they started.

</Example>

## What counts as a paid conversion?

An account counts once its first payment goes through.

- A card on file, a pending invoice, or a failed first charge isn't a payment.
- Count each account once, in both the starts and the paid count. [Recurly counts trial starts per subscription](https://recurly.com/resources/report/state-of-subscriptions-acquisition/), so one account with two trial subscriptions counts twice there.
- A first payment isn't a kept customer. Someone who pays once and cancels still counts as converted.

## How long should you wait before you count?

Wait until every account in the cohort has had its full follow-up window. Before then, some people who will pay haven't yet, so the rate is low.

There's no standard window. [ChartMogul notes](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/219-chart-trial-to-paid-conversion-rate) that a cohort's rate can still change after the signup month, as people pay later. Its 2026 survey counts conversions within six months. Pick one window that fits how long your customers take to decide, and use it for every cohort you compare.

The calculator marks a cohort `Provisional` until its age reaches the window. Age counts from the cohort's last start, so the newest account has had the full window too.

## What is a good free trial conversion rate?

There's no single good rate. It depends on whether people enter payment details to start, how long you wait, and who you sell to. Published figures also count conversions in different ways, so compare yours only with a figure that measures the same thing.

| Source | No card | Card upfront | Free plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ChartMogul survey, 2026 | Median 4 to 6%, top quarter 10 to 15% | Median 25 to 35%, top quarter 50 to 60% | Median 3 to 5%, top quarter 8 to 12% |
| First Page Sage, 2022 to 2025 | Average 18.2% | Average 48.8% | Average 2.6% |
| Recurly, 2022 | Average 12.0% | Average 39.0% | Not reported |
| ChartMogul help center | Median 3%, trial type not stated | | |

Each source measured something different:

- [ChartMogul's SaaS Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/) surveyed 200 software products in January 2026, typically $1 million to $10 million ARR. It counts paid within six months, as each respondent estimated it.
- [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-benchmarks/), updated September 5, 2025, averages 86 SaaS companies from 2022 to 2025. The figures above are for organic traffic. One paid month counts, and there's no fixed window.
- [Recurly](https://recurly.com/resources/report/state-of-subscriptions-acquisition/) averages 803 subscription sites from January to August 2022, including consumer services. It counts 22 million trial starts per subscription, not per account.
- [ChartMogul's help center](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/219-chart-trial-to-paid-conversion-rate) gives a 3% median for SaaS companies, with no sample size or window.

The calculator shows only the ChartMogul ranges. They're the only figures here split by trial type with a fixed window. It shows them when your follow-up window is six months, or when you haven't set one. A shorter or longer window counts something different, so it gets no range. Even so, they're survey estimates, and the report doesn't say how many products fall in each trial type.

<Note title="Small cohorts">

A rate from a few dozen trials can move a lot with one or two more payments. Check your likely range before comparing. If it covers the published median, your cohort is too small to tell the difference yet.

</Note>

## Why does a small cohort's rate swing so much?

With few trials, chance plays a big part. 30 paid out of 42 is 71.43%, but the likely range runs from 56.43% to 82.84%.

The likely range is a 95% [Wilson score interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_proportion_confidence_interval#Wilson_score_interval). It's the range of true rates that could easily have produced your counts. Four times as many trials make it about half as wide.

## Does requiring a card upfront improve conversion?

It raises the share of trial starts who pay, but fewer people start a trial. To compare the two, count paying customers per visitor, not just per trial.

First Page Sage's averages show both effects:

| Trial type, organic traffic | Visitor to trial | Trial to paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No card | 8.5% | 18.2% |
| Card upfront | 2.5% | 48.8% |

Multiplied out, both land between 1 and 2 paying customers per 100 visitors. These averages come from different companies, so treat them as a rough picture, not a forecast for yours.

## Is new MRR from trials the same as revenue?

No. New MRR is the monthly recurring revenue these paid accounts added: each monthly plan at its price, and each annual plan at a twelfth of its yearly price.

The ARR run rate is new MRR × 12, the same Annual Run Rate formula as [ChartMogul's glossary](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-go-to-market-report/). It's a pace, not revenue earned this year. It doesn't include churn, refunds, upgrades, or one-time fees.
