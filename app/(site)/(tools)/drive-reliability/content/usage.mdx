## How to read the result

Observed AFR is the share of drives that failed per year of running time. The approximate range next to it shows how sure that number is. A narrow range comes from many failures over a long time. A wide one means a few more or fewer failures would change the rate a lot.

Each row also carries an evidence label. A thin cohort has fewer than 500 drives or at most one failure, so read its rate with care. A model below Backblaze's reporting minimum for the period shows as below the source threshold.

Compare models within one period. Quarterly, annual, and lifetime figures use different minimums. The current snapshot has only a fleet summary for Annual 2025, with no model rows.

<Example title="A 22 TB and a 16 TB model in Q1 2026">

WUH722222ALE6L4: 42 failures over 3,992,942 drive days gives an AFR of 0.38%, with a range of 0.27% to 0.50%.

ST16000NM000J: 1 failure over 10,117 drive days gives 3.61%, with a range of 0.00% to 10.68%. The higher rate rests on one failure, so the two can't be ranked with confidence.

</Example>
