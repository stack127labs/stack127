## What to enter

For RAID 10, 50, and 60, enter the drives in the order you'll group them. In RAID 10, drives 1 and 2 form the first mirror pair, drives 3 and 4 the second, and so on. RAID 50 and 60 fill each group the same way, so put drives of the same size next to each other.

The address bar keeps your drive list, so you can bookmark or share the result.

## How to read the result

Theoretical usable capacity is the space the array gives you before a filesystem goes on it. It shows in TiB. Switch to TB to compare it with drive labels.

The bar splits the raw space of all drives into usable space, redundancy, and size mismatch. Redundancy is the mirror copies or parity that let the array survive a failed drive. Size mismatch is space above the smallest drive in a group, which the array can't use.

Failure tolerance first says how many drives can fail, whichever ones they are. For RAID 10, 50, and 60, it then gives a higher number that holds only when the failed drives sit in different groups.

<Example title="Four drives of 4, 4, 8, and 8 TB in RAID 5">

Usable capacity is 10.91 TiB, which is 12 TB. Redundancy takes 3.64 TiB. Size mismatch takes 7.28 TiB, because RAID 5 uses only 4 TB of each 8 TB drive. Any one drive can fail.
