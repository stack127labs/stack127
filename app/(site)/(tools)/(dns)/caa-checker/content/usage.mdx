## How to read the result

The result names the certificate authorities allowed for normal and for wildcard certificates. Each one appears as the domain it uses in CAA, such as `letsencrypt.org`.

| You see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Names, then `may issue.` | Only those certificate authorities may issue. |
| `CAA does not restrict this kind of certificate.` | CAA lets any certificate authority issue. |
| `No certificate authority is allowed.` | The policy blocks every certificate authority. |
| `Policy inherited from` a name | The records sit on a parent domain, not on the name you entered. |

`Where the policy came from` lists each name the check asked, in order.

<Example title="api.github.com">

`api.github.com` has no CAA records of its own, so the check moves up to `github.com` and finds 7. For normal certificates, `digicert.com`, `globalsign.com`, `letsencrypt.org`, and `sectigo.com` may issue. Wildcard certificates leave out `globalsign.com`, because the `issuewild` records don't list it.

Checked on 9 October 2026.

</Example>
