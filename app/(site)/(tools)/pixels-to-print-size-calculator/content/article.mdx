## How do you convert pixels to inches?

Divide the pixels by the PPI. For metric, multiply the inches by 2.54 for centimeters or 25.4 for millimeters. One inch is exactly 2.54 cm, as [NIST defines](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b8).

<Formula title="Print size">

`inches = pixels ÷ PPI`

`centimeters = inches × 2.54`

</Formula>

[Adobe's guide to print resolution](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/crop-resize-transform/resize-adjust-resolution/printed-image-resolution.html) explains the same relationship.

## How many pixels do I need for a full page?

Multiply the page size by 300. For common KDP trim sizes:

| Trim size | Pixels at 300 PPI | With interior bleed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 × 8 in | 1500 × 2400 | 1538 × 2475 |
| 5.5 × 8.5 in | 1650 × 2550 | 1688 × 2625 |
| 6 × 9 in | 1800 × 2700 | 1838 × 2775 |
| 8.5 × 11 in | 2550 × 3300 | 2588 × 3375 |

Bleed adds 0.125 in to the width and 0.25 in to the height. For other trims and for full covers, use the [KDP book setup calculator](/kdp-book-setup-calculator).

## Is PPI the same as DPI?

Not quite. PPI is how many image pixels fill an inch of paper. DPI is how many ink dots a printer puts down per inch. Print guides, KDP included, often say DPI when they mean image PPI, so treat a "300 DPI" request as 300 PPI here.

## What PPI should I use?

Use the number your printer or publisher gives you. KDP asks for at least 300 for images in print books, in its [formatting fixes guide](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834260). Large prints seen from farther away can use less, as [Adobe explains](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/crop-resize-transform/resize-adjust-resolution/resolution-specs-for-printing-images.html).

## Does changing the PPI change my image?

In this calculator, changing PPI changes only the calculated print size. In an image editor, turn off Resample to change PPI without changing the pixels. See [Adobe's guide to print size and resolution](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/crop-resize-transform/resize-adjust-resolution/change-print-dimensions-and-resolution.html).

## What can't this calculator tell you?

It works from the numbers you type and doesn't open the image. It can't tell you whether the image will look sharp. To check a real file at the size it fills in a KDP book, use the [KDP image DPI checker](/kdp-image-dpi-checker).
