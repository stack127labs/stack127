## How does KDP set the minimum price for a print book?

The minimum is the lowest price where your share of the list price covers printing. KDP divides the printing cost by the royalty rate that applies at that price.

<Formula title="Minimum print list price">

`printing cost ÷ royalty rate at that price`

</Formula>

On Amazon.com the rate is 50% up to $9.98 and 60% from $9.99. The [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator) explains how printing cost and royalty work. KDP's rules are on its [paperback printing cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340) and [hardcover printing cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHT976ZKSKUXBB6H) pages.

| Pages | Paperback cost | Paperback minimum | Hardcover cost | Hardcover minimum |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | $2.30 | $4.60 | $6.80 | $11.34 |
| 200 | $3.40 | $6.80 | $8.05 | $13.42 |
| 300 | $4.60 | $9.20 | $9.25 | $15.42 |
| 400 | $5.80 | $9.99 | $10.45 | $17.42 |
| 500 | $7.00 | $11.67 | $11.65 | $19.42 |

All rows are 6 × 9 in, black ink, white paper, Amazon.com. Hardcovers start at 75 pages.

## Why is my minimum price $9.99?

Because no price below $9.99 covers your printing cost. It happens when the printing cost is between $5.00 and $5.99.

Take the 400-page paperback above. Its $5.80 cost ÷ 60% gives $9.67. But $9.67 is in the 50% band, and 50% of any price up to $9.98 is less than $5.80. The first price that covers it is $9.99, where you earn $0.19. Dividing by 60% alone gives a price KDP won't accept.

## What's the lowest price for a Kindle eBook?

It depends on the royalty option, and at 35% on file size. These are KDP's [eBook price ranges](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560) for Amazon.com.

| Royalty option | File size | Lowest price | Highest price |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 35% | Under 3 MB | $0.99 | $200.00 |
| 35% | 3 MB to under 10 MB | $1.99 | $200.00 |
| 35% | 10 MB or more | $2.99 | $200.00 |
| 70% | Any size | $2.99 | $12.99 |

KDP raised the 70% top price from $9.99 to $12.99 on July 7, 2026. Its help page doesn't say exactly how it counts a megabyte at the 3 MB and 10 MB lines. If your file is close to one, check the price in KDP.

At 70%, KDP also takes a delivery cost of $0.15 per MB before paying you. See KDP's [eBook royalty rules](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634500).

## Why does a large eBook show a negative royalty at 70%?

Because the delivery cost can be higher than the price. A 20 MB file costs $3.00 to deliver, so at $2.99 the formula gives -$0.01. Enter a goal of `0` and the calculator returns $3.00 instead.

This isn't a prediction that KDP will charge you. It means 70% earns nothing at that price. A large file at a low price may earn more at 35%. Compare both in the [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator).

## What doesn't this calculator check?

<Note title="About this estimate">

It covers Amazon.com sales in US dollars through standard KDP distribution, using KDP rules checked on September 28, 2026. It doesn't cover other marketplaces, Expanded Distribution, taxes, price matching, or promotions. It can't confirm that your eBook qualifies for 70%. KDP pays 35% for books that are mostly public domain content, and for eBooks priced less than 20% below your print edition on Amazon.

</Note>

Check your price in KDP's pricing screen before you publish. For print, KDP's own [Printing Costs and Royalty Calculator](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/royalty-calculator) is a good cross-check. To compare a paperback with a hardcover at one price, use the [KDP price and profit calculator](/kdp-price-profit-calculator).
