## What to enter

- Word count. The body text only. Front and back matter have their own boxes.
- Font. Serif, sans, and monospaced are width classes, not specific fonts. Pick the one closest to your body text.
- Margins. Set the margins you plan to use. If the inside margin is smaller than KDP's minimum for the estimated page count, you get a warning, but your margin isn't changed.
- Chapter starts. With a positive word count, the estimator adds one page per chapter for space lost around chapter breaks. Right-hand chapter starts can add more.
- Image pages. Pages that hold only an image.
- Front and back matter. Pages like the title page, copyright, contents, acknowledgments, and about the author.

## How to read the result

The main result is a page range, with each end rounded up to an even number, the same way KDP counts print pages. Below it you'll see how many pages come from body text and how many you added.

The typography model shows the words per page, text area, characters per line, and lines per page behind the estimate. The KDP guidance list shows the minimum inside margin for every page band the range touches.

<Example title="A 50,000-word novel at 6 × 9 in">

Serif at 11 pt with 1.15 line spacing, a 0.5 in inside margin, 0.375 in outside, and 0.75 in top and bottom. That leaves a 5.125 × 7.5 in text area with 67 characters per line and 42 lines, or 450 to 676 words per page. The body text needs 74 to 112 pages. Adding 20 chapter starts, 4 front matter pages, and 2 back matter pages gives 100 to 138 pages. This is the current model's output, not a calibrated prediction for a typeset novel. It assumes dense text and can underestimate the final count.
