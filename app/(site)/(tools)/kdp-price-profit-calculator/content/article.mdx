## How does trim size change printing cost?

A large trim costs more per page. KDP counts a book as large trim if it's wider than 6.12 in or taller than 9 in. For a black ink paperback on Amazon.com, that's $0.017 a page instead of $0.012. See KDP's [paperback printing cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340) page.

But a bigger page also means fewer pages. Here's the same book at $14.99:

| Option | Pages | Printing cost | Royalty per sale |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6 × 9 in | 300 | $4.60 | $4.39 |
| 7 × 10 in, same page count | 300 | $6.10 | $2.89 |
| 7 × 10 in, if it fits in 240 pages | 240 | $5.08 | $3.91 |

Entering the same page count for both trims makes the larger trim look worse than it is. Use each layout's real count. KDP sets the final count from the file you upload. See its [paperback submission guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950).

## Is a hardcover worth it?

A hardcover costs more to print, so it needs a higher price to match the paperback's royalty. A 300-page 6 × 9 in hardcover costs $9.25 to print, against $4.60 for the paperback. Its minimum price is $15.42, so at $14.99 it can't be sold at all.

To earn the paperback's $7.39 at $19.99, the hardcover needs $27.73. You can find a price like that with the royalty goal in the [KDP minimum list price calculator](/kdp-minimum-list-price-calculator). Whether readers will pay it is something no calculator can tell you.

For how the royalty itself is worked out, see the [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator).

## What does estimated profit include?

Only what you enter.

<Formula title="Estimated profit per option">

`planned copies × (royalty per sale - other cost per sale) - one-time cost`

</Formula>

Profit can be negative. In the example above, a $250.00 one-time cost for the hardcover would make its profit -$76.00.

<Note title="What this number can't tell you">

It doesn't predict sales. It leaves out costs you don't enter, taxes, and refunds. Each option is a different edition of the same book, so don't add their profits together.

</Note>

## What doesn't this calculator cover?

It covers paperback and hardcover sales on Amazon.com in US dollars, using KDP rules checked on September 28, 2026. It doesn't cover:

- Kindle eBooks. Use the [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator).
- Expanded Distribution, which pays 40% rather than the standard Amazon rate of 50% or 60%, and has a different paperback minimum price.
- Other marketplaces, price matching, or promotions.

Before you set a price, check the finished edition in KDP's [Printing Costs and Royalty Calculator](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/royalty-calculator) and pricing screen.
