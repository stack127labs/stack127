## How do you calculate SaaS gross margin?

Subtract cost of revenue from revenue to get gross profit. Divide gross profit by revenue to get gross margin.

<Formula title="Gross margin">

Gross profit = revenue - cost of revenue

Gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue × 100

</Formula>

[Atlassian's quarterly filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000011/team-20251231.htm) uses the same definitions. Gross margin comes before sales, marketing, research, and admin costs, so it isn't your profit or your cash flow.

## What goes into cost of revenue?

Cost of revenue is what it takes to deliver the product to paying customers. Sales, marketing, and building new things stay out.

| Cost | In cost of revenue? |
| --- | --- |
| Hosting, servers, storage, and bandwidth | Yes |
| Outside APIs and software used to deliver the service | Yes |
| Customer support pay and tools | Yes |
| Payment processing fees | Often. Atlassian includes them. |
| Delivery work for professional services, when that revenue is included | Yes |
| Maintaining the existing product | Often. Stripe includes it. |
| Building new features | No |
| Sales, marketing, and advertising | No |
| Executive pay and general admin | No |

[Stripe's gross margin guide](https://stripe.com/resources/more/saas-gross-margin-explained-what-it-is-and-why-it-is-important) and Atlassian's filing draw these lines. Companies don't all draw them the same way, so pick a rule and keep it each period.

## What is a good SaaS gross margin?

Stripe's guide calls a gross margin above 75% typically good for a SaaS business, and says below 70% can concern investors. Top performers reach 80% or more. Treat those as rules of thumb, not targets.

Your own trend is more useful. Compare periods only when they use the same scope and cost rules. Some companies also publish an adjusted margin that leaves out costs such as stock-based pay, so check which one a figure is.
