## How is burn multiple calculated?

Divide net cash burn by net new ARR for the same period.

<Formula title="Burn multiple">

Burn multiple = net cash burn ÷ net new ARR

</Formula>

David Sacks of Craft Ventures [introduced the burn multiple](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/the-burn-multiple) to ask how much a startup burns to add each dollar of ARR. His example: a startup burned $2 million in a quarter while ARR grew $1 million, a burn multiple of 2.

## What is a good burn multiple?

Lower is better. These bands are the common rules of thumb for venture-backed startups.

| Burn multiple | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| Under 1 | Exceptional |
| 1 to 1.5 | Great |
| 1.5 to 2 | Good |
| 2 to 3 | Worth looking into |
| Above 3 | A warning sign |

[ChartMogul's burn multiple guide](https://chartmogul.com/saas-metrics/burn-multiple/) lists these bands. Craft Ventures calls its version rules of thumb for venture-stage startups, not targets for every business.

## Why does my burn multiple jump around?

Small ARR changes make big multiples. $12,000 of burn against $1,000 of net new ARR is 12×, even if the business is fine. A large deal, a lost customer, or a big payment near the edge of a period can swing it too. Longer periods smooth this out.

Burn multiple also doesn't show that the spending caused the growth. It only compares the two numbers.

## Where do the inputs come from?

Net new ARR is usually the change in run-rate ARR, which counts monthly plans too. The [ARR calculator](/arr-calculator) explains the two meanings of ARR. To see which gains and losses made up the change, use the [MRR waterfall calculator](/mrr-waterfall-calculator).
