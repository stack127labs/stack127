## How do you calculate MRR?

Divide each plan's price by the months in its billing term, multiply by its active subscriptions, and add up the plans.

<Formula title="MRR">

MRR = sum of (active subscriptions × price per term ÷ months in term)

Months in term: monthly 1, quarterly 3, every six months 6, annually 12

</Formula>

[ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/saas-metrics/mrr/) and [Chargebee](https://www.chargebee.com/docs/billing/2.0/reports-and-analytics/monthly-recurring-revenue) both spread a longer plan across its months, so a $1,200 yearly plan adds $100 to MRR. Counting the whole $1,200 in the month it's paid is the mistake ChartMogul warns about.

## What counts toward MRR?

Recurring subscription charges count. One-time charges don't.

| Charge | In MRR? |
| --- | --- |
| Plan price after recurring discounts | Yes |
| Recurring add-ons | Yes. Add them to the price, or give them their own row if only some subscriptions have them. |
| Setup fees and other one-time charges | No |
| Tax | No |
| Free trials | No, until they start paying |
| Cancelled or expired subscriptions | No |
| Past-due subscriptions, and ones set to cancel at the end of the term | Your call. Billing tools differ. |

[ChartMogul's MRR report](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/154-chart-monthly-recurring-revenue-mrr) counts past-due subscriptions. [Chargebee](https://www.chargebee.com/docs/billing/2.0/reports-and-analytics/monthly-recurring-revenue) counts subscriptions set not to renew, and keeps ones with a failed payment until its retries end. Pick one rule and use it every month, so your months compare. A different rule is also the usual reason a total here doesn't match your billing dashboard.

## Is MRR the same as cash or revenue?

No. MRR is a monthly rate, not money received or revenue in your books. In the example, the Pro yearly customers pay $5,700 once a year but add $475.00 to MRR every month. [Chargebee's MRR guide](https://www.chargebee.com/docs/billing/2.0/reports-and-analytics/monthly-recurring-revenue) draws the same line between MRR, payments received, and accounting revenue.

## What's the difference between MRR and ARR?

ARR on this page is MRR × 12, the annualized run rate. It shows a year at today's rate. It isn't a forecast or money you're owed.

ARR can also mean annual recurring revenue, which [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/saas-metrics/arr/) says counts only contracts of 12 months or longer. Check which one a report means before you compare.

## What doesn't MRR show?

MRR is a snapshot of today. It doesn't show how it changed, who left, or what an average account pays.

- The [ARPU and ARPA calculator](/arpu-arpa-calculator) divides MRR by your paying accounts.
- The [SaaS metrics calculator](/saas-metrics-calculator) shows one month's growth and retention.
