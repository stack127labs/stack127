## How is revenue churn calculated?

Add the MRR lost to cancellations and downgrades, then divide by MRR at the start of the month.

<Formula title="Gross revenue churn">

Gross revenue churn = (canceled MRR + reduced MRR) ÷ opening MRR × 100

</Formula>

[ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/saas-metrics/revenue-churn/) and [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/revenue-churn-101-how-to-calculate-it-and-why-it-matters) use the same formula.

## What's the difference between gross and net revenue churn?

Gross revenue churn counts only what you lost. Net revenue churn subtracts expansion and reactivation from those losses, so it can drop below zero. That's called negative churn: existing customers add more than they take away.

This calculator shows gross churn. Net revenue churn is 100% minus net revenue retention, which the [net revenue retention calculator](/net-revenue-retention-calculator) works out.

## How do churn and retention measures compare?

All of them follow the customers you had at the start of the month. They differ in what they count and whether gains offset losses.

| Measure | What it asks | Counts upgrades? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer churn | What share of paying customers left? | No |
| Gross revenue churn | What share of starting MRR was lost? | No |
| Gross revenue retention | What share of starting MRR is still paid? | No |
| Net revenue churn | What share was lost, after upgrades? | Yes |
| Net revenue retention | What share is still paid, after upgrades? | Yes |

The ones that count upgrades can pass the usual limits: net revenue churn can go below 0%, and net revenue retention above 100%. Gross revenue retention is 100% minus gross revenue churn. Net revenue retention is 100% minus net revenue churn.

## Why is revenue churn different from customer churn?

Customer churn counts accounts. Revenue churn counts dollars. The two split whenever the customers who leave pay more or less than average.

Say you have 10 customers and $1,000 of MRR. One customer paying $50 leaves. Customer churn is 10%, but revenue churn is 5%. If the one who left paid $300, revenue churn would be 30%.
