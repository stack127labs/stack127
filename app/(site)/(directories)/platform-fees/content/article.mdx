## How do I read a fee card?

The big number is what the platform keeps from a sale, and the bar shows what you keep before other fees. Those other fees are usually card processing, a fixed charge per sale, or a monthly plan. The three lines under the bar name the ones that matter most for that platform.

Open the link at the bottom of a card, such as "+ 3 more rates" or "All rates and sources", to see every rate, when it applies, the date we checked it, and its sources.

### Why do some cards have no bar?

Because the platform doesn't take a simple cut of your price. Subscription sites that split a shared pool by downloads, ad programs, and fixed pay per item show their own terms instead. These labels stand in for a rate:

<Terms>
<Term name="Share not disclosed">The platform pays creators a share but doesn't publish it.</Term>
<Term name="Fee not disclosed">The platform charges a fee but doesn't publish the rate.</Term>
<Term name="Set by agreement">The rate is in a contract that isn't public, as with console stores.</Term>
<Term name="Shown in dashboard">The platform shows the rate only inside your seller account.</Term>
<Term name="Base cost">You set the price, and the cost of making the product comes out of it.</Term>
<Term name="Your own checkout">The platform doesn't sell for you, so you need your own payment provider.</Term>
</Terms>

## Is the lowest fee the cheapest?

Not always. A platform that takes 0% often charges a monthly plan, so it only costs less once you sell enough. Card processing is usually extra, and a fixed charge per sale costs more on cheap items. [Digital products and stores](/platform-fees/digital-products-and-stores) works through an example.

## Where do these rates come from?

From each platform's own pricing, help, and terms pages. Rates are for US sellers in US dollars unless a card says otherwise. Fees change often, so check the date on each card and the platform's own page before you sign up.
