## Gumroad vs Payhip vs Etsy: what you keep from one sale

Payhip's free plan leaves you the most at every price below, and Gumroad leaves you the least. Gumroad's fixed 50¢ fee hurts most on cheap products.

<Example title="What you keep from one download sold to a US buyer by card, rates checked 1 November 2026">

| Price | Gumroad | Payhip free plan | Etsy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $5 | $3.55 | $4.30 | $4.07 |
| $20 | $16.62 | $18.12 | $17.65 |
| $100 | $86.30 | $91.80 | $90.05 |

Gumroad takes 10% plus 50¢, then card processing takes 2.9% plus 30¢. Payhip takes 5%, then Stripe takes its standard 2.9% plus 30¢. Etsy takes a 6.5% transaction fee, 3% plus 25¢ for payment processing, and the 20¢ listing fee. Totals are rounded to the cent.

</Example>

These numbers are for a sale you bring in yourself. Each platform charges differently when it finds the buyer for you:

- **Gumroad Discover:** 30% of the sale, card fees included.
- **Etsy Offsite Ads:** 15% of the order, or 12% once you pass $10,000 in yearly sales, up to $100 per order. You pay it only when a sale comes from one of Etsy's ads.
- **Payhip marketplace:** no extra fee on top of your plan's fee.

Payhip's paid plans cut the per-sale fee. Plus costs $29 a month and takes 2%, so it beats the free plan once you sell more than about $967 a month. Pro costs $99 a month and takes nothing, so it beats Plus above $3,500 a month. Etsy may also charge a one-time fee to open a shop, depending on where you live.

All three collect and pay US sales tax for you. Gumroad is the merchant of record, so it handles sales tax and VAT. Payhip has handled US sales tax since 1 July 2026, and it also handles EU and UK VAT on digital products. Etsy collects US sales tax where state law requires it, and VAT on digital items in the countries it lists.
