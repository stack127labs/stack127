## Who is Mediavine a fit for?

Content sites that already earn steady ad revenue, often in food, travel, home, and similar topics. Mediavine looks at what you earn from ads in a year across all your sites, not a traffic number. Your content needs to be original, high quality, and evergreen.

## What should you have ready before you apply?

Proof of what your ads earn now, since the bar is yearly ad revenue. You'll also need to give Mediavine read-only Google Analytics access during review.

You must be in good standing with AdSense and Ad Exchange, with no active policy issues. You don't need to have used AdSense before.

## What if your site is too small for Mediavine?

Mediavine runs a second program, [Journey](/ad-networks/journey-by-mediavine), for smaller sites. It has a lower bar to join and the same company behind it. Journey's page lists its rules and the sites that use it.

## What does the pay day mean?

Mediavine pays each month's earnings a set number of days after the month ends. Earnings from January arrive a couple of months later, not at the start of February. Plan for that gap if you switch from a network that pays faster.

## How does Mediavine compare with Raptive?

Raptive publishes a 75% share. Mediavine's starts at 75% for new sites and rises to 90% in its top tiers. [Raptive](/ad-networks/raptive) sets a pageview bar and a reader location rule, while Mediavine sets a revenue bar. Raptive pays on Net 45, sooner than Mediavine's Net 65. Mediavine says there's no long-term contract.

## How can you check if a site uses Mediavine?

Look for Mediavine in the site's ads.txt file, or paste the domain into our [ad network checker](/ad-network-checker).
