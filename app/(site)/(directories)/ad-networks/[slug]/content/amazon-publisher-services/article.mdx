## Who is APS a fit for?

Web publishers who already run Google Ad Manager and represent their own sites. UAM, its Unified Ad Marketplace, is built for them. APS says it works with publishers worldwide.

It's not a starting point for a new site. UAM is invitation only, with no public form. If you don't use Ad Manager yet, start with [Google AdSense](/ad-networks/google-adsense) or a managed network.

## How do you get access to APS?

Contact APS and ask. There's no public application or traffic rule, so have your Ad Manager setup and current header bidding partners ready to describe.

## Can you run APS with another ad network?

Yes, for UAM. It's built to run next to your other header bidding partners, and its code can sit outside your wrapper. You don't have to drop anyone.

## What does the UAM fee mean for you?

APS takes 10% out of each bid before the auction runs. So the bids that compete for your ad space are already net of it. Compare final earnings, not bid prices, when you test it.

## When does APS pay?

Once a month, on Net 60, with a $5 minimum. The low minimum means even small UAM earnings get paid out.

## How can you check if a site uses Amazon Publisher Services?

Look for Amazon Publisher Services in the site's ads.txt file, or paste the domain into our [ad network checker](/ad-network-checker).
