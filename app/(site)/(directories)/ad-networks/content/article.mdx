## What do ad networks require to join?

Most ad networks set a minimum to join, shown on each card under "To join". Many count monthly traffic, and some, such as Mediavine, count the ad revenue your site already earns. Self-serve networks such as Google AdSense take sites of any size. Where a card says "Not public", the network doesn't publish a fixed rule.

Networks count traffic in different ways. A session is one visit, and a pageview is one page loaded, so one session often includes several pageviews. A minimum of 50,000 sessions is harder to reach than 50,000 pageviews.

Traffic isn't the only rule. Many networks also check where your visitors come from, whether your content is original, and whether your site follows Google's ad policies. Each network's page lists its full rules.

## How do we confirm a site uses a network?

We read each site's own ads.txt file, the public list of companies allowed to sell its ad space. When the file names a network as its ad manager, or the site's pages load that network's ad code, the site counts as confirmed. A network's seller list can point us to a site, but it never confirms one on its own.

We re-check every site regularly, and a site that drops a network loses its confirmed status. That's why a network's confirmed count can be lower than its total.
