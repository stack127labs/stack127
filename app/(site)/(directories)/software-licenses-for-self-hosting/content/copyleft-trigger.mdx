## When does GPL or AGPL require you to share source code?

GPL applies when you distribute a copy, changed or not. Then you must pass on the license and make the source available to whoever gets the copy. Running it on your own server, or inside your company, isn't distribution.

AGPL adds a network rule in [section 13](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html#section13). If you change the program and people use your changed version over a network, you must offer them its source. That rule stops a changed, hosted copy from staying closed, which is why AGPL is common for self-hosted web apps.

Two points people often misread:

- Running unmodified AGPL software doesn't trigger section 13, even when family, coworkers, or customers log in.
- If you're the only user of your changed version, there's no one you owe source to.
