## What do the domain extension types mean?

The type is IANA's label for how an ending is run, and it hints at who can register under it.

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| generic | Most endings, such as `.com` and `.xyz`. Most arrived from 2013 on, through ICANN's New gTLD Program. Brand endings, such as `.bmw`, are used only by their owner. |
| country-code | Two-letter endings for a country or territory, such as `.uk`, plus versions in other scripts, such as `.中国`, shown with their `xn--` form. Each sets its own rules. |
| sponsored | Endings for one community, such as `.edu` and `.gov`, often with eligibility rules. |
| generic-restricted | Older generic endings that launched with limits on who could register, such as `.biz` and `.pro`. |
| infrastructure | `.arpa`, used for reverse DNS. You can't register a name under it. |
