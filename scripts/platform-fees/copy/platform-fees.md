## What do the terms on a card mean?

Most platforms charge in more than one way, so one card can show several kinds of fee.

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Platform fee, commission, or share | The platform's cut of each sale. It can be a percent, a fixed amount, or both, like 10% + 50¢. |
| Card fees | What a payment processor such as Stripe or PayPal charges to take the payment. Often on top of the platform fee. |
| Plan | A monthly or yearly price to sell. Higher plans often take a smaller cut. |
| Marketplace sale | A sale the platform's own marketplace found for you. Some platforms charge more for these. |
| Merchant of record | The platform is the legal seller, so it collects and pays sales tax or VAT for you. |
| Base cost | For print on demand, what the product costs to make. You keep your price minus this. |
| Pool | Your pay is a share of a fixed amount the platform splits among creators. |

The bar on a card shows only a checked percentage fee. "You keep 90%" means before fixed fees and card fees. Fixed amounts, base costs, and pools have no bar.

## How are the fees checked?

Every fee comes from the platform's own pricing page, help pages, or terms. Each section on a category page shows when it was last checked, and **Sources and dates** links the source for every fact.

Rates are for a US seller in US dollars unless the card says otherwise. If a platform doesn't publish a fee, the card says so instead of guessing.

These pages don't add up your final payout. That depends on your price, your plan, where the buyer lives, and how they pay.
