# ARPU / ARPA calculator

Route: /arpu-arpa-calculator

## Research

### User job

The [Notion roadmap item](https://app.notion.com/p/3e37bc97c4438138b7dbfea9c2682f27) says, “Measures recurring revenue generated per user or account.” The page is otherwise blank. The common job is to divide revenue by a matching population, but “user,” “account,” “active,” and even “revenue” depend on the business model.

For B2B SaaS, ARPA usually describes monthly recurring revenue per paying customer or account. A company account may contain several individual users or seats, and a customer may have several subscriptions. For consumer products, ARPU may use each distinct active user in a reporting window, including people who do not pay when free users generate ad or other revenue. A business that wants only average revenue among payers is measuring ARPPU. The denominator must follow the question: count companies for account revenue, people for user revenue, and paying people only for a payer metric.

The in-progress [SaaS metrics calculator](../saas-metrics-calculator/tool-spec.md) already reports monthly ARPA as ending MRR divided by ending paying accounts for a reporting month. It is one row in a broader monthly SaaS snapshot; it does not offer a user denominator or arbitrary-period active-user ARPU. The [MRR calculator](../mrr-calculator/tool-spec.md) normalizes plan prices to MRR and ARR but does not report per-customer revenue. A standalone tool would overlap materially if it only repeated month-end SaaS ARPA. The Notion brief does not say whether the intended task is that exact SaaS snapshot or the distinct per-user / same-period calculation.

### Competitor patterns

- [Mowt ARPU calculator](https://mowt.com/tools/arpu-calculator/) has controls for monthly versus annual revenue and users (ARPU) versus accounts (ARPA). After entering $50,000 of monthly recurring revenue and 500 active accounts, the account mode showed $100 monthly ARPA and $1,200 annualized ARPA. It also adds a go-to-market segment, rough segment bands, an MRR contribution figure, a +10% scenario, and a downloadable report card. Its labels and guidance center on paying users/accounts, so it does not demonstrate a universal free-user ARPU definition.
- [Do The Calculation ARPU, ARPA & ARPPU calculator](https://dothecalculation.com/calculators/average-revenue-per-account-calculator) combines recurring revenue, optional add-on/setup revenue, active accounts, and paying accounts. Its initial example displayed $125,000 divided by 500 = $250 ARPA, $125,000 divided by 400 = $312.50 ARPPU, 80% paid conversion, and a $1.5 million annual run rate. It includes save, share, print, and export actions, plus a bar comparison and explanation. In the operated browser session, edited field values did not change the displayed initial results; treat its page copy and initial example as evidence of its stated pattern, not proof that the calculation updated correctly.
- [IOTools ARPU calculator](https://iotools.cloud/tool/arpu-calculator/) accepts total revenue, active users/accounts, a period length in months, and optional prior monthly ARPU. With $75,000 over three months, 5,000 users, and prior monthly ARPU of $4, it returned $15 for the period, $5 monthly ARPU, $60 annualized ARPU, and 25% growth. It supports copy/download and CSV output. The field helper says “active, paying users/accounts,” while the page description says “active users”; this leaves the free-user denominator unclear.

These examples share revenue and a count as the core inputs. They differ on paying versus all active users, accounts versus users, recurring versus total revenue, and whether the result is period-specific or normalized to a month. Some add forecasts, benchmarks, or growth comparisons that need additional assumptions. The safest useful pattern is to state the revenue basis and denominator next to the result and keep the measurement window aligned.

### Technical facts and sources

- **A general ARPU definition uses a matching period.** Stripe says to choose a time window, total the revenue relevant to the business in that window, identify active users over that same window, and divide. Stripe notes that a company defines what counts as active. Google Analytics defines ARPU as total revenue per active user whether or not that user purchased; its numerator includes ad revenue, purchases, in-app purchases, and subscriptions, minus refunds. Its ARPPU uses purchase revenue from active users who made a purchase. These are platform definitions, not rules every company must adopt. Sources: [Stripe ARPU definition and formula](https://stripe.com/resources/more/what-is-average-revenue-per-user-why-it-matters-and-how-to-calculate-it), [Google Analytics metric definitions](https://support.google.com/analytics/table/13948007?hl=en).
- **SaaS ARPA commonly uses MRR and paying customer accounts.** ChartMogul’s report definition is MRR in a period divided by paying subscribers in that period. Its report notes count active and past-due subscribers, exclude free subscriptions, and count a customer with multiple subscriptions once. The exact subscriber-status policy is ChartMogul’s product methodology, not a universal definition. Sources: [ChartMogul ARPA report methodology](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/209-chart-average-revenue-per-account), [ChartMogul ARPA guide](https://chartmogul.com/saas-metrics/arpa/).
- **Keep the time basis explicit.** If revenue is $75,000 across three months and 5,000 distinct users were active in that same window, period ARPU is $15 per user; dividing by three gives $5 monthly ARPU. Multiplying that monthly value by 12 gives a $60 annualized rate, not revenue already earned over a year. For month-end SaaS snapshot ARPA, ChartMogul instead uses that period’s MRR and paying subscriber count. Do not pair a multi-month revenue numerator with a month-end count without labeling the different basis.
- **There is no universal definition for the count or numerator.** Primary sources and competitors vary between active users and paying accounts, between all relevant revenue and recurring MRR, and in how they define the count for a reporting period. State the chosen rule. If the question is recurring SaaS ARPA, use recurring revenue normalized to the stated period and count the distinct paying customer accounts covered by that metric; do not call a seat an account. If measuring user-level ARPU for a free or ad-supported product, the active-user definition and revenue sources need to be chosen for that product.
- A zero denominator makes the average undefined; a zero numerator with a positive denominator is a valid zero. Keep monetary inputs in one currency; revenue and counts must describe the same population and reporting window. Annualized output is a run-rate conversion, not a forecast.

### Opportunity and blockers

The supported opportunity is a manual per-user or per-account calculation that makes the denominator and measurement period visible, so a builder can tell whether the result describes a person, paying customer, or company account and whether it is monthly or for another interval. Its separation from the existing SaaS metrics calculator depends on supporting the user-level or same-period job rather than repeating only the existing end-of-month MRR/account ratio. The brief does not resolve whether the tool should cover all active app users, SaaS paying accounts, or both; product planning should preserve that distinction instead of pretending ARPU and ARPA always mean the same population.

No paid API, maintained dataset, account, or background job is needed. The user supplies the revenue and denominator; the tool cannot verify the source system’s active-user, subscription-status, revenue-recognition, refund, or recurring-revenue policy.

## Product

### Promise and scope

A founder or product operator has a revenue total and a population count from their own reports and wants to know the revenue per person or per paying customer account. The tool offers two explicit calculations. **ARPU** is net revenue during a named reporting window divided by distinct active users in that same window, including nonpaying users who meet the operator's active-user rule. **SaaS ARPA** is monthly-normalized MRR at a named month end divided by distinct paying customer accounts at that same month end. One account can contain many seats or subscriptions but is counted once. The result names its numerator, denominator, and time basis. This manual, direct calculation is useful when the wider `/saas-metrics-calculator` snapshot is unnecessary, and the active-user job is absent from that snapshot.

The two choices are separate definitions, not interchangeable labels for one calculation. ARPU may include subscription, purchase, advertising, and other revenue relevant to the product, net of refunds under the user's consistent source policy. It is not automatically the Google Analytics ARPU metric, and the calculator cannot define or verify active activity. ARPA uses recurring MRR, not total period revenue, one-time fees, recognized revenue, cash collected, a count of seats, or a count of subscriptions. It follows the user's consistent paying-account and MRR inclusion rules rather than promising to match ChartMogul's configured report. A person who wants revenue per *paying person* needs an ARPPU calculation; neither mode silently substitutes that denominator.

Launch is one population, one reporting window or month-end snapshot, and one currency per calculation. There is no period-to-month normalization for ARPU: dividing a three-month ARPU by three would keep the three-month distinct-user denominator and could be mistaken for monthly ARPU. Do not add annualized rates, growth, benchmarks, cohort or segment comparisons, prices, account imports, billing connections, currency conversion, export, or projections. The tool cannot establish whether two supplied figures came from matching reports.

### Technical contract

**Inputs and basis.** Default to the visible `ARPU per active user` choice, USD, and empty numeric and period fields. Keep the mode-specific values separate when switching choices so an active-user count is never relabeled as paying accounts. The currency choice is shared and changes formatting only. Accept USD, EUR, GBP, INR, CAD, and AUD as in the sibling SaaS tools; the supplied revenue is already in the selected currency.

| Choice | Required inputs | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ARPU per active user | A reporting-period label of 1 to 80 characters, net total revenue from -1,000,000,000,000.00 to 1,000,000,000,000.00, and 0 to 1,000,000,000 distinct active users. | The revenue and unique people cover exactly the period the user names. Count each active person once even if they had many sessions or purchases. Free users belong in the count when they meet the active rule. Negative net revenue is allowed when refunds or reversals exceed positive revenue. |
| Monthly ARPA per paying account | A reporting month in strict `YYYY-MM` form from `1900-01` through `2100-12`, nonnegative month-end MRR from 0 to 1,000,000,000,000.00, and 0 to 1,000,000,000 distinct paying customer accounts. | MRR is already normalized to a monthly recurring amount at that month's end. The count is the distinct paying-account population at that same instant, including each account once even with multiple subscriptions. |

The period label is context only; it never changes the quotient or creates a monthly equivalent. Trim its outer whitespace, require a nonempty value within the stated limit, and display it only as text. The month field must represent a real month; do not infer a day, timezone, or reporting window from it. The tool cannot verify that the two numeric sources match the entered label or month.

Keep raw numeric fields as strings. Trim outer whitespace. Counts accept only base-10 whole digits, including leading zeroes. Money accepts base-10 digits with an optional period and one or two decimal digits; only ARPU revenue also accepts one leading minus sign. Reject a leading plus, commas, currency marks, interior spaces, exponent notation, more than two decimal digits, fractional or negative counts, out-of-range values, unsupported currencies or modes, non-string values, and blanks. A blank is unknown, never an implicit zero. Validate the same contract in the pure engine. Parse money as integer cents and counts as integers; use exact integer arithmetic, including for large values, rather than binary floating-point division or pre-rounding the inputs.

**Calculation and display.** In ARPU mode, `period ARPU = net period revenue / distinct active users in that period`. In ARPA mode, `monthly ARPA = month-end MRR / distinct paying customer accounts at that month end`. Divide the exact amount by the count, then round the per-entity result half up in magnitude to four decimal places in the selected currency. Display at least two decimal places, omit unneeded third and fourth trailing zeroes, and use en-US digit grouping without abbreviating large values. If a nonzero quotient rounds to zero at four places, show `<$0.0001` (with the selected currency) for a positive value, or say `Negative, under $0.0001 in magnitude` for a negative value; never print it as `0.00`. An exact zero with a positive count is a valid `0.00` result. The signed ARPU result may be negative; ARPA cannot be negative under its input rules. Show the submitted revenue and count once in a compact calculation-basis line, plus the period label or month and population definition. Do not show a second, normalized, or annualized metric.

A known zero denominator is accepted but the average is undefined. Show `ARPU unavailable` or `ARPA unavailable` with the zero-count reason and the entered basis; do not show zero or infinity. If MRR is positive while paying accounts are zero, also prompt the user to check whether the two source reports describe the same month-end population. Do not fabricate a partial numeric result. A positive denominator with zero revenue gives zero. A missing count or revenue is invalid and does not produce any result, including an unavailable result.

**Action and handling.** `Calculate average` is the sole primary action for both choices. A valid submit shows the labeled average or the defined zero-denominator state and focuses it. An invalid submit clears any previous answer, keeps all entered values, places one precise error beside each invalid field, and focuses the first invalid visible field. Changing mode, currency, period, revenue, or count clears the old result and errors, while preserving each mode's draft. An unexpected local calculation failure clears the answer, preserves inputs, and leaves Calculate available to retry with one local status. Reset restores the default choice and USD, clears both drafts and messages, and focuses the choice group. The calculation is synchronous and local; there is no loading, network, or partial-result state.

Keep source values and period labels in client memory only. Do not send them in a URL, request, cookie, browser storage, log, or analytics event. Site analytics may record the page visit without tool inputs. Render entered labels and numbers as text, never HTML.

## Interface

### Flow and controls

The user's question is, "How much revenue did each active user or paying account represent on the basis I measured?" The lead answer is one labeled period ARPU or monthly ARPA amount, or an explicit unavailable state for zero people/accounts. The sole primary action is `Calculate average`. Control mode is `single`: the two visible measurement choices switch the entire numerator and denominator definition; there is no Basic/Advanced mode or hidden result-driving assumption.

Inside the shared full-width workbench, show the `Average for` `UiChoiceGroup` first with `Active users (ARPU)` and `Paying accounts (ARPA)`. A concise line directly below the chosen option names its data rule: net revenue and distinct active users in one window, or month-end MRR and distinct paying accounts. Then show the selected mode's period label or month, the selected mode's revenue field with joined currency, and its population count; follow with Calculate and quiet Reset. Every active field is visibly required, including fields whose known value can be zero. The ARPU reporting-period placeholder may show `Apr to Jun 2026`; the ARPA month placeholder may show `2026-04`. Neither is a supplied value.

Reuse `UiChoiceGroup` for the two visible radio choices, `UiTextField` for the reporting period or month, `UiQuantityInput` for raw-string money joined to the currency selector, and `UiTextField` with numeric input mode for the strict whole count. `UiNumberField` would coerce or increment a raw value before strict parsing, so it does not fit this contract. Reuse `UiActionRow`, `UiButton`, `UiPrimaryResult`, and `UiStatus` for actions, the answer, and one useful state announcement. The gallery at `/components-ui` and the ARR/MRR sibling tools show these primitives. A route-owned wrapper handles the mode-specific labels, simple field layout, divider, result basis, and mode drafts. No new shared component is needed.

Below one divider, show only the selected mode's lead average and one basis line: formatted numerator ÷ grouped count, with the selected period or month and the correct population words. For the zero-count case, use a plain result lead/status with its reason instead of `UiPrimaryResult` showing a number. Supporting content after the workbench explains the different numerator and denominator definitions. A bar chart, comparison cards, two simultaneous mode results, annual rate, or table would repeat facts or imply the two modes are comparable from the same inputs.

### States and recovery

- **Initial and incomplete:** ARPU selected; USD visible; period, revenue, and count empty; no answer or example result. Calculate stays enabled so an empty submission can show specific errors. Switching to ARPA reveals its own empty month, MRR, and account fields without copying ARPU values.
- **Valid:** focus the newly shown labeled result and announce it once. Negative ARPU, positive values smaller than one cent, ordinary values, and exact zero with a positive count keep neutral visual tone. Long amounts remain fully selectable and may wrap at safe separators.
- **Zero denominator:** show the unavailable label and reason in the result position and focus it; keep the submitted basis visible. A positive MRR with zero paying accounts adds the source-consistency prompt in that one result region. It is a known undefined average, not a blank-field error or a computed zero.
- **Invalid:** clear the old answer, retain typed strings and the active mode, show field-level period, syntax, precision, bound, or count errors without an extra error panel, and focus the first invalid visible control. The engine rejects unsupported mode and currency values even though the bounded UI does not offer them.
- **Edit or mode change:** clear the prior answer and its basis immediately and announce that calculation is needed again. Preserve each mode's own values when switching back; a shared currency change never converts either draft's numeric text. Do not show a stale ARPU value under ARPA labels or vice versa.
- **Unexpected failure and retry:** keep entered values, clear the answer, show one `UiStatus` failure beside Calculate, and allow the same action to retry. Reset clears both mode drafts, errors, status, and result; returns to ARPU and USD; moves focus to the choice group; and announces reset.
- **Restoration:** a clean `/arpu-arpa-calculator` URL remains unchanged through hydration. Do not serialize inputs to the URL or storage. Reload starts fresh; if browser back/forward cache restores the form and result together, an edit still clears that result. No loading, network retry, search no-match, or partial numeric result applies to this local calculation.

### Concepts, limits, and next step

Use the two Product examples consistently. The `$15` ARPU is revenue per distinct active user for the whole `Apr to Jun 2026` window; it is not April, May, or June ARPU. The `$100` ARPA is monthly MRR per distinct paying account at `2026-04` month end; it is not cash collected from each customer or revenue recognized in April. Neither average describes what every individual paid. ARPPU has a separate purchaser denominator and may have a different revenue basis; the page should not calculate or compare it without its own inputs. A zero denominator yields no average even when revenue is also zero.

The reader supplies the activity rule, revenue scope, refund treatment, month-end MRR, and paying-account policy. The page cannot inspect those choices or reproduce GA4, ChartMogul, or a company's accounting report. Avoid universal claims that ARPU always uses subscription revenue, that ARPA counts subscriptions or seats, or that this output can be divided or multiplied into a monthly or annual user metric. Avoid targets, health labels, valuation claims, growth inferences, and advice to change pricing from one average.
