# Domain age checker content brief

## September 6 content maintenance

## Who is here

Someone who wants to understand when a domain's current registration began and what that date can, and cannot, tell them. They may be checking a suspicious website, considering a domain purchase, researching a business, or reviewing a domain's SEO history.

## The job

Answer the question in plain language, then help the reader interpret the result without treating domain age as proof of trust, ownership history, business age, or website age.

## Show versus say

- Lead the working result with one large registration-age answer.
- Put registration, re-registration, transfer, change, today, and expiry dates on one ordered timeline when the registry publishes them.
- Keep registry source and fetch date as quiet metadata. Put raw RDAP evidence behind a contained disclosure.
- Explain the age calculation, website history, registration changes, SEO interpretation, and missing dates in connected sections with descriptive search-friendly headings.
- Compare renewal, registrar transfer, expiry, and deletion followed by registration in one two-column table. This is supporting explanation, not another result panel. Wrap its cells within the reading column on desktop and mobile.
- Use one short glossary list for unfamiliar terms actually shown in the result. Definitions should wrap without horizontal scrolling.
- Do not add a percentage, allocation bar, score, decorative callout, or another timeline. The live result already visualizes the dates; a marker illustration would repeat it.

## Content order

1. Introduce the problem, the domain input, and the answer it returns in one short paragraph.
2. Let the reader use the checker.
3. Explain the source, accepted input, and rounded age.
4. Follow a hypothetical domain purchase into website history and trust, with the Wayback Machine as a next check.
5. Separate events that continue a registration from deletion and re-registration in a comparison table.
6. Explain what age can contribute to SEO research without promising ranking benefits.
7. Explain missing or conflicting ages and link to the full registration lookup.
8. Define remaining unfamiliar result terms in one glossary.

## Trust boundary

The page must never imply that an old domain is trustworthy or that a new domain is suspicious. Domain age is most useful when it contradicts a specific claim. Missing historical data stays unknown.

## Editorial sequence

Use the Verge-inspired voice from the site writing guide. Start with the difference between a polished website and a verifiable registration date. Below the tool, explain what the date measures, then follow one hypothetical domain purchase into the limits of website age and trust. Explain which registration events preserve that date before discussing SEO research. Finish with incomplete records and unfamiliar terms. Each section should follow the reader's next question rather than list unrelated facts.

The 20-year-old domain is a hypothetical example, not a measured result or a required example for other tools. Review how the paragraphs connect, not only whether each topic appears.
