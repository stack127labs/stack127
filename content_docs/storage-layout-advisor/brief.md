# Brief: Storage Layout Advisor

## Current supporting-content direction

Approved September 6, 2026. Explain input grouping and protection requirements before ranking. Use a failure-placement comparison to show why equal capacity does not mean equivalent protection. Explain limits and platform verification without claiming universal optimality.

The working tool stays first. Use connected explanation at the depth the task needs, with no word, section, sentence, or visual-ratio quota. This direction supersedes earlier supporting-prose budgets in this page's planning artifacts; it does not change the engine or input contract. Store live copy only in the development tool-page row through the reviewed content command. Publication remains a separate operation.

## Who is here

Someone building or upgrading a homelab array has a real drive inventory and is about to choose a layout. They understand drives and basic RAID words, but they do not want to translate raw capacity, parity, mixed sizes, workload, and failure placement across several vendor calculators.

## The job

They leave with a short list of layouts that meet their minimum protected-failure target, one transparent fit for the workload they selected, exact capacity accounting, and the platform-specific facts they still need to verify before creating storage.

## Show vs say

Show the drive editor and goal choices in one full-width workbench. Show the top fit and eligible alternatives in an exact comparison table, use a capacity accounting bar for usable, redundancy, and mixed-size mismatch, and draw the candidate topology so the failure boundary is visible. Use a few sentences after the tool for how to choose representative inputs, what the theoretical result excludes, why a fit is not a universal best, how explicit sharing handles inputs, and what to verify on the real platform. No marker art or screenshot is needed for version 1.
