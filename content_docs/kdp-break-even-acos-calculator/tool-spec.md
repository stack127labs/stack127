# KDP Break-Even ACoS Calculator

Route: `/kdp-break-even-acos-calculator`

## Research

### User job

The existing [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator) already calculates royalty per sale, break-even ad spend, and break-even ACoS. Its current result is `royalty ÷ list price`, for a positive royalty, and its stated scope is US Amazon.com, standard distribution, USD. It does not use campaign CPC, clicks, conversion rate, or a profit target, so it cannot answer whether a planned bid or current campaign meets the author's unit-profit goal.

A distinct ads-management question is: given the royalty from one ad-driven book sale and an expected or observed click-to-purchase rate, what CPC can the author afford while keeping a chosen amount per sale, and what conversion rate would a known CPC require? A campaign review can also compare the author's entered spend, clicks, attributed purchases, and ad-attributed sales against that royalty. A tool that only repeats the royalty calculator's break-even ACoS would duplicate its current result.

For simple planning, use explicit definitions: conversion rate = attributed purchases ÷ clicks; average CPC = spend ÷ clicks; estimated direct-sale profit per order = royalty per order − CPC ÷ conversion rate. Enter conversion rate as a fraction in the formulas (for example, 10% = 0.10) and keep all money values in the same currency. For a chosen profit target per order, the allowable CPC is `(royalty per order − target profit per order) × conversion rate`; values at or below zero mean the target cannot be met with a positive bid under those assumptions. These are arithmetic from the stated inputs, not Amazon's official royalty or campaign forecasts. Unknown: the author's incrementality, organic lift, read-through, and any extra KENP earnings cannot be inferred from these inputs alone.

### Opportunity and blockers

Keep the task about ad economics after the author knows the royalty: translate conversion and CPC assumptions or entered campaign results into a bid ceiling, required conversion, and profit against a chosen per-sale target. Link to the existing KDP royalty calculator for a modeled royalty instead of building a second set of KDP print and eBook cost rules. State that campaign ACoS uses ad-attributed sales revenue, while author profit uses royalties; campaign outcomes can also include series read-through or KU earnings that simple per-order math does not capture.

The calculation can be local and synchronous from user-entered values. It needs no Amazon Ads account, paid API, live scraping, maintained external dataset, background job, or account. If campaign imports or account connections are considered later, their API access and data-handling requirements are unknown and outside this research scope.

## Product

### Promise and scope

Primary user: a KDP author who knows the royalty from one book sale and is considering a CPC bid for Sponsored Products. The job is to check whether that bid can leave a chosen amount per ad-attributed sale at an assumed click-to-purchase rate. The lead answer is the highest whole-cent CPC that meets the target under those inputs, followed by whether the entered CPC meets it and the purchase rate that CPC would require. This is a planning threshold, not a bid recommendation or a prediction of campaign results.

Keep the route `/kdp-break-even-acos-calculator`, but make its distinct task clear: the [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator) already gives the maximum ad spend per sale and break-even ACoS from royalty and list price. Here, CPC and purchase rate turn that per-sale headroom into a bid check. An optional entered ad-attributed sales value per purchase translates both the entered CPC and profit target into ACoS terms for comparison with an ads report. Without that value, no ACoS percentage is shown. The optional profit target defaults visibly to `$0.00`, so a zero target answers the literal break-even question.

Launch is one USD book and one assumed Sponsored Products click-to-purchase rate, entered locally. The author supplies the net KDP royalty per sale after KDP deductions and before ads; link to the royalty tool to estimate it. The author may lower that entered amount to allow for other known per-copy costs, but unentered costs are excluded. No KDP format, marketplace, price, or royalty rules are recalculated here. Exclude campaign imports, account connections, spend/click/order dashboards, ROAS, KU/KENP and series read-through, organic lift, attribution adjustments, taxes, refunds, fixed production costs, and claims about future conversion or sales. A separate ads profit or break-even CPC route should not reproduce this same bid-planning flow.

### Technical contract

**Inputs.** Required: net royalty per sale `R` in USD (`$0.00` to `$1,000.00`), assumed purchase rate `q` as a percentage of ad clicks (`0.00%` to `100.00%`), and contemplated average CPC `C` in USD (`$0.00` to `$1,000.00`). Profit target per ad-attributed sale `T` is visibly initialized to `$0.00`, editable from `$0.00` to `$1,000.00`. Optional ad-attributed sales value per purchase `V` is in USD (`$0.01` to `$1,000.00`); it is the sales-revenue denominator for ACoS, not the author's royalty. These numeric ceilings are input guardrails, not KDP price or royalty rules. All money inputs must refer to the same USD book and one attributed purchase per converting click. Do not substitute list price for `V` silently.

Parse trimmed decimal text strictly. Accept an optional leading `$` on money, at most two decimal places on money and on the entered percent, and no sign, commas, exponent, or trailing characters; the rate field's `%` is a UI suffix. Blank required fields are missing, not zero; the target's visible `$0.00` is an explicit value. Blank `V` omits ACoS output, while malformed or out-of-range `V` is invalid. Reject non-finite values and values outside the bounds. Parse money to integer cents and purchase rate to an integer number of hundredths of a percent `b` (`0` to `10,000`); use integer or rational arithmetic for all threshold comparisons. In the formulas below, `p = b / 10,000`.

**Calculation.** Headroom for ads and the chosen target is `H = R - T` in cents. If `H > 0`, the maximum whole-cent CPC at the assumed purchase rate is `floor(H × b / 10,000)` cents. The floor matters: rounding upward could show a bid that misses the target. For positive `b`, modeled click cost per attributed sale is `C × 10,000 / b` cents, and modeled amount left per attributed sale is `R - (C × 10,000 / b)` cents. Compare the entered bid with the target using the exact condition `C × 10,000 <= H × b`, before display rounding. For `H > 0` and `C > 0`, the minimum purchase rate required to meet the target is `C / H`; show it rounded *up* to the next `0.01` percentage point. A value above `100%` is unattainable within the input range. For `C = 0` and `H >= 0`, any positive purchase rate meets the per-sale target; do not present `0%` as a sale-producing rate. If `H = 0` and `C > 0`, no positive CPC can meet the target. If `H < 0`, even zero ad cost falls short, so report the target as unattainable rather than calling `$0.00` an affordable bid.

When `V` is present and `b > 0`, modeled ACoS at the entered CPC is `(C × 10,000 / b) / V × 100%`. When `V` is present and `H >= 0`, the ACoS ceiling for the chosen profit target is `H / V × 100%`; it can exceed `100%` if the entered royalty exceeds attributed sales value, so do not clamp it. These are sales-revenue ratios, separate from the royalty-based bid math. The existing royalty tool's `royalty ÷ list price` can differ because this tool uses an entered sales value and may reserve a profit target. ACoS does not determine author profit by itself.

Show money to two decimals and entered purchase rate to two percentage decimals. Display required purchase rate to two percentage decimals after upward rounding, and ACoS to one percentage decimal; preserve exact ratios for all comparisons. For a positive `b`, round the displayed modeled click cost and amount left to the nearest cent only after calculation. Label the values as modeled from the user's assumption. Show the entered assumptions beside the answer so a saved screenshot can be interpreted. Do not turn a one-cent displayed difference caused by rounding into an incorrect meets/misses verdict.

**States and action.** `Check bid` is the one primary action. Start with an empty royalty, purchase rate, and CPC, a visible `$0.00` target, and empty optional `V`; there is no result until valid submission. On a valid positive rate, show the maximum whole-cent CPC or an unattainable-target state, the entered CPC's meets/misses verdict, modeled amount left per attributed sale, and the required purchase rate or its unavailable reason. Show ACoS values only if `V` is supplied. At `q = 0%`, no sale-based amount, modeled ACoS, or meets/misses verdict can be calculated, even if CPC is zero; show no positive affordable bid and explain that a positive CPC spends money with no attributed purchases under this assumption. The required rate for a positive CPC may still be shown when `H > 0`. For `C = 0` and positive `q`, keep the no-cost case explicit. If `H < 0`, retain any calculable modeled amount but no bid ceiling or required-rate claim. An omitted `V` is an intentional partial result, not an error. Invalid input identifies its field and clears stale results. Editing any input clears the previous result until resubmission; reset restores the initial state. Calculation is synchronous, so no loading or normal retry state is needed. On an unexpected arithmetic failure, keep inputs, clear stale output, and show a recoverable unavailable message. Never show `NaN`, infinity, a negative bid ceiling, or an invented conversion outcome.

All values stay in the browser during use. No account, upload, server calculation, URL state, local storage, or input-bearing analytics event is needed. Treat inputs as plain text data and never render them as HTML. Amazon Ads attribution, observed CPC, and purchase rate must come from the author's own reports if they want a retrospective comparison; this tool neither fetches nor verifies them.

## Interface

### Flow

User question: "At my expected purchase rate, what CPC can I afford, and does the CPC I am considering leave my chosen amount per sale?" The page header may use one short line to explain the bid task because the route name alone suggests a royalty-only ACoS calculation. Use one control mode, `single`: every active assumption that changes the answer stays visible. There is no advanced mode, slider, preset conversion rate, or hidden default.

The workbench order is net royalty per sale (USD), desired amount left per sale (USD, visibly `$0.00`), assumed purchase rate (%, attributed purchases divided by ad clicks), CPC to check (USD), then optional ad-attributed sales value per purchase (USD). The royalty field has a nearby `Estimate royalty` link to `/kdp-royalty-calculator`; it does not fill or change this form. Brief field guidance explains that royalty is after KDP deductions and before ads, and that the optional sales value is the ACoS revenue denominator. The CPC field can use a planned or observed *average cost per click*; the visible wording must not equate it with the actual Amazon auction bid. `Check bid` is the only primary action; `Reset` is a quiet secondary action after it in reading and keyboard order.

After a single divider, show the answer in this order:

1. Lead `UiPrimaryResult`: `Maximum CPC` and the whole-cent ceiling. Its short detail names the entered royalty, profit target, and assumed purchase rate together, so the threshold has context without a second assumptions card. When the target exceeds royalty, use a plain `UiResultLead` sentence instead of a misleading `$0.00` result. At a zero purchase rate, lead with `No positive CPC at 0% purchases`; do not suggest that a sale was modeled. A positive rate with a calculated `$0.00` ceiling says that no positive whole-cent CPC meets the target.
2. Entered-CPC check: a compact sentence with `UiStatusWord` or equivalent text status, `Meets target` or `Misses target`, tied to the entered CPC. A `UiKeyValueList` then gives the modeled amount left per attributed sale and the minimum purchase rate needed at that CPC. Show modeled click cost per attributed sale only as a supporting calculation, with no repeat of the lead ceiling. For a free CPC, say `Any positive purchase rate` when the target is within royalty. For a required rate over 100%, say it is unattainable rather than display an actionable percentage.
3. Optional ACoS comparison, only when sales value was entered: a short heading that names that entered revenue value, then two labeled `UiKeyValueList` rows, `Modeled ACoS at entered CPC` and `ACoS ceiling for your target`. At zero purchases, omit modeled ACoS and keep only the target ceiling when it is defined, with an unavailable reason for the modeled value. Do not call either percentage author profit or repeat the royalty tool's `royalty ÷ list price` result.

Do not add a gauge or table. These are a threshold, one scenario verdict, and at most two optional ratios, not a measured series. Do not put the result regions in nested cards, add a second lead metric, or repeat the same formatted fact in a summary strip. The supporting article follows the workbench and can explain the formula and limits.

### Concepts and result interpretation

- **Net royalty and target:** `$4.39` is the net royalty per attributed book sale supplied by the author, after KDP deductions and before ad spend. `$1.00` is the amount the author wants to keep per such sale after ads under this model. - **Purchase rate and CPC:** A purchase rate is attributed purchases divided by ad clicks, expressed as a percent; it is not a guaranteed future conversion rate. Average CPC is ad spend divided by clicks and is not the same as the Amazon auction bid. Use Amazon Ads metric and purchase-rate reporting documentation to support these definitions. A planning percentage remains an assumption.
- **Maximum CPC and required purchase rate:** In the operated example, `$4.39 − $1.00 = $3.39` is the amount available for advertising per attributed sale. At a `10%` purchase rate, the raw maximum is `$0.339`, so the tool shows `$0.33` as the highest whole-cent CPC that stays within the target. At a `$0.30` CPC, modeled ad cost per sale is `$0.30 ÷ 0.10 = $3.00`, leaving `$1.39`. These figures assume one attributed purchase per converting path under the entered rate; they do not forecast clicks or orders.
- **ACoS:** Amazon Ads defines ACoS as advertising spend divided by ad-attributed sales revenue. With the example's `$14.99` sales value, `$3.00 ÷ $14.99` gives a modeled ACoS of `20.0%`; the `$3.39` headroom gives a target ceiling of `22.6%`. This revenue denominator is not the `$4.39` royalty, and ACoS is not the author's profit margin. If sales value is blank, ACoS is intentionally omitted.
- **Unavailable states:** At `0%` purchases, the tool cannot calculate a per-sale cost, amount left, ACoS at the entered CPC, or a meets/misses verdict; a positive CPC spends without an attributed purchase under that assumption. If the target exceeds royalty, even a `$0.00` CPC leaves too little, so there is no CPC ceiling or required purchase rate. If a required rate exceeds `100%`, it cannot be reached within the input range. These are distinct from a missing or invalid field, which prevents a result.
