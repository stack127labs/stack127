# Brief: software licenses for self-hosters

## Who is here

Someone met a license label in the wild: on a GitHub repo, a Docker Hub page, or an app card in our directory. They are about to install the thing, or already run it, and a label like AGPL or SSPL or BSL made them pause. They are not lawyers and do not want a law lesson. A second, smaller visitor is picking a license for their own app.

## The job

Answer their exact question in seconds: can I run this at home, at work, or as a service I host for others. Then, only if they keep reading, teach the two ideas that make every future label instantly readable: the open source vs source-available line, and how copyleft triggers. They leave able to judge any label they meet next, including ones we do not list.

## Show vs say

- Left sidebar is the license index: start-here section links, then license anchors grouped by family in table order. Every license has a stable anchor (`#mit`, `#agpl-3-0`, `#busl-1-1`) because directory cards link in by SPDX id.
- The title and one short introduction set the scope. The comparison follows immediately.
- One verdict table: 32 licenses, one-word values per decision (`yes`, `careful`, `depends`, `no`), an OSI column, semantic colors, and anchor-linked names. A "What the verdicts mean" block defines the columns and vocabulary; the table reports what the license permits, not what a legal department will approve.
- One marker figure shows the obligations that distinguish the four families without exact license lists or version labels. GPL distribution and AGPL's added network rule stay in the short, precise prose because the relationship does not need another diagram. Exact classification stays in the table, sidebar, and cards.
- Licenses render as compact cards, three per row on desktop, two on tablets, and one on mobile, grouped by family with a one-line family intro. Each card carries a summary following `stack127_docs/writing-style.md`, then links to matching directory apps when any exist and to the accurately labelled official source when one exists. Exact comparisons stay in the verdict table instead of being repeated inside every card.
- Supporting explanations follow the catalog: the open source and source-available distinction, a short path for creators choosing a license, and practical questions about AGPL at home, internal GPL patches, and repositories without a license.
