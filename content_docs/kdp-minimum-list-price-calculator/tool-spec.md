# KDP Minimum List Price Calculator

Route: `/kdp-minimum-list-price-calculator`

## Research

### User job

An author needs to know the lowest list price KDP will accept for a particular book and marketplace, before entering that price in KDP. For print, the floor depends on the printing cost and the royalty tier. For an eBook, the allowed floor depends on marketplace, royalty option, and in some cases file size. This is a rule lookup and calculation, not a recommended market price or a promise of sales.

The requested flat route is `/kdp-minimum-list-price-calculator`. Root-level tool routes match the repository's route convention.

### Competitor patterns

KDP's [Printing Costs and Royalty Calculator](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/royalty-calculator) is the authoritative print calculator. Its help page says it calculates paperback and hardcover printing cost, minimum list price, and royalties, but excludes Expanded Distribution. The page opened without an account, but an anonymous save/sign-in interstitial blocked access to its interactive controls during this review. The documentation still establishes direct overlap for print.

[kdpbook.io's KDP royalty calculator](https://kdpbook.io/kdp/royalty-calculator) combines paperback, hardcover, and Kindle eBook scenarios. Its visible controls include marketplace, binding, ink and paper, trim, page count, list price, and Amazon versus Expanded Distribution; its eBook branch exposes marketplace, file size, and royalty rules. It shows a minimum price and nearby price examples. I entered $6.50 against the displayed $6.80 print floor: it reported the price as unacceptable, while the detail math showed a negative royalty and the lead value showed $0.00. The below-floor state is visible but its displayed figures do not agree.

[Inkario's KDP print cost calculator](https://www.inkario.app/free/print-cost/) combines marketplace, binding, ink, regular/large trim class, pages, and an optional list price. It displays print cost, minimum list price, and royalty when a list price is supplied; its page dates its published KDP rate tables to August 2026. It covers eight marketplace choices and print only.

Across these examples, competitors place the floor beside the costs and royalty tier that produce it. The official KDP tool covers print; kdpbook.io already combines print and eBook floors across marketplaces. A standalone US-only floor calculator would overlap both KDP's print calculator and stack127's existing royalty tool. No distinct calculator job emerged from this review. A possible quality opportunity is to make the below-floor math and error state agree: the kdpbook.io example I entered showed $0.00 as its lead royalty but -$0.15 in the breakdown. Multi-marketplace coverage would also require substantially more maintained rules than the current stack127 calculator.

### Technical facts and sources

Checked 2026-09-28. KDP's own help pages control the rules; use its calculator or the final KDP pricing screen to confirm an edition-specific result.

| Rule | Verified fact | Primary source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Print royalty and tiers | Paperback and hardcover royalty is `(royalty rate × list price) − printing cost`. For Amazon sales the print rate is 50% below the marketplace threshold and 60% at or above it. Thresholds are $9.99 USD, €9.99 EUR, £7.99 GBP, C$13.99 CAD, A$13.99 AUD, ¥1,000 JPY, PLN 40, and SEK 110. Expanded Distribution paperback royalty uses a separate 40% rate. | [Paperback royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330); [hardcover royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHT976ZKSKUXBB6H) |
| Print-cost formula | Print cost is fixed cost plus page count times per-page cost. Paperback cost varies with marketplace, trim category, page count, ink, and paper; hardcover cost varies with marketplace, trim category, page count, and ink. The cost tables include fixed-only page-count bands. Paperback and hardcover have separate tables and page limits. | [Paperback printing cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340); [hardcover printing cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHT976ZKSKUXBB6H) |
| Print floor | KDP defines the minimum from printing cost divided by the royalty rate that applies at the candidate list price. Because the print rate changes at marketplace thresholds, solve the 50% and 60% cases against their own price bands; `printing cost ÷ 60%` alone can produce a price that falls in the 50% band and does not cover printing. Check the resulting floor against the marketplace maximum. The official help gives maximums by marketplace and notes that Expanded Distribution changes the minimum. | [Paperback printing cost and minimum](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340); [hardcover printing cost and minimum](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHT976ZKSKUXBB6H); [KDP print calculator scope](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GSQF43YAMUPFTMSP) |
| US eBook floor | On Amazon.com, the 35% option has minimum list prices of $0.99 below 3 MB, $1.99 from 3 MB to below 10 MB, and $2.99 at 10 MB or above. Its maximum is $200. The 70% option requires a list price from $2.99 through $12.99; the higher limit took effect July 7, 2026. These are eligibility floors and bands, not a cost-covering price formula. | [eBook list price requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560) |
| eBook royalty and file cost | 35% royalties are calculated on list price less applicable VAT, with no delivery-cost deduction. The 70% calculation deducts applicable VAT and delivery cost before applying 70%. US delivery is $0.15/MB; KDP rounds the file size up to the nearest KB and specifies a $0.01 minimum US delivery cost. A selected 70% option earns 35% outside eligible territories; Brazil, Japan, Mexico, and India also require KDP Select for 70% sales. | [Digital Book Pricing](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634500); [eBook list price requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560) |
| Marketplace scope | eBook minimums and maximums are table-driven by marketplace, currency, royalty option, and file-size tier. Print rates and print costs also vary by marketplace. For example, KDP's eBook requirements table gives separate USD, INR, CAD, BRL, GBP, EUR, JPY, MXN, and AUD bands. Do not convert US floors using an exchange rate and present the result as a KDP rule. | [eBook list price requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560); [price your book](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200641280) |

Unknowns to keep explicit: the eBook requirements page labels size tiers in MB but does not define whether its 3 MB and 10 MB boundaries use decimal or binary bytes. The help text describes the print floor formula but does not document every price-rounding detail used by KDP's calculator. An independently computed value should be described as an estimate and checked in the KDP pricing screen. Exact current account, territory, price-matching, tax, and Expanded Distribution outcomes are not supplied by these general tables.

### Opportunity and blockers

The requested floor is already an output of stack127's [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator): `lib/kdp/royalty.ts` calculates `minimumListPriceCents` for paperback, hardcover, and eBook, and the page displays it. That tool also rejects a selected print price below its computed floor. It currently models Amazon.com in USD, so it does not provide a multi-marketplace answer. A second US-only tool would duplicate the existing user job. A multi-marketplace tool could fill that coverage gap, but kdpbook.io already offers it; this research does not establish a unique task for a separate route. Consider a new route only if its scope or output differs materially from the existing royalty calculator.

A deterministic implementation does not need a backend: the calculation can use local arithmetic and versioned KDP rate and cost tables. No paid API, account, or background job is needed for that approach. The table values would need an owner and periodic review because KDP changes print rates, thresholds, and eBook price bands. No supported live pricing API was verified in this review; do not imply that static rules guarantee the exact price KDP will accept for every title. There is no research blocker, but the distinct scope should be resolved before the product contract is written.

## Product

### Promise and scope

An author who knows a book's print setup or eBook file size wants to choose a starting KDP list price before testing prices in the royalty calculator. Give the lowest modeled Amazon.com list price in USD for that setup. With no royalty goal, give the KDP rule floor. With a desired royalty per copy, give the lowest price in the allowed band whose estimated royalty meets that goal. The latter is the distinct job: the existing KDP royalty calculator starts with a list price and reports its royalty and minimum; this tool starts with a royalty goal and solves for a price. A goal of $0.00 asks for the lowest price with a nonnegative modeled royalty, which can differ from an eBook's allowed floor.

Launch with paperback, hardcover, and Kindle eBook sales to a US customer on Amazon.com through standard KDP distribution. Use only the versioned US print costs, print rate threshold, eBook price bands, and delivery rule already owned by `lib/kdp/royalty.ts` and `lib/kdp/specs.ts`. Do not add marketplace or currency choices until independently verified local tables exist. The answer is an estimate to check in KDP's pricing screen, not a guaranteed accepted price, sales recommendation, or net profit. Exclude Expanded Distribution, non-US marketplaces, VAT and taxes, price matching, promotions, refunds, ad spend, outside production costs, and actual sales. For the eBook 70% option, assume the book and US sale qualify; this tool cannot verify territorial rights, public-domain restrictions, or KDP's rule that the eBook list price sit at least 20% below an Amazon-listed physical edition.

### Technical contract

| Input | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Format | Required enum: `paperback`, `hardcover`, or `ebook`. Default `paperback`; it determines which other inputs are active. Marketplace and currency are fixed labels, `Amazon.com` and `USD`. |
| Print setup | For print, use the shared binding-compatible trim IDs, combined ink and paper profiles, and page limits in `lib/kdp/specs.ts`. Default `6x9`, black ink on white paper, 300 entered pages. Page count is a positive whole number; round an odd count up to the next even effective count, display the change, and reject a count whose effective value exceeds the selected limit. Do not accept free-text dimensions or incompatible profiles. |
| eBook setup | For eBooks, choose 35% or 70%, default 70%, and enter file size in MB, default `1`. Require a finite decimal from `0.001` through `650` MB, with at most three digits after the decimal point. This is a calculator input bound, not a claim about KDP's upload limit. Keep the entered size for the KDP price-band check; round it up to the nearest KB for the 70% delivery calculation. No file upload. |
| Desired royalty per copy | Optional USD amount, blank initially. Blank asks only for the permitted floor. An entered amount from `$0.00` through `$1,000,000.00` asks for a price that earns at least that amount under this model; zero is a real target. This is KDP royalty after printing or delivery costs, before all other author costs. |

Keep money as integer cents. For the goal field, trim surrounding whitespace and accept plain digits with an optional decimal point and one or two decimal digits; a leading `$` may be accepted. For eBook size, trim whitespace and accept plain decimal digits with up to three fractional digits. Reject signs, commas, exponents, fractions, other currency marks, extra decimal places, and amounts outside each bound without rounding. For print pages, accept digits only. Keep invalid raw text so the author can correct it. Format USD to two decimals.

Resolve compatible print options and effective pages before computing the printing cost from the shared US table. Round that cost to cents at the same boundary as the royalty calculator. For print, use its cost-covering floor: `floor50 = ceil(printingCostCents / 0.50)` when that price is at most `$9.98`; otherwise `max($9.99, ceil(printingCostCents / 0.60))`. The maximum is `$250.00`. At each candidate price, select 50% through `$9.98` or 60% from `$9.99`, then calculate `round(rate × priceCents) − printingCostCents`. Use the same half-up cent rounding as the existing engine. Start the goal search at this shared floor even if royalty rounding alone would make a price one cent below it appear to cover the cost; both tools must agree on the KDP floor.

For a 35% eBook, the permitted floor is `$0.99` below 3 MB, `$1.99` from 3 MB to below 10 MB, and `$2.99` at 10 MB or more; its maximum is `$200.00`. Its modeled royalty is `round(0.35 × priceCents)`, with no delivery deduction. For a 70% eBook, the allowed list-price band is `$2.99` through `$12.99`, regardless of file size. Use that `$2.99` floor explicitly: the current royalty engine's `minimumListPriceCents` reports the general eBook size-tier floor and is not the selected 70% option floor. Compute `deliveryCents = max(1, round(0.15 × ceil(fileSizeMB × 1024) / 1024 × 100))` and modeled royalty `round(0.70 × (priceCents − deliveryCents))`; assume zero US VAT. Do not silently switch a selected 70% scenario to 35% when its goal cannot be met inside the 70% band.

When a goal is entered, evaluate candidate prices in one-cent increments from the permitted floor through that option's maximum and return the first whose rounded royalty is at least the goal. This finite search handles the print rate jump and cent-rounding edges without a false continuous-price answer. For a blank goal, return the permitted floor without claiming it earns a positive royalty. At a valid price, show the lead price, the permitted floor, the desired royalty if present, the modeled royalty at the lead price, the selected rate, and the print cost or eBook delivery deduction that produced it. State the effective page count when it changed. For a goal result, show the difference above the permitted floor; for a floor-only result, label it as the permitted floor. Include source links, rule-check date, and a short instruction to verify the edition in KDP. Do not call the result an exact KDP acceptance decision or author profit.

If no candidate meets an entered goal, return `unreachable` with no goal price. Still show the permitted floor, option maximum, and highest modeled royalty within the allowed band, with a clear explanation that the selected goal cannot be reached under these assumptions. For a large 70% eBook, the permitted floor can produce a modeled royalty below zero; identify that as a formula estimate, never as a claim that KDP will collect money from the author. An unsupported print setup, a print floor above the maximum, malformed input, or missing versioned rules blocks the numeric result and names the reason. Do not substitute a rate or cost from another marketplace. KDP does not specify every print-cent rounding detail or the byte convention for its eBook MB thresholds in these help pages, so put the estimate and confirmation limit beside the result.

`Find minimum price` is the only primary action. Submit a local calculation after validation; editing any input clears the old result, and Reset restores defaults, blank goal, and no result. Calculation is synchronous, with no loading or retry state in the normal path. Unexpected arithmetic or missing source data shows a recoverable failure with no stale result. No account, file upload, calculation API call, saved scenario, URL state, local storage, input logging, or analytics payload with book inputs is needed. Render inputs as text, with no HTML interpretation.

### Known examples

| Setup and goal | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Paperback, 6 × 9 in, black ink on white, 300 pages; goal blank | Print cost `$1.00 + 300 × $0.012 = $4.60`; permitted floor `$9.20` in the 50% band; modeled royalty `$0.00`. |
| Same paperback; goal `$1.39` | `$9.98` earns `round(50% × $9.98) − $4.60 = $0.39`; the first meeting price is `$9.99`, where `round(60% × $9.99) − $4.60 = $1.39`. This crosses the rate boundary. |
| Same paperback; goal `$2.00` | First meeting price `$11.00`, with `$2.00` royalty. `$10.99` gives `$1.99`; difference above the `$9.20` permitted floor is `$1.80`. |
| Hardcover, 6 × 9 in, black ink on white, 100 pages; goal `$1.00` | Fixed-only print cost `$6.80`; permitted floor `$11.34`; first meeting price `$13.00`, where `60% × $13.00 − $6.80 = $1.00`. `$12.99` gives `$0.99`. |
| eBook, 1 MB, 70% option; goal `$2.00` | Delivery `$0.15`; permitted floor `$2.99` gives `$1.99`; first meeting price `$3.00` gives `round(70% × ($3.00 − $0.15)) = $2.00`. |
| eBook, 3 MB, 35% option; goal `$1.00` | Size-band floor `$1.99`; first meeting price `$2.85` gives `$1.00`; `$2.84` gives `$0.99`. At 10 MB the 35% permitted floor becomes `$2.99`. |
| eBook, 1 MB, 70% option; goal `$9.00` | Unreachable: `$12.99` is the option maximum and gives `$8.99`; show `$2.99` as the permitted floor but no goal price. |
| Print page count `300.5`, unsupported profile, or goal `1e3` | Field error, no numeric result; preserve the input for correction. |

## Interface

### Flow

User question: "What is the lowest Amazon.com list price for this book, or for the royalty I want per copy?" The lead answer is one USD list price. With the optional goal blank, label it `KDP minimum list price`; with a goal, label it `Lowest price for your royalty goal`. The only primary action is `Find minimum price`. Keep `Amazon.com · USD · standard KDP distribution` visible as a quiet scope line above the form; it is not a marketplace selector.

Use one control mode. Show every active price-driving assumption; no Basic/Advanced switch and no input disclosure. In reading and keyboard order: Format; the selected print or eBook setup; optional royalty goal; Find minimum price and quiet Reset; result. Format is a visible three-choice group. For print, show Trim size, Interior and paper, and Printed pages. For eBooks, show Royalty option (35% or 70%) and eBook file size (MB). Show `Desired royalty per copy (USD)` after either branch, with a short adjacent description: `Leave blank for KDP's minimum. Enter 0 for a price with no modeled royalty loss.` The field must remain visible because it changes the task. Defaults from Product are populated, so a reader can calculate immediately, but no result appears until submission.

On a valid submission, place one neutral `UiPrimaryResult` immediately after a divider. Its detail identifies the entered goal when present, or says `KDP rule floor` when blank. Follow it with one compact calculation receipt: `Estimated royalty per sale`, selected royalty rate, and either `Printing cost` or `Delivery deduction`. For an entered goal, show `KDP minimum` and `Above KDP minimum` after the receipt. With a blank goal, the lead already is the permitted floor, so omit those duplicate rows. Show an effective-page note only when odd entered pages became an even count. Put `Estimate. Check this edition in KDP's pricing screen.` beside the result, then a quiet `Calculation details` disclosure with the rule version, review date, relevant official KDP links, and the applicable eligibility limit. The receipt owns these numbers: no price table, chart, progress meter, second result card, ad-headroom estimate, or duplicate royalty figure.

For an unreachable goal, replace the price lead with a focusable `UiResultLead` saying `No price meets this royalty goal`. Do not display a dash or the allowed floor as if it met the goal. Directly below, show the KDP minimum, the selected option's maximum list price, and the highest modeled royalty within that band. A short note says to lower the goal or change the book setup or eBook option. The normal result's receipt is absent. The entered goal remains visible in the form; a concise lead detail may repeat it only to make the unreachable status understandable after focus moves to the result.

## Content

### Search intent and metadata direction

The main search intent is an author asking for the lowest KDP list price for a particular paperback, hardcover, or Kindle eBook. A related, more specific intent is finding the lowest price that reaches a chosen royalty per copy. Readers may know their trim, paper, and page count or their eBook file size, but may confuse KDP's permitted floor with a price that meets a royalty goal. Do not imply search-volume knowledge from these query phrases.

Use the tool-title pattern: tool name followed by the outcome or question, with the goal-based price made clear because that is the distinct job. The H1 and SEO title should include natural terms such as `KDP minimum list price calculator` and identify the US/Amazon.com scope where space permits. The SEO description should say that a reader can enter a print setup or eBook size and optionally a royalty goal to find a modeled lowest USD price. It must not suggest a price recommendation, guaranteed KDP acceptance, net profit, or coverage of other marketplaces. The writing worker supplies the final title and metadata; this plan does not prescribe character targets.

### Page promise

After using the page, the reader should know which modeled Amazon.com price first clears the rule floor or their entered royalty goal, what cost and rate produced it, and what to confirm in KDP before setting the edition's price. They should be able to tell a rule floor, a royalty-goal price, and an unreachable goal apart.

### Recommended outline and formats

The introduction before the workbench should start with the author's pricing decision, then give the basic print relationship `royalty = royalty rate × list price − printing cost` with the units and explain that the selected KDP rate and allowed band determine the lowest candidate price. State that eBook floors follow price bands and, for 70%, a delivery deduction. Connect that principle to the tool's two tasks: leave the goal blank for the rule floor, or enter a per-copy royalty goal to solve for a price. Keep the introduction useful on its own; put threshold search, cent rounding, and the worked calculation after the tool.

After the workbench, use this sequence of proposed headings and formats:

1. `How to use the price and royalty goal`: short numbered task guidance for choosing the correct format and final book details, leaving the goal blank versus entering `$0.00`, and reading the lead plus receipt. Include the unreachable case here. Do not narrate every button or repeat a live result.
2. `Why the print minimum can jump at $9.99`: short explanation followed by one `> [!EXAMPLE]` block using the 300-page paperback example in Product. Show the `$9.98` result below the goal and `$9.99` result meeting it, with the 50% and 60% rates. The introduction already owns the simple formula, so do not add a second formula callout that restates it.
3. `How eBook floors differ`: a compact Markdown table for the official 35% size bands and the 70% US band, followed by the delivery-cost and eligibility explanation. The table is for exact bands; use no chart or marker art for these values. State the uncertainty at the 3 MB and 10 MB byte boundaries beside the table.
4. `What the estimate does not include`: a short `> [!NOTE]` for the edition-specific checks and source freshness, then a concrete next step in KDP's pricing screen. Link the existing stack127 royalty calculator when the reader wants to start with a chosen price and see its royalty instead.

Normal paragraphs, one worked example, and one narrow table are enough. The catalog `toolSpot` artwork is a card asset, not a required article figure. Avoid a chart of a few price points because the exact cent threshold and band labels matter more than plotted shape.

### Usage, limits, and next step

Plan one real author task around a 6 × 9 in, 300-page black-ink paperback on white paper with a `$1.39` royalty goal. Show how the author chooses the final production settings, reads the lead price and receipt, and notices that the 60% tier starts at `$9.99`. Mention that changing a page count, trim class, or interior can change cost; for eBooks, file size and 35% versus 70% change the applicable rule. Explain that a missing goal means “find the permitted floor,” while zero means “find a price with no modeled royalty loss.”

If a field is invalid, the author fixes the named format or bound and calculates again; the previous price is cleared. If a goal is unreachable, they review the maximum and highest royalty before changing the goal or scenario. Finish with the KDP pricing screen for the exact edition and official calculator for a cross-check. The existing [KDP royalty calculator](/kdp-royalty-calculator) is the internal next step for testing a chosen list price and seeing its royalty. Avoid claims that the tool picks an ideal selling price, guarantees KDP approval or 70% eligibility, measures demand, calculates net profit, covers Expanded Distribution or other marketplaces, or knows current account-specific rules.
