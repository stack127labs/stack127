# Domain age checker content brief

## Who is here

Someone who wants to understand when a domain's current registration began and what that date can, and cannot, tell them. They may be checking a suspicious website, considering a domain purchase, researching a business, or reviewing a domain's SEO history.

## The job

Answer the question in plain language, then help the reader interpret the result without treating domain age as proof of trust, ownership history, business age, or website age.

## Show versus say

- Keep the working checker and its result as the page's main visual explanation.
- Show the sample registration result and the fields returned by the checker as short lists because readers need to scan repeated facts.
- Explain re-registration, trust, and the limits of registry data in prose because these ideas need context rather than another interface element.
- Do not add a chart, comparison table, screenshot, marker illustration, score, or decorative callout for this copy-only update.

## Content order

1. Say what the checker returns.
2. Let the reader use the checker.
3. Define domain age with a concrete example.
4. Explain the four practical reasons to check it.
5. Separate registration age from website, business, ownership, and content history.
6. Explain expiry and re-registration.
7. List what the result can show.
8. End with the direct trust limitation.

## Trust boundary

The page must never imply that an old domain is trustworthy or that a new domain is suspicious. Domain age is most useful when it contradicts a specific claim. Missing historical data stays unknown.
