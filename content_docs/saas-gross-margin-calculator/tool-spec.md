# SaaS gross margin calculator

Route: `/saas-gross-margin-calculator`

## Research

### User job

The Launch priority 8 Notion brief says: “Shows the share of revenue left after direct service delivery costs.” A solo SaaS founder or operator has revenue and delivery cost figures for a reporting period and wants to calculate gross profit and the portion of revenue left after those costs. They also need a dependable way to decide which service delivery costs belong in the calculation.

### Competitor patterns

- [SaaSCalc](https://saas.skyail.com/en/tools/saas-gross-margin-calculator/) uses monthly recurring revenue, hosting, support and delivery, payment processing, customer count, and a target margin. Its outputs are cost of revenue, gross profit, margin, cost per customer, and revenue at the target margin. I changed revenue from $100,000 to $120,000 and support and delivery cost from $8,000 to $10,000; with $12,000 hosting, $3,000 processing, 800 customers, and an 80% target, it showed $25,000 cost of revenue, $95,000 gross profit, 79.17% margin, $31.25 cost per customer, and $125,000 target revenue. At zero revenue it still showed 0% margin, while showing -$25,000 gross profit. A zero revenue denominator makes the ratio undefined. The page describes its result as a planning estimate and displays “Your inputs stay in this browser”; the privacy claim was not independently checked.
- [Nautis](https://www.getnautis.com/calculators/gross-margin-calculator) accepts period revenue and separate hosting/cloud, third-party API, payment processing, and customer success costs for direct delivery. It displays gross margin, revenue, total COGS, gross profit, a verdict, and category benchmark bands. With $200,000 revenue and costs of $20,000, $10,000, $6,000, and $14,000, it showed $50,000 COGS, $150,000 gross profit, and 75.0% margin. At zero revenue it showed 0.0% margin, -$50,000 gross profit, and a “below SaaS bar” verdict. The tool assigns a categorical grade even though the ratio is undefined. The page says it runs in the browser; network behavior was not checked.
- These two operated pages repeat manual revenue and cost inputs with a headline gross profit and margin result. Added outputs are either per-customer and target-revenue planning or broad benchmarks and verdicts. The benchmark claims did not show a source or definition sufficient to establish a universal SaaS threshold. [CalcMastery](https://www.calcmastery.com/saas-gross-margin-calculator/) was another candidate, but direct browser navigation failed with an SSL name error; it is not used as evidence.

### Technical facts and sources

- **Formula and basis:** Gross profit = revenue for the period - cost of revenue for that period. Gross margin (%) = gross profit / revenue for the same period and scope * 100. Atlassian states this definition in its FY2026 second-quarter Form 10-Q and separately reports total revenue, cost of revenue, gross profit, and gross margin. For a company-wide margin, use company-wide revenue and cost of revenue. For a product, service, or recurring revenue view, match its revenue to the corresponding costs and label the result accordingly. Revenue and costs must use the same currency and period.
- **Recognized revenue, MRR, and ARR:** For reported gross margin, start with revenue recognized in the reporting period under the business's applicable accounting basis. Do not treat invoices, cash collected, bookings, MRR, and ARR as interchangeable substitutes; use one only when it equals revenue recognized for that period. Atlassian reports that its cloud subscription revenue is recognized as service is performed over the term, and that its MRR and annualized recurring revenue are point-in-time operating measures that do not represent GAAP revenue. Annual prepayment therefore need not equal one period's revenue. A management recurring-margin metric can use recurring revenue recognized in the period with its matching costs. If it uses an MRR or ARR run rate instead, label it a run-rate estimate and make the cost basis clear. Revenue recognition timing depends on the contract and performance obligation; do not assume every SaaS or implementation fee is recognized evenly over time. FASB says the measure of progress for an obligation satisfied over time must depict performance; a straight-line measure fits when the customer's benefit is even, not as a universal default.
- **Cost of revenue examples:** Public SaaS filings show that reported cost of revenue can include third-party cloud hosting, infrastructure and network costs, server depreciation, payment processing fees, customer support and infrastructure staff or contractors, benefits and stock-based compensation, amortization of capitalized software or acquired technology, and allocated facilities and IT overhead. Professional services or implementation delivery costs can sit in cost of revenue when they relate to those services. These examples are not a universal chart of accounts. GitLab's FY2026 filing separates costs for SaaS and subscription revenue from costs for professional services and other revenue. Atlassian describes employee compensation, hosting, payment fees, support and infrastructure contractors, acquired technology amortization, and allocated overhead in cost of revenue.
- **Classification caveat:** COGS is not limited to expenses that vary directly with each customer. Reported cost of revenue can include fixed capacity, depreciation, amortization, compensation, and a share of overhead. Customer success, engineering, onboarding, and shared infrastructure need classification or allocation based on actual duties, the service promised, and the company's consistent accounting policy. Do not move all payroll, R&D, sales, marketing, or G&A into COGS, and do not exclude an assigned service cost solely because it is fixed. When an internal estimate uses a different scope from the books, name that scope and do not compare it as though the definitions matched.
- **Interpretation and edge cases:** Product mix can move blended gross margin even when each product's margin is stable; Atlassian explicitly notes this. GAAP and adjusted or non-GAAP margins can also differ: Atlassian's non-GAAP gross margin excludes stock-based compensation, acquired intangible amortization, and restructuring charges. A zero revenue denominator makes margin undefined, not zero. If cost exceeds revenue, a negative gross profit and negative margin are valid arithmetic results. The competitor outputs tested above show why both states need distinct handling. No universal healthy margin benchmark was established by the primary sources reviewed.

**Primary sources**

- [Atlassian FY2026 second-quarter Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000011/team-20251231.htm): subscription revenue recognition, MRR/ARR distinction, cost of revenue examples and allocations, gross profit and margin definitions, product mix, and non-GAAP reconciliation.
- [GitLab FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653482/000165348226000087/a2026gitlabars.pdf): SaaS and subscription cost of revenue, cloud hosting, support personnel, payment processing fees, allocated overhead, and separate professional services and other costs.
- [FASB Revenue Recognition Implementation Q&As](https://storage.fasb.org/Rev_Rec_Implementation_QAs.pdf): the measure of progress for a service satisfied over time must depict performance; straight-line recognition is conditional on even benefit.
- [IFRS 15 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-15-revenue-from-contracts-with-customers/): performance obligations, allocating transaction price, and recognizing revenue as obligations are satisfied.

### Opportunity and blockers

The existing [SaaS Metrics Calculator spec](../saas-metrics-calculator/tool-spec.md) already calculates one optional gross margin from recognized recurring revenue and recurring service COGS, alongside a broad snapshot of one month's metrics. It does not break service costs into categories. The standalone page should help readers build and interpret a period revenue-to-delivery-cost picture, explain the accounting basis and cost classification, and show gross profit as well as the ratio. Avoid repeating the broad snapshot or turning a source-dependent result into a universal health grade. Its clearest trust gaps to close are mismatched revenue and cost periods, confusion between MRR and revenue, an undefined zero-revenue denominator, and overly narrow “variable costs only” definitions.

A manual calculation needs no paid API, account, background job, or maintained dataset. It cannot verify ledger classification or allocate shared costs without the user's records and policy. No benchmark source with definitions comparable across business model, stage, accounting basis, and revenue mix was established; a benchmark would require maintained, segmented source data and is not needed for this research scope. No other approval blocker was identified.

## Product

### Promise and scope

A SaaS founder or operator has recognized revenue and the costs of delivering that revenue for one reporting period. The tool calculates gross profit and the share of revenue left after those costs, with a breakdown the reader can check against their records. It is useful when the broad `/saas-metrics-calculator` snapshot is unnecessary or its single recurring-COGS field does not show how the cost total was assembled.

Launch is a manual calculation for one reporting period, one currency, and one stated revenue scope. The reader chooses entire company revenue, subscription service revenue, or a named product or service. They enter revenue recognized during that period, then five mutually exclusive cost categories for delivering that same revenue scope during that same period. Costs that do not fit a named category go in Other, once. Enter `0` when a category does not apply. The reporting-period label and scope make the basis visible; neither proves the figures were drawn from matching records.

This is an accounting-basis calculation, not an MRR or ARR run-rate calculator. It does not turn billings, cash received, bookings, MRR, or ARR into recognized revenue. It does not allocate shared expenses, prescribe a chart of accounts, reconcile GAAP to adjusted figures, compare periods or segments, forecast, set target revenue, estimate cost per customer, or give a benchmark grade. A company may include fixed capacity, depreciation, amortization, support staff, and allocated overhead in cost of revenue under its consistent policy. Include service delivery work where it belongs; do not automatically count all customer success, engineering, sales, marketing, R&D, or G&A as delivery cost. Matching subscription-only revenue to company-wide costs, or excluding implementation costs while including implementation revenue, gives a misleading margin. The tool cannot detect either mistake.

### Technical contract

**Inputs and basis.** Start with USD visibly selected and every other field empty; choose `Entire company` as the visible default scope. The same submitted period, currency, and revenue scope apply to every amount. No currency conversion or period normalization occurs.

| Input | Accepted value and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting period | Required plain-text label, 1 to 80 characters after trimming outer whitespace, such as `April 2026` or `Q2 FY2026`. It identifies the accounting window; it does not drive date arithmetic. Render it only as text. |
| Revenue scope | Required choice: `Entire company`, `Subscription service`, or `Named product or service`. The third choice requires a separate 1 to 80 character scope name after trimming. The scope affects the label and which source figures the reader should enter, not the formula. |
| Currency | Required choice USD, EUR, GBP, INR, CAD, or AUD. All amounts are already in this currency. Use two decimal places for monetary display. |
| Recognized revenue | Required nonnegative amount from `0` to `1,000,000,000,000.00` inclusive, recognized for the stated period and scope under the reader's applicable accounting basis. |
| Hosting and infrastructure | Required nonnegative cost from `0` to `1,000,000,000,000.00`, including attributable hosting, network, and infrastructure capacity. |
| Third-party APIs and delivery software | Required nonnegative cost in the same range for services used to fulfill the sold service. Exclude subscriptions used only for sales or administration. |
| Payment processing | Required nonnegative cost in the same range for fees attributable to the recognized revenue scope. |
| Support and service delivery people | Required nonnegative cost in the same range for attributable compensation and contractors who deliver or support the sold service. Allocate mixed duties under the reader's consistent policy. |
| Other cost of revenue | Required nonnegative cost in the same range for matched delivery costs not already entered, such as delivery-related depreciation, amortization, allocated overhead, or professional-services delivery. Do not double count another category. |

Keep raw amount inputs as strings. Trim outer whitespace. Accept plain base-10 digits with an optional period and one or two fractional digits; reject blank, minus or plus signs, commas, currency symbols, interior spaces, exponent notation, more than two decimals, nonfinite values, and out-of-range values. Explicit `0` is valid, while an empty category is unknown and blocks calculation. Validate the same contract at the pure engine boundary, including non-string values, unsupported scope or currency, and a missing name for the named scope. Treat period and scope name as plain text only; reject control characters and never interpret them as markup.

Parse each amount into exact integer cents and use integer arithmetic, including for percentage rounding at the input bounds. Sum the five cost values without rounding: `cost of revenue = hosting + third-party delivery + processing + people + other`. `gross profit = recognized revenue - cost of revenue`. If recognized revenue is greater than zero, `gross margin (%) = gross profit / recognized revenue × 100`; calculate the ratio from exact cents, then round only its displayed percentage to two decimals, half up in magnitude. A negative gross profit and margin are valid when cost exceeds revenue. If revenue is exactly zero, show gross profit and cost of revenue but label gross margin `Undefined` with the zero-denominator reason, even when every cost is zero. Never substitute 0%, infinity, or a health verdict. Money displays exact cents with currency formatting; do not abbreviate large values. If a nonzero margin would round to `0.00%`, preserve its sign and show that its magnitude is below `0.01%` rather than a false zero.

**Action and handling.** `Calculate gross margin` is the sole primary action. On valid submission, show the result for the stated period and scope. An invalid submission clears any prior result, preserves inputs, places a precise error at the affected field, and focuses the first invalid control in reading order. Editing any result-driving field clears the old result immediately and asks for another calculation; currency changes labels only and do not convert entered amounts. An unexpected local calculation failure clears the result, keeps every input, and permits the same action to retry. Reset clears inputs, errors, and result, restores the visible USD and Entire company choices, and focuses the reporting-period field. The calculation is synchronous and local: there is no loading, request retry, search empty state, or partial numeric result. A valid zero-revenue submission is an undefined-ratio result, not an error.

Do not send entered period labels, scope names, or financial values to a server, URL, cookie, browser storage, log, or analytics event. The clean canonical route remains unchanged on load and after calculation. Reload begins empty; browser back/forward cache may restore a form and result together, and the next edit still clears that result.

## Interface

### Flow and control mode

The reader asks, "How much of this period's recognized revenue remains after delivering the service?" The lead answer is gross margin for the stated period and revenue scope, or an explicit undefined ratio when revenue is zero. `Calculate gross margin` is the one primary action. Control mode is `single`: period, scope, revenue, and every cost category affect the meaning or answer, so keep them visible. No Basic/Advanced mode or disclosure hides an active assumption.

Use the shared full-width workbench band with a measured inner form width. Reading order: reporting period; revenue-scope choices and conditional name; recognized revenue and currency; five delivery-cost fields; Calculate and quiet Reset; one divider; margin, gross profit, and the cost breakdown. Keep the period-and-scope guidance short and next to the fields: use recognized revenue and costs for the same period and scope. Say once by the cost group, `Enter 0 when a cost does not apply.` The supporting article handles classification examples and accounting caveats.

### Result hierarchy

Start with one neutral `UiPrimaryResult` labeled `Gross margin`, showing the percentage or `Undefined`. Its detail names the period and revenue scope; for zero revenue, the detail gives the denominator reason. Directly below, show one quiet labeled `Gross profit` amount, retaining a minus sign when negative. Do not color negative values as a business grade. Then show one `UiDataTable` headed `Revenue and delivery costs`: a recognized-revenue row, the five cost-category rows, and one total-cost-of-revenue row. The table makes the numerator and cost total auditable; the lead margin and gross-profit value do not appear again as table rows or cards. Keep the formula `gross profit ÷ recognized revenue × 100` beside the result without repeating its formatted numbers. A zero cost category remains visible as `$0.00` because the reader explicitly entered it.

No allocation bar is needed: a cost total can exceed revenue, so a part-of-whole bar could misstate a valid negative margin. The table carries exact values at any sign and scale. Do not add cost per customer, target revenue, MRR, a health badge, benchmark, or recommendation to the result. The article can explain where a cost belongs and why a reported margin may differ from a management estimate.

### States and recovery

- **Initial and incomplete:** period, name, and amounts are empty; Entire company and USD are visible; no example number or result is shown. A blank submit places one specific error by each missing required field and focuses the first.
- **Valid:** display the lead margin, gross profit, and breakdown together; focus the labeled lead result and announce it once. All-zero costs with positive revenue give 100% margin; cost above revenue gives a negative margin with neutral styling. A fully entered zero-revenue case shows `Undefined`, its reason, the signed gross profit, and the cost table.
- **Invalid:** clear any old result, retain entered strings and choices, show each syntax, bound, missing-value, or conditional-name error by its field, and focus the first invalid visible control. Do not add an error result panel or repeat the same error by the action. A missing cost is invalid even when another category has a value.
- **Edit after result:** clear margin, profit, and breakdown immediately; announce `Inputs changed. Calculate again.` once without moving focus. Switching scope clears the result and reveals or removes the name field in the normal reading order; keep its draft if the reader switches back.
- **Unexpected failure and retry:** preserve every input, clear the result, show one `UiStatus` error by the action, and leave Calculate available to retry. The engine is synchronous and local; no loading spinner, network retry, or partial calculation applies.
- **Reset and restoration:** clear all drafts, errors, statuses, and result; restore Entire company and USD; focus Reporting period and announce reset. A clean `/saas-gross-margin-calculator` URL remains unchanged through hydration. Reload starts fresh. If browser back/forward cache restores form and result together, the next edit still clears the result.

Use one polite status announcement for changed, reset, and failure states; focused result output announces the answer. Field errors are associated with their inputs and reached by the validation focus. Do not announce success both through a live message and an identical focused output.

### Recommended outline and formats

**Introduction before the tool:** Start with the reader's task: they have one period's revenue and need to see what remains after delivering the service. State the simple gross-profit and gross-margin formulas with recognized revenue and matching cost of revenue, then say the calculator adds the cost categories and shows both answers. The formula belongs here because it is short enough to be useful before the workbench. Keep classification detail, exceptions, and the worked example below the tool. Do not imply that MRR is the default numerator or that gross margin measures what remains after every business expense.

**Usage after the tool:** A short numbered sequence should cover preparing one period and revenue scope, entering recognized revenue in one currency, assigning each delivery cost to one category and entering `0` for a confirmed nonapplicable category, then checking the result table against the source totals. Mention that Currency changes the displayed unit and never converts amounts. Give the named-product choice a concrete purpose: its revenue and costs must describe that same product or service. Explain blank-versus-known-zero recovery without narrating routine button clicks.

**Supporting content after usage, in this order:**

1. `What the margin measures`: use one shared `> [!EXAMPLE]` block with the Product's $200,000 recognized-revenue and $50,000 total-cost example. Show $150,000 gross profit and 75% gross margin, then explain that the other 25% of this period's revenue went to the entered delivery costs. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a benchmark. The live table already shows each user's five costs; the example need not copy a full table of input fields. Do not repeat the formula in a second `[!FORMULA]` block after placing it in the introduction.
2. `Use revenue recognized for the period`: explain the same-period and same-scope rule, then distinguish recognized revenue from point-in-time MRR/ARR, invoicing, and cash received. One small Markdown table may compare what each figure measures if it is clearer than prose. An annual prepayment is a useful example of cash and recognized revenue arriving on different schedules, but recognition depends on the contract and performance obligation; do not claim every SaaS subscription or implementation fee is recognized evenly.
3. `What belongs in cost of revenue`: explain that the five fields are buckets for the reader's own consistent policy, not a mandatory SaaS chart of accounts. Use a short list or compact table for borderline cases: support staff with mixed duties, shared infrastructure and overhead, depreciation or amortization, and implementation delivery when its revenue is included. Give each cost one bucket. Explain that fixed service capacity can belong while sales, marketing, R&D, and general administration usually sit outside this calculation unless an actual delivery portion is allocated under the company's policy. Keep the classification caveat adjacent to these examples.
4. `Reading unusual and differing results`: interpret negative gross profit and margin with the Product's $1,000 revenue and $1,150 cost example; then explain the zero-revenue `Undefined` ratio and still meaningful signed gross profit. ### Concepts and result interpretation

- **Gross profit and gross margin:** $200,000 recognized revenue less $50,000 matched cost of revenue is $150,000 gross profit; dividing by $200,000 gives 75%. Gross profit is money; gross margin is a percentage of recognized revenue before expenses outside cost of revenue. Neither is net income or cash flow. The formula and cost basis come from the Product contract and Atlassian's filing in Research.
- **Recognized revenue versus MRR:** a paid annual invoice can produce cash before all of its subscription revenue is recognized. MRR and ARR describe a point-in-time recurring run rate in Atlassian's filing, not the reported period's revenue. Use recognized subscription revenue with corresponding subscription costs for the Subscription service scope. FASB's progress guidance and the IFRS 15 overview prevent a blanket straight-line claim for every service or implementation promise.
- **Cost classification:** GitLab separates SaaS/subscription and professional-services cost of revenue; Atlassian reports hosting, support, payment fees, compensation, amortization, and allocated overhead in cost of revenue. These filings are examples of actual policies, not a universal rule for each reader. The reader must allocate mixed functions consistently and match costs to the revenue scope chosen.
- **Negative and undefined:** $1,000 revenue minus $1,150 delivery cost is -$150 gross profit and -15% gross margin. With zero recognized revenue, the percentage is undefined regardless of cost, while gross profit remains revenue minus cost. Neither state authorizes a blanket verdict about the business. Compare periods or companies only after checking like-for-like revenue mix and accounting definitions; Atlassian notes mix and GAAP-versus-adjusted differences.
