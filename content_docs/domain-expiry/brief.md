## Who is here

A domain owner or prospective buyer needs the registry expiry date and wants to understand what happens after it.

## The job

They leave with time remaining or elapsed, the current lifecycle state, and an honest understanding that expiry is not a predicted availability date.

## Show vs say

- Lead with one large answer: time remaining during normal registration, or the current expiry state once the domain enters grace, redemption, or pending delete.
- Put the exact registry expiry date in the lead answer. Do not repeat it in a second date timeline.
- Show the conditional post-expiry process as an ordered stage list with stage, typical length, and consequence. Do not draw it on a time axis because the stages are not guaranteed and the durations are uneven.
- Compare registry and registrar expiry dates only when both exist and their displayed calendar dates differ.
- Show expiry-related EPP statuses in a table only when the registry returns them. Keep source and fetch time as quiet metadata and raw RDAP evidence inside a contained disclosure.
- Do not show a progress percentage or predict a release date. Neither has a reliable denominator or schedule.
- Explain variable grace behavior, ccTLD differences, auto-renew uncertainty, and drop-catching limits below the tool.
