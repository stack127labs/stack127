# KDP Price & Profit Calculator

Route: `/kdp-price-profit-calculator`

## Research

### User job

The distinct job is to compare feasible KDP print configurations for the same manuscript before choosing a format and price. A publisher needs to see how a change such as paperback versus hardcover, regular versus large trim, or black ink versus color changes print cost, cost-covering price, and royalty at a common marketplace and price assumption. Each configuration needs a page count that fits its actual or planned layout; changing trim can change pagination, so a shared page count is an assumption rather than a guarantee.

This must be a configuration comparison, not another single-book royalty estimate. The existing [KDP royalty calculator brief](../kdp-royalty-calculator/brief.md) handles one selected paperback, hardcover, or Kindle eBook scenario at a time. It already returns the per-sale royalty and cost receipt, a minimum price, a small set of price-boundary rows, break-even ad headroom, and optional monthly unit math. Readers can change the setup and compare results manually, but it does not compare named print configurations side by side. A generic price-to-royalty or price-versus-competitor calculator would overlap with that route and with SmartKDP's price sweep.

### Technical facts and sources

Checked official KDP help pages on 2026-09-28. Use them as the source of truth; third-party calculators can expose common user tasks but should not set formulas or current constants.

- Paperback printing cost is a fixed amount plus page count times the per-page rate. Current cost depends on marketplace, page count, ink and paper choice, and trim class. KDP's regular/large distinction is based on width or height; bleed and cover finish do not change print cost. Its tables include different page ranges and rates for black ink, groundwood, standard color, and premium color. Source: [Paperback Printing Cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340).
- Hardcover uses the same fixed-cost-plus-per-page structure, with separate rates by marketplace, page count, ink, and trim class. Its cost table is separate from paperback. Source: [Hardcover Printing Cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHT976ZKSKUXBB6H).
- KDP calculates a paperback's page count from its submitted manuscript, rounds an odd count up to an even number, and sets eligible page ranges by trim and interior. For a trim comparison, page counts may need to differ by scenario; this is an inference from the layout and eligibility rules, not an automatic calculation the price tool can make. Source: [Paperback Submission Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950).
- For Amazon.com, current paperback and hardcover print royalties use 50% at list prices up to $9.98 and 60% at $9.99 or more. The rate thresholds vary by marketplace. For Amazon.com, the print royalty is `(rate × list price) − printing cost`; the minimum list price must cover printing cost under the rate applicable at that price. Paperback and hardcover prices have a $250 US maximum. Sources: [Paperback Royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330) and [Hardcover Royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G77F3WPD3KQLJTFS).
- Verified example: a 300-page regular-trim black-ink paperback on Amazon.com costs `$1.00 + (300 × $0.012) = $4.60` to print. At a `$14.99` list price, the 60% rate gives an estimated royalty of `$4.39`. The cost example is in [KDP Paperback Printing Cost](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340); the royalty rule is in [KDP Paperback Royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330).
- KDP's public [Printing Cost and Royalty Calculator](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/royalty-calculator) is for paperback and hardcover, and excludes Expanded Distribution. Paperback Expanded Distribution uses a separate 40% royalty equation and changes the minimum price, so it should not be silently mixed into standard Amazon sale comparisons. The new task should stay print-only; the existing stack127 calculator already covers eBook pricing and delivery-cost rules.
- KDP royalty is not net author profit after editing, cover design, formatting, ads, taxes, refunds, or other author expenses. A result called profit would need explicit author-supplied costs and a stated sales quantity. KDP's official print calculator estimates printing cost and royalty, not those expenses.

Unknown: exact prices and eligibility depend on the chosen print options, marketplace, and KDP's current tables; future rate or cost changes cannot be predicted. Use a dated, versioned copy of the selected marketplace's published constants and recheck it against KDP before release or maintenance updates.

### Opportunity and blockers

Keep the distinct task centered on comparing multiple feasible KDP print configurations under the same manuscript, marketplace, and price assumptions. This makes the result a production-choice comparison; the current royalty tool remains the single-scenario economics calculator, while price sweeps and competitor price entry are already covered by SmartKDP. Call KDP's formula output a royalty unless the tool also includes the author's other expenses.

The calculation can be local-only: the inputs are user-entered print settings, candidate prices, and any optional author costs, while KDP's public rules can be stored as versioned constants. It needs no KDP account, paid API, live price scraping, server request, maintained third-party dataset, or background job. The constants do need a manual source check when KDP updates its rules. A live KDP data feed is not required, and its availability is unknown.

No implementation blocker found. The remaining product risk is naming and scope: if the tool does not compare print configurations side by side, the proposed route duplicates existing and observed competitor work. Competitor coverage for this specific comparison gap is not exhaustive because the targeted search failed.

## Product

### Promise and scope

Primary user: a self-publishing author deciding which KDP print edition of one manuscript to produce. The job is to compare two or three feasible paperback or hardcover configurations at one Amazon.com list price. The answer is a side-by-side view of each edition's printing cost, KDP minimum price, and estimated KDP royalty per sale. Each edition has its own binding, trim, interior and paper, and page count, since a new layout can change pagination. A shared price holds the selling assumption steady while the author compares production choices.

An optional cost mode projects profit after the author's entered expenses at the same planned sales count for each alternative. Without those explicit expenses and a sales count, the result is called **KDP royalty**, never author profit. With them, call it **estimated profit after entered costs**; unentered expenses, taxes, refunds, and actual demand remain outside that number. The alternatives are separate possible editions, so their projected profits are not added together.

This route is a configuration comparison. The published KDP royalty calculator already compares prices for one book setup, and the observed SmartKDP tool has a price sweep and manual competitor-price markers. The roadmap's simultaneous multi-price comparison is therefore deferred. Editing the shared price still lets the author rerun the same production comparison at another price. Launch covers Amazon.com, USD, standard Amazon sales, paperback and hardcover. It excludes Kindle eBooks, Expanded Distribution, other marketplaces or currencies, automatic repagination, competitor data, a price curve, demand forecasts, ACoS, taxes, refunds, price matching, promotions, and actual KDP acceptance decisions.

### Technical contract

**Inputs and bounds.** Start with two alternatives: paperback and hardcover, both 6 × 9 in, black ink on white paper, 300 entered pages, and one shared `$19.99` list price. The user may add a third alternative or remove one down to two. Order stays as entered. Each alternative selects a `paperback` or `hardcover` binding, one trim ID in `KDP_TRIMS` that supports that binding, one compatible interior and paper pair, and its own entered page count. Use the shared `getKdpTrimOptions`, `getKdpInteriorOptions`, `getKdpPaperOptions`, and `getKdpPageLimit` rules; do not create an independent option list. A profile can match another alternative because the author may be testing its page count. No label, title, ISBN, upload, or sales history is needed. The shared list price is `$0.01` to `$250.00` in exact USD cents; prices below a configuration's computed floor produce an ineligible row, rather than a global input error.

The optional `Include my costs` mode starts off. When enabled, require one planned copy count from `1` to `1,000,000` whole copies and, for **each** alternative, an explicit other cost per sale from `$0.00` to `$1,000.00` and an explicit one-time cost from `$0.00` to `$100,000.00`. Zero is a deliberate entry; blank means unknown and blocks the profit calculation. These author costs may differ by edition. The same planned count applies to every alternative for a controlled comparison and is an assumption, not a sales forecast. Cost mode does not alter KDP royalty or the KDP minimum price.

**Parsing and eligibility.** Trim outer whitespace from numeric text. Accept only plain base-10 digits for pages and copies, and digits with at most two decimal places for USD; reject signs, commas, currency symbols, exponents, non-finite values, and extra decimals. Convert money to integer cents before arithmetic. Entered pages must be a positive safe integer no greater than 828; KDP charges an effective even page count, `enteredPages + (enteredPages mod 2)`. Validate that count against the selected binding, trim, interior, and paper page limits. Changing binding or trim must leave the alternative with a supported profile or require the user to choose one; never silently calculate an unsupported combination. A missing or malformed shared price, unsupported setup, out-of-range page count, wrong alternative count, or incomplete enabled cost mode is an input error. Keep all entered text available for correction and return no comparison until structural inputs are valid.

**KDP calculations.** Use the versioned US rules in `lib/kdp/specs.ts` and `lib/kdp/royalty.ts`, with official KDP paperback and hardcover cost, royalty, and submission pages in Research as the authority. Third-party calculators never set constants. For each alternative, select its applicable fixed and per-page print-cost rule, then `printingCostCents = fixedCents + roundHalfUp(perPageUSD × 100 × effectivePages)`. Use the shared engine's cent rounding and cost-class rules; trim dimensions alone do not add a charge within a class. Find the cost-covering floor as follows: if `ceil(printingCostCents / 0.50) <= 998`, use that value; otherwise use `max(999, ceil(printingCostCents / 0.60))`. The maximum is 25,000 cents. For a shared price at or above a row's floor, the Amazon.com rate is 50% through 998 cents and 60% from 999 cents, and `royaltyCents = roundHalfUp(priceCents × rate) − printingCostCents`. Do not compute a sellable royalty or profit for a row below its floor. Never substitute another market's costs or Expanded Distribution's 40% formula.

For an eligible row with cost mode enabled, calculate in cents: `profitForPlannedCopiesCents = plannedCopies × (royaltyCents − otherCostPerSaleCents) − oneTimeCostCents`. Show that exact total in USD. Negative profit is a valid outcome. Multiply rounded per-sale royalty by copies before subtracting costs; do not multiply an unrounded rate. Expenses are entered separately for each alternative and apply to that alternative only.

**Output and states.** `Compare print options` is the one primary action. After valid submission, show the shared price and a comparison in entered order with binding, trim, interior/paper, effective pages, printing cost, KDP minimum price, eligibility at the shared price, royalty rate, and estimated royalty per sale. When cost mode is complete, add each eligible row's estimated profit after entered costs for the planned copy count and the entered cost assumptions. Keep royalty and profit as different labeled columns. A simple difference in royalty or modeled profit from the first eligible row may help the author compare, but do not declare the highest number the best book or recommend a price. Show an odd-page note only where entered and effective counts differ. For a row below its floor, show the floor and printing cost plus `Below KDP minimum at this price`; leave royalty and profit unavailable. This is a useful partial comparison if another row is eligible. If every row is below its floor, show all floors and a clear no-eligible-options result, with no fabricated zero royalties. If a cost input is blank while cost mode is enabled, block the submitted comparison and identify the field; do not silently treat it as zero.

Editing any input or adding, removing, or reordering an alternative clears the previous comparison until resubmission. Reset restores the two defaults, `$19.99`, cost mode off, and no result. Calculation is synchronous and local, so no network loading or normal retry state is needed. If versioned rules are missing, a cost is not finite, the floor exceeds the `$250.00` maximum, or calculation fails unexpectedly, preserve inputs, clear stale economics, and show a recoverable unavailable state naming the cause. Unknown future KDP rules are never filled with guesses. Show the local rules version and source review date with links to the applicable official KDP pages, and ask the author to confirm each edition in KDP before pricing it. The current shared rules version is `2026-09-16`; Research checked the primary pages on `2026-09-28`, and the builder must recheck them before release.

All book settings and cost assumptions stay in the browser during use. No account, file upload, live KDP request, server calculation, saved scenario, URL state, local storage, or input-bearing analytics event is required. Treat all input as data and render it as text, never HTML.

## Interface

### Flow

User question: "At the same Amazon.com price, how do these print editions compare in KDP royalty and, if I enter my costs, estimated profit?" The minimum task needs one shared USD list price and two print setups, each with binding, trim, interior/paper, and entered pages. The only primary action is `Compare print options`. Keep `Amazon.com · USD · standard Amazon sales` visible as a quiet scope line by the form; it is a fixed rule, not a marketplace control.

Use one control mode. In reading order: shared list price; `Print options` with two numbered setup sections and an optional third; `Include my costs` checkbox; conditional planned copies and a cost row for each current option; Compare print options and quiet Reset; result. The default paperback and hardcover setups from Product are visible and ready to compare, but no result appears until submission. Each setup section has a visible `Option 1`, `Option 2`, or `Option 3` legend, a two-choice binding group, trim select, combined `Interior and paper` select, and raw-text `Printed pages` field. The combined profile maps to the shared interior/paper pair and offers only valid combinations. `Add option` appears only while there are two; `Remove option` appears for each section only while there are three. Rows keep stable internal IDs, and removing one renumbers the visible legends without moving another row's values. Keep insertion order; no drag or reorder control is needed for three options.

Cost mode is a checkbox because its fields take effect on the next submit. When checked, reveal the required shared `Planned copies sold per option` field, then one compact `Option N costs` row for each setup with `Other cost per sale (USD)` and `One-time cost (USD)`. A short line beside this group says to enter `0` when a cost does not apply; blank is unknown. The option number stays matched to its print setup. These fields are not placed in a disclosure: once cost mode is on, they change the answer and are required. Turning the mode off hides and excludes them from the result while keeping any typed values available if the author turns it back on; Reset clears them. Do not show a profit column, profit lead, or zero-cost assumption while the mode is off.

The comparison itself is the answer. On a valid submission, put one focusable `UiResultLead` after the workbench divider: `2 of 2 options meet KDP's minimum at $19.99` for the default case. Directly below, identify the shared 60% rate for eligible rows; the rate belongs here once because the price is shared. If cost mode is on, also identify the shared planned copy count once. Then show one `UiDataTable`, with rows in the same order as the editors. Its row header is `Option N` plus binding, trim, interior/paper, and effective pages. Columns are `Printing cost`, `KDP minimum price`, and `Estimated KDP royalty per sale`; cost mode adds `Your costs` (`$X per sale + $Y one time`) and `Estimated profit after entered costs`. A row below the floor says `Below KDP minimum at this price` in the royalty column, and its profit cell says `Unavailable`; neither cell presents `$0.00` as a payout. No extra price sweep, chart, metric cards, separate cost receipt, ranking, or duplicated royalty figure is needed. A short note after the table identifies only rows whose odd entered count became an even effective count. Follow with `Estimate. Confirm each edition in KDP before setting its price.` and one quiet `Calculation details` disclosure for the rule version, review date, applicable KDP links, and exclusions. The table and the row-specific status own the numbers; supporting prose explains limits without restating them.

### Usage, limits, and next step

Use one realistic decision path: an author compares a paperback and hardcover at one intended Amazon.com price, enters the page count each layout would actually have, and reads printing cost, minimum price, and royalty in the same row. If the hardcover is below its minimum, show how to reconsider the price or setup and compare again, without inventing a royalty for that row. If they know their per-sale and one-time expenses, they can turn on cost mode, enter an explicit zero where a cost does not apply, use one planned copy count for all alternatives, and interpret the conditional profit column. A blank enabled cost, non-whole page count, or unsupported profile gets an inline field error; correct the named field and compare again. Keep this guidance about choices and recovery, not a sequence of obvious clicks.

Make limits adjacent to the claims they qualify: USD and Amazon.com only, standard Amazon sales, paperback and hardcover, local rules rather than a live KDP response, no automatic pagination, and profit limited to entered expenses and assumed copies.
